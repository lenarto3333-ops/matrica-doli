# 15. The Devil — Career and Professional Fulfillment

## Brief Essence

The career story of the fifteenth energy is one of personal magnetism: a person is fulfilled through the ability to attract attention, resources and people, to see what the market or audience really wants, and to translate that vision into influence and income. Professions related to persuasion, sales, publicity, psychology of influence or work with finances and resources are close to such a person.

## Natural Strength

A natural force manifests itself in the role of a negotiator, salesperson, public figure, psychologist, financier or anyone who works with human motivation and desires. A person feels the hidden needs of customers, partners or the audience and knows how to offer exactly what really resonates.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, a person builds influence and wealth honestly: their charisma attracts opportunities, and a deep understanding of human nature helps them create products, services or deals that really meet the needs of others. Career grows due to the reputation of a person who is trusted and who knows how to negotiate on mutually beneficial terms.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the same insight turns into manipulativeness: a person uses knowledge of other people's weaknesses and desires to pressure, sell unnecessary or maintain his own power over colleagues and clients. Workaholic dependence on work, status or income is also possible, when career becomes the only source of self-worth.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a work environment built on competition and the cult of results at any cost, where manipulative tactics are rewarded rather than honest ones. The second trigger is one's own insecurity, which a person drowns out with status, income, or power over others, substituting them for a real sense of worth.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to consciously check whether the offer or agreement is really in the other party's interests, not just your own. The second step is to develop sources of self-worth outside of work, so that career success is not the only support for a sense of self-importance.

## Main Task

Learn to use your natural attraction and deep understanding of people to build influence and wealth that benefits all parties, not just for personal gain or control.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a situation in my work where I know that I am pressuring or persuading a person for my own benefit, and not for his real benefit?
- How much does my sense of self-worth depend on my career status or income?

## Practical Plan

1. Before the next important agreement or proposal, consciously check whether it honestly meets the needs of the other party.
2. Identify one source of self-worth outside of work and dedicate time to it during the week.
3. Review your working relationship once a month for hidden pressure or manipulation—on both sides.
