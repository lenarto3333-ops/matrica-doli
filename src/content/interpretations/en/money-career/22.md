# 22. The Fool — Career and Professional Fulfillment

## Brief Essence

The career story of the twenty-second energy is the story of a person who naturally gravitates towards freedom and new experiences and is best revealed where you can learn from scratch, try different things and not stick to rigid frameworks. Such a person is close to professions related to travel, creative experiments, startups, or any activity where a fresh perspective is valued rather than strict regulations.

## Natural Strength

Natural strength is manifested in the ability to easily start a new business without fear of the unknown and in sincere trust that the necessary opportunities and people will appear on time. The talent here is flexibility and quick adaptation, which allows you to feel natural even in a completely new field or role.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a person boldly tries new directions, easily changes course when necessary, and inspires the team with his sincerity and spontaneity. She trusts her own path, but at the same time brings what she has started to a real result and is responsible for the commitments she has made.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, a person either changes work, projects or directions too often and recklessly, not having time to bring any of them to fruition and avoiding responsibility for the consequences of such decisions, or, on the contrary, clings to an uninteresting, restrictive job for years, fearing any professional change. It is also possible to take serious work commitments lightly, when promises are made but not fulfilled.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a feeling of routine and monotony, which pushes you to suddenly jump into another project without a plan and preparation. The second trigger is the experience of an unsuccessful attempt to change work or direction, after which there is a fear that any change will end just as badly.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to consciously weigh the consequences before each significant professional change and take responsibility for the commitments made, rather than leaving things halfway. The second step is to regularly allow yourself controlled professional experiments, so that fear of the new does not replace development with stagnation. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

To learn to combine the freedom of choosing a new path with responsibility for commitments already made and not to allow the fear of change to turn a career into a frozen routine.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- How many projects or work lines have I started and not completed due to loss of interest?
- Am I holding on to my current job only out of fear of the unknown, and not because it really suits me?

## Practical Plan

1. Make a list of unfinished professional endeavors and choose one that should either be completed or deliberately closed.
2. If you've been wanting to change direction for a long time, take one small tentative step—a course, a conversation, a test project—instead of a sudden leap or complete inaction.
3. Once a quarter, check whether your work decisions are dictated by an informed choice, or rather by momentary impulse or fear.
