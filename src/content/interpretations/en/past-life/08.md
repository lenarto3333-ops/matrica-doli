# 8. Justice — Tasks from Past Incarnations

## In Brief

This position points to experience closely connected with law, judgment, arbitration, or personal responsibility for the truth—perhaps the role of a judge, elder, witness, or someone whose decisions affected other people's destinies; or, conversely, the role of someone treated unjustly whose voice went unheard. There was likely an unsettled account in the past: either the soul harmed someone and had no time or opportunity to make amends, or someone wronged this soul and justice was never restored. The task of this incarnation is to complete what began before: to settle old karmic accounts not through revenge or another trial, but through mature understanding and forgiveness. The past brings a rare sense of law and honesty, yet leaves a tendency to carry the burden of matters that should have been released long ago.

## Natural Strength

From past experience, this energy brings an innate sense of law and balance: the ability to recognize instantly when something is unfair, even without an explanation. This is not an acquired skill—it is like the soul's memory of a time when accurately distinguishing truth from falsehood was a matter of the community's survival, not merely a character trait.

A second inherited quality is the natural ability to make balanced decisions. In past circumstances, this skill was often a form of service: people sought this person's fair verdict precisely because they trusted their impartiality.

A third strength is steadfast inner honesty that operates almost automatically. Past experience probably taught this soul that a broken promise or concealed lie has far-reaching consequences, hence the deep, almost instinctive need to keep one's word in this life.

A fourth quality is an acute sense of incompletion wherever justice was not restored. Past experience in which truth never prevailed or guilt was never acknowledged has left a heightened sensitivity to similar situations today—an ability that can become either a source of anxious rumination over old grievances or, conversely, a gift for mature, wise mediation.

## Positive Expression

In its positive expression, past involvement with law and justice appears as a natural ability to be fair without constantly needing to prove it. The person trusts their own sense of truth while allowing themselves to forgive where they might once have regarded forgiveness as a betrayal of principle.

The honesty carried from the past becomes a consciously used resource: the ability to resolve conflict, see the truth behind opposing versions of events, and support someone who has been wronged—applied selectively, rather than as a permanent mission to set the whole world right.

In its positive expression, past experience of injustice, if there was any, becomes a wise rather than bitter attitude toward fairness. The person values the opportunity to restore balance without demanding that someone must lose or be punished for an old offense.

Another positive sign is the ability to settle an old account through a symbolic act of forgiveness, ending a long-standing karmic pattern with a new, more integrated experience of inner peace instead of continually waiting for compensation.

### Expression in Women in the Positive

In women, past experience involving truth and law appears positively as calm, mature honesty. She speaks the truth where it is needed and allows herself to release an old grievance without feeling that forgiveness means accepting injustice as normal.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

In men, past responsibility for justice or fair decisions appears positively as calm, measured integrity. He stands up for the truth when necessary and does not cling to personal resentment once the lesson has been learned.

## Negative Expression

In its negative expression, past experience of unresolved injustice appears as a chronic tendency to carry old grievances as though they happened yesterday. The person continues to put long-past events on trial in their mind, seeking belated recognition or punishment for those at fault.

### Expression in Women in the Negative

In women, this may appear as a deeply rooted feeling that they were treated unjustly and that the world still owes them compensation, sometimes becoming chronic bitterness that colors new, unrelated relationships with old, unspoken pain.

### Expression in Men in the Negative

In men, this may appear as excessive severity in the role of “the one who administers justice,” even when no one has asked them to do so. Every situation becomes grounds for a verdict, and others are continually required to prove that they are right.

If the past included an unjust verdict, a betrayal of trust, or personal guilt that could not be made right, this may appear in the present life as irrational anxiety around any ambiguity, an excessive need to control “who is right,” or, conversely, a fear of making decisions that affect others.

## What Can Pull the Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a situation that recalls past injustice or personal guilt: deception, a broken promise, or a moment when someone plainly evades responsibility. An old, disproportionately intense reaction may awaken here.

A second trigger is the need to remain silent about an obvious falsehood to preserve peace, which conflicts with the deeply rooted role of “the truth-teller” inherited from past experience.

A third trigger is the sense that an old wrong has never received recognition or compensation. This is especially painful for a soul accustomed to a clear and visible restoration of balance.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to recognize that past involvement with law and justice taught valuable skills, but does not require carrying the burden of every unresolved account from the past in this life.

The second step is to practice forgiveness and symbolic closure consciously whenever the familiar impulse arises to revisit an old grievance or guilt in one's thoughts.

The third step is to explore where a disproportionately strong reaction to ambiguous, “unclear” situations may come from, and gradually learn to release the need for immediate certainty about “who is right.”

The fourth step is to return regularly to practices that help settle old accounts symbolically—writing and burning a letter, a forgiveness ritual, or an honest conversation with oneself—as a way to make room for the present.

The fifth step is to allow oneself to trust that not every injustice of the past must be personally corrected in this lifetime, noticing how releasing an old burden frees energy for a new, unclouded experience of honesty.

## Main Task

The main task of this incarnation is to complete the cycle of unsettled karmic accounts begun in the past and learn to be honest and fair from a present place of calm rather than an ancient place of pain. Past involvement with law and justice is a genuine gift worth preserving, but not at the price of constantly carrying old grievances. The true maturity of this energy lies in the ability to defend the truth when necessary and release what should have been let go long ago.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there an old grievance I still carry as though it happened yesterday?
- Do I allow myself to forgive without requiring the other person to admit guilt?
- Where does my anxiety around ambiguous situations come from—the real circumstances of this life or an older source?
- Can I distinguish genuine injustice from a situation I view through the lens of old pain?
- What do the words “truth,” “judgment,” “guilt,” and “forgiveness” awaken in me—calm or tension?
- What honesty from the past would I like to preserve, and what old grievance would I like to leave behind?

## Practical Plan

1. This week, write a letter about an old grievance or guilt, then symbolically destroy it as an act of closure.
2. Record one situation in which uncertainty about “who is right” provoked a disproportionately strong reaction, and explore where that reaction may have come from.
3. Practice unconditional forgiveness: mentally or aloud, release one debt without waiting for acknowledgment from the other person.
4. Once this week, allow yourself not to deliver a verdict in someone else's situation and simply listen.
5. If the pattern of carrying old grievances recurs, consciously interrupt it once this week and choose present peace over returning to past pain.
