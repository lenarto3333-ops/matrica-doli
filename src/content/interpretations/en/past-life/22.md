# 22. The Fool — Tasks from Past Incarnations

## Brief Essence

This position indicates an experience in which the person had a prominent role as one who lives freely—perhaps as a wanderer or wanderer without a fixed home, as someone outside the rigid rules of his environment, or simply as someone with a highly developed lightness of character who was turned to when a breath of freshness and hope needed to be brought into a difficult, stagnant situation. Probably, in that life, this ability either led to the severe consequences of irresponsibility - to escape from important obligations, which left behind destroyed relationships or unfinished business, and someone else paid a high price for this ease, or, on the contrary, turned into a painful experience of punishment for the very attempt to be free - for spontaneity, risk or otherness, which the world around perceived as a threat and cruelly limited. Perhaps, in that life, a person either realized too late the price of his own irresponsibility and lost something precious due to a thoughtless step, or, on the contrary, learned too early and too harshly that freedom is dangerous, and locked himself in a forced framework, losing natural lightness forever. That is why in this incarnation there is simultaneously a natural feeling of freedom and trust in life, and a deep inner tension around the right to take risks without punishment. The task of this incarnation is to complete what has been done before: to learn to live freely and trust fate, keeping responsibility for the consequences of one's decisions, without needing either chaotic escape or defensive self-restraint.

## Natural Strength

From past experience, this energy brings a rare, deeply developed ability to experience life as a flow that can be trusted — as if the soul's memory of a time when the ability to easily start over was already actively used. That is why today this person often feels a strange confidence in what is a real challenge for others - being able to let go of the old and go to meet the new without unnecessary hesitation.

The second inherited trait is a deep, almost instinctive understanding of the value of freedom. This soul knows intuitively how much power and joy is hidden in life without rigid frameworks, a knowledge that can be either a gift of conscious, mature ease or a burden of hidden fear before any commitment that once cost dearly.

The third strength is the ability to feel the line between healthy risk and recklessness. Past experience probably taught this soul dearly that both excessive caution and reckless adventurism cost too much—it left behind a rare ability to recognize in time when to take a risk and when to stop and think about the consequences.

The fourth feature is a deep understanding of the price of lost freedom or, conversely, the price of harm caused to others due to one's own irresponsibility. Past experiences in which freedom may have never been achieved or, on the contrary, came at too high a price of someone else's pain, have left a heightened sensitivity to the question "do I have the right to make this decision" - a capacity that in this life can be either a gift of wise, measured freedom, or a burden of chronic fear of repeating the same loss or the same harm.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, past experience turns into a mature wisdom about freedom and responsibility: a person intuitively understands when to trust a new path and when to stay and see things through, and lives life with a rare inner balance between lightness and a realistic assessment of the consequences.

The gift of trusting the flow of life, inherited from the past, is revealed as the ability to show others that it is possible to live more easily - not from the position of escaping from difficulties, but from a sincere desire to pass on the freedom that she once received or learned to find herself. Her trust in fate carries weight because it is based on lived rather than ostentatious experience of risk.

Respect for the price that previously had to be paid for lost freedom or for harm done to others turns into the ability to build new relationships with risk on a stronger, more honest foundation - not because a person lacks courage, but because he already knows: true freedom does not require either mindless escape or complete self-restraint to be real.

In addition, there is also the ability to trust one's own sense of the right moment without fear that it will again lead to loss or punishment: a person consciously allows himself to take risks, while maintaining a connection with the valuable lesson that the experience of the past gave him.

Another sign is the ability to appreciate quiet, real freedom, in contrast to the ostentatious carefreeness of previous experiences. Past experience has taught me to take my time with a decision, and in this life this wisdom translates into the ability to consciously and patiently choose when to move on and when to stay, taking my time and not freezing with fear.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, past experience in the positive manifests as a deep inner sense of freedom without a chronic need to immediately abandon everything or, on the contrary, to control everything: she naturally sees when it is worth taking a risk, and allows herself and others time for a balanced decision, and this ability is no longer connected with the fear of punishment for a past lost opportunity or damage caused.

Her sense of the right moment in difficult circumstances is not a manifestation of the need to escape from everything at once, but a sincere expression of experienced wisdom. She has an intuitive sense of when a situation calls for a bold move and when it calls for patience and caution, and this sensitivity comes with a clarity that is surprising to her.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, the inherited experience manifests itself as a refined, calm freedom - he does not take excessive risks because of every opportunity to start something new and does not lock himself in the denial of his own need for movement, because, probably, past experience has already taught him that both extremes are equally expensive.

He naturally knows when the case or the people around him need his decisive freedom of action, and when - patient constancy without hasty escape, reacting from this clarity, not succumbing to the need to be the only one who determines the limit of risk. It comes across as a rare poise for him, which is actually a well-honed skill from the past to carry his gift without excess.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, past experience manifests itself as a hidden tendency to escape suddenly, which has not yet been released: a person can subconsciously abandon important affairs or relationships again and again, as if internally convinced that any commitment will sooner or later become a trap from which it will be necessary to escape.

Another manifestation of the negative is excessive, almost obsessive caution as a tool to protect against the repetition of a past loss or punishment. In this embodiment, it manifests as a chronic inability to trust new experiences, as if internally the person is still afraid of the pain they experienced earlier, when the risk was too expensive.

Minus can also look like a wobble between two extremes without a stable middle: a person either tries to escape from any responsibility at once, demanding complete freedom in everything, then suddenly completely refuses any risk, fearing to repeat the past loss - and this cycle repeats itself again and again, preventing real, stable freedom from taking hold.

In the deeper negative, there is a tendency to internal condemnation - one's own or another's - for any spontaneity or, conversely, for any caution - as if the mind remembers a time when both excessive freedom and excessive control were very expensive. This manifests as a habit of punishing yourself for every choice that involves risk or commitment.

The fifth manifestation of the minus is a chronic fear of new, unpredictable experiences with no guarantee of a result, paradoxically hidden behind an external calmness: a person fears that any attempt to trust life is actually a trap for new disappointment or loss, and unconsciously avoids even healthy, reasonable opportunities to expand one's own limits.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, the negative manifestation of past experience often looks like a chronic distrust of one's own right to calm, not chaotic freedom - even when the internal state fully allows a sincere and realistic assessment of the risk. She can seek confirmation of her own rightness in how carefree she is able to act, instead of allowing herself a sincere, measured lightness that does not cost her strength.

In business or relationships, this manifests as a fear of allowing oneself to fully trust in a new path that seems "too risky to trust", which forces one to either keep a constant readiness to control everything on one's own, or, on the contrary, to suddenly and thoughtlessly flee from large obligations under the influence of exhaustion, in order to finally get rid of the unbearable pressure.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, the negative manifestation is often disguised as relentless caution - a way to protect himself from the fear of reliving a missed opportunity or a punishment that was once very costly. He may deliberately avoid accepting the right to freedom of action in any sphere or relationship, fearing that any risk will again lead to a loss of status or respect from those around him.

The tendency to hidden caution in the minus manifests itself as unexpected, sharp attempts to prove one's carelessness just when external restraint has lasted too long - as if an inner voice convinces that it is better to bring everything to extreme freedom immediately than to allow oneself to remain "not free enough".

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is situations that remind of a past missed opportunity or a painful experience of punishment for freedom: sudden criticism of a risky decision, a feeling that the act was evaluated unfairly harshly, the pressure of circumstances that remind "I am being punished again for spontaneity" or "I am again losing something valuable because of my own lightness." Even the slightest hint of such a scenario can activate disproportionately strong anxiety, the root of which is an unlived experience.

The second trigger is the lingering uncertainty about whether it is worth taking a risk, which paradoxically causes internal tension, because past experience has taught us not to trust unpredictability as something safe. Since the past has developed a readiness for sudden, sharp punishment, the very state of "not yet resolved" can feel like a threat.

The third trigger is the need to make a clear choice about whether to stay in a familiar, safe routine or, on the contrary, decisively trust in a new experience - stick to the proven or risk the unfamiliar. Because past experience has taught us that any extreme can be very costly, the forced certainty at such times is especially strong in activating anxiety and the desire to avoid the decision altogether.

The fourth trigger is contact with people or circumstances that remind - in atmosphere or behavior - of a past lost opportunity or, on the contrary, of an open experience of punishment for freedom, even if the person is not consciously aware of it. The reaction in such a contact is often disproportionate to the situation, because it comes not from the present moment, but from ancient experience.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to realize that the current tendency toward chaotic escape or, conversely, toward drastic self-restriction may not be rooted in the current situation, but in a long-standing experience of loss or an unfinished cycle of freedom and responsibility. A simple recognition that "maybe it's not about now, but about then" already reduces the intensity of the reaction and gives space for a more conscious choice.

The second step is to consciously practice a moderate, realistic assessment of risk where previously there would be an automatic desire to either run away without looking back or to refuse any movement at all: if you notice a repeating scenario, it is worth stopping and asking yourself what is driving it - a real need or a long-standing habit.

The third step is to learn to feel the difference between a healthy desire to trust the new available today and the ancient fear of repeating a past loss or punishment that is left over from the past but no longer corresponds to present security.

The fourth step is to consciously appreciate and allow yourself the freedom that this life gives you: notice at least one moment every day when you managed to honestly combine lightness and responsibility, and consciously allow yourself to trust him, even in small things.

The fifth step is to restore confidence in one's own ability to take decent risks, gradually, where in the past it probably cost a lot: in small steps, to allow oneself both freedom and caution, which this time do not carry a real threat.

The sixth step is to consciously choose freedom as a conscious choice, not as a way to avoid repeating the past, by doing it gradually: practicing alternating between realistic risk assessment and concrete, conscious stepping into a new experience, rather than being stuck at one of the poles.

## Main Task

The main task of this position is to end the long-standing cycle in which freedom and risk were very expensive, and to learn to live one's own ability to trust fate and easily start anew as a gift, not as a memory of a lost experience or an unresolved experience of punishment. It means learning to consciously alternate between a realistic assessment of risk and trust in life, while maintaining full confidence in one's right to real, not chaotic, freedom regardless of external circumstances, something one probably failed to realize in past experience.

When this task is accomplished, the gift of trusting the flow of life and the ability to easily start anew, inherited from the past, cease to be a source of hidden tension and swing between poles and become a real gift - a tool that one uses generously and freely in moments when one really needs to take a risk or let go of the old, no longer afraid that any freedom or any commitment will turn into a new disappointment.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a decision in my life that I avoid making more strictly than it really is necessary, for fear of repeating a past loss due to my own lightness?
- What do I feel at the moment when I can either allow myself to take an honest and calm risk, or strain again from the old habit of running away or shutting down?
- To what extent is my tendency to irresponsibility or, on the contrary, to self-limitation connected with the fear of reliving a lost opportunity or painful experience of punishment for freedom?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one situation of vacillating between excessive flight and total aversion to risk that is repeated and describe it in writing from the first manifestation to the present.
2. Once a day, make one small, concrete gesture of trusting your own sense of the right moment—to allow yourself a little freedom or a little caution where before there would have been an extreme.
3. Pick one unfinished business from the past and consciously take a step toward completing it this week, even on a small scale.
4. Before a strong desire to escape or, on the contrary, completely limit yourself, ask yourself: "Is this really necessary now, or is it an ancient fear from another time?"
5. Create one moment of conscious, balanced summing up this week — where before there would have been a desire for either chaotic risk-taking or complete avoidance of decisions.
6. Consciously practice alternating: devote one day to a realistic assessment of readiness for risk, the other to a conscious step into a new experience without too much looking back.
