# 11. Strength — Tasks from Past Incarnations

## Brief Essence

This position points to an experience in which a person's fate was closely tied to taming something powerful, wild, or dangerous—perhaps as a warrior, a trainer of wild animals, a healer working with other people's severe and turbulent states, or a guardian whose strength determined whether someone lived or died. The soul may have held real power over the life, body, or will of others, and the way it used that power—with cruelty or mercy—defines the central task of this incarnation. This life's task is to complete what was begun before: preserve the acquired endurance and inner power while learning to apply them through gentleness rather than coercion or fear. The past gives rare fortitude, but leaves a tendency either to fear one's power excessively or to use it too directly, without enough sensitivity to others.

If strength was used primarily with wisdom and care in the past—to protect the vulnerable, heal, or justly tame danger—this incarnation inherits an especially luminous, mature inner foundation. The task is then to trust and share that strength rather than hide it through excessive modesty.

## Natural Strength

From past experience, this energy brings rare physical and emotional endurance: the capacity to sustain prolonged strain, danger, or responsibility where others would have broken much sooner. This is not an acquired skill, but resembles a memory of body and soul from a time when survival depended directly on inner resilience.

A second inherited quality is an intuitive understanding of power over something strong. In earlier circumstances, this soul may have learned that brute force cannot truly tame anything wild; patience, calm, and consistency are needed. This gives the soul a natural ability today to soothe tense situations through presence alone.

A third strength is profound inner courage before danger or conflict. Past experience of facing a genuine threat left the capacity not to panic when others lose composure.

A fourth quality is heightened sensitivity to the consequences of using force. Past experience likely taught this soul how much the manner in which power is used matters. In this life, that sensitivity may become either excessive anxiety about any expression of personal power or, conversely, a gift for exercising influence wisely and responsibly.

## Positive Expression

In the positive, past experience of taming appears as wise, gentle strength. A person trusts their power while applying it with care and consideration, understanding deeply that true authority lies not in subjugation but in protection.

Past endurance becomes a resource used consciously: the ability to work patiently on a difficult task, support others in a crisis, and remain a stable foundation where endurance rather than hasty decisions is needed.

In the positive, a difficult past experience of real authority becomes a mature, responsible attitude toward any influence over others. The person carefully weighs the consequences of their actions, remembering how serious they can be.

Another positive sign is the ability to trust one's strength without fear, bringing an old pattern of excessive caution—or excessive directness—to a close through a mature balance of firmness and compassion.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, positive past experience of taming appears as calm resilience combined with deep empathy. She accepts her strength with dignity, neither fearing nor abusing it, and uses this resource to support those around her.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, positive past experience of authority or protection appears as calm, measured courage. He neither fears conflict or danger nor deliberately seeks them, trusting a strength refined through long experience of responsible use.

## Negative Expression

In the negative, past experience of taming something dangerous appears as chronic anxiety about personal power. A person continues to fear their strength even in entirely safe circumstances, as if still living where any error in the use of authority carried an unbearable cost.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, this may appear as a deeply rooted habit of diminishing her influence and strength, including continually understating her achievements and needs, as though acknowledging her power would automatically endanger others.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, it may appear as using force too directly and harshly while disguising it as necessity, when it is actually an attempt to recreate control that once seemed the only way to survive or protect what mattered.

If the past included direct abuse of authority or, conversely, helplessness before another's cruelty, this life may bring an irrational fear of any dominance, excessive softness in situations requiring firmness, or a tendency toward excessive control, as though subconsciously testing whether strength will be used correctly this time.

## What Can Shift the Energy into the Negative

The strongest triggers are situations reminiscent of past encounters with danger or the need to apply strength: conflicts requiring defense of oneself or another, and moments when a decision affects other people's fate.

A second trigger is a prolonged demand to remain constantly strong and enduring without the right to rest, recalling past circumstances where weakness was an unaffordable luxury.

A third is the feeling of having to exercise authority or influence without sufficient confidence that it is right, which is especially difficult for a soul that understands deeply how serious the consequences of error can be.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to acknowledge that past experience of taming taught valuable endurance and strength but does not require constant anxiety about personal power in this life.

The second is to practice applying influence gently and gradually, checking each time that present circumstances do not demand the harshness that may once have been necessary for survival.

The third is to explore the source of either an irrational fear of personal strength or an urge toward excessive control, gradually learning to trust the mature, responsible use of one's power.

The fourth is to return regularly to practices that combine strength and care: setting boundaries consciously, followed by sincere attention to their impact on others.

The fifth is to allow yourself to be strong without constantly expecting that your strength will inevitably harm someone, observing how trust rather than anxiety becomes a new and genuine experience.

## Main Task

The main task of this incarnation is to complete a cycle begun in the past—a fear of personal power or, conversely, excessive control—and learn to become a responsible, gentle steward of one's strength. Past endurance and courage are genuine gifts worth preserving, but not at the cost of constant anxiety or harshness. The true maturity of this energy lies in being strong and caring at the same time.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I feel that my strength is dangerous even without real evidence?
- Do I exercise influence gently and responsibly, or always keep harsh control in reserve?
- Does my fear of personal power come from real circumstances in this life or from an older source?
- Can I distinguish measured firmness from an attempt to recreate the control of past experience?
- What do the words “authority,” “strength,” and “control” evoke in me—calm or anxiety?
- What endurance from the past would I like to preserve, and what constant anxiety or harshness would I leave behind?

## Practical Plan

1. Once this week, consciously use your strength to protect or support someone and do not be afraid to acknowledge it.
2. Write down one situation in which irrational anxiety about your influence arose, and explore where it may have come from.
3. Practice setting a conscious boundary: identify one situation requiring firmness and apply it calmly, without aggression.
4. Allow yourself one moment of complete trust in your strength this week, without expecting it to harm someone.
5. If a pattern of excessive control or excessive softness recurs, interrupt it once this week and make a measured rather than automatic choice.
