# 18. The Moon — Tasks from Past Incarnations

## Brief Essence

This position indicates an experience in which a person has had a marked gift of intuition, premonition, or a deep connection with the subconscious—perhaps as a seer, a healer, a person they come to for advice in dark times, or simply someone whose sensitivity sets them apart from others. It is likely that in that life this gift either led to persecution, mistrust or fear from those around them who did not understand or feared what they could not explain rationally, or, on the contrary, the person himself abused this sensitivity, allowing fears and suspicions to guide decisions, which cost him dearly himself or those around him. Perhaps in that life a person either lost trust in their own intuition after a painful mistake or betrayal, or became so confused between reality and illusion that it led to devastating consequences. That is why, in this embodiment, there is simultaneously a natural, deep sensitivity, and an inner tension around trusting one's own inner voice. The task of this incarnation is to complete what was done earlier: to learn to carry your gift clearly and calmly, distinguishing true knowledge from a disturbing illusion.

## Natural Strength

From past experience, this energy brings a rare, deeply developed intuition — as if the soul's memory of a time when this gift was already actively used. That is why today this person often feels a strange ease in what for others is a real challenge - reading hidden moods, premonitions of events, understanding symbols and dreams.

The second inherited trait is a deep, almost instinctive understanding of how the imaginary affects the real. This soul knows intuitively how an image, intention, or inner vision can materialize, a knowledge that can be either a gift of conscious creation or a burden of unsettling premonitions that are hard not to succumb to.

The third power is the ability to feel the difference between subtle energies and simple fiction. Past experience must have taught this soul dearly that not every feeling can be trusted without verification—it left behind a rare ability to value real, verified knowledge more than a momentary impression.

The fourth feature is a deep understanding of the price of lost self-confidence. A past experience in which intuition may have been dismissed or misused just when it most needed recognition has left a heightened sensitivity to others' fear of being misunderstood, an ability that in this life can be either a gift of genuine support from others or a burden of chronic fear of repeating the same rejection.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, past experience turns into mature wisdom about intuition and trust: a person intuitively understands when to act on gut feeling and when to wait and check with facts, and lives life with a rare inner clarity without fearing his own depth. She knows how to consciously trust her own gift, and not perceive every premonition as a threat of new disappointment or every doubt as evidence of her own weakness.

The gift of feeling the hidden, inherited from the past, is revealed as the ability to help others hear their own inner voice - not from a position of superiority, but from a sincere desire to convey the understanding that she herself once received. The words of such a person have weight, because they are based on lived, not theoretical, experience and exaltation, and doubts.

Respect for the price that previously had to be paid for untested trust or for lost self-belief turns in a positive way into the ability to build a new realization on a stronger, more honest foundation - not because one lacks sensitivity, but because one already knows: true knowledge does not need anxiety to be true.

The plus is also the ability to trust one's own intuition without fear that it will again turn into a painful mistake: a person consciously allows himself to feel, predict and share this knowledge, while maintaining the prudence developed through lived experience.

Another sign is the ability to appreciate the slow, gradual confirmation of inner knowledge, as opposed to the abrupt, reckless reliance on prior experience. Past experience has taught us not to accept every sensation unconditionally, and in this life this wisdom translates into the ability to consciously check and not rush for an illusory sense of certainty.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, the past experience in the positive manifests as a deep inner trust in her own intuition without a chronic need to immediately act on every feeling: she naturally feels when it is worth listening to the inner voice openly, and when it is possible to let the situation clarify itself, and this ability is no longer associated with the fear of punishment for past gullibility or rejected sensitivity.

Her ability to sense the hidden in difficult circumstances is not a manifestation of disturbing suspicion, but a sincere expression of experienced wisdom. She has an intuitive sense of when someone needs support and when they need space to go their own way, and this sensitivity comes with a clarity that is surprising to her.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, the inherited experience manifests itself as a refined, calm trust in his own intuition - he does not throw himself into excessive anxiety due to every premonition and does not lock himself in the denial of his own sensitivity, because, probably, past experience has already taught him that both extremes are equally expensive.

He naturally knows how to feel the hidden deeper than superficial suspicion, to see the real reason for someone else's behavior and react from it, without panicking where quiet, attentive observation is enough. This appears to be a rare wisdom for him, which is actually a well-honed skill from the past to carry his gift without anxiety.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, past experience manifests itself as a hidden tendency towards disturbing illusions, which has not yet been released: a person may subconsciously seek confirmation of the worst where a calm fact-check would be enough, or feel acute anxiety every time the situation is ambiguous, as if internally convinced that uncertainty always hides a threat.

Another manifestation of the negative is an excessive, almost panicked need to hide one's own sensitivity as a tool to protect against a repetition of past rejection or persecution. In this embodiment, it manifests itself as a chronic inability to trust one's own intuition openly, as if inside one is still afraid of the pain one experienced earlier.

Minus can also look like oscillating between two extremes without a stable middle: a person either immerses himself in excessive suspicion and anxious expectation of the worst, then suddenly completely denies any intuition, punishing himself for previous credulity - and this cycle repeats itself again and again, not allowing a real, stable trust in one's gift to take hold.

In the deeper negative, there is a tendency to internal condemnation—one's own or another's—for any display of sensitivity or, conversely, for any rational caution—as if the mind remembers a time when both overconfidence and overdoubt were very costly. This manifests itself as a habit of punishing oneself for every choice related to one's own intuition.

The fifth manifestation of the minus is a chronic fear of any deep trust in one's own knowledge, paradoxically hidden behind an external indifference: the person fears that any deep trust in oneself is actually a trap of a new illusion, and unconsciously avoids even healthy, reasoned opportunities to listen to oneself, so as not to risk a repetition of past pain.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, the negative manifestation of past experience often looks like a chronic distrust of one's own intuition - even when the internal signal is quite clear and well-founded. She may look for confirmation of her own rightness in how strictly she manages to control everything rationally, instead of allowing herself a sincere, unforced trust in her own feeling.

In relationships or business, this manifests as a fear of allowing oneself to fully trust a hunch that seems "too dangerous to be true", which forces one to either keep an inner distance from one's own feelings, or, on the contrary, to act suddenly and thoughtlessly under the influence of anxiety in order to finally get rid of the unbearable uncertainty.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, the negative manifestation is often disguised as prudent caution - a way to protect himself from the fear of reliving the rejection or persecution that once cost so much. He can deliberately avoid a deep manifestation of his own sensitivity in any matter or relationship, fearing that any appearance will again lead to painful misunderstanding or mistrust of those around him.

The tendency to hidden anxiety in the minus manifests itself as sudden, sharp attempts to act on the basis of suspicion just when the external calm has lasted too long - as if an inner voice convinces that it is better to act immediately yourself than to wait until something irreparable happens.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is situations that remind of past persecution or a painful mistake of trust: sudden uncertainty, a feeling that intuition is devalued, pressure of circumstances that remind me of "I'm not understood again" or "I trusted the wrong knowledge again." Even the slightest hint of such a scenario can activate disproportionately strong anxiety, the root of which is an unlived experience.

The second trigger is prolonged uncertainty, which paradoxically causes internal tension, because past experience has taught us not to trust peace as something too fragile or illusory. Because the past has built up readiness for a sudden disruption, the very lull can be felt as a harbinger of trouble.

The third trigger is the need to make a clear choice about how openly to trust one's own sensitivity - to declare a premonition out loud or, conversely, to remain silent for safety. Because past experience has taught us that any extreme can be very costly, the forced certainty at such times is especially strong in activating anxiety and the desire to avoid the decision altogether.

The fourth trigger is contact with people or circumstances that remind - in atmosphere or behavior - of past persecution or, on the contrary, of betrayal of trust, even if the person is not consciously aware of it. The reaction in such a contact is often disproportionate to the situation, because it comes not from the present moment, but from ancient experience.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to realize that the current tendency to over-anxious or, conversely, to deny one's own intuition may not be rooted in the current situation, but in a long-ago experience of a rejected gift. A simple recognition that "maybe it's not about now, but about then" already reduces the intensity of the reaction and gives space for a more conscious choice.

The second step is to consciously practice moderate, honest trust in one's own knowledge, where before one would automatically want to either worry excessively or completely deny what is felt: if one notices a repeating scenario, it is worth stopping and asking oneself what is driving it - a real signal or an old habit.

The third step is to learn to feel the difference between the common sense available today and the ancient fear of punishment for past credulity or open sensitivity that is left over from the past but no longer meets the safety of the present.

The fourth step is to consciously appreciate and allow yourself the clarity that this life provides: to notice at least one moment every day when the intuition was confirmed calmly, without drama, and consciously allow yourself to trust it, even in small things.

The fifth step is to restore confidence in one's own ability to bear the gift of sensitivity with dignity, gradually, where in the past it probably cost very dearly: in small steps to allow oneself both openness and caution, which this time do not carry a real threat.

The sixth step is to consciously choose a sincere, calm manifestation of intuition as a conscious choice, not as a way to avoid repeating the past, by doing it gradually: practicing alternating between trusting yourself and concrete, conscious fact-checking, rather than getting stuck in one of the poles.

## Main Task

The main task of this position is to end the long-standing cycle in which intuition and self-trust were very expensive, and to learn to live one's own sensitivity and ability to feel the hidden as a gift, and not as a memory of an experienced rejection or a painful mistake. This means learning to consciously alternate between trust and verification, while maintaining complete confidence in one's own inner knowledge regardless of external circumstances, something one may have failed to realize in past experience.

When this task is accomplished, the gift of hidden feeling and the capacity for deep imagination, inherited from the past, cease to be a source of hidden anxiety and wavering between poles and become a real gift, a tool that a person uses generously and freely in moments when one really needs to understand oneself or others, no longer fearing that any trust or any caution will turn into a new disappointment.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a situation in my life in which I hide my own sensitivity more strictly than it really is necessary, for fear of repeating a past rejection?
- What do I feel at the moment when I can either allow myself to sincerely trust my own intuition, or strain again from the old habit of worrying?
- To what extent is my tendency to be anxiously suspicious or, on the contrary, to deny my own knowledge, related to the fear of experiencing betrayal of trust or rejection again?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one recurring situation of wavering between excessive anxiety and denial of one's own intuition and describe it in writing from the first manifestation to the present.
2. Once a day, make one small, concrete gesture of trusting your own gift—allowing yourself a little openness or a little quiet inspection where before there would have been an extreme.
3. Choose one form of clarity not available in past experience and consciously allow yourself to experience it this week, even on a small scale.
4. Before a strong desire to worry or sharply deny the feeling, ask yourself: "Is this really necessary now, or is it an ancient fear from another time?"
5. Create one moment of conscious, balanced self-expression this week—where previously there would have been a desire for either excessive anxiety or complete denial.
6. Consciously practice alternating: devote one day to sincere trust in one's own intuition, the other to conscious, calm fact-checking without anxiety.
