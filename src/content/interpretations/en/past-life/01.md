# 1. The Magician — Tasks from Past Incarnations

## In Brief

This position points to an experience in which survival or status depended on mental agility—the ability to quickly get one's bearings, persuade, negotiate, and find a way out where others saw none. This talent was probably used at times not only for honest creation, but also to get around rules or obtain what was wanted indirectly. The task in this incarnation is to complete what was begun before: to learn to act and speak so that words and intentions align, without losing that natural resourcefulness. The past grants rare ingenuity and the courage to begin something new, yet it also leaves behind a habit of resorting to cunning where direct action would be possible.

## Natural Strength

From past experience, this energy brings an exceptional speed of response: the ability to assess a situation instantly and offer a solution where others need time to think. This is not an acquired skill; it is as though the mind remembers a time when quick thinking was a matter of survival or success rather than simply a useful personal quality.

The second inherited trait is a natural gift for persuasion: an ability to find precisely the words that lead another person to believe, agree, or place their trust in you. In past circumstances, this skill was often more than a pleasant social quality—it was a way to secure resources, gain an ally, or escape a difficult situation. This is the source of the natural mastery with which it is exercised today.

The third strength is the ability to act amid uncertainty without becoming paralyzed by fear. Past experience probably taught this soul to begin moving even before the full picture was clear, relying on the ability to adapt along the way. It has left behind a rare courage to take on something new where others wait for complete certainty.

The fourth trait is a subtle sensitivity to human weaknesses and desires. Past experience in which reading people was crucial has left an increased awareness of exactly what another person wants—a capacity that in this life can become either an instrument of manipulation or, conversely, a gift for sincere empathy and precise communication.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, past experience is transformed into mature confidence in action. The person intuitively understands when it is worth taking a risk and when it is better to wait, doing so without needless boasting or hidden calculation. They can begin new things quickly while remaining honest about their intentions and capabilities.

The gift for persuasion inherited from the past unfolds as a talent for negotiating outcomes in which both sides benefit. This person's words carry a quality of thoughtful sincerity that never feels contrived, because it rests on long-developed insight into people rather than a recently learned technique of influence.

Action itself is no longer frightening: the person can take up something new quickly and calmly, guided by an inner sense already tested in previous circumstances. This appears as surprising ease where others hesitate—a decision about a new direction, project, or meeting is made almost instantly, without inner drama.

The positive expression also brings an ability to conduct affairs honestly without repeating the cunning of the past. The person consciously chooses direct communication where they may once have had to circumvent rules or downplay the truth for personal advantage. This is not naïveté but a deliberate choice of transparency, despite the memory that deception once seemed more effective.

Another sign is the ability to value a finished result as much as an exciting beginning. Past experience taught this energy to value the moment of action itself; in this life, that trait develops into an ability to carry things through rather than abandoning them halfway for the next exciting pursuit.

### Expression in Women in the Positive

For a woman, the positive expression of past experience appears as mature confidence without a need to prove her cleverness. She is naturally persuasive, but her persuasiveness does not depend on constantly resorting to tricks. Perhaps there were enough situations in the past in which she had to prevail through wit that in this life she can rely on an inner sense of her own capability instead of a constant need to outsmart circumstances.

Her ability to begin new things quickly and lead others is not a way to prove her importance, but a sincere expression of natural initiative. She intuitively senses which endeavors merit an investment of energy and which do not, and this discernment comes with a maturity surprising for her age.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

For a man, inherited experience in the positive appears as a refined yet honest art of persuasion—in his words, proposals, and agreements. He is not afraid to act decisively, because past experience has probably already taught him that true strength lies not in cunning, but in taking responsibility for one's word.

He naturally finds common ground with very different people, reads their intentions with ease, and responds at the right moment without wasting time on detours. It appears as an unusually polished diplomatic agility, one that is in fact a skill honed long ago.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, past experience appears as a hidden tendency toward cunning that borders on dishonesty. The person may instinctively search for a shortcut where direct action would take more time, applying this habit even when there is no real need because an honest route is entirely available.

Another negative expression is excessive reliance on personal persuasiveness as a tool for obtaining what is desired. In this incarnation, it appears as an anxious need to constantly “negotiate” and “talk one's way out” instead of simply asking directly or honestly acknowledging the circumstances.

The negative may also take the form of the same painful pattern of starting and abandoning: an exciting launch, loss of interest, a move to a new project—and then the same cycle with another idea. This is a sign of unresolved past experience that still governs present choices instead of becoming a lesson about the value of completion.

At a deeper negative level, there is a tendency to conceal true intentions even in minor matters, as though the mind remembers a time when openness about one's purpose carried too high a cost. It manifests as a habit of withholding part of the truth even when direct honesty would pose no risk at all.

A fifth negative expression is impulsiveness as a way to escape the discomfort of waiting. When inner anxiety rises, the person may rush into a new endeavor or decision too quickly, as though speed of action could compensate for a lack of inner confidence.

### Expression in Women in the Negative

For a woman, the negative expression of past experience often looks like a chronic need to control circumstances through words—to persuade, influence, and negotiate even when it would be enough simply to wait or trust. She may seek confirmation of her own ingenuity in how skillfully she manages to circumvent an obstacle.

In practical matters, this appears as a fear of being deceived, which drives her either to control every agreement too closely or, conversely, to use cunning first so she can get ahead of possible deception from the other side.

### Expression in Men in the Negative

For a man, the negative expression is often disguised as conspicuous confidence and quick-wittedness—a way to protect himself from the fear of being insufficiently prepared or not quick enough. He may deliberately avoid direct questions about his intentions, fearing that an honest answer will weaken his position.

In the negative, a tendency toward manipulative schemes appears as constant, unspoken calculation about how to turn each situation to his advantage. This exhausts both him and those around him, because trust that takes so much effort to build can be destroyed by the very first deception that comes to light.

## What Can Pull This Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger is any situation reminiscent of the past need to survive through resourcefulness: a sudden lack of resources, competition, or the sense that honesty will lead to defeat. Even a slight resemblance to this scenario can activate a disproportionately strong urge to resort to cunning, rooted in unresolved experience.

A second trigger is a prolonged lack of room for initiative and novelty. Because the past created a deep dependence on a sense of movement and control over circumstances, their absence gradually erodes the person's inner resources more than may be apparent at first.

A third trigger is having to wait a long time for a result without being able to influence the process. Past experience taught that swift action and ingenuity solved everything, so forced waiting in such moments activates anxiety and the temptation to find a shortcut with particular intensity.

A fourth trigger is contact with people whose behavior or manner of influence resembles figures from past experience connected with deception or manipulation, even when the person is not consciously aware of the association. Their reaction in such an encounter is often disproportionate because it comes not from the present moment but from an old scar.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to recognize that the present temptation to use cunning or downplay the truth may be rooted not in the current situation but in distant experience. Simply acknowledging that “this may not be about the present” already reduces the intensity of the reaction and creates space for a more honest choice.

The second step is to consciously practice completing cycles. When a recurring pattern of enthusiastic beginnings and quick retreats becomes apparent, pause and ask what makes this pattern so compelling instead of automatically seeking a new idea as an escape from the boredom of finishing.

The third step is to learn to recognize personal capability without constantly demonstrating cleverness. A helpful practice is to act directly each day in a situation where there would previously have been a temptation to take a clever shortcut, then observe the outcome.

The fourth step is to consciously practice complete honesty in small matters, gradually moving toward more important situations. Each small truth stated directly weakens the old pattern of concealing intentions.

The fifth step is to restore initiative to life not as a way to control circumstances or compensate for anxiety, but as a sincere source of joy—to value the process of creating something new rather than only the advantage or benefit it may bring.

The sixth step is to consciously choose directness in new endeavors despite the memory of advantages once gained through cunning, making the transition gradually, step by step, rather than trying to leap into total openness all at once.

## Main Task

The main task of this position is to complete an old cycle in which survival and success were linked to resourcefulness at the edge of honesty, and to learn to experience this talent as a source of trust rather than a tool for circumventing rules. It means learning to act swiftly and persuasively while keeping intentions fully transparent—something that was probably not achieved in past experience.

When this task is fulfilled, the quick mind and gift for persuasion inherited from the past cease to be a source of hidden anxiety and become a true gift: a tool shared generously and honestly with the people around you, without fear that directness will cost you an advantage.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a recurring “brilliant beginning, rapid loss of interest” pattern in my life?
- What do I feel at the moment when I can either use cunning for personal gain or speak directly and honestly?
- Can I recall a situation in which I downplayed the truth even though honesty would have carried no real risk?
- To what extent is my need to persuade others connected with a fear that I will not be heard otherwise?
- Are there people or situations that provoke a disproportionately strong desire to control everything through words?
- What happens inside me when I have to wait a long time for a result without being able to speed events up?
- Do I trust myself enough to act directly, without resorting to cunning, even when that seems like the slower path?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one recurring pattern in your undertakings and describe it in writing from its first appearance to the present day.
2. Once a day, practice complete directness in one situation where you would previously have been tempted to downplay the truth.
3. Choose one undertaking you began and abandoned halfway, and take one concrete step toward completing it this week.
4. Before acting on a strong desire to use cunning, ask yourself: “Is this truly necessary now, or is it an old habit from another time?”
5. Create one moment of honest, direct conversation this week—with no subtext or hidden aim, simply for the sake of sincerity itself.
6. Deliberately slow the beginning of a new endeavor or project, giving yourself time to test the reality of your intentions before making promises.
7. Write a short note of thanks—even if you send it to no one—to the experience from the past that taught you resourcefulness, while acknowledging that a different quality of action is needed now.
