# 20. Judgement — Tasks from Past Incarnations

## Brief Essence

This provision indicates an experience in which a person had a prominent role as one who evaluates, judges, or makes a decision—perhaps as someone empowered to decide the destinies of others, as someone whose word in a family or community carried the weight of a final verdict, or simply as someone with an extremely keen inner sense of truth that was listened to by those around them. Probably, in that life, this ability either led to excessive severity - to judgments passed on oneself or others without mercy, or, on the contrary, turned into a heavy burden of unsettled family accounts, when resentments, debts or non-recognition remained unresolved until the very end of that incarnation. Perhaps, in that life, a person either heard his own inner call for change too late and lost the chance for renewal that could save the situation, or, on the contrary, too sharply and painfully severed the connection with the family, losing its power along with it. That is why in this incarnation both the natural acuteness of the inner feeling and the deep inner tension around the right to one's own, honest verdict without excessive severity arise at the same time. The task of this incarnation is to complete what has been done before: to learn to take stock honestly and mercifully, to hear the inner call for renewal in time and to maintain a connection with the family without carrying its unhealed wounds.

## Natural Strength

From past experience, this energy brings a rare, deeply developed ability for honest assessment — as if the soul's memory of a time when the ability to see the truth and make a balanced decision was already actively used. That is why today this person often feels a strange confidence in what is a real challenge for others - the ability to immediately feel the essence of a situation and make a clear judgment about it.

The second inherited trait is a deep, almost instinctive understanding of the importance of family ties. This soul intuitively knows how much power or pain is passed down through the generations, a knowledge that can become either a gift of conscious healing of the race, or a burden of a hidden duty to carry all its unsettled accounts.

The third power is the ability to feel the moment of the end of the cycle. Past experience has probably taught this soul dearly that delaying an honest conclusion is too costly—it has left behind a rare ability to recognize in time when the old stage has exhausted itself and it is time for a renewal.

The fourth feature is a deep understanding of the price of excessive severity or, conversely, the price of open conflict. A past experience in which a verdict may have been passed too harshly or, on the contrary, was never passed at all, has left a heightened sensitivity to justice, a capacity that in this life can be either the gift of wise, balanced judgment or the burden of a chronic fear of repeating the same harshness or the same incompleteness.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, past experience translates into a mature wisdom of appreciation and forgiveness: one intuitively understands when to draw a clear conclusion and when to give a situation or person (including oneself) room to grow, and lives a life with a rare inner balance between honesty and mercy. She knows how to consciously draw conclusions, and not to perceive every memory of the past as a reason for a new trial.

The gift of seeing the essence of things, inherited from the past, is revealed as the ability to help others look honestly at their own path - not from a position of superiority or the need to judge, but from a sincere desire to convey the clarity that she once received or learned to find herself. Her point of view carries weight because it is based on lived experience, rather than ostentatious evaluation.

Respect for the price that previously had to be paid for excessive severity or for an open family conflict turns into a plus into the ability to build new relationships with the family on a stronger, more honest foundation - not because a person lacks the sharpness of senses, but because he already knows: true justice does not need either cruelty or denial to be true.

On the plus side, the ability to trust one's own call to renewal without fear that it will again lead to loss or rupture appears: a person consciously allows himself to leave behind the exhausted stages of life, while maintaining a connection with what is valuable, which gave him the experience of the past.

Another sign is the ability to appreciate quiet, calm justice, in contrast to the ostentatious severity of previous experience. Past experience has taught us not to rush to a final verdict, and in this life this wisdom turns into the ability to consciously and patiently draw conclusions, taking our time to judge neither ourselves nor others.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

A woman's positive past experience manifests as a deep inner justice without a chronic need to pass judgment immediately: she naturally sees the essence of situations when it is appropriate and allows herself and others time to grow, and this ability is no longer associated with the fear of punishment for past excessive severity or rejected mercy.

Her sense of truth in difficult circumstances is not a manifestation of the need to judge, but a sincere expression of experienced wisdom. She has an intuitive sense of when a situation calls for a clear conclusion, and when it calls for patience and time, and this sensitivity comes with a clarity that is surprising to her.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, the inherited experience manifests itself as a refined, calm justice - he does not rush into excessive severity at every opportunity to pass a verdict and does not lock himself in the denial of his own sense of truth, because, probably, past experience has already taught him that both extremes are equally expensive.

He naturally knows when family or loved ones need his clear decision, and when - patient support without jumping to a conclusion, reacting from this clarity, not succumbing to the need to be the only judge where quiet wisdom is enough. It looks like a rare poise for him, which is actually a well-honed skill from the past to carry his gift without cruelty.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, past experience manifests itself as a hidden tendency to endless self-judgment, which has not yet been released: a person can subconsciously re-judge his own decisions over and over again, as if internally convinced that any mistake deserves a harsher punishment than he has already received.

Another manifestation of the negative is an excessive, almost obsessive need to prove one's own rightness as a tool for protection against the repetition of past injustice or an open conflict. In this embodiment, it manifests as a chronic inability to let go of an argument or resentment, as if internally the person is still afraid of the pain they experienced earlier, leaving the matter unresolved.

Minus can also look like a wavering between two extremes without a stable middle: a person tries to be a strict judge for himself and others, demanding perfect justice in everything, then suddenly completely abandons any assessment, fearing to repeat past cruelty - and this cycle repeats itself over and over again, preventing real, stable wisdom from taking hold.

In the deeper minus, there is a tendency to internal condemnation - one's own or someone else's - for any imperfection or, on the contrary, for any softness - as if the mind remembers a time when both excessive severity and excessive indulgence were very expensive. This manifests itself as a habit of punishing oneself for every choice related to the evaluation of one's own or someone else's act.

The fifth manifestation of the minus is a chronic fear of any renewal without a guarantee of a result, paradoxically hidden behind an external determination: a person fears that any attempt to start anew is actually a trap of new disappointment or loss, and unconsciously avoids even healthy, reasonable opportunities to leave behind an exhausted phase of life.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, the negative manifestation of past experience often looks like a chronic distrust of one's own right to a calm, rather than harsh, judgment - even when the internal state fully allows for a sincere and gentle assessment of the situation. She may seek confirmation of her own rightness in how hard she is able to defend her position, instead of allowing herself sincere, measured justice that does not cost her strength.

In relationships or business, this manifests as a fear of allowing oneself to completely end an old conflict or a phase that seems "too risky to let go", forcing either to keep a constant readiness to fight, or, conversely, to suddenly and thoughtlessly cut ties under the influence of exhaustion, in order to finally get rid of the unbearable pressure.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, the negative manifestation is often disguised as an unrelenting principle - a way to protect himself from the fear of reliving an injustice or an open conflict that once cost a lot. He may deliberately avoid admitting his own guilt or weakness in any matter or relationship, fearing that any admission will again lead to a loss of status or respect from those around him.

The tendency to hidden severity in the minus manifests itself as unexpected, sharp attempts to prove one's own rightness just when external restraint has lasted too long - as if an inner voice convinces that it is better to pass a verdict immediately than to allow the case to remain unresolved.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is situations that remind of a past injustice or an unresolved family conflict: a sudden accusation, a feeling that a person has been treated unfairly, the pressure of circumstances that remind of "I'm being judged unfairly again" or "I can't close this case again." Even the slightest hint of such a scenario can activate disproportionately strong anxiety, the root of which is an unlived experience.

The second trigger is prolonged uncertainty, which paradoxically causes internal tension, because past experience has taught us not to trust incompleteness as something safe. Since the past has developed readiness for a sudden, sharp judgment, the very state of being "not yet decided" can feel like a threat.

The third trigger is the need to make a clear choice about whether to stay with the old or start anew — stick to the usual or, on the contrary, decisively change everything. Because past experience has taught us that any extreme can be very costly, the forced certainty at such times is especially strong in activating anxiety and the desire to avoid the decision altogether.

The fourth trigger is contact with people or circumstances that remind - in atmosphere or behavior - of a past injustice or, on the contrary, of an open family grudge, even if the person is not consciously aware of it. The reaction in such a contact is often disproportionate to the situation, because it comes not from the present moment, but from ancient experience.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to realize that the current tendency to self-judge or, conversely, to rigidly deny one's own guilt may have its roots not in the current situation, but in a long-standing experience of injustice or unfinished conflict. A simple recognition that "maybe it's not about now, but about then" already reduces the intensity of the reaction and gives space for a more conscious choice.

The second step is to consciously practice moderate, honest assessment where previously there would have been an automatic desire to either judge too harshly or to avoid any conclusion altogether: if you notice a repeating pattern, it's worth stopping and asking yourself what is driving it - a real need or a long-standing habit.

The third step is to learn to feel the difference between a healthy desire for redress available today and an ancient fear of repeating a past injustice or punishment for a past wrong that remains from the past but is no longer consistent with present security.

The fourth step is to consciously appreciate and allow yourself the completeness that this life gives you: notice at least one moment every day when you managed to honestly close a case without being overly harsh, and consciously allow yourself to trust him, even in a small way.

The fifth step is to restore confidence in one's own ability to make a just decision with dignity, gradually, where in the past it probably cost very dearly: in small steps, to allow oneself both clarity and mercy, which this time do not carry a real threat.

The sixth step is to consciously choose renewal as a conscious choice, not as a way to avoid repeating the past, by doing it incrementally: practicing alternating between honest stocktaking and a concrete, conscious step into a new phase, rather than being stuck at one of the poles.

## Main Task

The main task of this position is to end the long-standing cycle in which justice and honest assessment were very expensive, and to learn to live one's own ability to see the truth and the ability to renew as a gift, and not as a memory of an injustice experienced or an open conflict. It means learning to consciously alternate between a fair bottom line and mercy, while remaining fully confident in your own right to a fair, balanced decision regardless of external circumstances, something you probably failed to realize in past experience.

When this task is accomplished, the gift of seeing the essence of things and the capacity for deep renewal, inherited from the past, cease to be a source of hidden tension and swing between poles and become a real gift - a tool that a person uses generously and freely in moments when one really needs to honestly assess a situation or start a new phase, no longer afraid that any honesty or any renewal will turn into a new disappointment.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a situation in my life in which I judge myself or others more harshly than is really necessary, out of fear of repeating past injustices?
- What do I feel at the moment when I can either allow myself to honestly and calmly close the case, or strain again from the old habit of proving myself right?
- To what extent is my tendency to self-judge or, on the contrary, to deny my own guilt, related to the fear of experiencing injustice or unresolved family conflict again?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one recurring situation of swinging between excessive severity and denial of responsibility and describe it in writing from the first occurrence to the present.
2. Once a day, make one small, concrete gesture of trusting your own sense of justice—allowing yourself a little clarity or a little mercy where there would have been extremes before.
3. Choose one open topic from the past and consciously take a step toward completing it this week, even on a small scale.
4. Before a strong desire to judge yourself or others more harshly than necessary, ask yourself: "Is this really necessary now, or is it an ancient fear from another time?"
5. To create one moment of conscious, balanced summation of the results of this week - where before there would be a desire to be either overly strict or completely avoid the assessment.
6. Consciously practice alternation: devote one day to an honest analysis of the past, the other to a conscious step into a new, updated stage without looking back too much.
