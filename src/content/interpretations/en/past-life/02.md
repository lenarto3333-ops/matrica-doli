# 2. The High Priestess — Tasks from Past Incarnations

## In Brief

This position points to an experience in which survival or safety depended on knowing when to remain silent and conceal inner knowledge—perhaps as a keeper of secret wisdom, an oracle, or someone whose insight was both a gift and a danger in the eyes of others. Openness or directness probably once came at a high price: distrust, suspicion, or even persecution for seeing more than others did. The task of this incarnation is to complete what was begun before: to learn to live openly with inner knowledge instead of hiding it behind a veil of silence born of an ancient fear. The past grants rare intuitive depth, but leaves behind the habit of concealing oneself where trust would now be possible.

## Natural Strength

From past experience, this energy brings rare insight: the ability to sense the truth of a situation or person long before it becomes obvious to others. This is not an acquired skill; it is as though the soul remembers times when such sensitivity was a matter of survival or spiritual service rather than simply a useful personal quality.

The second inherited trait is a natural gift for safeguarding what has been entrusted: secrets, another person's pain, sacred knowledge. In past circumstances, this ability was often not merely a character trait but a form of service or protection, which explains the natural seriousness with which it is exercised today.

The third strength is the ability to sense the atmosphere of a place or a person's intentions without words. Past experience probably taught this soul to read the invisible where others relied only on what was stated directly, leaving behind a rare insight that operates almost automatically.

The fourth trait is a deep respect for silence as a space of revelation. Past experience in which solitude or ritual practice was crucial left an increased need for moments of complete quiet—a capacity that in this life can become either a means of isolation or, conversely, a gift of genuine spiritual depth.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, past experience is transformed into mature trust in personal intuition. The person uses their insight without hiding it completely or putting it on display, sharing it when it is truly needed. They can sense the truth of a situation while remaining open to others and preserving their own boundaries.

The inherited gift for safeguarding confidences unfolds as an ability to be a reliable space for another person's vulnerability without turning secrecy into a burden for oneself. The person knows how to distinguish what deserves to be held in silence from what should be shared openly.

Silence and solitude cease to be forms of escape. The person can spend time alone consciously and peacefully, supported by an inner sense already tested in previous circumstances. This appears as unexpected ease where others fear loneliness: solitude is experienced not as punishment, but as a natural resource.

The positive also brings the ability to speak the truth openly without repeating past fears. The person consciously chooses direct communication where knowledge once probably had to be concealed for safety. This is not naïveté but a deliberate choice of sincerity despite the memory that openness once seemed dangerous.

### Expression in Women in the Positive

For a woman, positive past experience appears as mature confidence in her intuition without a need to hide it. She is naturally perceptive, but her insight does not require constant mystery. Perhaps the past held enough situations in which inner knowledge had to be concealed for safety that in this life she can rely on an inner sense of her own worth rather than a constant need to protect herself through silence.

Her ability to sense what is hidden is not a way to control distance from others, but a sincere expression of natural depth. She intuitively knows when to share what she perceives and when simply to remain quietly present, and this choice comes with a maturity surprising for her age.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

For a man, inherited experience in the positive appears as quiet but profound wisdom—the ability to sense the truth of a situation and share it at the right time without unnecessary drama. He is not afraid to be open, because past experience has probably already taught him that true strength lies not in concealment but in being sincere at the right moment.

He naturally creates a space of quiet and trust around himself in which others feel safe sharing what lies deepest within them. It appears as an unusual spiritual insight, one that is in fact a skill carefully developed in the past.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, past experience appears as a hidden tendency toward excessive secrecy bordering on alienation. The person may instinctively conceal even harmless information about themselves, applying the habit when there is no real need because openness is entirely possible.

Another negative expression is excessive reliance on silence as a means of self-protection. In this incarnation, it appears as an anxious need to constantly “stay in the shadows” and avoid attention instead of simply being oneself openly.

The negative may also look like a recurring painful pattern of unspoken truth: the person senses important knowledge or truth but remains silent, then suffers the consequences of not speaking—and the same cycle repeats in a new situation. This is a sign of unresolved past experience still governing present choices instead of becoming a lesson about the value of openness.

At a deeper negative level, there is a tendency to hide true feelings even in minor matters, as though the soul remembers a time when openness about one's inner state cost too much. It appears as a habit of remaining silent about personal needs even when direct conversation would carry no risk.

A fifth negative expression is excessive suspicion of others' intentions as a way to avoid the discomfort of trust. When inner anxiety rises, the person may assign hidden meanings to other people's words, as though constant vigilance could compensate for a lack of inner safety.

### Expression in Women in the Negative

For a woman, the negative expression of past experience often looks like a chronic need to remain unseen—to avoid attention and conceal her true abilities or knowledge even when this harms her fulfillment. She may look for proof of safety in how successfully she manages to go unnoticed.

In practical matters, this appears as a fear of being exposed or judged for her insight, causing her either to conceal her conclusions too carefully or, conversely, voice them abruptly and unexpectedly when accumulated tension becomes unbearable.

### Expression in Men in the Negative

For a man, the negative expression is often disguised as imperturbable detachment—a way to protect himself from the fear of being too transparent to others. He may deliberately avoid direct questions about his feelings or knowledge, fearing that openness will make him vulnerable.

In the negative, the tendency toward excessive secrecy appears as constant concealment of even neutral information about himself. This exhausts both him and those around him, because closeness that is already difficult to establish is equally difficult to maintain amid such reserve.

## What Can Pull This Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger is any situation reminiscent of the past need to conceal truth for safety: a sudden demand to express an opinion publicly, a sense that openness is dangerous, or pressure to “show your hand” before feeling ready. Even a faint resemblance to this scenario can activate a disproportionately strong urge to close off, rooted in unresolved experience.

A second trigger is a prolonged lack of space for quiet and solitude. Because the past created a deep dependence on the ability to retreat into calm, its absence gradually erodes inner resources more than may be apparent at first.

A third trigger is the need to prove intuitive knowledge through rational arguments. Past experience taught that inner knowing had value in itself, so being forced to explain “why I think this” can activate anxiety and the temptation to say nothing with particular intensity.

A fourth trigger is contact with people whose behavior or energy resembles figures from past experience associated with distrust or persecution for being open, even if the person is not consciously aware of it. The reaction is often disproportionate because it comes not from the present moment but from an ancient scar.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to recognize that the present temptation to leave something important unspoken may be rooted not in the current situation but in distant experience. Simply acknowledging that “this may not be about the present” reduces the intensity of the reaction and creates space for a more honest choice.

The second step is to consciously practice small acts of openness. When a recurring pattern of withholding something important becomes apparent, pause and ask what draws you into it instead of automatically hiding in silence.

The third step is to learn to acknowledge your own value without constantly concealing it. A helpful practice is to share one genuine feeling or thought each day in a situation where you would previously have been tempted to remain silent, then observe the outcome.

The fourth step is to consciously practice complete honesty about personal needs in small matters, gradually moving toward more important situations. Each small need stated directly weakens the old pattern of concealment.

The fifth step is to restore quiet to life not as a way to escape or compensate for anxiety, but as a sincere source of renewal—to value the process of listening within rather than only the safety it may bring.

The sixth step is to consciously choose openness in new endeavors despite the memory that transparency was once dangerous, making this transition gradually, step by step, rather than through a single abrupt move into complete exposure.

## Main Task

The main task of this position is to complete an old cycle in which safety and survival were linked to concealing the truth, and to learn to experience personal insight as a source of connection rather than a tool of protection through detachment. It means learning to feel deeply and share sincerely while maintaining healthy boundaries—something that was probably not achieved in past experience.

When this task is fulfilled, the insight and gift for silence inherited from the past cease to be a source of hidden anxiety and become a true gift: a resource shared generously and honestly with others, without fear that openness will cost the person their safety.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a recurring pattern in my life of “sensing the truth, remaining silent, and suffering the consequences”?
- What do I feel when I can either conceal an important feeling or speak about it directly?
- Can I recall a situation in which I left an important need unspoken even though openness posed no real risk?
- To what extent does my need for solitude come from sincere renewal, and to what extent from fear of being seen?
- Are there people or situations that provoke a disproportionately strong desire to hide?
- What happens inside me when I have to express my opinion or intuitive knowledge publicly?
- Do I trust myself enough to be open without hiding, even when this seems like the riskier path?

## Practical Plan

1. Notice one recurring pattern of keeping silent and describe it in writing from its first appearance to the present day.
2. Once a day, practice complete openness in one situation where you would previously have been tempted to say nothing.
3. Choose one important truth you have been concealing and take one concrete step toward expressing it this week.
4. Before giving in to a strong desire to fall silent, ask yourself: “Is this truly necessary now, or is it an old habit from another time?”
5. Create one moment of honest, direct conversation this week—with no hidden subtext, simply for the sake of sincerity itself.
6. Consciously spend time in silence not as an escape, but as a deliberate restorative practice, paying attention to your inner state.
7. Write a short note of thanks—even if you send it to no one—to the experience from the past that taught you depth, while acknowledging that a different quality of openness is needed now.
