# 16. The Tower — Tasks from Past Incarnations

## Brief Essence

This position refers to an experience in which a person has experienced a sudden, devastating collapse of something that seemed indestructible—home, status, power, security—and the collapse probably came suddenly, without warning, leaving a deep trail of fear of any stability that might prove illusory. Perhaps, in that life, a person either caused the catastrophe himself, not wanting or not being able to recognize in time that the structure could no longer withstand, or became a victim of someone else's sudden destruction, losing everything in one moment. That is why in this embodiment there is simultaneously a keen sense of the approaching crisis and a deep, almost instinctive fear of sudden changes. The task of this incarnation is to complete what has been done before: to learn to trust the process of reconstruction, without constantly waiting for a new blow and without causing it himself/herself from an old, not yet healed fear.

## Natural Strength

From past experience, this energy brings a rare wisdom about the cost of ignored instability—like a soul memory of a time when warnings of impending collapse went unheeded. That is why today this person has an acute, almost bodily sense of when the situation begins to move towards a breakdown, long before it becomes obvious to others.

A second inherited trait is inner resilience, which was likely honed through past experiences of surviving a major loss or disaster. This soul intuitively knows how to gather and act even when it seems that the ground has finally slipped from under the feet, a knowledge that can be either a gift of calm crisis resilience or a burden of chronic readiness for new trouble.

The third power is the ability to feel the difference between a structure worth saving and one worth letting go. Past experience probably taught this soul dearly that trying to hold on to the doomed is too costly—it left behind a rare ability to recognize in time when it was time to let go instead of holding on until last.

The fourth feature is a deep understanding of the price of ignored pain in any form. Past experience, in which collapse may have occurred precisely because warnings or alarm signals were not heard in time, has left a heightened sensitivity to the slightest signs of a brewing problem, an ability that in this life can be either a gift of early, honest warning or a burden of chronic anxiety even in quiet times.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, past experience turns into mature wisdom about change and crisis: a person intuitively understands when to act in anticipation, and when to trust the process and wait, and lives life with a rare inner resilience, without fearing his own sensitivity to approaching difficulties. She knows how to consciously trust her own ability to survive any shock, and not perceive every signal of change as a threat of disaster.

The gift of feeling the approach of a crisis, inherited from the past, is revealed as the ability to help others see a crack in their own life in time - not from the position of a harbinger of misfortune, but from a sincere desire to save someone from the pain that she herself had to experience. The advice of such a person has weight, because it is based on lived, not theoretical, experience of collapse and recovery.

Respect for the power that helped to survive the past catastrophe turns into the ability to build a new life on a stronger, more honest foundation, not because a person lacks caution, but because he already knows: true stability is born not from avoiding change, but from an honest willingness to meet it and go through it.

The positive expression is also the ability to trust calm, stable periods of life, without expecting a new blow: a person consciously allows himself to enjoy the balance, instead of automatically straining from the old habit of expecting a disaster. This is not carelessness, but a conscious choice to trust the present moment, despite the memory of the times when this trust was very expensive.

Another trait is the ability to value slow, gradual recovery, as opposed to the abrupt reversals of previous experiences. Past experience has taught us not to take destruction lightly, and in this life this wisdom translates into the ability to consciously build the strong, rather than rushing for an illusory sense of control over the future.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, past experience in the positive manifests as a deep inner resilience without a chronic expectation of new trouble: she naturally feels when the situation calls for caution, and when she can allow herself to sincerely trust the present moment, and this ability is no longer associated with the fear of punishment for lost vigilance. She must have had enough experiences of collapse in the past, so that in this life she could consciously appreciate the opportunity to live peacefully by her own choice, and not under the pressure of constant anxiety.

Her ability to sense impending difficulties in difficult circumstances is not a manifestation of panicked mistrust, but a sincere expression of experienced wisdom. She intuitively feels when the situation calls for decisive action and when it calls for patient waiting, and this decision comes with a clarity that is surprising to her.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, the inherited experience manifests itself as a refined, calm readiness for trials - he does not rush into excessive control over every little thing and does not close in fear of any change, because, probably, past experience has already taught him that both extremes are equally expensive.

He naturally knows how to keep his balance during serious upheavals, see the situation deeper than others and act in time, not forcing destruction where it is still possible to save, and not clinging to the doomed where the time has already come. This appears to be a rare wisdom for him, which is actually a well-honed past skill of living through a crisis without panic and without denial.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, past experience manifests itself as a hidden readiness for a disaster that has not yet been released: a person can subconsciously provoke a crisis where there is none, or destroy a stable situation due to an old habit of not trusting peace, as if internally convinced that any balance is just a lull before a new blow.

Another manifestation of the negative is excessive, almost panicked control over every little thing in life as a tool to protect against the repetition of the past disaster. In this embodiment, it manifests itself as a chronic inability to relax, as if internally a person is still preparing for the trouble he experienced earlier.

The negative can also look like oscillating between two extremes without a stable middle: a person sometimes plunges into excessive caution and avoidance of any risk, then suddenly and destructively breaks everything around, punishing himself for the previous loss of vigilance - and this cycle repeats itself again and again, preventing true stability from taking hold.

At a deeper negative level, there is a tendency to internal condemnation - of oneself or others - for any carelessness or untimely reaction to danger signals - as if the mind remembers a time when an ignored signal was very expensive, and prefers to control every nuance so as not to repeat the same thing. This manifests itself as a habit of punishing oneself for the slightest oversight or delayed reaction.

The fifth manifestation of the negative is a chronic fear of any attachment to the stable, paradoxically hidden behind an external indifference: the person fears that any deep trust in the strength of circumstances is actually a hidden trap, and unconsciously avoids even healthy, stable situations, so as not to risk a repetition of the past collapse.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, the negative manifestation of past experience often looks like a chronic distrust of one's own stability - even when the external circumstances are quite safe. She may look for confirmation of her own "readiness" in how strictly she manages to control every detail of life, instead of allowing herself a sincere, relaxed trust in the present moment.

In business, this manifests itself as a fear of allowing oneself complete stability or success, which seems "too fragile to be true", which forces one to either keep an inner distance from one's own achievements, or, on the contrary, to suddenly and thoughtlessly destroy everything in order to finally feel at least some release from constant tense waiting.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, the negative manifestation is often disguised as prudent caution - a way to protect himself from the fear of reliving the catastrophe that once cost so much. He may deliberately avoid becoming deeply involved in any business or relationship, fearing that any attachment will lead to sudden loss again.

The tendency to latently expect a catastrophe in the negative manifests itself as unexpected, sharp destructive actions just when external stability has lasted too long - as if an inner voice convinces that it is better to destroy oneself than to wait for an inevitable blow from the outside.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is situations that remind you of a past disaster: a sudden change of plans, a feeling of loss of control, the pressure of circumstances that remind you that "everything is falling apart again." Even the slightest hint of such a scenario can activate disproportionately strong anxiety, the root of which is an unlived experience.

The second trigger is prolonged stability and calm, which paradoxically causes internal tension, because past experience has taught us not to trust prolonged calm as something too fragile. Because the past has built up readiness for a sudden fall, the very well-being and balance can be felt as a harbinger of disruption.

The third trigger is the need to make a clear choice about risky change - to destroy the old for the new or, conversely, to stick with the usual for safety. Because past experience has taught us that any extreme can be very costly, the forced certainty at such times is especially strong in activating anxiety and the desire to avoid the decision altogether.

The fourth trigger is contact with people or circumstances that remind - in behavior or atmosphere - of a past disaster or the power of destructive forces over someone, even if the person is not consciously aware of this. The reaction in such a contact is often disproportionate to the situation, because it comes not from the present moment, but from ancient experience.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to realize that the current tendency to overcontrol or, conversely, to suddenly destroy the stable may have its roots not in the current situation, but in the long-standing experience of the catastrophe experienced. A simple recognition that "maybe it's not about now, but about then" already reduces the intensity of the reaction and gives space for a more conscious choice.

The second step is to consciously practice moderate, honest trust where previously there would have been an automatic desire to control everything or, on the contrary, to destroy everything: if you notice a repeating scenario, it is worth stopping and asking yourself what exactly pushes you to this - a real need or an old habit.

The third step is to learn to feel the difference between the healthy caution available today and the ancient fear of punishment for lapses in vigilance, which is left over from the past but no longer corresponds to present safety.

The fourth step is to consciously appreciate and allow yourself the stability that this life provides: to notice at least one moment of healthy peace every day that was unavailable in the past, and consciously allow yourself to trust it, even in small things.

The fifth step is to restore confidence in one's own ability to survive difficulties gradually, where in the past it probably came at a high cost: in small steps, allowing oneself both risk and peace, which this time do not carry a real threat of disaster.

The sixth step is to consciously choose stability as a conscious choice, not as a way to avoid repeating the past, by doing it gradually: practicing alternating between conscious risk and concrete, conscious rest, rather than being stuck at one of the poles.

## Main Task

The main task of this position is to end the long-standing cycle in which sudden collapse was very costly, and to learn to experience the sense of impending crisis and inner resilience as a gift, not as a memory of an experienced or expected catastrophe. It means learning to consciously alternate between vigilance and trust, while maintaining full confidence in one's own ability to survive any shock—something one may have failed to realize in past experience.

When this task is accomplished, the wisdom about the price of ignored instability and the ability to value tranquility, inherited from the past, cease to be a source of hidden anxiety and wavering between poles and become a real gift - a tool that a person uses generously and freely in moments when vigilance or action is really needed, no longer afraid that any change or any decision will turn into a new disaster.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a situation in my life in which I control everything more strictly than it is really necessary, out of fear of repeating a past disaster?
- What do I feel at the moment when I can either allow myself a sincere trust in peace, or strain again from an old habit?
- To what extent is my tendency to destroy or, on the contrary, to excessive control, related to the fear of experiencing a sudden loss of stability again?

## Practical Plan

1. Observe one situation of vacillation between excessive control and sudden destruction that recurs and describe it in writing from the first manifestation to the present.
2. Once a day, make one small, concrete gesture of trusting stability—allowing yourself a little peace or a little risk where extremes would have previously occurred.
3. Choose one stability not available in past experience and consciously allow yourself to experience it this week, even on a small scale.
4. Before a strong desire to destroy everything or sharply control everything, ask yourself: "Is this really necessary now, or is it an ancient fear from another time?"
5. Create one moment of conscious, balanced risk this week—where previously there would have been a desire for either excessive caution or a devastating breakdown.
6. Consciously practice alternating: devote one day to sincere trust in the present moment, another to conscious vigilance for real signals.
