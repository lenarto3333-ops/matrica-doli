# 3. The Empress — Tasks from Past Incarnations

## In Brief

This position points to an experience in which a person's role was to feed, protect, and provide abundance for others—perhaps as the matriarch of a large family or community, a steward of the land, or someone whose care sustained the survival and well-being of many. This role probably demanded constant self-denial: personal needs were postponed so others would not go hungry or cold. There may also have been a sudden loss of abundance—famine, ruin, or loss of land or home—which left deep anxiety around resources. The task of this incarnation is to learn to create and receive abundance simultaneously, without sacrificing oneself for others. The past grants rare generosity and practical wisdom, but leaves a habit of giving everything away where receiving could also be allowed.

## Natural Strength

From past experience, this energy brings rare practical wisdom: the ability to create comfort from nothing, feed, warm, and cultivate—skills once tied to the survival of an entire family or community rather than merely useful personal qualities.

The second inherited trait is a natural gift for patient cultivation—of land, an endeavor, relationships, or children. In past circumstances, this often required years of consistent work without immediate results, explaining the natural endurance with which it is practiced today.

The third strength is a deep sense of cycles of abundance and scarcity: an intuitive understanding of when to save and when to share generously. Past experience probably taught this soul to feel the rhythms of nature and resources where others rely only on calculation.

The fourth trait is a profound capacity for embodied, tangible care. Past experience in which physical survival depended on such care left heightened sensitivity to bodily needs, personal and those of others—a capacity that can become either exhausting self-sacrifice or a gift of genuine generosity.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, past experience of care and cultivating abundance appears as a natural ability to create a generous, warm environment for oneself and others without believing it must require complete self-denial. The person trusts their practical gift and enjoys the fruits of their work as generously as they share them.

Past practical wisdom becomes a consciously used resource: the ability to nurture an endeavor, home, or relationship patiently and consistently, trusting its natural pace instead of forcing results from an old fear of scarcity.

If the past included a loss of abundance, that experience becomes a wise rather than anxious attitude toward resources. The person values what they have without hoarding or fearing generosity, inwardly knowing they can create abundance again.

Another sign of the positive is the ability to receive care and abundance from others as easily as giving it, completing the old pattern of one-sided self-sacrifice through a balanced experience of reciprocity.

### Expression in Women in the Positive

For a woman, past experience of matriarchal care in the positive appears as a generous, warm presence that does not require completely dissolving into caring for others. She allows herself to be a center of abundance for her family while tending to her own needs as well.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

For a man, past experience of providing and protecting abundance in the positive appears as calm, reliable generosity. He creates material support for those around him without turning it into an exhausting duty and allows himself to enjoy the process of creating.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, past experience of constant self-denial appears as a chronic inability to stop and receive care from others. The person continues giving without measure as though still living in circumstances where a family or community's survival depended on it.

### Expression in Women in the Negative

For a woman, this may appear as a deeply rooted belief that her worth is measured by how much care she can give others, to the point of completely forgetting her own physical and emotional needs.

### Expression in Men in the Negative

For a man, this may appear as chronic anxiety about materially providing for the family, driving him to accumulate resources beyond any real need or work to exhaustion because he is afraid to stop even for a moment.

If the past included sudden loss—famine, ruin, or loss of land—this may appear now as irrational anxiety about resources even amid prosperity, stockpiling “for later” beyond reason, or fear of enjoying present abundance as though it could vanish at any moment.

## What Can Pull This Energy into the Negative

The strongest triggers are situations reminiscent of past scarcity or lost abundance: financial instability, the need to economize, or fear there will not be enough for everyone. An ancient and disproportionately powerful anxiety may awaken here.

A second trigger is the need to receive care or help, which conflicts with the deeply rooted role of “the one who always gives” inherited from past experience.

A third trigger is a prolonged lack of physical comfort, coziness, or contact with nature, felt acutely by this soul because of its close connection with the cycles of the earth and abundance.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to recognize that past experience of caring for others taught valuable practical skills, but does not require repeating complete self-denial in this life.

The second step is to consciously practice receiving care, help, and abundance whenever the familiar impulse appears to refuse or provide everything alone.

The third step, if irrational anxiety around resources arises, is to explore its source and gradually learn to trust the real, present level of abundance rather than an ancient fear of scarcity.

The fourth step is to return regularly to simple, embodied sources of pleasure—earth, nature, food, and comfort—to restore connection with natural generosity.

The fifth step is to slow down and patiently cultivate a new result instead of hurriedly stockpiling “just in case,” trusting that the ability to create abundance remains intact.

## Main Task

The main task of this incarnation is to complete the cycle of one-sided self-denial begun in the past and learn to create abundance while receiving it as generously as it is given. Past experience of caring for others is a true gift worth preserving, but not at the cost of constant exhaustion. The maturity of this energy lies in being a source of abundance while also allowing oneself to be filled by it.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I feel that my worth is measured by how much I can give to others?
- Do I allow myself to receive care, help, or abundance as easily as I give them?
- Does my anxiety around resources come from the real circumstances of this life or a more distant source?
- Can I stop and enjoy the abundance already present, or do I always accumulate more “for later”?
- What do the words “care,” “earth,” “harvest,” and “abundance” awaken in me—calm or anxiety?
- What practical wisdom from the past would I like to preserve, and what habit of self-sacrifice would I leave behind?

## Practical Plan

1. This week, consciously receive care, help, or a gift without trying to immediately repay it in kind.
2. Write down one situation in which disproportionately strong anxiety about resources arose, and explore where it may have come from.
3. Spend time in contact with nature or the earth—in a garden, park, or nearby green space—to restore your natural connection with abundance.
4. Allow yourself one generous gesture this week that feels good to your body, without guilt.
5. If a pattern of self-sacrifice at your own expense recurs, consciously interrupt it once this week and choose self-care instead of automatically giving.
