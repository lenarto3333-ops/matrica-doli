# 19. The Sun — Tasks from Past Incarnations

## Brief Essence

This position indicates an experience in which a person had a notable gift of generosity, charisma, or the ability to be a source of warmth and inspiration to a large number of people—perhaps as a leader, a patron, someone whose light attracted others, or simply someone whose sincerity and joy set her apart from those around her. Probably, in that life, this gift either led to exhaustion and narcissism, when a person gave himself so much to others that he lost touch with his own needs, or, on the contrary, turned to pride, when the status of the main source of light led to the depreciation of others or the burning of loved ones for the sake of his own glow. Perhaps, in that life, a person either lost confidence in his own generosity after betrayal or ingratitude on the part of those to whom he sincerely gave warmth, or he was so used to being the center of attention that he paid a high price for losing modesty and connection with the real needs of those around him. That is why in this incarnation both natural, generous charisma and internal tension around the right to be yourself without a constant display of light arise at the same time. The task of this incarnation is to complete the work done earlier: to learn to carry your joy sincerely and freely, without exhausting yourself and without demanding that others match your brightness.

## Natural Strength

From past experience, this energy brings a rare, deeply developed charisma — as if the soul's memory of a time when the ability to inspire and attract others was already actively used. That is why today this person often feels a strange ease in what is a real challenge for others - being able to ignite people, lead them, fill the space with warmth and confidence.

The second inherited trait is a deep, almost instinctive understanding of how generosity affects the environment. This soul intuitively knows how warmth, attention, or recognition can change someone else's condition, a knowledge that can become either a gift of conscious service or a burden of a hidden need to always be in the role of a giver.

The third power is the ability to feel the difference between the true scale and its ostentatious version. Past experience had probably taught this soul dearly that not every grand idea was worth carrying out, leaving behind a rare ability to appreciate true, deep generosity more than showy display.

The fourth feature is a deep understanding of the price of lost modesty or, on the contrary, lost trust in one's own light. A past experience in which generosity may have been abused or devalued just when it most needed recognition has left a heightened sensitivity to others' need for warmth, an ability that in this life can become either a gift of genuine support for others or a burden of chronic fear of repeating the same exhaustion or falling from the pedestal.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, past experience turns into mature wisdom about generosity and boundaries: a person intuitively understands when to share warmth and when to take care of one's own resource, and lives life with a rare inner balance, without being afraid of one's own brilliance. She knows how to consciously give joy, and not to perceive every act of generosity as a duty or every pause in her own light as proof of her own insignificance.

The gift of inspiring others, inherited from the past, is revealed as the ability to help people believe in their own right to joy - not from a position of superiority or the need to be in the center, but from a sincere desire to convey the warmth that she once received or learned to give herself. Her presence has weight because it is based on a lived, rather than ostentatious, experience of generosity.

Respect for the price that previously had to be paid for exhausting giving or for the hubris of the central luminary turns in the plus into the ability to build a new realization on a stronger, more honest foundation - not because the person lacks charisma, but because he already knows: true warmth does not need exhaustion or dominance to be real.

In addition, there is also the ability to trust one's own generosity without fear that it will again turn into pain or ingratitude: a person consciously allows himself to share, inspire and lead, while maintaining the prudence developed through lived experience.

Another sign is the ability to appreciate quiet, unobtrusive generosity, as opposed to the ostentatious need to always be the center of attention from previous experience. Past experience has taught us not to seek recognition for every generous gesture, and in this life this wisdom turns into the ability to consciously give without expecting applause.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, past experience in the positive manifests as a deep inner generosity without a chronic need for immediate recognition: she naturally shares warmth and attention when appropriate, and allows herself to rest from the role of a source of light when needed, and this ability is no longer tied to the fear of punishment for past exhaustion or rejected modesty.

Her charisma in difficult circumstances is not a manifestation of the need to dominate, but a sincere expression of experienced wisdom. She has an intuitive sense of when someone needs her warmth and when she needs her own space, and this sensitivity comes with a clarity that is surprising to her.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, the inherited experience manifests itself as a refined, calm generosity - he does not rush into excessive demonstrativeness at every opportunity to show charisma and does not close himself in the denial of his own light, because, probably, past experience has already taught him that both extremes are equally expensive.

He naturally knows when a team or family needs his inspiration, and when others need his own initiative, reacting from this clarity, not succumbing to the need to be the only source of energy where quiet support is enough. This seems like a rare wisdom for him, which is actually a well-honed skill from the past to carry his gift without pride.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the past experience manifests itself as a hidden tendency to narcissism, which has not yet been released: a person may subconsciously exhaust himself, trying to live up to the image of an always generous, always shining person, or feel acute anxiety every time he cannot give enough warmth to those around him, as if internally convinced that his value is measured only by how much he gives.

Another manifestation of the downside is an excessive, almost panicked need to stay in the spotlight as a tool to protect against a repeat of a past decline or depreciation. In this embodiment, it manifests as a chronic inability to yield attention or recognition to another, as if inwardly one is still afraid of the pain one experienced earlier, losing one's own light.

Minus can also look like oscillating between two extremes without a stable middle: a person either exhausts himself, trying to be the main source of joy for everyone around him, then suddenly shuts down completely, punishing himself for previous generosity - and this cycle repeats itself over and over again, not allowing a real, stable trust in one's own gift to take hold.

In a deeper minus, there is a tendency to internal condemnation - one's own or someone else's - for any manifestation of brightness or, on the contrary, for any modesty - as if the mind remembers a time when both excessive shining and excessive hiding of oneself cost a lot. This manifests as a habit of punishing yourself for every choice related to your own light.

The fifth manifestation of the minus is a chronic fear of any generosity without calculation, paradoxically hidden behind an external lightness: the person fears that any sincere giving is actually a trap of new exhaustion or ingratitude, and unconsciously avoids even healthy, justified opportunities to share warmth, so as not to risk a repetition of past pain.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, the negative manifestation of past experience often looks like a chronic distrust of one's own right to joy without exhaustion - even when the internal state fully allows for sincere and easy sharing of warmth. She may seek confirmation of her own value in how much she manages to give to others, instead of allowing herself sincere, casual generosity that does not cost her effort.

In relationships or business, this manifests as a fear of allowing oneself to fully rest from the role of the source of light, which seems "too risky to stop", forcing one to either maintain constant activity and vigor, or, on the contrary, to suddenly and thoughtlessly shut down under the influence of exhaustion in order to finally get rid of the unbearable pressure.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, the negative manifestation is often disguised as tireless activity - a way to protect himself from the fear of reliving the fall from the pedestal or devaluation, which once cost a lot. He may deliberately avoid admitting his own fatigue or vulnerability in any business or relationship, fearing that any weakness will again lead to a loss of status or respect from those around him.

The tendency to hidden pride in the minus manifests itself as unexpected, sharp attempts to prove one's own importance just when external modesty has lasted too long - as if an inner voice convinces that it is better to remind everyone of one's own light immediately than to wait until it is forgotten.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is situations that remind us of past exhaustion or painful betrayal of generosity: sudden ingratitude, feelings of warmth being devalued, pressure of circumstances that remind us that "I'm being used again" or "I'm losing my own light for others again." Even the slightest hint of such a scenario can activate disproportionately strong anxiety, the root of which is an unlived experience.

The second trigger is prolonged invisibility, which paradoxically causes internal tension, because past experience has taught us not to trust modesty as something safe. Since the past has built up readiness for a sudden loss of status, being in the shadows itself can feel like a harbinger of a new fall.

The third trigger is the need to make a clear choice about how openly to show one's brightness - to shine at full strength or, on the contrary, to hold back for the sake of others. Because past experience has taught us that any extreme can be very costly, the forced certainty at such times is especially strong in activating anxiety and the desire to avoid the decision altogether.

The fourth trigger is contact with people or circumstances that remind - in atmosphere or behavior - of past exhaustion or, on the contrary, of ingratitude of those who were sincerely given warmth, even if the person is not consciously aware of it. The reaction in such a contact is often disproportionate to the situation, because it comes not from the present moment, but from ancient experience.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to realize that the current tendency to self-absorption or, conversely, to the haughty need to be the center of attention may not be rooted in the current situation, but in a long-standing experience of exhaustion or failure. A simple recognition that "maybe it's not about now, but about then" already reduces the intensity of the reaction and gives space for a more conscious choice.

The second step is to consciously practice moderate, honest generosity where previously there would be an automatic desire either to give without a residue or to refuse to share warmth altogether: if you notice a recurring scenario, it is worth stopping and asking yourself what is driving this - a real desire or an old habit.

The third step is to learn to feel the difference between the healthy desire to rejoice and share available today, and the ancient fear of punishment for past generosity or open flamboyance that is left over from the past but no longer meets the safety of the present.

The fourth step is to consciously appreciate and allow yourself the ease that this life brings: to notice at least one moment every day when generosity brought joy without exhaustion, and consciously allow yourself to trust it, even in small things.

The fifth step is to restore confidence in one's own ability to bear the gift of warmth with dignity, gradually, where in the past it probably cost a lot: in small steps, allow oneself both brightness and modesty, which this time do not carry a real threat.

The sixth step is to consciously choose a sincere, calm expression of joy as a conscious choice, not as a way to avoid repeating the past, by doing it gradually: practicing alternating between generous giving and concrete, conscious rest, rather than being stuck at one of the poles.

## Main Task

The main task of this position is to end the long-standing cycle in which generosity and brightness were very expensive, and to learn to live one's own warmth and ability to inspire others as a gift, not as a memory of an experienced exhaustion or a painful fall. It means learning to consciously alternate between giving and restoring, while maintaining complete confidence in your own inner light regardless of external circumstances, something you probably failed to realize in your past experience.

When this task is accomplished, the gift of radiating warmth and capacity for large-scale ideas, inherited from the past, ceases to be a source of hidden exhaustion and wavering between the poles and becomes a real gift - a tool that one uses generously and freely in moments when one really needs to warm oneself or others, no longer fearing that any generosity or any modesty will turn into a new disappointment.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a situation in my life in which I restrain my own light more strictly than it really is necessary, out of fear of repeating a past exhaustion or fall?
- What do I feel at the moment when I can either allow myself sincere, free generosity, or strain again from the old habit of giving myself without a residue?
- To what extent is my tendency to selfishness or, on the contrary, to proud demonstrativeness connected with the fear of experiencing again the betrayal of generosity or the loss of one's own light?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one situation of vacillating between exhausting generosity and a recurring need for attention, and describe it in writing from the first manifestation to the present.
2. Once a day, make one small, concrete gesture of trust in your own gift—allowing yourself a little generosity or a little modest relaxation where before there would have been an extreme.
3. Choose one form of ease not available in past experience and consciously allow yourself to experience it this week, even on a small scale.
4. Before a strong desire to exhaust yourself for the sake of others or sharply withdraw, ask yourself: "Is this really necessary now, or is it an ancient fear from another time?"
5. Create one moment of conscious, balanced self-expression this week — where previously there would have been a desire for either excessive self-giving or complete withdrawal.
6. Consciously practice alternation: devote one day to sincere generosity to others, the other to conscious, calm restoration of one's resources without guilt.
