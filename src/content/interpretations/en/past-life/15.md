# 15. The Devil — Tasks from Past Incarnations

## Brief Essence

This position indicates an experience in which a person had considerable power, influence, or wealth—and probably at some point lost control of that power, allowing it to either enslave themselves or become a tool to subjugate others. Perhaps in that life, power, temptation or passion led a person to a place from which it was difficult to return: dependence, manipulation, a destructive bond that held stronger than any external circumstances. That is why in this embodiment there is simultaneously a strong desire for influence, wealth and magnetism, and a deep, almost instinctive fear of losing control over them. The task of this incarnation is to complete what has been done before: to learn to own one's own power consciously, not allowing it to turn into a cage again - neither for oneself nor for others.

## Natural Strength

From past experience, this energy brings rare wisdom about the cost of unchecked power—like a soul memory of a time when force or temptation led life to a dead end. That is why today this person has a keen, almost bodily sense of when an influence or desire begins to cross the line of healthy control, long before it becomes apparent to others.

A second inherited trait is a natural magnetism and a deep understanding of human psychology, likely honed through past experiences of power or influence. This soul intuitively understands the mechanisms of seduction, persuasion, and control—a knowledge that can either be a gift of honest leadership or a burden of temptation to misuse it.

The third power is the ability to feel the true essence of wealth and resources. Past experience has probably taught this soul that material power without inner freedom is empty—it has left behind a rare ability to build wealth that serves life rather than enslaves it.

The fourth trait is a deep understanding of the price of addiction in any form. Past experience, in which passion or habit may have cost dearly, has left a heightened sensitivity to the subtle difference between pleasure and servitude, a capacity that in this life can be either a gift of confident mastery over one's desires or a burden of chronic fear of any intensity of feeling.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, past experience translates into mature wisdom about power and desire: a person intuitively understands where the line is between healthy influence and manipulation, between pleasure and addiction, and lives life with a rare inner freedom, unafraid of one's own power or depth of passion. She knows how to consciously trust her own ability to control her desires, and not perceive every urge as a threat of losing control.

The gift of feeling the mechanisms of influence, inherited from the past, is revealed as the ability to help others free themselves from addictions and manipulations, not from a position of superiority, but from a sincere desire to share the wisdom gained at a high price. The advice of such a person has weight, because it is based on lived, not theoretical, experience of power and temptation.

Respect for freedom, which helped to survive in former dependence, turns into the ability to build influence and wealth that serve real life, not enslave it, not because a person lacks ambition, but because he already knows: real wealth is not born from controlling others, but from honest, conscious interaction with the world.

The positive expression is also the ability to trust passion and pleasure, without repeating the trauma of loss of control right away: a person consciously allows himself to want, attract, enjoy to the fullest, instead of automatic self-limitation from an old habit. This is not carelessness, but a conscious choice to trust in one's own ability to remain free, despite the memory of times when that freedom was lost.

Another sign is the ability to appreciate the honest influence that this life has as opposed to the previous one. Past experience has taught us not to take power lightly, and in this life this wisdom translates into the ability to consciously build influence, rather than taking it for granted or the right to control others.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, the past experience in the positive manifests as a deep inner freedom without fear of her own magnetism: she naturally feels where the healthy limit of influence or pleasure passes, but this ability is no longer connected with the fear of punishment for power or the disgust of someone else's control over her. She must have had enough experience of power and dependence in the past that in this life she could consciously appreciate the opportunity to live freely by her own choice, and not under the pressure of fear or temptation.

Her ability to attract the desired in difficult or tempting circumstances is not an escape from responsibility, but a sincere expression of experienced wisdom. She intuitively feels when the situation calls for restraint and when for generous acceptance of what she wants, and this decision comes with surprising ease for her.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, the inherited experience manifests itself as a refined, calm confidence in situations of influence and temptation - he does not rush into unlimited power and does not close in fear of his own power, because, probably, past experience has already taught him that both extremes are equally expensive.

He naturally knows how to keep his balance during the trials of power or wealth, see the situation deeper than others and act in time, without forcing control and without suppressing natural ambitions. This appears to be a rare wisdom for him, which is actually a well-honed past skill of living with power without servitude to it.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the past experience manifests itself as a hidden attraction to the same dependence or power, from which perhaps it has not yet been possible to get out: a person can subconsciously reach for control over others, for accumulation, for intense, destructive pleasures - despite the awareness of harm, as if the old habit is stronger than the current reason.

Another manifestation of the negative is an excessive, almost panicky rejection of one's own strength as a tool to protect against the repetition of the past extreme. In this embodiment, it manifests as a chronic denial of influence, wealth or pleasure, as if inwardly a person is still punished for the power or dependence that he experienced earlier.

The negative can also look like oscillating between two extremes without a stable middle: a person sometimes plunges into excessive control or temptation, then sharply limits himself, punishing himself for the previous failure - and this cycle repeats itself again and again, preventing true freedom from taking hold.

At a deeper negative level, there is a tendency to internal condemnation - of oneself or others - for any manifestation of desire or ambition - as if the mind remembers a time when power was very expensive, and prefers to control every nuance so as not to repeat the same thing. This manifests as a habit of punishing oneself for the slightest craving for wealth, influence, or pleasure.

The fifth manifestation of the negative is a chronic fear of any intimacy, paradoxically hidden behind external indifference: a person fears that any deep attachment is actually a hidden addiction, and unconsciously avoids even healthy, nourishing relationships, so as not to risk repeating the past.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, the negative manifestation of past experience often looks like a chronic distrust of one's own magnetism and desires - even when the external circumstances are quite safe. She may seek confirmation of her own "correctness" in how severely she manages to limit herself, instead of allowing herself a sincere, casual enjoyment of influence or wealth.

In business, this manifests itself as a fear of allowing oneself the full realization of success or wealth that seems "too good to be true", which forces one to either keep an inner distance from one's own power, or, on the contrary, suddenly and thoughtlessly dive into control or accumulation in order to finally feel at least some release from constant self-limitation.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, the negative manifestation is often disguised as a philosophical indifference to power and wealth - a way to protect himself from the fear of reliving the addiction that once cost so much. He may deliberately avoid a deep immersion in influence or passion, fearing that any intensity will again lead to a loss of control.

The propensity for a hidden urge to extremes in the negative manifests itself as unexpected, sharp breakdowns into excess - control, temptation, hoarding - precisely when external restraint has lasted too long - as if an inner voice convinces that it is better to give in to yourself than to wait for an inevitable breakdown from the outside.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is situations that remind of the past extreme: the temptation of power or wealth, the pressure of someone else's control, the feeling of having to choose between "conquering" and "being conquered." Even the slightest hint of such a scenario can activate disproportionately strong anxiety, the root of which is an unlived experience.

The second trigger is long-term freedom and affluence, which paradoxically cause internal tension, because past experience has taught me not to trust long-term power as something too fragile. Because the past has built up readiness for a sudden fall, the very lull and success can be felt as a harbinger of disruption.

The third trigger is the need to make a clear choice about power or dependence—to take on more influence or, conversely, to let go of control. Because past experience has taught us that any extreme can be very costly, the forced certainty at such times is especially strong in activating anxiety and the desire to avoid the decision altogether.

The fourth trigger is contact with people or circumstances that remind, whether in behavior or atmosphere, of past dependence or power over someone, even if the person is not consciously aware of it. The reaction in such a contact is often disproportionate to the situation, because it comes not from the present moment, but from ancient experience.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to realize that the current tendency to control or, on the contrary, to give up one's own power excessively may have its roots not in the current situation, but in a long-standing experience of lost freedom. A simple recognition that "maybe it's not about now, but about then" already reduces the intensity of the reaction and gives space for a more conscious choice.

The second step is to consciously practice moderate, honest influence where previously there would be an automatic desire to rush to control or, conversely, to withdraw completely: if you notice a repeating scenario, it is worth stopping and asking yourself what exactly pushes you to this - a real need or a long-standing habit.

The third step is to learn to feel the difference between the healthy desire available today and the ancient fear of punishment for it, which is left over from the past but no longer corresponds to present safety.

The fourth step is to consciously appreciate and allow yourself the freedom that this life gives you: every day notice at least one moment of healthy mastery of your own power, unavailable in the past, and consciously allow yourself to trust it, even in small things.

The fifth step is to restore trust in one's own authority gradually, where in the past it probably came at a high cost: in small steps to allow oneself both influence and letting go of control, which this time do not carry a real threat of extremeness.

The sixth step is to consciously choose freedom as a conscious choice, not as a way to avoid repeating the past, by doing it gradually: practicing alternating between conscious influence and concrete, conscious letting go, rather than getting stuck at one of the poles.

## Main Task

The main task of this position is to end the long-standing cycle in which power, temptation or addiction were very expensive, and to learn to live personal magnetism and the power of desire as a gift, not as a memory of forced or punishing extreme. This means learning to consciously alternate between influence and release, while maintaining complete confidence in one's own ability to remain free or free, something one may have failed to realize in past experiences.

When this task is accomplished, the wisdom about the price of uncontrolled power and the ability to value freedom, inherited from the past, cease to be a source of hidden anxiety and wavering between poles and become a real gift - a tool that a person uses generously and freely in moments when influence or self-influence is really needed, no longer fearing that any desire or any decision will turn into a new addiction.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a situation in my life in which I limited myself more strictly than is really necessary, out of fear of repeating the past extreme of power or dependence?
- What do I feel at the moment when I can either allow myself the sincere pleasure of the influence, or restrain myself again from an old habit?
- To what extent is my tendency to control or, on the contrary, to give up my own power, related to the fear of experiencing the loss of freedom again?

## Practical Plan

1. To notice one recurring situation of wavering between control and giving up one's power and to describe it in writing from the first manifestation to the present.
2. Once a day, make one small, concrete gesture of healthy self-mastery — allowing yourself a little influence or a little letting go where you would have gone to extremes before.
3. Choose one freedom not available in past experiences and consciously allow yourself to experience it this week, even on a small scale.
4. Before a strong desire to rush into control or sharply limit yourself, ask yourself: "Is this really necessary now, or is it an ancient fear from another time?"
5. Create one moment of conscious, balanced influence this week — where previously there would have been a desire for either excessive control or self-restraint.
6. Consciously practice taking turns: devote one day to a sincere manifestation of your own strength, the other to a conscious release of control.
