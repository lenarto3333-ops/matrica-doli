# 15. The Devil — Changing the Financial Pattern After 40

## Brief Essence

After about forty, this energy passes a financial milestone: the income that previously might have depended on the temptation of quick results, on controlling people, or on the chaotic pursuit of status proofs of success, must grow into income that rests on conscious, honest mastery of one's own magnetism and insight - stable wealth built on real value, instead of risky scams or exhausting dependence on external recognition. This is a transition from a financial strategy of "take because I can" to a strategy of "building something that serves both me and others" — and it is this shift that determines whether the second half of life will become financially free and stable, or will turn into an endless pursuit of another dose of status or control, which over time becomes more and more expensive. (It should be remembered: money here is only one of the manifestations of vital energy, and the transformation of the financial scenario always goes hand in hand with changes in other areas of life.)

## Natural Strength

By the age of forty, this energy accumulates a unique resource — the experience of attracting opportunities, people, and resources thanks to natural magnetism and the ability to see real value where others see only the surface. Years lived with a keen instinct for bargains, promising people, and hidden opportunities that often seem risky or overly aggressive actually build a rare capital: the ability to build wealth faster and more accurately than more cautious people.

The natural strength of this milestone is the ability to turn accumulated insight into an accurate sense of real value: what was once just an instinct or a temptation for a quick profit, after forty can become a conscious strategy of investing in real, long-term value instead of betting on a quick but unstable result.

The second power is the ability to see where the financial system or one's own behavior is veering toward dangerous extremes—excessive risk, dependence on others' approval, or compulsive hoarding—before it becomes a problem. If this ability is not blocked by the fear of admitting one's addiction, it is after forty that it begins to work as a real advantage: a person corrects the course in time, while others lose everything in pursuit of another dose of success.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, after forty, a person stops chasing another proof of his own value through status things or control over others, and begins to use the accumulated wisdom about people and resources as a tool for precise, stable growth. Earnings and savings begin to be based on conscious, honest influence, and not on the anxious need to prove one's own strength to oneself and others.

The financial scenario in the positive often shifts from aggressive, sometimes manipulative earning to conscious, consistent building of true wealth: real value investing, partnerships based on mutual benefit, incremental, not risky, growth. What used to be the fear of being left without power or resources is transformed into the ability to move forward with confidence without the need to be controlled by anyone.

In addition, the ability to monetize the natural gift of insight and magnetism also appears: consulting on investments, conducting negotiations, roles where the ability to see the real essence of a deal or a person is valued. People are willing to pay for this wisdom, and it is this wisdom that becomes the competitive advantage of adulthood.

Another sign of a positive transition is the ability to finally let go of the compulsive pursuit of another dose of success or control: a person understands that true wealth is measured by the freedom it gives, not by the number of people or resources over which it was possible to establish power.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

For a woman over forty, financial realization in the positive often takes the form of a conscious, confident use of her own magnetism and insight - she stops proving her worth through control or external approval and begins to trust her own instincts about people and opportunities, combining ambition with inner freedom.

She allows herself to make bold financial decisions that are long overdue, even if before it seemed easier and safer to stay in the shadows or adjust to other people's expectations, realizing that true financial strength is born from honest influence, not from constant self-limitation.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

For a man over forty, the financial scenario in the positive often shifts to the role of someone whose ability to see real value and build honest, stable influence becomes a source of trust: partners or clients turn to him precisely because they see in him a person who does not give in to risky adventures or paralyzing greed.

He allows himself to decisively make important financial decisions based on his own insight, and at the same time he does it honestly, without the need to manipulate or pressure people for a quick result.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative after forty, a person continues to chase another dose of status, control or recognition, just as in their twenties and thirties, for fear that without external evidence of success, they will lose their self-worth. This begins to cost more and more, as the time required to recover from a risky loss decreases, and the compulsive need to prove one's position only increases.

Financial anxiety in the negative is exacerbated by comparisons with those who have apparently more power, status, or wealth. Instead of relying on one's own wisdom about real value, one begins to take more and more risks, or, on the contrary, clings to control over existing resources so tightly that one loses the ability to see new opportunities.

Another manifestation of the negative is dependence on a certain financial strategy or source of income, which has long ceased to be healthy, but it seems impossible to let it go due to the fear of losing a sense of power and control over one's life.

In the deeper negative, there is bitterness from the feeling that years spent chasing external evidence of success have not brought true inner peace, and this bitterness, if not realized, can turn into the belief that "now is too late to change," a thought that itself becomes the biggest obstacle to the change that is needed.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger of this age is the realization that the habit of proving one's worth through status, control, or risky decisions has long cost internal exhaustion, combined with the fear that without this pursuit one will lose meaning and significance.

The second trigger is the need to let go of power over a resource, project, or people, when the usual strategy of "keeping everything under control" stops working for the first time in your life, and trust seems like a scary and unfamiliar skill.

The third trigger is comparing one's situation with those who, having similar experience or resources, ventured on an honest, addiction-free financial path much earlier and now have visible inner freedom.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to take an honest inventory of how much inner peace has been lost through the pursuit of status, control, or external recognition, and honestly assess what that addiction has really cost.

The second step is to consciously reconsider the approach to financial decisions, untying them from the fear of losing power or status and tying them to a clear question: does this decision serve real value and freedom, or just another dose of control.

The third step is to begin systematically applying the accumulated wisdom about people and resources as a separate, monetized resource, even on a small scale—consulting, negotiating, investment partnerships—to test how natural magnetism works as a stand-alone, honest asset.

The fourth step is to consciously diversify your sources of financial security this year, spreading value across several honest avenues instead of betting on power over a single resource or person.

The fifth step is to practice letting go of bitterness about the time wasted through addiction: recognizing the experience as a valuable lesson about the true value of external success, not as evidence of wasted time.

## Main Task

The main task of this financial boundary is to move from earning money driven by the fear of emptiness or the need to control to earning money that is based on a wise, honest mastery of one's own magnetism and insight. This requires an internal reassessment: to recognize that the years spent chasing status and power were not in vain, but became a real capital of financial wisdom, which after forty can and should finally turn into confident, free, stable growth that corresponds to the current stage of life.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- To what extent is my financial ambition based on mature wisdom rather than fear of losing power, status or control?
- Do I have a financial strategy or source of income that I should have let go of long ago, but am holding on to for fear of losing my sense of control?
- What do I feel when I imagine an honest financial path free from dependence on someone else's approval instead of chasing another proof of success?

## Practical Plan

1. Make a list of financial decisions that are dictated by the fear of losing status or control, and honestly assess what this addiction costs.
2. Identify one area of your financial life where you should let go of excessive control and take the first conscious step toward trust.
3. Start one small project that monetizes the natural gift of insight and influence — consultations, negotiations, investment partnership.
4. Consciously diversify the sources of financial security this quarter, distributing the value between several honest directions.
5. Write a list of moments when the pursuit of status cost inner peace, and consciously thank yourself for the experience gained, letting go of bitterness for lost time.
6. Once a month, review financial decisions to see if they were made out of fear of emptiness or out of mature, conscious influence.
