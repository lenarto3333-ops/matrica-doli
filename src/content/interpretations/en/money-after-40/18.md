# 18. The Moon — Changing the Financial Pattern After 40

## Brief Essence

After about forty, this energy passes a financial milestone: the income that previously could be sustained by an inconsistent alternation of anxious accumulation "for a rainy day" and sudden illusory hopes for easy success, or, conversely, on the avoidance of any real financial decisions for fear of making a mistake, must grow into an income that rests on a mature trust in one's own intuition, supported by a sober analysis of the facts and consistent actions This is the transition from the financial strategy "I'm either afraid to look at the numbers, or hope that somehow it will take care of itself" to the strategy "I trust my gut where it is tested by experience, and at the same time act on the basis of real facts", and it is this shift that determines whether the second half of life will become financially stable and calm, or will continue to oscillate between anxiety and illusion. (It should be remembered: money here is only one of the manifestations of vital energy, and the transformation of the financial scenario always goes hand in hand with changes in other areas of life.)

## Natural Strength

By the age of forty, this energy accumulates a unique resource — the experience of living with financial anxiety and illusions, which, if honestly considered, teaches to recognize the difference between real danger and exaggerated fear, between a real premonition and a wish passed off as a fact. After forty, the years spent oscillating between anxiety and dreaminess often turn not into wasted time, but into a deep knowledge of one's own emotional patterns in relationships with money.

The natural strength of this milestone is the ability to turn accumulated experience into accurate financial intuition: what was previously just anxiety or fantasy, after forty can become a conscious ability to sense real risks and opportunities in advance, trusting yourself calmly, not panicking.

## Expression in the Positive

In the plus after forty, a person stops living either in constant financial anxiety or in the expectation that everything will somehow take care of itself, and begins to use the accumulated sensitivity as a real decision-making tool: he trusts an intuitive feeling about deals, partners or development directions, but always checks this feeling with facts.

The financial scenario in the plus often shifts from a chaotic alternation of panic and illusions to conscious, stable money management: a person creates a financial security cushion in time, not out of anxiety, but out of a sober understanding of risks, invests in directions that he intuitively feels promising, and exits projects or relationships that cause a persistent feeling of anxiety, supported by real signals.

The plus is also the ability to monetize the accumulated sensitivity and deep understanding of human emotions: counseling, psychological support, mentoring, working with atmosphere and trust in teams or businesses — areas where the very ability to feel the unobvious and help others cope with their own anxieties is valued.

Another sign of a positive transition is the ability to finally accept one's own financial emotions, without suppressing or denying them: a person admits that anxiety or doubt sometimes arises, calmly lives them, and thanks to this, makes financial decisions clearly, and not under the power of panic or flight into illusion.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

For a woman over forty, financial realization in the plus often takes the form of a calm trust in her own intuition: she allows herself to feel which offer or partnership is really safe, and not only check everything exclusively with logic, and at the same time has the courage to check her feelings with facts before acting decisively.

She learns to distinguish real financial anxiety that needs attention from outdated, inherited fear, and thanks to this, she makes bold, long-delayed decisions not out of panic or a desire to prove something to herself, but from a clear, calm understanding of the situation.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

For a man over forty, the financial scenario in the plus often shifts to the role of a person who knows how to combine sober analysis with trust in his own sense: partners or colleagues appreciate his ability to sense risks and opportunities earlier than others, supported by real experience and facts.

He allows himself to openly admit doubts and anxieties about financial decisions instead of hiding them behind an image of coolness, and it is this honesty with his own emotions that makes his decisions more mature and accurate.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative after forty, a person continues to either live in chronic financial anxiety, imagining the worst-case scenarios of the development of events more often than it is justified by reality, or, on the contrary, avoid any serious financial decisions, hiding in the passive hope that the situation will somehow resolve itself. It starts to cost more and more, because there is less time to correct the accumulated consequences.

Financial anxiety in the red is exacerbated by comparison with those who seem to manage their money easily and calmly. Instead of relying on his own accumulated experience, a person either falls into obsessive control and mistrust of every financial decision, or lashes out at loved ones in moments of financial fear, transferring his own unprocessed anxiety onto them.

Another negative manifestation is getting stuck in illusory financial thinking: a series of "almost ready" plans or investments based more on wishful thinking than on real analysis, repeated financial disappointments, because admitting that the decision needs a sober fact check, and not just a "good feeling", seems more difficult than continuing to dream.

In the deeper downside, there is bitterness about feeling that years of worry or delusion have taken away too much financial stability, and this bitterness, if left unaddressed, can turn into the belief that "my gut is not to be trusted" or, conversely, "I'm doomed to constant financial anxiety," thoughts that themselves become the biggest obstacles to the change needed.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger of this age is the realization that years of anxious avoidance of financial decisions or, conversely, illusory hopes have long since cost lost opportunities, combined with the fear that there is less time left to correct the situation than one would like.

The second trigger is the need to trust and act on your own financial intuition for the first time in your life, instead of either controlling everything in a panic or avoiding decisions, when the usual strategy of anxiety or avoidance for the first time in your life is no longer justified.

The third trigger is a comparison of one's own financial path, full of anxieties or illusions, with those who, having similar circumstances, managed to build a calm, stable financial system much earlier and now have a visible, tangible result.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to take an honest inventory of how much real opportunity has been lost due to chronic anxiety or avoidance of financial decisions, and honestly assess what the model has actually cost.

The second step is to consciously re-evaluate your approach to financial decisions by learning to distinguish between a real intuitive signal and an anxious fantasy: intuition is usually calm and concrete, while anxiety is emotionally charged and repetitive.

The third step is to begin systematically applying the accumulated sensitivity to people and situations as a separate, monetized resource, even on a small scale—consulting, mentoring, work that relies on understanding human emotions and motivations—to test how intuition works as an independent, valuable asset.

The fourth step is to consciously strengthen this year's financial structure where it depends too much on anxiety or momentary mood, building simple, sustainable decision-making systems instead of panicked or delusional reactions.

The fifth step is to practice letting go of bitterness about troubling or illusory decisions of the past: recognizing these experiences as valuable lessons about how to trust yourself, not as evidence of wasted time.

## Main Task

The main task of this financial boundary is to move from earning money guided by chronic anxiety or illusory hopes to earning money based on a mature trust in one's own intuition, supported by a sober analysis of facts. This requires an internal reassessment: to recognize that the years lived in fluctuations between fear and dreaminess were not in vain, but became a real capital of self-discovery, which after forty can and should finally turn into a confident, calm, stable financial decision that corresponds to the current stage of life.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- To what extent is my trust in my own financial intuition based on real experience rather than anxiety or wishful thinking?
- Is there a financial decision that I have been putting off for years out of fear, or, on the contrary, continue to put off, hoping that it will take care of itself?
- How do I feel when I imagine a financial path built on calm trust in myself, instead of the usual cycle of anxiety or illusion?

## Practical Plan

1. Make a list of financial decisions dictated by chronic anxiety or avoidance and honestly assess what this pattern is worth.
2. Identify one financial decision that has been put off until now because of fear or illusory hope, and take the first conscious, concrete step toward solving it this week.
3. Start one small project that monetizes the accumulated sensitivity to people and situations — consulting, mentoring, work based on understanding the emotions of others.
4. Consciously strengthen the weakest link in the financial structure of this quarter by building a simple, stable decision-making system instead of anxious or illusory reactions.
5. Write a list of moments when anxiety or illusion cost financial peace, and consciously thank yourself for the experience gained, letting go of bitterness for lost time.
6. Once a month, review financial decisions to see if they were made out of anxiety or illusion, or out of mature, calm self-confidence.
