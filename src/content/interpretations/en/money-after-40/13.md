# 13. Death — Changing the Financial Pattern After 40

## Short summary

Around forty, this energy passes a financial milestone: the income that used to depend on the willingness to hold on to a proven source of income must grow into an income that rests on the conscious courage to finish what has exhausted itself and open up space for a new direction. This is a transition from a financial strategy of "keeping what you already have, because it is safer that way" to a strategy of "letting go of the old, even with losses, for the sake of something that truly corresponds to the current stage" - and it is this shift that determines whether the second half of life will become financially more vibrant, or will turn into a slow fading in a role or business that has long ceased to bring both money and meaning. (It should be remembered: money here is only one of the manifestations of vital energy, and the transformation of the financial scenario always goes hand in hand with changes in other areas of life.)

## Natural force

By the age of forty, this energy accumulates a unique resource — the experience of going through crises, collapses, and sudden changes in circumstances that others would be afraid to even imagine. Years lived through several significant transformations, often seen as chaos or failure, actually build a rare capital: the ability not to panic when a usual source of income suddenly disappears, and to quickly find a new path where others are stuck in despair.

The natural power of this milestone is the ability to transform the accumulated readiness for change into a precise sense of the moment of completion: what used to be just instability or tossing, after forty can become a conscious choice to close the old direction in time and start a new one, while it still has a resource for development.

A second strength is the ability to see when an entire industry, revenue model, or professional role is exhausting itself, before it becomes obvious to most. If this ability is not blocked by the fear of loss of stability, then it is after forty that it begins to work as a real advantage: a person moves in time to a new direction, while others continue to hold on to something that is already losing value.

## Manifestation in the plus

In the plus after forty, a person stops holding on to an outdated income only because of the fear of the unknown, and begins to use the accumulated courage as a tool for accurate, timely renewal. Earning begins to rely on the conscious choice to complete what has exhausted oneself, rather than anxiously maintaining the usual just for the illusion of stability.

The financial scenario in the plus often shifts from passively maintaining an outdated earning model to consciously launching a new direction that corresponds to who a person has become over the years. What used to be a fear of sudden changes turns into the ability to quickly recognize the moment when it is worth closing one career chapter and starting the next.

The advantage is also the ability to monetize the experience of going through transformations: consulting on changes, anti-crisis management, roles where the ability to recognize the end in time and start anew is valued. People are willing to pay for this courage, and it is precisely this that becomes the competitive advantage of adulthood.

Another sign of a positive transition is the ability to let go of financial projects or sources of income that have exhausted themselves, even if they were once primary and provided a sense of security. A person understands: holding on to the old for fear of loss is more expensive than the very fact of a conscious change of direction.

### Manifestation in women is positive

For a woman over forty, financial realization in the plus often takes the form of consciously closing a case or role that has long ceased to correspond to her internal development, and a decisive start in a new direction, based on years of accumulated experience. She stops holding on to the usual income "just in case" and starts to trust her own feeling that it's time for an update.

She allows herself to abandon financial obligations or directions that have long been out of line with her real values, even if they seem stable, understanding that true security is born from conformity to the present stage, not from simply holding on to the past.

### Manifestation in the husband is positive

For a man after forty, the financial scenario in the plus often shifts to the role of someone whose courage to end the old and start anew becomes a source of income: younger colleagues or partners turn to him for advice precisely because they see in him a person who is not afraid to close even a successful chapter for the sake of an even more precise next step.

He allows himself to decisively complete the financial lines that have exhausted him, without succumbing to the pressure to hold on to the usual for the sake of the illusion of security, and at the same time he does it with dignity, without destroying everything around him for the sake of the very feeling of control.

## Manifestation in the minus

In the red after forty, a person continues to hold on to an outdated source of income just as he did in his twenties and thirties, fearing that any change will take away what little stability he already has. It starts to cost more and more, because there is less time to recover after a failed transition, and the fear of loss only grows.

Financial anxiety in the red is exacerbated by comparison with those who dared to change direction and achieved results while the person continued to cling to the usual. Instead of relying on its own courage to transform, it begins to question its value, stuck in an outdated model where a decisive step should have been taken a long time ago.

Another manifestation of the negative is a sudden, chaotic destruction of financial stability due to accumulated irritation, instead of a gradual and conscious transition to a new direction. A person can impulsively quit a stable business without a plan, only to finally feel at least some control over a situation that has long seemed suffocating.

In the deeper negative, there is bitterness because of the feeling that the years invested in an outdated business are irretrievably lost, and this bitterness, if not realized, can turn into the belief that "it's too late to start over" - a thought that itself becomes the biggest obstacle to the necessary change.

## What can be negative

The strongest trigger of this age is the realization that the usual source of income has long since exhausted itself, combined with the fear that there is no time or resource to start over.

The second trigger is the need to make a decision about a sudden change of direction in a short period of time, when the usual strategy of "sticking to the proven" for the first time in life stops working, and the determination to finish seems a scary and unfamiliar skill.

The third trigger is comparing one's stagnation with those who, having similar experience or resources, dared to transform much earlier and now have a visible advantage.

## How to return energy to the plus side

The first step is to take an honest inventory of how many years and effort have been invested in an outdated business out of fear of losing stability, and to honestly assess what it has really cost to hold onto it.

The second step is to consciously revise the approach to financial decisions, untying them from the fear of loss and tying them to a clear, time-bound transition plan: what exactly needs to be completed and when to start anew.

The third step is to begin to systematically apply the accumulated courage of transformation as a separate, monetized resource, even on a small scale - change consulting, crisis mentoring - to test how the experience of going through failures works as an independent asset.

The fourth step is to consciously end one outdated financial direction or commitment this year, doing so gradually and with dignity, rather than a sudden, devastating break.

The fifth step is to practice letting go of bitterness about the years invested in a cause that has exhausted itself: to recognize this experience as a valuable foundation for a new phase, not as evidence of wasted time.

## The main task

The main task of this financial milestone is to move from earning money paralyzed by the fear of loss of stability to earning money that relies on the courage to finish the old things on time and start new ones. This requires an internal reassessment: to recognize that the years invested in a business that exhausted itself were not in vain, but became a real capital of experience, which after forty can and should finally turn into a confident, timely transition to something that really corresponds to the current stage of life.

## Questions for self-reflection

- How many years and strength have I held on to a financial direction that has exhausted itself long ago, only because of the fear of losing stability?
- To what extent is my willingness to make financial changes based on mature courage, rather than irritation or panic?
- Do I have a source of income or an obligation that should have been completed a long time ago, but I am holding on to it out of fear?
- What do I feel when I imagine a decisive, complete transition to a new financial direction?
- Do I trust my own ability to go through the financial transformation enough to take the first step?

## Practical plan

1. Make a list of financial areas that have exhausted themselves and honestly assess what it costs to maintain each of them.
2. Set a clear, time-bound deadline for completing one outdated financial obligation that is currently being held "just in case."
3. Start one small project that monetizes the experience of going through financial transformations — consultations, anti-crisis support, mentoring.
4. Consciously and gradually complete one outdated financial direction of this quarter, with dignity, not a sharp break.
5. Write a list of years or decisions invested in a business that exhausted itself, and consciously thank yourself for this experience, letting go of bitterness.
6. Once a month, review financial decisions to see if they were made out of fear of loss or out of mature readiness for renewal.
