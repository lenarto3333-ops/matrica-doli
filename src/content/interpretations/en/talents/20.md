# 20. Judgement — Natural Talents

## Brief Essence

The talent of the twentieth arcana is the ability to honestly and fairly assess the situation, to see what is really happening behind the facade of words and excuses, and to help finish what has exhausted itself so that a new one can begin. This is the talent of balanced judgment, sharp intuition and the ability to turn difficult endings into real revival - for yourself and for those around you.

## Natural Strength

The basis of this talent is a keen ability to distinguish between truth and self-deception: a person feels when a situation or a person is pretending to be true, and knows how to call a spade a spade without excessive drama.

The second aspect of talent is strong intuition, almost clairvoyance: a decision or understanding comes not through long analysis, but through a sudden clear sense of how things really are.

The third facet of talent is the ability to help end the old and begin the new: where others are stuck in protracted forgiveness or denial, this person finds an honest point of completion, after which true rebirth is possible.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, this talent is revealed in roles that require a fair assessment - mentoring, counseling, accompanying people through crises, working with archives, history or genealogy, any matter where it is important to honestly understand what is really happening.

Talent also manifests itself in the ability to help other people honestly end a difficult phase of life - relationships, work, the old way of being - and support them in the transition to the new, without forcing them to either linger in the past or run away from it.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, this talent is often manifested as a subtle sense of the truth in relationships and situations — she helps loved ones to see what they themselves avoid admitting, doing it gently, without judgment, and supports them on the path to renewal.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, this talent is often manifested as a sober, unbiased assessment of things and people - he knows how to honestly sum up where others draw uncertainty, and help a team or family complete an old stage and move on.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, this talent turns into endless re-judgment - one's own or someone else's: a person returns again and again to already made decisions, doubts the correctness of the choice made and exhausts himself with internal judgment instead of trusting the already made conclusion.

Another negative manifestation is excessive, sometimes harsh criticism of others, especially parents or family: a person uses a keen sense of the truth not for an honest conclusion, but for protracted disputes in which old resentments only deepen, not heal.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

Most often, this talent is distorted in an environment where the correctness of decisions was constantly questioned or punished - then a person learns not to trust his own conclusions and endlessly check them. The second trigger is an unfinished family conflict or an old grudge, which every time activates the sharpness of judgment not for clarity, but to protect the old position in the dispute.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is useful to learn to trust an honest conclusion that has already been made and not to return to it every time, distinguishing renewal of thought from obsessive doubt. The second step is to consciously direct your acute sense of truth to end rather than continue an argument, especially in relationships with parents or family.

## Main Task

The main task is to bring the natural ability to see the truth, intuition and sense of a just conclusion to the mastery of helping yourself and others to honestly close old stages and start new ones, without getting stuck in endless re-judgment of the past.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I trust the conclusions already made, or do I check and doubt them again and again?
- Do I use my sense of truth to help end a difficult situation, or to prove myself right in an argument?
- What family history or image do I still not consider truly complete?

## Practical Plan

1. This week, for once, consciously stop the internal re-judgment of an already made decision, reminding yourself of the conclusion that has already been made.
2. Use your sense of truth to help someone look at a difficult situation honestly — gently, without judgment.
3. Take one concrete step toward ending a long-standing family dispute or grudge, even if it's just an internal decision.
