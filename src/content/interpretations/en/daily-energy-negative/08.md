# 8. Justice — Daily Energy in the Negative

## In Brief

Today, even small things can leave you feeling wronged and mentally keeping score: who owes whom, who acted unfairly, and who deserves reproach. The day's risk is confusing the desire to restore justice with the need to prove you are right at any cost.

## Natural Strength

Even on a challenging day, the eighth energy retains its keen inner compass for honesty. Direct it toward calmly checking the facts rather than searching for someone to blame: what actually happened, and what did hurt feelings add to the story?

## Expression in the Negative

You may judge other people's actions harshly, divide everyone into those who are “right” and “wrong,” or feel compelled to lecture someone or bring up an old offense right now. A minor injustice—someone cutting in line, a broken promise, an unfair comment—can provoke a disproportionately strong reaction.

## What to Avoid Today

It is best to avoid categorical statements such as “you always do this,” publicly exposing other people's mistakes, or making decisions while hurt. Also steer clear of old arguments that seemed long settled: today brings the temptation to reopen them so you can finally “prove your point.”

## How to Move Through the Day More Gently

Before voicing a judgment, ask yourself whether you want to clarify the situation or simply punish someone. Create a pause between the facts and your conclusion. If you feel hurt, first acknowledge it honestly to yourself, then decide whether it is worth discussing and how best to do so.

## The Day's Main Message

Truth spoken with the intent to wound ceases to be justice. Today, it is more valuable to remain an honest witness to the situation than to become its harsh judge.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I want to clarify the situation right now, or punish the person who hurt me?
- What old grievance am I still carrying even though its original cause has passed?

## Practical Plan

1. Before responding sharply, pause and write down exactly what felt unfair.
2. Set aside one judgment or complaint that you are not yet ready to express calmly.
3. Recall one situation in which you also bore some responsibility.
4. End the day without opening any new “accounts”—even if one remains unspoken.
