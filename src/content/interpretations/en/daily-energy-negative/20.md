# 20. Judgement — Daily Energy in the Negative

## Brief Essence

Today there is a risk of getting stuck in endless revision of own decisions or old grudges, instead of making one honest conclusion and moving on. The day can bring either excessive severity to yourself, or an unexpected aggravation of old family tension.

## Natural Strength

Even on a negative day, the twentieth energy carries a rare gift — the ability to honestly and soberly look at one's own path, feel the support of the family and see the situation with a clearer than usual eye. This gift never goes away, even if today it is suppressed by anxiety or old pain.

## Expression in the Negative

Internal self-judgment is possible - scrolling through the same decision or action over and over again, as if the verdict has not yet been passed, although life has long since moved on. Or outwardly — a sudden aggravation of unspoken claims to parents or family, when an old grudge breaks through more sharply than one would like.

## What to Avoid Today

It is better to avoid the decision to "deal with the past finally" today under the influence of emotion - such a decision is rarely sober and more often deepens guilt or conflict. You should also avoid harsh conversations with parents or relatives if there is more irritation than clarity inside.

## How to Move Through the Day More Gently

If you catch yourself on the endless "it should have been different", try to record one honest conclusion and consciously allow yourself to close the topic at least for today. It is also useful to remember that family and the past are a support, not a judge, and to turn mentally to this support instead of self-blame.

## Main Message of the Day

Today asks you to remember that true self-judgment is one honest conclusion, not lifelong persecution. Allowing the past to be lived does not mean justifying it, but means finally moving on.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What decision or action do I keep replaying, even though life has long since moved on?
- Is there an unspoken grievance toward parents or family that's surfacing more sharply than I'd like today?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one honest conclusion about a situation you keep replaying, and let yourself close the topic, at least for today.
2. If an old grievance is flaring up, hold off on a sharp conversation with family today — give yourself time for clarity.
3. Remember one way your family or past experience is a support for you, not a judge.
