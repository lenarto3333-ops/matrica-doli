# 13. Death — Point of Inner Comfort

## Explanation of the point of mental comfort

The point of mental comfort shows a state in which it is easier for a person to recover, make decisions and feel that he lives in harmony with himself. It's not a requirement to stay in one mood all the time, and it's not an assessment of character. Rather, it is a hint about the conditions that support internal balance and about signals that the usual way of life has begun to exhaust.

For the thirteenth energy, comfort is associated with the right to end what has already died and start anew, without holding on to a form that no longer works. It is important for a person to feel that the space around him is not "frozen forever" - that there is room for change, for saying goodbye to the old and for the birth of a new stage without excessive fear. This is not a need to destroy for the sake of destruction, but a deeper trust in the natural cycle: something must end to make room for something else. When this right is recognized, the strength to move forward appears; when it is taken away or devalued, there is an internal resistance and fear that is difficult to explain in words.

## What fills you with energy (13)

The most fulfilling thing is the permission to end something that no longer makes sense to continue - without feeling guilty for "betraying" the past and without the pressure to justify the decision to everyone who is used to the old order of things. When endings are accepted as a normal, rather than shameful, part of life, the tension subsides on its own.

The resource provides a sense that change is not a disaster: an opportunity to calmly say goodbye to a role, habit, relationship or phase that has exhausted itself, knowing that in their place something alive will appear, not emptiness. Thirteenth energy comes alive when it doesn't need to keep things the same just for a sense of stability.

An environment where change is not judged is important - where no one scolds with the words "you always destroy everything" or "why did it start", and transformation is perceived as a natural movement, not betrayal. When there is at least one person around who can support the decision to close the old section, it significantly relieves internal pressure.

The experience of conscious farewell is also filling — a small closing ritual, an honest conversation that puts an end to it, a symbolic gesture that allows you to let go of what has already passed. It's not about the rush to "forget and move on," but about the right to live the ending to its fullest without being stuck in it forever.

A resource is also a space free from imposed expectations of remaining the same or unchanged: the opportunity to allow yourself to become a different person than a year or five years ago, without fear that loved ones will refuse to recognize these changes. It is there that the feeling is born that life can be renewed without losing its integrity.

It is also important to physically free up space — to disassemble things that have not been used for a long time, to remove things that are tied to the previous stage. When the outer space is freed from excess, it also becomes easier to breathe and see the next step internally.

## Resource state of energy

In the resource, a person knows how to let go of what has exhausted himself, without perceiving it as a defeat or loss of a part of himself. She calmly lives the moment of completion, without trying to artificially prolong what has already lost life, and does not panic about the uncertainty that inevitably follows any change.

There is a rare courage to start anew — without the need to have a pre-prepared plan for each next step. The resourceful thirteenth energy trusts that after completion, something new always comes, even if it is not yet clear what exactly it will be. Others notice in this person a certain ease in his attitude to change: he does not cling to what has already passed its time.

In such a state, a person is not afraid of the intermediate space between "no longer" and "not yet" - the time when the old has ended and the new has not yet taken shape. She trusts this transition as much as she trusts her own ability to build something new. Her calm about changes is contagious: next to her, others also allow themselves to close old chapters, rather than dragging them out for years out of a sense of duty.

## Signs of accumulated tension

The first sign is often stubbornly holding on to things that have long since lost meaning: relationships, jobs, habits or roles that are exhausting, but the person continues to hold on to them out of fear of the unknown. The consummation that would have given relief is postponed again and again, and the longer it goes on, the harder it is to decide on a step.

The second sign is abruptness and intolerance to changes that come from the outside: irritation when someone suggests something new, resistance to any violation of the usual order, even if this order has long ceased to be useful. This is no longer stability, but protective rigidity, which appears where a person is afraid to admit that the old is no longer working.

Another signal is the constant mental return to the previous stage, comparison of the present with "how it was before", idealization of what has already ended. There is a feeling that the present life is only a pale shadow of what has been lost, and the future seems empty and worthless.

In relationships, the accumulated tension is often revealed by the inability to put an end to what has long been exhausted: a person knows that it is worth closing the chapter, but postpones a decisive conversation, fearing the very finality. If it lasts for a long time, is accompanied by a constant feeling of being stuck, anxiety or deep sadness for what has been lost, you should contact a specialist.

## To regain strength

1. Identify one area of ​​life where you are holding on to a form that has already exhausted yourself, and honestly name it yourself.
2. Allow yourself a small closing ritual—a word, action, or symbolic gesture that puts an end to an old chapter.
3. Ask yourself: is this decision to hold on to the old about true value or fear of the unknown?
4. Free some physical space from things that are tied to the previous stage and have not been used for a long time.
5. Allow yourself not to have a ready-made plan for "after" - trust that clarity will come already in the process of movement.
6. Notice the irritation at the changes around you and ask yourself what exactly is behind it.
7. Talk to someone who can support your decision to complete something important without devaluing your choice.
8. Try to live one day as if a new stage had already begun, and note how it feels.
9. Remind yourself that the end of one thing does not cancel the value of the past - it only makes room for the next.
10. If you feel that you are stuck between the past and the future for a long time and it is exhausting, consider talking to a psychologist who will help you live the transition consciously.

## Summary of the gradual return to equilibrium

Thirteenth energy balance is not restored when a person destroys everything in a row for the sake of a sense of renewal, and not when they hold on to each old chapter of life for fear of change. It returns when ending once again becomes a conscious choice, not an escape or a catastrophe, and when the new begins not out of despair, but out of trust in one's own ability to build anew.

You don't have to choose between loyalty to the past and openness to the future. Mature comfort unites them: the memory of the past remains valuable, and the determination to complete what has exhausted itself makes room for a new stage. Return to balance in small steps — through one honest ending, one freed space, and one trust that after a closed chapter, the next one always begins.
