# 22. The Fool — Point of Inner Comfort

## Understanding the Inner Comfort Point

The point of mental comfort shows a state in which it is easier for a person to recover, make decisions and feel that he lives in harmony with himself. It's not a requirement to stay in one mood all the time, and it's not an assessment of character. Rather, it is a hint about the conditions that support internal balance and about signals that the usual way of life has begun to exhaust.

For the twenty-second energy, comfort is associated with freedom and new experience — the right to start with a clean slate, without dragging along the burden of old expectations and other people's scenarios. It is important for a person to have a space where he can act easily and spontaneously, to trust the flow of life instead of trying to calculate everything in advance. It is equally important to maintain sincerity - to speak and act without a mask, without needing to appear more serious or "right" than one actually is. When this right is recognized, a rare lightness appears: a person is not afraid of the unknown, because he feels that fate is leading him somewhere good, even when the route is not planned in advance. When the need for freedom is ignored — forcing either to live exclusively according to someone else's plan, or, on the contrary, to recklessly run away from any responsibility — either quiet suffocation or chronic anxiety from one's own groundlessness settles inside.

## What Fills You with Energy (22)

The most fulfilling thing is the opportunity to start something new without fear of making a mistake — a new project, a new city, a new circle of people, a new habit. The twenty-second energy comes to life where it is allowed to act without having a ready-made plan for each step forward, and where the process of discovery itself is more important than a guaranteed result.

The resource gives spontaneity — a decision made "now" and not according to a weekly schedule, an unexpected trip, a conversation with a stranger, a change of route simply because it suddenly felt like it. For this energy, it is important to feel that the day is not scheduled until the last minute, that there is room for surprise in it.

Trust in fate fills you - the feeling that not everything needs to be controlled, that life will lead to the right people and opportunities if you don't cling to a single scenario. Twenty-second energy comes to life when it is allowed to let go of the wheel at least partially and believe in its own path.

The resource is sincerity in relationships — the ability to speak directly, without playing to the public, to show real emotions, not a socially approved version of yourself. This energy loves a space where you don't have to keep a mask of decency or seriousness all the time.

An important right to ease is to perceive difficulties not as a catastrophe, but as another plot twist from which you can leave with a smile. When a person allows himself this ease, excess tension disappears, which is often more difficult than the problem itself.

The lack of rigid frameworks also fills us up — the ability not to be attached to one place, status or role for a long time if they no longer correspond to our inner state, but to try something else, remaining open to change.

## Resourceful State of the Energy

In a resource, a person knows how to combine freedom with responsibility: he easily starts something new, is not afraid of the unknown, but at the same time does not run away from the consequences of his own decisions and brings the beginnings to a reasonable conclusion. This is not the carelessness of a teenager, but the mature lightness of an adult who knows that what is lost can be made up for, and a mistake can be corrected.

Sincere openness to people appears - resource twenty-second energy does not play roles and does not hide real reactions, and that is why it becomes easier for others to be themselves with it. A person feels a deep trust in life: he believes that even an unplanned turn can turn out to be successful, and this removes unnecessary anxiety about the future.

In such a state, a person knows how to take deliberate risks: to try new things, without risking what he is not ready to lose, and to be responsible for the consequences of his adventures, and not to blame the circumstances. There is a calm confidence that freedom and reliability can exist side by side, and not mutually exclusive.

## Signs of Accumulated Tension

The first sign is often unbridled adventurism - a person grabs a new idea without thinking about the consequences, promises and does not fulfill, changes plans at the last moment, leaving others to deal with the results of his recklessness.

The second sign is the opposite: instead of freedom, a rigid limitation fueled by fear appears. A person is afraid of any new step, clings to the usual routine even when it has not brought joy for a long time, and begins to perceive the very idea of ​​change as a threat.

Another signal is a loss of sincerity: a person hides behind a mask of frivolity or, on the contrary, excessive seriousness, being afraid to show true feelings. The twenty-second energy is especially sensitive to how honestly a person allows himself to be with others.

The tension also manifests itself as a chronic avoidance of responsibility — for words, for promises, for the consequences of one's own choices, when it is easier to say "that's how it turned out" than to admit one's role in the situation. If this is accompanied by a long-term inability to complete anything, a growing fear of any change, or a constant feeling of internal suffocation, you should consult a specialist.

## How to Restore Your Strength

1. Do something today that you haven't done before - even a small one: a new route, a new dish, a conversation with a stranger.
2. Allow yourself one spontaneous decision without a prior plan — where to go, what to cook, who to call.
3. Notice a promise that hasn't been kept yet, and either follow through on it or be honest that plans have changed.
4. Find an area of ​​life where the rules have become too rigid without real need, and consciously give yourself a little more space.
5. Spend the day without a detailed plan - allow yourself to follow the mood and circumstances, not the schedule.
6. Tell someone the truth about your feelings instead of the usual polite version.
7. Ask yourself: am I avoiding responsibility for fear of consequences, or am I really just trying something new?
8. Notice the fear that keeps you in the routine and name it out loud - it often relieves half the tension.
9. Trust the circumstances for once instead of trying to control everything in advance and see what happens.
10. If the long-term inability to get things done or, on the contrary, the fear of any changes does not go away on its own, consider talking to a psychologist who will help you understand the reasons.

## Summary: A Gradual Return to Balance

The balance of the twenty-second energy is not restored when a person recklessly runs away from any obligations, and not when he locks himself in the usual routine out of fear of the new. It returns when freedom ceases to be an escape and becomes a conscious choice: to try something new and at the same time answer for the consequences of that choice.

You don't have to choose between reckless adventurism and life within a rigid framework. Mature comfort combines lightness and reliability: the ability to trust fate and open up to new experiences remains, and the willingness to take responsibility for one's decisions saves from the chaos that would otherwise accumulate around unfinished business. Return to balance in small steps - through one spontaneous act, one fulfilled promise, and one sincere conversation without a mask.
