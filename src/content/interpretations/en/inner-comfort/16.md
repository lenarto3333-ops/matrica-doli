# 16. The Tower — Point of Inner Comfort

## Understanding the Point of Inner Comfort

The point of mental comfort shows a state in which it is easier for a person to recover, make decisions and feel that he lives in harmony with himself. It's not a requirement to stay in one mood all the time, and it's not an assessment of character. Rather, it is a hint about the conditions that support internal balance and about signals that the usual way of life has begun to exhaust.

For the sixteenth energy, comfort is associated with the right to let go of what has been accumulated from time to time - to honestly admit that some structure in life is already standing on the word of honor and cracks, and allow yourself to destroy it consciously, and not wait for it to fall by itself and take with it more than necessary. It is important for a person to have a space where the truth can be spoken out loud, even if it is harsh and uncomfortable, and where it is not perceived as betrayal or destruction, but as the only honest way to move forward. This is not the need to constantly tear everything down and start over, but a deeper trust in the fact that after a fall, you can always build something stronger than it was. When this right is recognized, there is a rare ability to withstand a crisis without panic and emerge from it stronger; when the need to face the truth honestly is suppressed, forced to pretend that everything is fine, the tension does not go away, but only builds up until the next explosion.

## What Fills You with Energy (16)

The most fulfilling thing is being able to call a spade a spade — admitting that an old arrangement, relationship, job, or belief no longer holds its weight, and not pretending otherwise for the sake of others. When the truth can be told directly, without mitigations that don't really change anything, the tension subsides by itself.

The resource makes it possible to survive the earthquake and see it not as a catastrophe, but as liberation from what has been pressing for so long. The sixteenth energy comes to life when it is allowed to go through a drastic change consciously, maintaining the trust that there is a simpler, more honest and stronger life on the other side of the blockage.

It fills with a felt, not feigned, calm after a crisis: when the storm has already passed, and not just seemed to subside, and one can feel a real relief from the fact that one no longer has to maintain a facade that was about to crack.

The resource is the recognized inner strength—the experience gained precisely through the experience of falling, not through the avoidance of any upheaval. It is important to the sixteenth energy that its resilience is seen and appreciated precisely as an acquired rather than an innate lightness.

An important right to anger and harshness at the moment when the patient breaks down - without the requirement to always remain calm and "comfortable". When there are people around who are not afraid of someone else's intensity and do not run away from it, there is a space for sincerity.

The opportunity to build anew on one's own terms is also fulfilling: to choose exactly what to restore after the earthquake, and what to leave forever in ruins, instead of being obliged to rebuild everything as it was before.

## Resourceful State of the Energy

In a resource, a person is not afraid to admit that something in his life has rotted or outlived its usefulness, and is able to take this step consciously, before circumstances force him to do so. She knows how to let go of old structures without too much drama or drag—just as sharply as necessary, and not a beat more.

There is a rare ability to turn the accumulated intensity into action: instead of lashing out at random people or suppressing the tension in yourself, the resourceful sixteenth energy directs it to where changes are really needed - in an honest conversation, in a decisive step, in completing something that has long exhausted itself.

In such a state, there is a hardening that has nothing to do with callousness: a person who has already experienced more than one fall stops panicking at the illusion of stability and instead trusts in his own ability to survive and rebuild again if he has to. It gives inner peace even in the midst of turbulence around.

## Signs of Accumulated Tension

The first sign is often irritation, which has been building up for weeks, and then erupts suddenly and out of proportion to the occasion, a sudden outburst over a trifle that really does not concern the moment, but everything that has been held back for a long time. This is not a "sudden aggressiveness of character", but a sign that the truth has not been spoken out loud for too long.

The second sign is a constant feeling "on the edge", as if any little thing could be the last straw: tense shoulders, shortness of breath, readiness to defend even before someone says something. This often masks an old experience where a concussion has already come without warning once, and the body is still prepared for it.

Another signal is a series of small, almost imperceptible “explosions”: harsh words, slamming doors, short bursts of anger in small situations that seem insignificant in themselves, but add up to a pattern of constantly pent-up pressure seeping out in small portions instead of one honest release.

Accumulated tension also manifests itself as numbness before necessary changes - when a person clings to an apparently unsustainable situation for years because he fears the very moment of destruction more than the exhaustion of what is ongoing. If this is accompanied by repeated crises that do not teach anything, or the feeling that tension and anger control life more than one's own will, it is worth contacting a suitable specialist.

## To Restore Your Strength

1. Honestly name a situation, relationship or habit in your life lately that has been holding on to bare patience rather than real support.
2. Allow yourself to briefly and safely vent pent-up tension—a movement, a shout in an empty room, an honest note on paper—instead of waiting for it to come through.
3. Ask yourself: am I avoiding the inevitable change because I fear the very moment of failure more than the exhaustion of something that has not been working for a long time?
4. Notice if you get frustrated over small things and if this is not a sign that the greater truth has never been spoken out loud.
5. Notice if the body is constantly ready to strike—shoulders tense, jaw clenched—and consciously give it permission to relax, even for a few moments.
6. Allow yourself to recognize your own hard-earned hardiness, without belittling or ashamed that the road to it was not easy.
7. Choose one outdated structure in your life to dismantle consciously and gradually, instead of waiting for it to fall by itself.
8. Talk to someone who can tolerate your bluntness and harshness when the pent-up finally comes out.
9. Remind yourself that admitting brokenness is not defeat, but often the only honest path to stronger footing.
10. If you feel that anger, anxiety, or recurring crises rule your life for a long time and it is debilitating, consider talking to a psychologist who can help you safely live the accumulated tension.

## Summary: A Gradual Return to Balance

The balance of the sixteenth energy is not restored when a person holds back for years everything that asks to come out, and not when he crushes everything in a row every time he feels the slightest tension. It returns when the shake becomes a conscious tool and not a blind element: when the old is released before it has time to fall itself, and when the accumulated intensity is channeled into honest action, not into sudden outbursts or silent exhaustion.

You don't have to choose between constant calm and a destructive storm. A mature comfort unites them: the ability to withstand the necessary shock remains a valuable strength, and the ability to call the truth out loud in time liberates from accumulation, which would otherwise break through itself, and often at the worst moment. Return to balance in small steps—one honestly named crack, one safe release of tension, and one trust that after a fall you can always build back stronger than before.
