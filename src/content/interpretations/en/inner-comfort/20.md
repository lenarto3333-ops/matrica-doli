# 20. Judgement — Point of Inner Comfort

## Understanding the Inner Comfort Point

The point of mental comfort shows a state in which it is easier for a person to recover, make decisions and feel that he lives in harmony with himself. It's not a requirement to stay in one mood all the time, and it's not an assessment of character. Rather, it is a hint about the conditions that support internal balance and about signals that the usual way of life has begun to exhaust.

For the twentieth energy, comfort is associated with the right to honestly look back on the path traveled and one's own decisions - without the need to justify every step and without endless self-flagellation for what once went wrong. It is important for a person to have a space where he can calmly admit the mistakes of the past, draw a conclusion from them and live on, and not carry them as a sentence that will never be closed. It is equally important to feel a connection with the family - not as a burden of responsibilities, but as a support, the strength of the roots, which helps to stand more confidently on one's own feet. When this right is recognized, a rare clarity appears: a person hears his own inner call for renewal and dares to live according to his true values, even if it does not coincide with other people's expectations. When the need for an honest, but merciful summary of the past is ignored — forcing either to immediately "forgive and forget" or, on the contrary, to hold the grudge indefinitely — the court inside turns into a permanent meeting without a verdict, and the forces go not to a new life, but to the re-examination of an already closed case.

## What Fills You with Energy (20)

The most fulfilling thing is being able to honestly take stock of a period of life—recognize what was done right and what should have been done differently—and then truly let go of the subject rather than returning to it again and again. Twentieth energy comes to life where the past can be closed with dignity, without final judgment on oneself.

The resource gives a sense of connection with the family - conversations with elders, memories of ancestors, awareness of where one's own strength came from. For this energy, it is important to feel the roots: not the obligation to repeat the path of the family, but the quiet confidence that there is experience and support of generations behind it.

A fulfilling opportunity to live according to one's own values, even when it means giving up other people's expectations. Twentieth energy comes to life when a person hears his own inner call for change and allows himself to respond to it, rather than drowning it out for the sake of others.

Trust in one's own intuition is a resource - when decisions are made based not only on logic, but also on an inner feeling of the right moment. This energy loves clarity that comes not from doubt, but from a quiet inner yes.

An important right to self-justice is to recognize achievements as honestly as mistakes, without diminishing either the former or the latter. When a person can tell himself the truth without excessive severity, true lightness appears.

The possibility of sincere reconciliation in the family is also fulfilling - when an old grudge can be called out loud, discussed and let go, instead of carrying a silent reproach to parents or oneself for years.

## Resourceful State of the Energy

In the resource, a person is able to look at his past calmly and soberly: he sees both his strong decisions and his mistakes, draws a conclusion from them and moves on, without carrying guilt as a burden for the rest of his life. This is not indifference to one's own history, but a mature honesty that allows you to close some chapters of life and open new ones.

There is a feeling of reliance on the family — the resource twentieth energy feels the support of the roots, even when relations with living relatives are not easy. This gives inner stability: a person knows where his strength comes from and does not need constant approval to feel entitled to his own path.

In this state, a person hears his own inner call and dares to renew: to change direction, start something new, leave behind what has already died. Courage to live according to one's own values ​​appears, even when it requires unpopular decisions, and trust in one's own intuition, which tells the right moment for changes.

## Signs of Accumulated Tension

The first sign is often being too hard on yourself - when the same old mistake is replayed in the head over and over again, and the inner voice passes the same guilty verdict, not giving the case a chance to close.

The second sign is getting stuck in the past instead of living it and moving on: a person seems to be constantly reviewing his life, analyzing decisions already made, instead of directing energy into the present.

Another signal is unspoken resentment towards parents or family, which accumulates silently for years, and then bursts out with sharp conflicts or, on the contrary, complete detachment. Twentieth energy is especially sensitive to what remains unspoken in family relationships.

The tension also manifests itself as exhaustion from old family scripts—when a person feels like they are repeating someone else's, inherited patterns of behavior over and over again, without the strength or permission to break away from them. If this is accompanied by a long-lasting feeling of guilt that does not go away for years, an inability to let go of long-ago events, or a constant internal condemnation of what has already happened, you should contact a specialist.

## How to Restore Your Strength

1. Choose one decision from the past that you still regret, and tell yourself honestly: the conclusion is made, the case can be closed. 2. Talk to an older relative or remember a family history — this restores a sense of support, even if the relationship is not perfect. 3. Name out loud (to yourself or someone close to you) one old grudge against your parents or family that you still carry silently. 4. Take one step according to your own values, even if it goes against someone else's expectations. 5. Listen to the inner "call" - what you are drawn to now, what changes have been brewing for a long time - and allow yourself to recognize it without putting it off for later. 6. Trust your intuition in one small decision instead of weighing everything with your mind alone. 7. Ask yourself: have I really let go of this story or have I just stopped talking about it out loud? 8. Acknowledge your one achievement out loud as honestly as you usually admit your mistakes. 9. Allow yourself one calm conclusion instead of another harsh sentence - just admit: that's how it was, it's over, then it's different. 10. If the feeling of guilt or unresolved resentment persists for a long time and does not go away on its own, consider talking to a psychologist who can help you live and let go of what is still holding you in tension.

## Summary: A Gradual Return to Balance

The balance of the twentieth energy is not restored when a person forces himself to immediately forgive and forget everything, and not when he keeps unspoken reproach to himself or his family for years. It returns when an honest look at the past ceases to be a punishment and becomes a simple, calm conclusion: it happened, a conclusion is drawn from it, and then there is something new.

You don't have to choose between endless self-flagellation and pretending that everything is fine. Mature comfort combines honesty and self-compassion: the ability to soberly acknowledge the past remains, and the right to close the chapter and move on saves from the exhaustion that would otherwise accumulate over the years. Return to balance in small steps - through one honest summary, one voiced resentment, and one step towards your own values, not someone else's.
