# 7. The Chariot — Social Purpose

The social mission of this energy is to lead a team or cause toward a concrete, tangible goal: to overcome obstacles publicly and show that progress is possible even under difficult circumstances.

## Positive

+ **Purposeful leadership.** Drives a team or project toward a clear result without losing focus.
+ **Overcoming obstacles.** Finds a way forward even when circumstances are difficult.
+ **Mobilizing in a crisis.** Quickly rallies resources and people when a situation demands immediate action.
+ **An example of persistence.** Inspires the team not to give up halfway.
+ **Tangible progress.** Regularly shows the community or colleagues concrete results of the effort.
+ **Composure under competitive pressure.** Holds course even under public comparison with rivals or alternatives.
+ **Fast tactical adaptation.** Changes approach without losing sight of the end goal when circumstances shift.
+ **Confidence in public challenges.** Steps up to represent the team in difficult, competitive situations.
+ **Disciplined follow-through.** Carries what she starts to the finish, even when enthusiasm fades.
+ **Inspiration through action.** Motivates those around her by moving forward, not by long speeches.
+ **Strategic persistence.** Can hold a long course toward a big goal without getting scattered on trifles.

## Negative

- **Excessive pushiness.** Pressures colleagues or partners for the sake of speed, provoking conflict.
- **Sole control of the wheel.** Doesn't trust the team with it, even when responsibility should be shared.
- **Intolerance of others' slower pace.** Reacts sharply when colleagues or partners can't keep up.
- **Exhaustion from constant struggle.** Treats every situation as a competition, even where that isn't needed.
- **Ignoring the team's fatigue signals.** Keeps pushing for results without noticing burnout around her.
- **Losing direction without an external goal.** Gets lost without a clear rival or deadline pushing her forward.
- **Conflicts over control of the wheel.** Fights for the right to lead even when another leader is doing better.
- **Neglecting details for the sake of speed.** Skips important nuances while rushing to the finish.
- **Publicly demonstrating victory at any cost.** The importance of winning sometimes outweighs the ethics of the path.
- **Bluntness in feedback.** Criticizes the team head-on, without accounting for colleagues' emotional state.
- **Struggles to acknowledge someone else's win.** Finds it hard to genuinely support a competitor's or colleague's success in the same field.

## Recommendations

- Before a new push, ask the team whether they can keep the pace, and adjust it if needed.
- Celebrate intermediate results together with colleagues, not just the final win.
- Once per project, deliberately hand control of part of the process to someone else, practicing trust.
