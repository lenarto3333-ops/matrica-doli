# 13. Death — Social Purpose

The social mission of this energy is to guide a team or community through transformation: to close what has run its course and clear space for the next stage of growth.

## Positive

+ **Honest recognition of an ending.** Is the first to see when a project, structure, or approach has run its course.
+ **Courage to close what's outdated.** Dares to end what others are afraid to admit is already dead.
+ **A guide through crisis.** Helps the team get through a difficult transition without panic.
+ **Freeing up resources for what's new.** Clears space, time, and attention for the organization's next stage of growth.
+ **A calm attitude toward loss.** Doesn't dramatize the end of projects or roles, but sees it as part of a cycle.
+ **A catalyst for reorganization.** Initiates structural change once the old order no longer serves the purpose.
+ **Stability during upheaval.** Keeps a clear head while everything around her is changing.
+ **Helping others let go.** Supports colleagues in accepting the end of a project, position, or stage.
+ **Honesty about the real state of things.** Doesn't sustain the illusion that an outdated system still works.
+ **Birth of the new from the ashes of the old.** Knows how to turn the closing of one direction into the start of a stronger one.
+ **Accepting the cyclical nature of a career.** Moves easily between roles, industries, and stages without fear of losing her identity.

## Negative

- **Abruptness in ending things.** Closes projects or relationships too quickly, without giving the team time to adjust.
- **Fear of her own stagnation.** Initiates change even when stability would actually be beneficial.
- **Emotional detachment during losses.** Comes across as indifferent while colleagues are grieving the end of a project.
- **Anxious anticipation of an ending.** Constantly braces for the end even when the situation is stable.
- **Loss of trust through frequent breaks.** The team or partners never get used to a stable, long-term collaboration.
- **Struggles to commit to the long term.** Avoids lasting commitments out of a subconscious fear of getting stuck.
- **Dramatizing the inevitable.** Overstates the scale of an ending where a smooth transition would suffice.
- **Isolation through association with crisis.** Colleagues keep their distance, wary of another piece of bad news.
- **Devaluing the stage that just ended.** Forgets to honor what was built while rushing toward the new.
- **Fatigue from constant transformation.** Wears herself out initiating change after change without periods of stability.
- **Abrupt decisions without preparing the team.** Announces major changes without giving the team time to adjust.

## Recommendations

- Before closing a project or role, give the team time to process and say goodbye to the previous stage.
- Deliberately plan periods of stability between major transformations, rather than launching change after change.
- Acknowledge out loud the value of what's ending before moving on to what's new.
