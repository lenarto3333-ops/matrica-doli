# 17. The Star — Social Purpose

The social mission of this energy is to inspire a community or team with a vision of a better future: to be the voice of hope that keeps up the group's morale even in difficult times.

## Positive

+ **An inspiring vision of the future.** Articulates a perspective that gives the team a reason to move forward.
+ **Sustaining morale.** Her presence restores hope to the team during difficult, draining periods.
+ **Genuine enthusiasm.** Infects others with belief in the possibility of positive change.
+ **Openness to big ideas.** Isn't afraid to set ambitious goals for the community or organization.
+ **Natural trust from those around her.** People gravitate toward her for support and inspiration in hard moments.
+ **Visionary thinking under difficult conditions.** Sees a way forward even when the situation looks hopeless to others.
+ **Generosity with inspiration.** Shares her energy and belief in the future without expecting anything in return.
+ **Emotional support for the team.** Notices when the team needs encouragement, and gives it in time.
+ **Authenticity in her public voice.** Inspires not through empty slogans but through genuine belief in her own words.
+ **Hope that holds under pressure.** Keeps faith in a positive outcome even when the facts are conflicting.
+ **A catalyst for the community's long-term dreams.** Helps the group articulate a shared vision for years ahead.

## Negative

- **Detachment from reality.** Gets so caught up in dreaming that she loses touch with the practical steps needed to make it happen.
- **Disappointment when reality doesn't keep pace with the vision.** Loses enthusiasm too quickly when progress is slower than expected.
- **Avoiding difficult, unpleasant topics.** Focuses only on the positive, sidestepping the team's real problems.
- **Unrealistic promises.** Inspires the team with promises not always backed by a concrete plan.
- **Fatigue from the eternal-optimist role.** Wears herself out sustaining others' hope when she herself needs support.
- **Idealizing people and projects.** Sees more potential in colleagues or initiatives than actually exists, and ends up disappointed.
- **Struggles to admit failure.** Avoids an honest look at the situation when the vision doesn't come true.
- **Surface-level inspiration without a plan.** Inspires a start but doesn't always carry things through to a practical result.
- **Mood dependent on outside recognition.** Enthusiasm fades when the vision doesn't get support from others.
- **Loss of trust through unfulfilled promises.** The team grows disillusioned when a bright vision never turns into action.
- **Emotional exhaustion from others' expectations.** Tries to live up to the role of a source of hope even when she has nothing left to give.

## Recommendations

- Back up every inspiring vision with a concrete action plan and defined steps.
- Allow yourself to speak openly about difficulties, not just about a bright future.
- Regularly check your own emotional resources before sustaining others' hope.
