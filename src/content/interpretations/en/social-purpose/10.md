# 10. Wheel of Fortune — Social Purpose

The social mission of this energy is to catch the right moment for a community or organization: to spot trends before others and open new opportunities through timely connections.

## Positive

+ **A sense of the right moment.** Recognizes when it's time for a new initiative or a change of course.
+ **A broad network of contacts.** Easily makes and maintains connections that later open new doors.
+ **Flexible adaptation.** Quickly rebuilds plans when external circumstances change.
+ **A positive influence on the atmosphere.** Brings the sense that change is an opportunity, not a threat.
+ **A knack for connecting people.** Brings together contacts who can be useful to each other.
+ **Openness to new directions.** Readily tries new roles, projects, and fields when the chance arises.
+ **Optimism amid instability.** Stays calm and sees the bigger picture even during a crisis for the organization.
+ **A natural sense of cycles.** Understands when to invest effort and when to wait for a better time.
+ **Quick recovery from setbacks.** Doesn't dwell in disappointment but quickly looks for the next opportunity.
+ **Ease in networking.** Naturally expands the community's or company's circle of influence through new acquaintances.
+ **A catalyst for renewal.** Brings fresh ideas and outside contacts into a stagnant system.

## Negative

- **Unstable engagement.** Loses interest in a project or role as soon as the novelty wears off.
- **Superficial connections.** Accumulates many contacts that never grow into deep professional trust.
- **Dependence on external circumstances.** Struggles to hold course without a favorable outside push.
- **Unpredictability for the team.** Colleagues can't always count on her steady presence in a project.
- **Scattered across opportunities.** Takes on too many directions at once, without going deep on any of them.
- **Losing focus during stable periods.** Gets bored and looks for change even when consolidating gains would serve better.
- **Gambling with decisions.** Relies on luck or intuition where careful calculation is needed.
- **Avoiding long-term commitments.** Shies away from roles that require a steady presence for years.
- **Envy of others' luck.** Compares her own success cycles to others', forgetting her own rhythm.
- **Neglecting preparation.** Counts on a lucky moment instead of systematic work toward the result.
- **Others tiring of the instability.** The team or partners grow weary of constant changes in direction.

## Recommendations

- When choosing a new direction, check whether you're ready to hold course for at least a year, not just until the first bout of boredom.
- Turn some of your superficial contacts into a few deeper professional relationships.
- During stable periods, deliberately consolidate what you've achieved instead of seeking change for its own sake.
