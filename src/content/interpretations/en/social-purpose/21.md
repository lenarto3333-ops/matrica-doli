# 21. The World — Social Purpose

The social mission of this energy is to bring the scattered parts of a community or system together into a whole: to complete major cycles and present the result as a unified, recognized achievement.

## Positive

+ **Integrating scattered parts.** Brings different directions, teams, or ideas together into one coherent system.
+ **Seeing things through to completion.** Doesn't leave projects halfway, but builds them to a complete result.
+ **A sense of the big picture.** Sees how the individual parts of an organization or community add up to a larger meaning.
+ **A high-level representative role.** Naturally becomes the face of a completed project or achievement to the outside world.
+ **Creating a lasting legacy.** Builds things that remain valuable even after her direct involvement in the project ends.
+ **Synthesizing different viewpoints.** Merges conflicting positions into a single, coherent decision.
+ **Recognized authority based on results.** Her achievements earn recognition thanks to the visible wholeness of the outcome.
+ **The ability to complete major cycles.** Carries multi-year initiatives to a tangible, finished conclusion.
+ **Mentoring through the example of completion.** Shows others what quality work carried through to the end looks like.
+ **Creating a shared sense of achievement.** Gives the team or community the sense that the shared journey had meaning and a result.
+ **Global thinking in local decisions.** Considers each step's impact on the system as a whole.

## Negative

- **Perfectionism about closure.** Delays a project's finale until everything seems "perfectly ready."
- **Struggles to draw a line under things.** Holds on to a project longer than necessary, fearing an insufficiently finished result.
- **Exhaustion from the scale of responsibility.** Takes on integrating systems so large that it's draining.
- **Intolerance of unfinished work in others.** Sharply criticizes colleagues whose work seems insufficiently whole.
- **Fear of starting a new cycle.** Becomes so attached to a completed achievement that moving toward a new stage is hard.
- **Overloaded by details of wholeness.** Loses momentum trying to account for absolutely every aspect of the system.
- **Struggles to call a project done.** Keeps finding something more to add or improve, postponing the finale.
- **Fatigue from being the one who pulls everything together.** Wears herself out integrating others' work instead of focusing on her own contribution.
- **Isolation at the peak of achievement.** After a major success, feels an emptiness and disconnection from the team.
- **Self-worth tied to the scale of results.** Values herself only for big, finished achievements, dismissing intermediate steps.
- **Slowdown from a global view.** Weighs the impact on the whole system so heavily that small decisions drag on.

## Recommendations

- Set a concrete "good enough" criterion for a project, so perfectionism doesn't block the finale.
- Celebrate the end of one cycle before immediately taking on the next big challenge.
- Entrust part of the integration work to the team, instead of keeping responsibility for the whole entirely on yourself.
