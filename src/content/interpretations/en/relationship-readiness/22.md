# 22. The Fool — Readiness for a Relationship

## Brief Essence

Readiness for relationships in this energy is tested by whether a person is able to bring real lightness, sincerity and openness to new experiences with another into the relationship, without running away from responsibility when the relationship ceases to be "new" and easy. When there is trust in fate and at the same time a willingness to stay close when things get difficult, the connection becomes a space of freedom for both, and not an escape from closeness or a prison of habit.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this energy in readiness for a relationship is the ability to bring lightness and spontaneity into a relationship: a person does not turn relationships into a heavy duty, but brings play, curiosity and freshness to them, even when partners have been together for a long time.

The second strength is sincerity without masks: this person does not play the role of the "ideal partner", but shows himself as a real person, and it is this openness that gives the other person permission to be himself, without pretense and unnecessary strain.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, readiness manifests itself as the ability to build relationships without suffocating frameworks: partners trust each other and the flow of relationships, allow them to develop naturally, try new things together and are not afraid that changing something familiar will destroy the relationship.

Readiness in a plus also means sincerity in the manifestation of feelings and needs: a person openly talks about what he feels, does not hide disappointments behind a mask of indifference, and at the same time remains close when the relationship passes through difficult periods, and not only in easy and new ones.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, readiness in the plus is manifested as the ability to bring lightness and play into the relationship without turning the partner into a project of control, and sincere trust in fate and the chosen person even when the future of the relationship is not written in advance.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, readiness in the plus is manifested as direct, without tricks communication about his own feelings and intentions, as well as a sincere willingness to take risks for the sake of a serious relationship when the moment comes, instead of hiding behind ease for the sake of ease.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, unpreparedness manifests itself as adventurism and avoidance of obligations: a person easily gets carried away by new relationships, but runs away as soon as the need for real responsibility appears, clings to "freedom" even when in fact he is simply afraid of serious intimacy and the consequences of his own choices.

Another manifestation of unpreparedness is the opposite extreme, limitation: a person, out of fear of a new experience of intimacy, clings to a familiar, safe, even unhappy relationship scenario, is afraid to open up in a new way and prefers a familiar routine instead of the risk of true vulnerability.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, unpreparedness in the minus can manifest itself as a habit of easily getting excited about new acquaintances and just as easily disappearing as soon as the relationship requires seriousness and permanence, or as a fear of new, real intimacy, because of which she clings to a familiar, safe, but empty connection.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, unpreparedness in the negative can manifest as avoiding serious commitments under the pretext of "need for freedom", when in fact it is a fear of responsibility for someone else's feelings, or as being stuck in a familiar, safe relationship pattern out of fear of a new experience of true intimacy.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The trigger is often the moment when a relationship requires real responsibility — for a joint future, for other people's feelings, for the consequences of one's own promises — and instead one wants to escape into another novelty or, on the contrary, hide in a proven routine. The second trigger is a past experience when trust or openness in a relationship once turned into pain, after which a person begins to fear any risk of intimacy and prefers to either constantly change partners or not let anyone really close at all.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is useful to learn to combine freedom with relational responsibility: to allow yourself ease and new experiences together with your partner, not instead of a relationship with him, and to honestly ask yourself if another "need for freedom" is actually a fear of true intimacy. The second step is to allow yourself and your partner a small new experience together: a new topic for conversation, a new joint action, where fear and routine have settled, to restore trust in fate and each other.

## Main Task

The main task is to learn to bring freedom, sincerity and trust in fate to the relationship, while remaining responsible for the consequences of one's own choices, and not to run away either into adventurism for the sake of constant novelty, or into self-restriction due to fear of true intimacy.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I run away from relationships when they stop being easy and new, instead of staying and going through a difficult period?
- What change or new level of intimacy in a relationship am I putting off because of fear, even though I feel inside that I am ready for it?
- Do I bring my natural sincerity to the relationship, or do I hide my true feelings behind a mask of lightness or indifference?

## Practical Plan

1. This week, talk honestly with my partner about one fear or need that I usually hide behind a mask of lightness.
2. Invite your partner to try something new together — a topic, activity, or habit — to bring a sense of freshness and trust back into the relationship.
3. If I notice the urge to run away from a serious step in a relationship, stop and honestly identify what exactly I'm afraid of before deciding whether to run or stay.
