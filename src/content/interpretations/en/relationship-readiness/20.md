# 20. Judgement — Readiness for a Relationship

## Brief Essence

Readiness for relationships in this energy is tested by whether a person is able to honestly and without unnecessary harshness end old relationships and family histories before building new ones. When past "sentences" to oneself and others are released, and resentments against parents are not transferred to the partner, the relationship becomes a space of honesty and renewal, and not a continuation of old open cases.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this energy in readiness for a relationship is the ability to honestly assess what is happening between people: the partner feels that he is not being played with and the truth is not being hidden for convenience.

The second strength is a deep connection with ancestral experience: a person intuitively feels what relationship patterns he inherited from his parents, and with awareness, he is able to consciously choose a different, healthier path instead of automatically repeating it.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, readiness is manifested as the ability to build relationships on honesty: a person admits his own mistakes from past relationships without excessive self-flagellation, draws real conclusions from them and does not transfer old "judgments" - about himself or others - to a new partner.

Readiness as a plus also means a completed, calm relationship with parents or at least conscious work on it: a person does not drag unresolved family grievances into a new couple and does not expect a partner to compensate for what the parents did not add.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, readiness in a plus is manifested as the ability to honestly look at one's own relationship experience, draw calm conclusions from it and trust a new partner, and not compare his every step with old resentments or disappointments.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, readiness in the plus is manifested as a sober, honest assessment of his own share of responsibility in previous relationships and the ability to build new relationships based on this conclusion, and not on the desire to prove something to himself or a former partner.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, unpreparedness manifests itself as endless re-judgment of the past: a person scrolls through old relationships again and again, judges himself or a former partner, and this severity takes so much attention that there is almost no room left for a new, lively intimacy.

Another manifestation of unpreparedness is the transfer of unresolved resentments against parents to a new partner: a person unconsciously expects him to correct what was not achieved in childhood, or blames him for behavior that actually belongs to an old family history.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, unpreparedness can manifest itself as a habit of comparing a new partner with a list of "faults" of previous husbands or fathers, judging him in advance for other people's mistakes, instead of honestly seeing what he really is.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, unpreparedness in the minus can manifest as an internal judgment of his own past love, which prevents him from trusting in a new relationship, or as a transfer of tension with his parents to his partner in the form of excessive criticism and control.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The trigger is often an unfinished conflict with parents, which a person carries for years without realizing how much it affects new relationships. The second trigger is a painful experience in a past relationship, from which a calm conclusion was not drawn, so it continues to live as an internal "sentence" to itself or the opposite sex in general.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It's helpful to take stock of past relationships honestly, but without being overly harsh—to acknowledge your part, draw a conclusion, and allow yourself to let go of the story instead of endlessly revisiting it. The second step is to work separately with the resentments against the parents, so as not to transfer them to the partner, and to notice the moment when the reaction to the new person actually belongs to an old family wound.

## Main Task

The main task is to learn to honestly and calmly end past relationships and family stories, draw real conclusions from them instead of endless self-judgment, and build a new intimacy on the basis of this renewed, free from old "judgments" condition.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I still carry an internal "sentence" for myself or a former partner from a past relationship?
- How much does my reaction to my partner really concern him, and not unresolved resentments against my parents?
- Did I allow myself to calmly end the previous relationship, or do I still mentally return to it?

## Practical Plan

1. This week, honestly sum up some past relationships that still cause internal judgment, and formulate a conclusion that allows you to let them go.
2. Notice one reaction to a partner, or a potential partner, that may actually belong to an old grudge against a parent, and name it directly to yourself.
3. If there is an unfinished conflict with the parents, take one concrete step towards reconciliation or internal release of this history.
