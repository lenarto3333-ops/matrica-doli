# 9. The Hermit — Readiness for a Relationship

## Brief Essence

For the ninth arcana, readiness for a relationship is the ability to let another person cross the boundary of one’s solitude without losing oneself. When this energy is balanced, a person values time alone but does not use it to hide from intimacy. They can be fully present and then return peacefully to quiet time with themselves, while their partner understands this not as rejection but as part of who they are. When balance is lost, “I need space” becomes a permanent wall or a reason to choose partners who will never truly ask for genuine intimacy.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this energy is the ability to know and understand a partner deeply rather than superficially, because this person is not satisfied with surface-level interaction and seeks genuine contact rather than social politeness.

Another strength is the ability to give a partner space without resentment or anxiety. A person with this energy naturally respects boundaries and another person’s need to be alone because they value the same for themselves.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, readiness appears as an ability to invite a partner consciously into one’s inner world without fearing that intimacy will consume independence. A person shares genuine thoughts and feelings rather than only a convenient outward version of themselves.

Positive readiness also means asking openly for needed space instead of disappearing in silence and forcing a partner to guess. “I need to be alone for a while” becomes an honest boundary rather than punishment or escape.

### Expression in Women in the Positive

In women, positive readiness appears as an ability to be deeply and sincerely present while preserving her inner space. She neither dissolves into her partner nor demands that he fill every free moment, yet remains genuinely close.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

In men, positive readiness appears as enough trust in a partner to reveal what is usually hidden from others—doubts, reflections, and genuine motives—instead of maintaining a convenient but distant role of silence and composure.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, unreadiness appears as a wall built under the guise of needing personal space. The person withdraws not because solitude is genuinely needed, but because intimacy is frightening, and “I need to be alone” becomes the standard answer to every attempt by a partner to come closer.

Another expression is choosing partners who do not seek depth themselves, making it possible to avoid a real test of intimacy and remain at a safe but empty distance.

### Expression in Women in the Negative

In women, negative unreadiness may appear as retreating into silence whenever the relationship requires vulnerability. She disappears into her thoughts or activities precisely when her partner most needs her presence, then wonders why he feels lonely beside her.

### Expression in Men in the Negative

In men, negative unreadiness may appear as emotional unavailability disguised as independence. He is physically present but always somewhat distant inwardly, treating every attempt by his partner to come closer as a threat to autonomy rather than an invitation to deeper connection.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The most common trigger is an experience in which closeness led to pain, rejection, or loss of self, reinforcing the belief that distance is safer. Another is imposed rather than chosen solitude, when a partner ignores or mocks the need for space and the person begins defending that need even more rigidly, eventually withdrawing completely.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

Learn to distinguish a genuine need for solitude from an automatic escape from vulnerability, and tell your partner honestly which situation is occurring. It is also important to practice small steps toward intimacy rather than the extremes of “all or nothing”: share one real thought or feeling at a time instead of revealing everything at once or closing off completely. Another step is to choose consciously a partner who wants and is prepared for depth rather than someone with whom it is easy to remain on the surface.

## Main Task

The main task is to let another person enter your fortress voluntarily while preserving the right to solitude without turning that right into continual escape from intimacy.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I ask for space honestly, or use it to avoid an uncomfortable conversation or feeling?
- Do I choose partners who genuinely want to come close, or those with whom it feels safe to maintain distance?
- What exactly frightens me about the thought of someone seeing me completely?

## Practical Plan

1. The next time you want to withdraw, tell your partner the reason honestly instead of disappearing in silence.
2. Share one thought or concern with your partner that you would usually keep to yourself.
3. Once this week, consciously remain in a conversation one minute longer when instinct tells you to retreat into silence.
