# 21. The World — Readiness for a Relationship

## Brief Essence

Readiness for a relationship in this energy is tested by whether a person is able to accept a partner as a whole person, with all its facets, and not as a part that still needs to be perfected. When there is real openness to another world, which is another person, and at the same time the ability to feel the relationship complete in its sufficiency, and not forever "unfinished", the connection becomes a space of unity, and not of endless improvement or defense of one's own boundaries.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this energy in readiness for a relationship is the ability to see a partner holistically, as a single entity with all strengths and weaknesses, and not to collect impressions only from individual episodes or moments.

The second strength is sincere openness to the other as to the whole world: curiosity about the experience, views and way of life of the partner, readiness to learn from each other and combine two different worlds into one common story, without losing oneself.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, readiness is manifested as the ability to accept the relationship and the partner as they are now, without the requirement of constant "refining" of each other to some imaginary ideal: a person sees a real person next to him and values ​​him, not a project of future perfection.

Readiness as a plus also means the ability to build a common whole from two different worlds: partners preserve their own individuality, culture, circle of interests, and at the same time are sincerely interested in each other's lives, turning differences into the richness of the relationship, and not into a source of conflict.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, readiness in a plus is manifested as the ability to accept a partner holistically, with his history and habits, without trying to remake him according to his own idea of ​​the ideal, and at the same time, sincere openness to his world, views and people who are important to him.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, willingness in a plus is manifested as the ability to build a complete, stable whole from a relationship - without the need to constantly prove something to himself or others - and a sincere willingness to let the world of his partner into his life, and not just demand that she fit into an already ready arrangement.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, unpreparedness manifests itself as perfectionism in relationships: a person endlessly looks for flaws in a partner that "still need to be corrected", postpones real closeness until the moment when everything will be perfect, and because of this, the relationship remains in a state of waiting for years, instead of real life together.

Another manifestation of unpreparedness is narrowing and defensiveness: a person closes himself off from the partner's world, is suspicious of his friends, interests or views that differ from his own, and instead of combining the two worlds, he builds a narrow, protected circle around himself, where it is difficult for the other person to truly enter.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, unpreparedness in the minus can manifest itself as endless "finishing" of a partner - appearance, habits, career - after which the acceptance of him as a living person is lost, or as wariness of everything new in his life, which is perceived as a threat, and not as an opportunity to learn more widely.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, unpreparedness in the minus can manifest as postponing a serious relationship until the moment when "everything will be ready" - finances, career, own life - even when the partner has long been ready to build a common whole here and now, or as closedness to her world, friends and hobbies.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The trigger is often the fear that an imperfect relationship or an imperfect partner will become a reason for condemnation - one's own or someone else's - then a person prefers to postpone real intimacy until an imaginary ideal moment. The second trigger is an experience when openness to a foreign world once turned into pain or betrayal of trust, after which a person begins to protect his own borders so tightly that there is almost no room left for another.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is useful to learn to see the relationship and the partner as a whole right now, and not as an unfinished project, and to consciously mark the moments when intimacy is already complete and real enough to trust it. The second step is to regain openness to the other person's world in small steps: to be interested in one new aspect of their life at a time, without immediately demanding complete trust in everything unfamiliar.

## Main Task

The main task is to learn to accept the partner and the relationship as a complete, sufficiently perfected whole here and now, to bring important steps in the relationship to a real completion instead of eternally waiting for an ideal, and to keep the heart open to the world of another person, turning the natural desire for unity into real closeness, and not into the defense of one's own boundaries.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I accept my partner as a whole, as he is now, or do I wait for him to become "finished enough"?
- How open am I to my partner's world — his people, views, hobbies — and not just the part that coincides with mine?
- What step in the relationship do I postpone until the "perfect moment", although in fact I am already ready to take it?

## Practical Plan

1. This week, notice one habit of mentally "finishing" your partner and consciously replace it by accepting one specific feature of him as it is.
2. To show sincere interest in one part of the partner's world that is not close to me - a topic, friend or hobby - and ask a sincere question about it.
3. If there is a step in the relationship that is postponed until the "perfect moment", determine what specifically prevents you from taking it now, and take the first small step.
