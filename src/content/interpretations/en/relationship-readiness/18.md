# 18. The Moon — Readiness for a Relationship

## Brief Essence

The readiness for a relationship in this energy is tested by whether a person is able to trust his own intuitive feeling of a partner, without replacing it with disturbing illusions and projections of old fears. When deep sensitivity is aimed at really seeing and understanding the partner, and not imagining betrayal or rejection that does not exist, the relationship becomes a space of emotional intimacy, and not a field of hidden suspicions.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this energy in readiness for a relationship is a subtle sensitivity to the state of a partner: a person catches his mood, anxiety or joy even before he says it, and can respond exactly to a real need.

The second power is a rich imagination, capable of creating a vivid, warm image of a joint future and inspiring both partners to move towards it, giving dreams of a relationship a concrete form.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, readiness manifests itself as the ability to build relationships on a deep intuitive understanding: partners feel each other without unnecessary words, and a person knows how to distinguish a real signal coming from a partner from his own disturbing conjecture.

Readiness in the plus also means being able to accept and experience one's own emotions in a relationship openly—sharing anxieties or insecurities with a partner instead of silently suspecting or repressing feelings within oneself.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, readiness in a plus is manifested as the ability to deeply feel a partner and create a space of emotional warmth and trust from a relationship, without turning subtle sensitivity into constant anxiety or control.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, readiness in the plus is manifested as the ability to trust one's own feeling about the partner and the relationship, combining intuition with open conversation, instead of silently carrying guesses and doubts.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, unpreparedness manifests itself as the power of disturbing illusions: a person imagines betrayal, indifference or rejection of a partner where there are no real grounds for this, and begins to check, suspect or accuse based on his own fantasies.

Another manifestation of unpreparedness is emotional numbness: a person is so afraid of experiencing vulnerable feelings in a relationship that he suppresses them or hides behind cold rationality, and the partner gradually ceases to understand what is really happening inside him.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, unpreparedness in the negative can manifest as a tendency to paint disturbing scenarios about a partner — to suspect betrayal or loss of interest without real evidence — and to live in constant emotional tension, which exhausts both the woman herself and the relationship.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, unpreparedness in the negative can manifest as an escape from his own emotions in the relationship - he feels anxious or vulnerable, but hides it behind silence or aloofness, leaving his partner alone with the feeling that something is wrong, without explanation.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The trigger is often a past experience of betrayal or emotional deception, after which the subconscious begins to look for confirmation of the threat in any little thing, even when the partner behaves honestly. The second trigger is an environment where showing emotions is considered a weakness, because of which a person learns to hide true feelings even in close relationships, accumulating internal anxiety.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It's helpful to learn to test anxious guesses by talking directly with your partner before jumping to conclusions or making accusations based on your imagination. The second step is to consciously share your own emotions and doubts with your partner instead of silently living them alone or suppressing them as something superfluous.

## Main Task

The main task is to learn to distinguish the real intuitive feeling of a partner from a disturbing illusion, and to build relationships on trust and openness to one's own and other people's emotions, and not on silent suspicions or suppressed feelings.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I rely on the real words and actions of my partner, or on my own disturbing guesses about him?
- Do I share my doubts and emotions openly, or do I hide them until they develop into suspicion or alienation?
- What exactly in my past experience makes me expect betrayal or disappointment where there is no reason for it?

## Practical Plan

1. This week, having noticed a disturbing guess about a partner, check it with a direct conversation before drawing conclusions.
2. For once openly share with your partner an emotion or doubt that is usually hidden in silence.
3. Write down a specific fact or action of the partner that refutes one of the common disturbing scenarios that the imagination draws.
