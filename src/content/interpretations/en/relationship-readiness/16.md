# 16. The Tower — Readiness for a Relationship

## Brief Essence

Readiness for a relationship in this energy is tested not by how smoothly the weekdays pass, but by how the partners go through inevitable crises, ruptures of illusions and difficult truths about each other: whether they are able to withstand the shock, without destroying the relationship for good and not running away from it, but rebuilding the relationship on a more honest foundation. When past upheavals are not lived through, relationships easily become an arena for repeated explosions or chronic anticipation of disaster.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this energy in being ready for a relationship is the ability to speak the hard truth to a partner directly, even if it shakes the usual arrangement of the relationship, instead of maintaining a comfortable but false illusion of harmony for years.

The second strength is inner resilience, which allows you to survive a serious crisis in a relationship, a betrayal of trust or a painful breakup, and come out of it not embittered, but wiser and able to build on.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, readiness is manifested as the ability to go through conflicts and crises together with a partner, using them to deepen honesty, and not to destroy the connection - a person is not afraid of the necessary changes in the relationship and does not hold on to what has already died.

Being prepared also means being able to bounce back from a painful breakup or crisis of trust, turning the experience into wisdom rather than new trauma or cynicism about relationships in general.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, readiness is manifested as the ability to honestly tell her partner what is no longer working in the relationship, even at the risk of a shock, and at the same time remain open to rebuilding the relationship on a new, more honest basis.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, readiness in the positive is manifested as the strength to withstand a difficult crisis in a relationship, not running away into silence or aggression, but taking responsibility for honestly rebuilding trust step by step.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, unpreparedness manifests itself as destructive outbursts of anger in conflicts - the partner "explodes" instead of speaking directly, leaving behind fragments of trust that are difficult to put back together.

Another manifestation of unpreparedness is the chronic expectation of betrayal or disaster due to the unlived trauma of a past relationship, when a person either keeps a partner at a distance for fear of a new blow, or provokes crises himself to confirm an old fear.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, unpreparedness in the negative can manifest as emotional explosions or sharp breaks instead of a direct conversation about pain - relationships are built on the principle of silence until the explosion, when the accumulated resentment one day suddenly and destructively comes out.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, unpreparedness in the negative can manifest itself as a hidden accumulation of aggression that breaks out sharply and uncontrollably at a moment of tension, or, on the contrary, as chronic mistrust and readiness to break off the relationship at any moment, as soon as there is a threat of repeating the old pain.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The trigger is often the unlived trauma of a previous painful breakup or betrayal, which causes the new intimacy to be perceived as a threat that requires protection through control, distance, or preemptive aggression. The second trigger is the accumulation of unspoken dissatisfaction in the current relationship, which sooner or later erupts into sharp conflict instead of gradual honest conversation.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

It is useful to learn to name a disagreement or pain to a partner immediately, in small, honest portions, and not to keep silent until everything builds up to an explosion. The second step is to consciously live with and resolve the trauma of the past breakup, with support if necessary, so that it does not determine the reactions in the current relationship.

## Main Task

The main challenge is to learn how to move through necessary crises and difficult truths in relationships consciously and constructively, turning shocks into deeper trust rather than destruction or repeated trauma.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I tell my partner the hard truth right away, or do I keep silent until it builds up to an explosion?
- What past upheaval in the relationship still affects how I react today?
- Am I able to survive a serious crisis in a relationship without destroying the relationship completely and without running away from it?

## Practical Plan

1. To tell my partner one honest, possibly uncomfortable thing that I have been holding back for fear of upsetting the usual structure of the relationship.
2. Notice the moment of tension this week and consciously choose a direct conversation instead of silence or explosion.
3. Take steps to relive the past trauma of the relationship—talking, supporting, or being aware—so that it is less driven by current reactions.
