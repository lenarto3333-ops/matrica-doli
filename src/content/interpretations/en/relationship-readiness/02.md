# 2. The High Priestess — Readiness for a Relationship

## Brief Essence

For this energy, readiness for a relationship is connected with the ability to be sincere while maintaining healthy boundaries. When a person trusts their intuition about a partner and is willing to open up gradually, the relationship is built on deep, genuine intimacy. When inner reserve remains unresolved, partnership often becomes either a constant concealment of true feelings or the choice of someone who is difficult to trust because of a longstanding fear of vulnerability.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this energy in relationship readiness is the ability to sense subtly whether someone truly resonates before words or facts make it clear. A person with this energy rarely enters a partnership thoughtlessly; they tend to listen to their inner sense before trusting someone completely.

Another strength is a natural ability to create a space of safe intimacy—not forcing closeness, but allowing trust to grow at its own pace, making the bond deeper and more resilient.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, readiness appears as an ability to open up to a partner gradually while trusting one’s intuition about the pace of closeness. A person enters a relationship with a clear sense of whom they can trust, making the partnership deep rather than superficial.

Positive readiness also means combining the need for personal space with genuine intimacy. A person is not afraid to share vulnerable feelings when inwardly ready, understanding that true intimacy requires gradual rather than forced openness.

### Expression in Women in the Positive

In women, positive readiness appears as trust in her own intuition about a partner, allowing her to open up gradually without forcing intimacy or hiding herself for too long out of fear.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

In men, positive readiness appears as an ability to be emotionally available at his own pace and entrust deep feelings to his partner without fearing that vulnerability will weaken the bond.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, unreadiness appears as chronic reserve. A person may remain in a relationship for years without ever opening fully, leaving their partner feeling that genuine intimacy never developed.

Another expression is choosing a partner precisely because that person is unavailable or closed off, making it possible to avoid one’s own vulnerability under the pretext that “they are not ready either.”

### Expression in Women in the Negative

In women, negative unreadiness may appear as continually testing a partner through silence and things left unsaid instead of directly expressing trust or doubt.

### Expression in Men in the Negative

In men, negative unreadiness may appear as avoiding emotional intimacy under the guise of independence, leaving his partner perpetually uncertain about his true feelings.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

A common trigger is the experience of betrayed trust in a past relationship, which intensifies the fear of becoming vulnerable again. Another trigger is pressure to reveal oneself quickly and completely when the person is not inwardly ready for that pace.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

Honestly explore your own pace of trust, allowing yourself to open gradually rather than according to someone else’s timetable. It is equally important to practice trust in small matters before expecting complete vulnerability in major ones. A third step is to speak openly with a partner about the need for gradual closeness instead of silently maintaining distance.

## Main Task

The main task is to enter a relationship with healthy trust in your intuition and a gradual willingness to open up, rather than chronic reserve or fear of vulnerability.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I trust my intuition about my partner, or do I ignore inner signals?
- How much do I allow myself to open gradually instead of hiding for too long?
- Do I choose a partner because of a genuine bond, or because their unavailability lets me avoid my own vulnerability?

## Practical Plan

1. Write honestly about the pace at which you feel comfortable opening up in a new relationship.
2. Practice a small act of trust this week by sharing a vulnerable feeling.
3. Have an open conversation with your partner about the need for gradual rather than forced closeness.
