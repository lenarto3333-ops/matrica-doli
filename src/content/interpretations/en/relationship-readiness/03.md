# 3. The Empress — Readiness for a Relationship

## Brief Essence

For this energy, readiness for a relationship is connected with the ability to give and receive care at the same time. When generosity in sharing warmth is balanced by enough self-respect not to disappear entirely into caring for a partner, the relationship is built on mutual nourishment. When personal generosity is not yet balanced by self-care, partnership often becomes one-sided self-sacrifice.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this energy in relationship readiness is the ability to create tangible warmth and comfort for a partner from the earliest stages of a connection. A person with this energy rarely enters a relationship formally; they are inclined to care sincerely and share warmth.

Another strength is a natural ability to create a sense of home and stability with a partner, even early in the relationship, through simple shared rituals of warmth.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, readiness appears as an ability to care generously for a partner without forgetting personal needs. A person enters a relationship with an open heart and a clear understanding of their own boundaries.

Positive readiness also means being able to receive a partner’s care in return, allowing the relationship to become a mutual rather than one-sided source of warmth.

### Expression in Women in the Positive

In women, positive readiness appears as sincere generosity in caring for a partner without requiring constant recognition, combined with an ability to receive care openly in return without feeling weak.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

In men, positive readiness appears as an ability to be a physically and emotionally warm source of support for a partner, creating comfort and stability without a continual need to prove it.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, unreadiness appears as entering a relationship prepared to give oneself completely before the partner has demonstrated reliability, often leading to disappointment or exhaustion.

Another expression is expecting a partner to meet every need without a personal willingness to offer care in return.

### Expression in Women in the Negative

In women, negative unreadiness may appear as premature, excessive care for a partner—an attempt to “earn” the relationship through continual caregiving instead of allowing closeness to develop naturally and gradually.

### Expression in Men in the Negative

In men, negative unreadiness may appear as expecting a partner to provide all comfort and care without being willing to invest warmth in return.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

A common trigger is uncertainty about one’s value without continually caring for others, compensated for through excessive caregiving early in a relationship. Another is a past relationship in which care was taken for granted and not reciprocated.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

Learn first to care for your own needs rather than expecting a relationship to fill that gap. It is equally important to give care gradually, in proportion to the relationship’s real depth, instead of offering everything at once.

## Main Task

The main task is to enter a relationship able both to give warmth and receive it, building a mutual rather than one-sided cycle of care from the first steps.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I give care gradually, in proportion to the relationship’s depth, or immediately and completely?
- How ready am I to receive care in return rather than only give it?
- Can I care for my own needs without relying solely on a relationship?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down three personal needs you meet independently of a relationship.
2. Practice receiving care from your partner in one small situation this week.
3. Discuss with your partner what kind of care suits the relationship’s current stage and what may still be premature.
