# 13. Death — Birthday, Visible Character, and First Talent

## Short summary

The thirteenth arcana at the point of birth describes a person who, from childhood, feels when something has run out of him - before others notice it. She rarely clings to a form that has lost its meaning: to a friendship that no longer nourishes, to a way of doing things that has long become obsolete, to an image of herself that no longer corresponds to who she has become. On the outside, this is often read as harshness or impermanence, although it is actually a special kind of sensitivity: the ability to feel the limit of the cycle and not be afraid to cross it. The first talent of this energy is the ability to close one chapter of life and open the next one without the paralysis that other people cannot get out of for years. The main challenge is not to turn this natural willingness to change into a habit of destroying everything in a row as soon as discomfort appears, and to learn to distinguish between what has truly survived and what just requires patience.

## Natural force

The main feature of this energy is a sense of completion. Where others cling to the usual out of inertia, the person of the thirteenth arcana intuitively catches the moment when the stage has reached its natural end, and does not try to artificially prolong the reason why time has passed. This is a rare inner honesty that has become her trademark over the years.

From this grows a special gift of renewal: the ability to start anew where others are stuck in regret for what has been lost. When circumstances destroy the usual plan, this person does not waste a lot of time in despair - he finds, faster than others, what to build the next step from, and that is why those around him are often surprised by his ability to "get up" after falls.

Another natural strength is the courage to let go of what no longer makes sense to hold: a relationship, a role, a habit, even a part of one's identity. This is not frivolity, but an accurate internal calculation: when retention is more expensive than loss.

The basic motivation of this energy is movement, not preservation for preservation's sake. The internal compass is tuned to the question "what is really needed now", not "how to preserve what is already there". Therefore, this person rarely clings to the status quo simply because of fear of the unknown.

The natural talent of this point is the ability to be a catalyst for change for others. At moments when the environment is stuck in the usual and is afraid to move, it is this person who first dares to say out loud what everyone feels, but is afraid to admit: it's time to change something.

## Manifestation in the plus

When this energy is unleashed, a person appears surprisingly alive and free—not clinging to past versions of themselves, but moving easily from one role, profession, or stage of life to another when they feel internally ready. It is interesting with her, because she is constantly updated, and does not repeat the same patterns for years.

In work and everyday affairs, the positive manifestation gives a rare ability to close in time what has exhausted itself - a project, an approach, a familiar procedure - and open space for a new one, while others continue to hold on to the old out of fear of uncertainty.

In relationships with people, a positive manifestation manifests itself as a sincere willingness to let go of what causes pain or inhibits development, without drama and without trying to maintain appearances where the essence is no longer there. A person does not keep others in toxic bonds out of regret for the past, and does not allow himself to be kept in such a bond.

The courage to let go, which is the natural strength of this arcana, turns into a wise determination in a plus: a person knows how to recognize when a situation calls for completion, and not to fight to preserve a form that has already emptied.

Another sign of a positive state is a healthy attitude towards crisis moments in one's own life. A person does not panic when something is destroyed, realizing that something more accurate and truer than what was before often grows in the place of the destroyed.

In addition, the ability to distinguish a true completion from an escape also appears. A person understands: it is worth letting go of what is really exhausted, and not anything, in order to avoid the discomfort of long-term relationships or commitments.

Finally, the positive manifestation of this energy is often visible in how a person reacts to a crisis or loss: they do not deny the pain, but they do not get stuck in it either - they move through it faster than others precisely because they do not fight the inevitable.

### Manifestation in women is positive

In a woman, this positive energy often manifests as an inner freedom to reincarnate — the ability to easily let go of the image of herself that she had before and accept a new version without fear of "losing herself." She easily changes her lifestyle, circle of communication, direction of work when she feels an inner need for it.

Such women usually have a distinct sense of the moment of completion: they sense when a relationship, friendship or project has exhausted its resource, and rarely cling to a form for the sake of form. Decisions about changes are made not because of a whim, but because of an honest recognition that everything has already been said inside.

Another trait is the ability to combine determination with care: she knows how to complete what has been exhausted without causing unnecessary pain to others, finding the words and time for a dignified goodbye, rather than a sharp breakup.

### Manifestation in the husband is positive

In a man, the positive manifestation of this energy often looks like a determination that commands respect even in difficult situations. He does not cling to a job, status or habit that has exhausted himself, but instead faces the truth and takes a step, even when it means giving up something familiar and comfortable.

Such men usually have a well-developed sense of the cycle: when it is appropriate to end one stage and start the next. This is not impulsiveness, but a form of strategic thinking that allows you not to spend years maintaining something that no longer works.

In making decisions, a man with this energy in the plus knows how to combine the determination of completion with nobility: he does not burn bridges unnecessarily, but closes the chapter in such a way that he leaves behind respect, not ruins.

## Manifestation in the minus

When this energy does not find a healthy outlet, the main trait - the ability to finish - turns into a tendency to destroy everything in a row, as soon as discomfort appears. A person can break off relationships, work, plans abruptly and irreversibly, without giving himself time to check whether the stage has really run out or if it is just a momentary fatigue.

The natural ability to renew in the negative turns into the inability to finish something: a person constantly abandons what he has started halfway, looking not for completion for the sake of a new beginning, but to escape from any difficulties or obligations.

The ability to let go turns into harshness in relationships with people: instead of having an honest conversation, a person breaks off contact silently, abruptly, without explanation, leaving others in confusion and pain where it was possible to part with dignity.

In a relationship with one's own past, the negative manifestation often looks the opposite — not as ease of letting go, but as being stuck in the image of something that once ended not as desired: a person carries the bitterness of an unreleased breakup for years, despite the outward appearance of "moving on."

Fear of inevitable changes is another typical negative trait. A person is so afraid of losing control over what is already ending that he begins to force the ending where there was still a chance to save the situation, destroying what could be saved.

The relationship with one's own identity in the negative also weakens: a person is so used to constantly "rewriting oneself" that he loses the sense of a stable core, replacing real renewal with a chaotic change of masks.

Another sign is rigidity in imposing changes on others. A person can demand that the environment just as sharply and uncompromisingly part with the past, not taking into account that not everyone is ready for such a pace of transformation.

### Manifestation in a woman is negative

In a woman, the negative state of this energy often looks like a chaotic series of sharp breaks - relationships, friendships, work - that happen impulsively, without an attempt to first have an honest conversation or give the situation a chance for a natural conclusion.

The tendency to destroy instead of ending in the negative is exacerbated: instead of a decent farewell, there is a desire to burn everything to the ground so that there is absolutely no way back, even when part of the destroyed could remain valuable.

In a relationship with a partner, the minus can manifest itself as sudden, sharp ultimatums where it would have been better to talk calmly a long time ago: she breaks the connection impulsively, and then suffers the consequences of this haste for a long time and painfully.

### Manifestation in the husband is negative

In a man, the negative manifestation of this energy is often disguised as principle — in fact, it is a way to avoid difficult, slow work on a relationship or project. Instead of admitting that it's worth trying again, he convinces himself and others that "it's over" and chops off the shoulder where something valuable could have been saved.

The tendency to sharply close topics in the negative manifests itself especially noticeably in the career: promising directions are cut off halfway due to impatience or fear of long, gradual work that does not give immediate results.

In decision-making, a man with this minus tends to either destroy everything around him at the first discomfort, or, on the contrary, get stuck in a toxic routine due to fear of the chaos that, as he thinks, change will bring.

## What can be negative

The most common trigger is long-term maintenance of a situation that has long required completion, but for some reason is not completed: an open project, a relationship that has long been exhausted, a job with no prospects. Tension builds up and eventually comes out in a sharp, uncontrolled explosion.

The second trigger is an experience when a previous attempt to change something turned into a painful loss. After such an experience, there is either excessive caution before new changes, or, on the contrary, a willingness to destroy everything in advance, so as not to feel like a victim of circumstances again.

The third trigger is the pressure to maintain the status quo where internally everything has long been ready for change. The constant expectation of "wait a little longer, everything will be fine" depletes this energy faster than anything else.

Another typical trigger is the feeling of losing control over the pace of one's own changes: when the environment forces a decision faster than a person is ready, or, on the contrary, slows down what he has already completed internally.

Finally, accumulated fatigue from constant changes without pauses for stabilization leads to the negative. When the transformation becomes chronic, without any period of rest for the integration of the new, there is exhaustion, which manifests itself as abruptness and impatience with everything and everyone.

## How to return energy to the plus side

The first step is to learn to check whether a phase is truly exhausted before abruptly ending it. It is useful to give yourself a pause before a decisive step: whether it is a real completion of the cycle or a momentary impulse under the pressure of fatigue or irritation.

The second step is to practice gentleness in the completion process itself. You don't have to burn bridges to let go of what's gone — you can close the chapter with respect for what it was and the people involved.

The third step is to practice bringing what you have started to a natural end instead of quitting halfway through. Not every discomfort means that you should give up everything; sometimes difficulty is just part of the process worth going through.

The fourth step is to consciously give yourself pauses to stabilize after each major change, so that the transformation does not turn into chronic chaos without any point of support.

The fifth step is to learn to distinguish between one's own pace of change and the pace of other people, without demanding from the environment the same speed and uncompromisingness in letting go of the past.

The sixth step is to consciously work with the unreleased past experience that still holds you in bitterness, instead of pretending that everything is long gone, when in fact the old pain is driving the new decisions.

The seventh step is to regularly remind yourself that the real power of this energy is not in destruction for destruction's sake, but in the precise sense of the moment when one chapter has truly ended and the next begins.

## The main task

The main task of this energy at the point of birth is to learn to use the natural readiness for change not as a tool to escape from discomfort, but as a precise gift of timely completion. This involves the development of inner prudence: the ability to distinguish between the moment when it is really time to close the chapter and the moment when it is easier to destroy everything than to go through a difficult but necessary stage.

When this prudence is formed, the need to either destroy everything in a row at the first discomfort disappears, or, on the contrary, to get stuck in what has long been exhausted, out of fear of the unknown. The ability to let go ceases to be an escape and becomes a true gift—one that a person uses consciously to move through life more precisely and freely than most of the people around them. At the same time, courage and liveliness do not disappear, but are revealed more fully, because they are no longer spent on hiding fear of their own instability.

## Questions for self-reflection

- How many times have I abruptly cut off something important, without giving myself time to check if it was really finished?
- Have I ever destroyed something that could be saved just because the difficulty seemed unbearable?
- How honestly am I distinguishing the true end of the cycle from the escape from discomfort?
- Do I give myself pauses for stabilization after big changes, or do I immediately rush into the next transformation?
- For me, what distinguishes a dignified farewell to the past from a sharp, painful breakup?
- Is there an unhealed rift in my past, the bitterness of which still subtly guides my current decisions?

## Practical plan

1. Write down all the situations that now feel "exhausted", and honestly determine for each: it is a real completion or temporary fatigue.
2. During the week, before each sudden decision to break up with something, mentally check: "am I really ready to close this chapter, or am I just running away from difficulties?".
3. For one week, consciously finish at least one thing that you wanted to quit halfway through.
4. Find someone you can confide in before making big decisions about ending relationships, jobs, or projects.
5. Once a month, arrange a pause for stabilization after any significant change in life.
6. Write a letter (not necessarily send) about one unforgiven break from the past to honestly close this chapter within yourself.
