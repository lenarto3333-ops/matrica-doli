# 10. Wheel of Fortune — Birthday, Visible Character, and First Talent

## Brief Essence

The tenth arcana in the birthday position describes a character that does not cling to the same things from childhood but moves easily with circumstances—wherever life turns. Such a child is often noticed for a special lightness: they rarely remain stuck in resentment or disappointment for long, quickly find a new interest when the previous one has run its course, and have an astonishing ability to be in the right place at the right time, as though someone were quietly guiding their path. Their first talent is sensing the rhythm of events: when to act and when simply to wait until everything falls into place.

The image of the wheel reminds us that this lightness has meaning only when a person does not merely drift with the current, but learns consciously to seize the moment and make an effort where it is truly needed. The mature expression of the tenth energy is not blind hope in luck, but trust in life combined with readiness to act when the wheel turns in a favorable direction. It is important to learn the difference between trust that opens doors and passivity that waits for doors to open by themselves.

## Natural Strength

The Wheel of Fortune's natural strength is an innate sense of cycles: a child with this energy intuitively understands that every run of bad luck passes just as every run of success does, and therefore rarely remains in despair for long. Where uncertainty frightens other children, the child of the tenth energy approaches change with curiosity: a new class, a new home, or new rules of a game are perceived not as a threat but as another turn that may prove fortunate.

They possess natural adaptability—the ability to adjust quickly to new circumstances without wasting energy resisting the inevitable. They learn early not to cling to what has already changed, but to look for opportunities in the new situation instead of mourning the old one. This makes them a flexible, easygoing conversational partner with whom interaction feels pleasant and simple.

Another strength is natural good fortune, which is often not accidental but the result of openness to opportunity: a child with this emphasis notices possibilities where others see only obstacles and is not afraid to use an unexpected turn of events. They may easily meet the right person, find themselves exactly where something interesting is happening, or receive an offer they were not deliberately seeking.

This energy often appears as an attraction to variety: the child is interested in trying many different things rather than exploring one pursuit deeply for years. They enjoy games involving wheels, lotteries, playful elements of chance, travel, and changes of environment—anything that brings a sense of movement and novelty.

The basic motivation of the Wheel of Fortune is to trust the flow of life while participating actively in its creation. When this motivation is conscious, the person becomes a source of hope and lightness for others. When it is not, they risk spending a lifetime waiting for luck to find them instead of moving toward it.

## Expression in the Positive

In a resourceful state, a person with the tenth energy radiates a contagious lightness and trust in life. They do not panic when plans change, but adjust quickly and often find something in the new situation that the old one lacked. Those around them sense that even difficult circumstances are temporary and solvable.

In communication, the positive is felt as natural optimism without naivety: this person acknowledges difficulties but does not become trapped in them, instead looking for the nearest way out or opportunity. Friends and colleagues value their ability not to dramatize where others panic and the ease with which they help people see the situation more broadly.

In study and work, this is someone who can adapt quickly to new requirements, seize the right moment for an important step, and take a risk when the opportunity is genuinely worthwhile. They do not cling to outdated methods merely because they are familiar and readily learn something new when circumstances demand it.

In friendship and family life, the tenth energy in the positive appears as a person who brings a sense of movement and freshness into relationships: they keep routine from stagnating, suggest new ideas, easily forgive minor hurts, and do not hold grudges for long. Their presence reminds loved ones that change can be a gift rather than a threat.

A real-life example of the resourceful tenth energy is a child who, the day after an unsuccessful performance in a competition, is already eagerly planning the next attempt instead of remaining stuck in disappointment. Over the years, this trait becomes the ability to serve as the same source of hope in a group, family, or team when everyone else is tired and doubtful.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In women, the Wheel of Fortune may appear as a graceful lightness of being: she does not cling desperately to plans and expectations, allows life to surprise her, and through this openness attracts people and opportunities. She knows how to enjoy the moment without worrying about the future while still taking conscious steps when she sees a genuine opportunity.

In a resourceful state, she trusts her intuition about timing: she senses when to wait and when to act decisively. Those around her especially value this ability to bring lightness into tense situations without minimizing the seriousness of what is happening.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In men, the tenth energy in the positive often appears as a calm confidence that life offers second chances: he does not cling to one opportunity as though it were the last, but knows how to move on when a door closes, trusting that others will open. His strength lies in flexibility, which is not spinelessness but a form of wise trust in the process.

Such a man has a natural ability to seize the right moment for important decisions—a change of job, an investment, an important conversation—without delaying too long or acting impulsively. The resourceful expression allows him to take considered risks rather than relying blindly on luck.

## Expression in the Negative

In a depleted state, trust in life's flow becomes passive waiting for a miracle. The person begins to hope that everything will resolve itself without any effort on their part, and this stance gradually becomes a habit of postponing important decisions while awaiting the “right moment,” which never arrives because no action brings it closer.

Another negative pattern is laziness disguised as philosophical acceptance: “whatever happens, happens” becomes a convenient excuse not to make an effort where one is genuinely required. Natural lightness turns into reluctance to take responsibility for the outcome of one's own life.

The tenth energy in the negative can also become a fear of change, paradoxically opposed to its natural adaptability: the person grows so accustomed to one state of affairs that any prospect of change—even for the better—causes anxiety and resistance. They cling to the familiar “swamp” even when it has long ceased to bring joy.

In relationships, the negative appears as unpredictability and irresponsibility: the person may make a promise easily and forget it just as easily because “something changed,” expecting everyone around them simply to adapt to the latest turn. Loved ones grow tired of the instability that only yesterday seemed like lightness.

Another extreme is gambling or recklessness that spins out of control: the person relies so heavily on luck that they risk what should not be risked—money, relationships, health—hoping the wheel will inevitably turn in their favor even when reality has long been signaling otherwise.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In women, the negative pattern may look like chronically postponing their own life “until later, when everything falls into place by itself.” She waits years for the right moment to make important decisions, when in truth she is simply afraid to take responsibility for the choice. The way forward is not to lose trust in life, but to add her own active participation to it.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In men, the shadow of the Wheel of Fortune may appear as a habit of constantly waiting for a major opportunity without making the daily, inconspicuous efforts that actually create the conditions for luck. Passivity accumulated over years eventually becomes justified as “it simply isn't my time,” although the time arrived long ago and was left unused.

## What Can Shift the Energy into the Negative

The most common triggers for the tenth energy are connected with a sense of stagnation and enforced stability. These include routine without any change, situations with no room to maneuver or find a new solution, and prolonged monotony that removes any sense of movement or possibility.

A series of consecutive setbacks can also shift the energy into the negative by undermining its natural trust in life. When the wheel seems to remain on the unfavorable side for too long, the temptation arises to give up and stop trying. A sense of lost control is especially painful—when important decisions are made without the person's participation and they are left only to adapt to someone else's turns.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The return begins with an honest question: am I trusting life now, or am I simply avoiding responsibility for a choice? If waiting for the “right moment” has become a way of doing nothing, pause and remember that this energy's genuine trust works together with action, not in its place.

The second step is consciously to define small, concrete steps toward the desired change instead of waiting for a great, sudden turn. Action taken today brings the right moment closer far more reliably than passive waiting.

The body of this energy is often helped by the practice of conscious risk: choose one opportunity that evokes both interest and mild anxiety and take a step toward it instead of waiting for guarantees that life cannot provide.

In relationships, one rule helps: if a promise has been made, keep it even if circumstances have already changed and the temptation to postpone is strong. Loved ones can forgive unpredictability when they see responsibility behind it, not only lightness.

It is important to train resilience during temporary stagnation: remind yourself that a run of bad luck is just as temporary as a run of success, and that precisely now, during a difficult period, it is worth continuing to take small steps rather than giving up.

It is also helpful consciously to release what has already changed instead of holding on to the old out of fear of a new turn. A wheel held artificially in place eventually breaks; it does not remain still forever.

## Main Task

The main task of the tenth arcana in the character position is to learn to combine trust in life's flow with active, responsible participation in its creation. Do not abandon lightness and adaptability, but combine them with readiness to act when the moment is genuinely right: trust the cycles while making an effort where something depends on you.

A person matures when they can accept both good fortune and misfortune with equal balance and allow themselves to be flexible and dependable at the same time. Then their natural lightness becomes a gift to those around them rather than a source of instability.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- When did I last wait for the right moment, and when was I simply avoiding responsibility for a decision?
- How many opportunities have I missed because I waited for guarantees instead of taking a risk?
- Am I holding on to something familiar out of fear of change even though it has long ceased to bring joy?
- Where does the thought “whatever happens, happens” live within me, and is it a convenient excuse for inaction?
- Do I keep my promises even when circumstances have changed?
- How much do I trust life after a series of setbacks, or do I give up for a long time?
- At a deeper level, do I feel trust in life's movement or fear of its unpredictability?

## Practical Plan

1. This week, take one concrete step toward an opportunity you have postponed while waiting for the “right moment.”
2. Choose a decision you are delaying while waiting for guarantees, and give yourself a specific deadline for action.
3. Keep one promise that would be easier to abandon because circumstances have changed.
4. Write down one area of stagnation in your life and identify the first small step you can take this week.
5. Recall one past run of setbacks that eventually ended, and use it as support in your current difficulty.
6. Consciously release something outdated—a habit, circumstance, or relationship—that you are holding on to out of fear of change.
