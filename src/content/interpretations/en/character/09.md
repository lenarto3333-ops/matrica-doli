# 9. The Hermit — Birthday, Visible Character, and First Talent

## Brief Essence

The ninth arcana in the birthday position describes a character drawn from childhood not toward the crowd but inward—to the place where one can remain alone with a question until it reveals itself fully. Such a child is often noticed not for being loud, but for the opposite: they may spend hours silently taking apart a construction set, reread the same book while others play outside, and ask questions for which adults do not always have ready answers. Their first talent is seeing beneath the surface: noticing what is truly happening behind words, events, and someone’s behavior while others stop at the first impression.

The image of the Hermit’s lantern reminds us that this depth has meaning only when it eventually illuminates something for someone else instead of remaining hidden in a dark room. The mature expression of the ninth energy is not flight from people, but the ability to emerge from silence with a considered statement whose weight comes from not having been born in haste. A person must learn to distinguish the need for nourishing solitude from withdrawal that isolates.

## Natural Strength

The Hermit’s natural strength is the ability to concentrate deeply and for a long time without constant outside confirmation that they are on the right path. Where being alone and bored frightens other children, a child of the ninth energy comes alive in solitude: when no one interrupts, there is room to think independently rather than repeat someone else’s conclusions.

They possess a naturally analytical mind that seeks not the first convenient explanation, but the real cause. They learn early not to trust “that is just how it is done” or “everyone does it.” They need to discover why for themselves, and only then do they truly believe. This makes them a challenging but valuable conversational partner: they ask the questions that are easier to avoid.

Another strength is natural selectivity in relationships. The ninth energy rarely pursues a wide circle of acquaintances. One or two people with whom genuine conversation is possible matter far more than dozens of superficial friends. A child with this emphasis may seem withdrawn among classmates while being remarkably warm and open with the one friend they truly trust.

This energy often appears as an attraction to subjects that demand immersion: reading, studying nature or technology, psychology, spiritual questions—anything that allows a person to go deeply into one theme instead of jumping from one amusement to another. The child likes to take things apart rather than merely observe them; they want to understand how everything really works.

The Hermit’s fundamental motivation is to reach the essence through their own mind and preserve their inner integrity without dissolving into other people’s opinions. When this motivation is conscious, the person becomes a source of rare, measured wisdom for those around them. When it is not, they risk spending a lifetime hiding from the very people they truly need.

## Expression in the Positive

In a resourceful state, a person of the ninth energy radiates calm, unobtrusive depth. They do not rush to conclusions and are not afraid to say, “I need to think,” when they genuinely need time, rather than produce an immediate but superficial answer merely to avoid silence. Their words carry weight precisely because they are considered.

In conversation, the positive is felt as a rare quality of attention: when this person listens, they truly hear rather than wait for their turn to speak. Friends and colleagues often come to them not for entertainment but for advice on a difficult matter because they know this person will not dismiss them with a stock phrase, but will genuinely explore the heart of the issue.

In study and work, this is someone capable of spending years going deeply into one field and developing mastery that may not be obvious at first but becomes indispensable when real understanding, rather than passing familiarity, is needed. They do not chase quick recognition and often earn a deeper, more lasting respect because of it.

In friendship and family, the ninth energy in the positive is a person of few but sincere words. They do not fill silence with empty phrases or demand constant attention, yet are wholly present when someone close truly needs support. Their solitude does not repel others because they know how to step out of it and meet them when it matters.

A real-life example is a child who sits apart with a book during recess, then suddenly voices an idea in class that makes everyone fall silent and think. Over time, this trait becomes the ability to be the same quiet voice of reason in a group, family, or team—the voice others heed precisely because it is not heard constantly.

### Expression in Women in the Positive

In a woman, the Hermit can appear as refined inner independence. She does not need constant approval or company to feel whole, and this self-sufficiency draws people in rather than pushing them away. She can be deeply engaged in a conversation and then calmly return to silence; both feel sincere rather than performative.

In a resourceful state, she allows herself to be wise without hiding it behind modesty or uncertainty. When asked, she shares what she sees without diminishing her own insight. Those around her value that she speaks infrequently but always to the point.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

In a man, the ninth energy in the positive often appears as calm, understated authority. He does not compete for attention in a group, yet when he speaks others grow quiet because they know his words will not be empty. His restraint is not coldness, but a form of respect for his own words and those of others.

He naturally becomes the person others consult in difficult situations because he does not offer instant, ready-made answers. He takes time to think and returns with an honest, balanced view. In the positive, he can be emotionally available to those closest to him, not merely intellectually present.

## Expression in the Negative

In a depleted state, the need for solitude becomes protective isolation. The person avoids communication not because doing so nourishes them, but because they fear being misunderstood, mocked, or rejected for being different. The silence that once restored them becomes a cage that is increasingly difficult to leave.

Another negative pattern is analysis paralysis. Instead of acting, the person endlessly considers every side of a situation in search of a perfect solution, until the moment when they simply needed to take a step has passed. Depth of thought becomes a trap with no visible path into action.

The ninth energy in the negative can also become quiet superiority: “I see what others cannot, and they will not understand anyway, so why explain?” This thought justifies distance, but in truth conceals a fear of vulnerability and of being heard differently than intended. The person withdraws from those who might support them on the grounds that they “will not understand anyway.”

In relationships, the negative appears as emotional unavailability. A person may share daily life with someone for years while remaining behind an invisible wall that even those closest to them cannot cross. Loved ones sense that something important will never be shared with them and gradually grow tired of knocking on a closed door.

There is also the paradox of wanting solitude while suffering acutely from the loneliness one has created. Over time, escaping people produces precisely the sense of abandonment the person was trying to avoid.

### Expression in Women in the Negative

In a woman, the negative pattern may look like quiet detachment that others mistake for coldness or indifference. She finds reasons not to attend gatherings and answers briefly and formally while privately longing for the warmth she denies herself. The way forward is not to eliminate her need for solitude, but to stop confusing self-protection with safety.

### Expression in Men in the Negative

In a man, the Hermit’s shadow may appear as silent detachment easily mistaken for strength. He convinces himself and others that he is “fine on his own,” avoiding conversations about feelings even with those closest to him. Distance accumulated over years begins to seem like his personality, although it is really an old protective habit worth reconsidering.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The ninth energy’s most common triggers involve forced superficiality and intrusion into personal space: a loud gathering one cannot leave, constant noise or chaos with no right to pause, and situations where privacy or personal boundaries are violated without invitation.

Devaluing depth is another trigger: mocking seriousness or thoughtfulness, or pressuring someone to decide or speak immediately without time to reflect. Imposed solitude is especially painful—not solitude freely chosen, but isolation, rejection, or the absence of people capable of understanding one’s depth.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The return begins with an honest question: am I resting in silence right now, or hiding from people out of fear? If silence has become a way to avoid the risk of vulnerability, pause and remember that the true solitude of this energy nourishes rather than freezes.

Set a conscious boundary around reflection: give yourself a specific amount of time to analyze a situation and then take a step, even if it is imperfect. Acting with eighty percent confidence is better than endless consideration that never ends.

This energy often benefits from small, deliberate departures from silence: choose one person and share something you would ordinarily leave unsaid. One honest paragraph today is worth more than years of carefully considered but unspoken wisdom.

In relationships, use a simple rule: when you need a pause and time alone, say so directly instead of disappearing without explanation. Loved ones can respect a need for quiet when they understand it, but feel abandoned when they meet only silence.

Practice vulnerability as a strength rather than a weakness. Let yourself be seen not only through wise conclusions but also through doubts, fears, and unfinished thoughts. Depth that one is afraid to reveal loses half its meaning.

It also helps to share accumulated wisdom consciously when it is requested rather than stand apart thinking, “They will not understand anyway.” A lantern kept beneath a cloak lights no one’s path—not even your own.

## Main Task

The main character task of the ninth arcana is to learn to bring the wisdom found in silence into the world instead of keeping it only for oneself. Do not abandon the need for depth and reflection; combine it with a willingness to be heard and vulnerable. Enter solitude to find the truth, and leave it to share that truth with those who need it.

A person matures when they can ask for closeness as naturally as they seek quiet for reflection, and when they allow themselves to be understood rather than merely profound. Then their natural insight becomes a gift to others rather than a barrier between themselves and the world.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- When did I last seek solitude to rest, and when did I use it to hide from the fear of being misunderstood?
- How many decisions am I postponing by continuing to think about what should have been put into action long ago?
- Are there people I keep at a distance even though I truly want to be closer to them?
- Where does the thought “they will not understand anyway” live in me, and have I genuinely given anyone a chance to prove it wrong?
- Can I tell loved ones directly that I need time alone instead of simply disappearing?
- How much does my wisdom benefit anyone besides me?
- Which do I feel more deeply: peace in my own company, or fear of being abandoned?

## Practical Plan

1. This week, share with one person close to you a thought or feeling you would usually keep to yourself.
2. Choose a decision you have postponed through endless reflection and set a specific deadline for action.
3. The next time you want to disappear into silence, tell your loved ones directly instead of withdrawing without a word.
4. Write down one situation in which you decided that “they will not understand anyway,” and honestly test that assumption in conversation.
5. Set aside one hour of complete, unforced solitude this week—not as an escape, but as conscious rest.
6. Share an experience or insight with someone who did not ask directly but may benefit from it.
7. When you notice the urge to answer condescendingly or remain silent instead of explaining, choose to explain.
