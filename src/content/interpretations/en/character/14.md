# 14. Temperance — Birthday, Visible Character, and First Talent

## Brief Essence

The fourteenth arcana at the point of birth describes a person who, since childhood, knows how to find the middle where others see only two irreconcilable poles. She rarely rushes to extremes: instead of a sharp conflict, she looks for a word that will reconcile both sides, instead of a sharp "yes" or "no" — a decision that takes into account everything important. From the outside, this is often read as calmness, diplomacy, almost angelic patience, although in fact it is a separate type of talent: the ability to bring together different things - people, ideas, styles, approaches - and get something whole and harmonious from it. The first talent of this energy is a natural gift of combination and balance, which can unfold as art, diplomacy, healing, or any endeavor that requires a fine sense of balance. The main challenge is not to turn this natural softness into a fear of any decisive step, when balance is more important than the actual result, and harmony is more important than honesty.

## Natural Strength

The main feature of this energy is a sense of proportion. Where others tend to pull in one direction—either too emotionally, or too coldly, or too quickly, or too slowly—the fourteenth person intuitively finds the point of balance where everything works best. This is a rare internal sensitivity to proportion, which over the years has become her hallmark in any business.

From this grows a special gift of synthesis: the ability to take disparate elements - the opinions of several people, styles, cultures, approaches - and merge them into a single whole, which has its own beauty, different from the sum of its parts. Such people often feel a vocation where it is necessary to combine: in art, design, cooking, music, mediation, psychology, any business where the ability to mix the right proportions is valued.

Another natural strength is angelic patience and the ability to act gradually, step by step, without rushing to the point where haste will spoil everything. This person rarely breaks the door where it is enough to carefully open it, and it is this slow, measured gait that often brings a more lasting result than a sudden jerk.

The basic motivation of this energy is harmony, not victory for the sake of victory. The internal compass is tuned to the question of "how to make everyone feel good", not "how to prove yourself right". Therefore, this person rarely goes to an open conflict, if there is even the slightest chance to find a common language.

The natural talent of this point is the ability to be a peacemaker and a guide of peace for others. In moments when the dispute or chaos around escalates, it is this person who first finds the words to relieve the tension, and it is to him that those who need advice, support or just the feeling that there is someone balanced next to them gravitate.

## Expression in the Positive

When this energy is revealed, a person looks surprisingly harmonious and friendly - he naturally knows how to find a common language with very different people, without losing his own position. It's easy with her because there is rarely any pressure or need to defend herself around her.

In work and everyday affairs, the positive manifestation gives a rare ability to combine different ideas, styles or approaches into something coherent and workable - where others see incompatible elements, this person sees material for synthesis. Creativity, design, cooking, music, consulting - everything that requires a precise sense of proportion comes naturally to her.

In relations with people, a positive manifestation manifests itself as sincere diplomacy: a person knows how to tell a difficult truth gently, to find a compromise that really suits both sides, and not just smooth over a conflict about the human eye. She is a natural peacemaker who is sought after for advice in stressful moments.

Patience, which is a natural strength of this arcana, turns into a wise gradualism in the positive: a person knows how to go to the goal step by step, without forcing events where time is needed, and that is why he often achieves more sustainable results than those who rush.

Another sign of a positive state is the ability to maintain inner peace even in chaotic circumstances. A person does not panic when conflict or uncertainty is escalating around him, but becomes a point of support for others precisely thanks to this balance.

The positive expression is also the ability to feel when a compromise is really needed, and when it is just a way to avoid an important decision. A person understands: harmony is valuable when it is honest, not when it is just convenient.

Finally, the positive manifestation of this energy is often visible in a creative approach to life's difficulties: a person knows how to combine seemingly incompatible things - work and inspiration, duty and pleasure, different areas of interests - and make a whole, beautiful lifestyle out of it.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, this positive energy often manifests itself as a natural grace in the way she combines different roles and aspects of her life without being torn between them. She knows how to be a support for others, remaining soft, and find balance where it seems that one has to sacrifice something for the sake of another.

Such women usually have a distinct aesthetic and diplomatic flair: they intuitively feel which colors, words or decisions create harmony and which disrupt it. Decisions are made not out of pressure or fear, but out of a genuine sense of what really works best for everyone involved.

Another feature is the ability to combine softness with inner stability: she knows how to remain gentle without losing her own boundaries, finding words for difficult topics in such a way as not to hurt anyone, but also not to betray the truth.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, a positive manifestation of this energy often looks like diplomacy that is rare for the environment, which commands respect. He does not try to impose his opinion by force, but finds a solution that takes into account the interests of all parties, while remaining firm in matters important to him.

Such men usually have a well-developed sense of proportion in business: when to insist, and when to give in for the greater good. This is not a weakness, but a form of strategic thinking that allows you to achieve long-term results without unnecessary conflicts.

In making decisions, a man with this energy is able to combine patience with diplomacy: he does not rush events, but builds them gradually, step by step, and it is this measured course that often earns him respect as a person you can rely on.

## Expression in the Negative

When this energy does not find a healthy outlet, the main trait — the ability to find a middle ground — turns into chronic indecision. A person is so afraid of breaking the balance or offending someone with a choice that for years he cannot make an important decision, remaining "on the fence" where a clear step was needed long ago.

Natural diplomacy in the negative turns into excessive yielding: a person is so eager to avoid conflict that he agrees to compromises that do not suit anyone, including himself, just so as not to face someone's displeasure.

The ability to restrain emotions turns into suppression of feelings, which sooner or later breaks through the opposite extreme: a person who has been patient and silent for months or years suddenly explodes with a sharp, disproportionate reaction, surprising those around him who are used to seeing him as the embodiment of peace.

In a relationship with one's own desires, the negative manifestation often appears as chronic procrastination: a person is so afraid of making the wrong choice that they make none, letting circumstances decide for them, and then regretting the time they wasted.

Fear of extremes is another typical negative trait. A person avoids any sharpness or unequivocalness so much that he loses the ability to defend his own position when it is really necessary, dissolving in the desire to please everyone.

The relationship with one's own identity in the negative also weakens: a person is so used to adapting to the balance around him that he loses the sense of his own "I", replacing true harmony with endless self-denial.

Another sign is passivity where action is needed. A person can wait for years for the "right moment" to take an important step, when in fact the moment has long come, and waiting has become only a way to avoid risk and responsibility.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, the negative state of this energy often looks like chronic indecision in important life choices — she can weigh options for years, fearing to upset the fragile balance, instead of trusting her own intuition and finally choosing.

The tendency to excessive compliance in the negative is exacerbated: instead of an honest compromise, there is a habit of silently giving in to one's desires for the sake of peace in the family or relationships, accumulating unspoken dissatisfaction, which over time becomes a heavy burden.

In a relationship with a partner, the negative can manifest itself as long patience where it would have been worthwhile to have an honest conversation a long time ago: she holds back her irritation for months, and then suddenly explodes with emotions that look disproportionate to the environment that is used to her calmness.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, the negative manifestation of this energy is often disguised as prudence — in fact, it is a way to avoid a risky but necessary decision. Instead of daring to take a clear step, he convinces himself and others that "it's worth waiting a little longer" and delays the process where it was possible to move forward a long time ago.

The tendency to avoid confrontation in the negative manifests itself especially noticeably in the career: promising opportunities are lost due to endless weighing of "pros" and "againsts" until someone more determined takes them away.

When making decisions, a man with this disadvantage tends to either endlessly postpone an important choice, or, on the contrary, after a long restraint, suddenly act impulsively, surprising those around him with a sharpness that is not at all characteristic of his usual image of a calm person.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The most frequent trigger is the long-term need to hide one's true feelings in order to maintain external harmony: unspoken irritation, fear of offending someone with honesty, the habit of putting someone else's peace before one's own needs. Tension builds up and eventually comes out in a sharp, uncontrolled explosion.

The second trigger is a situation that requires a quick, unequivocal decision without the opportunity to weigh options for a long time. The pressure of time activates a deep fear of making a mistake, and a person either becomes paralyzed in indecision, or grabs for the first-best option to get rid of unbearable uncertainty.

The third trigger is the long-term conflict around, which cannot be avoided by diplomacy. Constant tension, without the possibility of smoothing it out, exhausts this energy faster than anything else, because it violates the very basis of its comfort - a sense of controlled balance.

Another common trigger is the feeling that the compromises a person has agreed to over the years have not really satisfied anyone, including themselves. The realization of the futility of accumulated self-denial can cause deep disappointment or even sharp rebellion against one's usual role as a peacemaker.

Finally, the accumulated fatigue from the constant role of "the one who fixes everything" leads into the negative. When the responsibility for someone else's peace is systematically transferred to a person, without any recognition of their own needs, exhaustion appears, which is manifested either by passivity or unexpected abruptness.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to learn to distinguish between genuine, honest compromise and compromise to avoid discomfort. It is useful to pause before agreeing to another "middle ground solution": is it really the best option for everyone, or is it just a way to avoid someone's displeasure.

The second step is to train the courage to speak the uncomfortable truth in small portions at the right time, instead of building up the silence to the point of explosion. Diplomatic honesty, said in advance, is much cheaper than a sudden outburst that occurs after months of restraint.

The third step is to practice making decisions within a given time frame instead of endlessly weighing options. Not every decision requires perfect certainty; sometimes "good enough now" is better than "perfect someday".

The fourth step is to consciously give yourself the right to extremes in justified cases: sometimes you need to firmly say "no" without softening, and this does not destroy harmony, but preserves it in the long term.

The fifth step is to learn to distinguish one's own desires from the desire to adapt to the expectations of others, regularly asking oneself: "what do I want, if I put aside the thought of what others will think or feel."

The sixth step is to consciously work with the accumulated, unspoken resentments that still influence behavior, instead of pretending that things have long been balanced, when in fact old resentments are driving new decisions.

The seventh step is to regularly remind yourself that the real power of this energy is not to avoid any tension, but to know exactly when to maintain balance and when to act decisively.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy at the point of birth is to learn to use the natural gift of balance not as a way to avoid difficult decisions, but as a precise tool for creating true, honest harmony. This involves the development of inner courage: the ability to distinguish between the moment when a compromise is really needed and the moment when it is easier to avoid a choice than to take responsibility for it.

When this courage is formed, the need to either indefinitely postpone a decision for fear of breaking the balance, or, on the contrary, to explode with emotions after a long restraint, disappears. The ability to find the middle ceases to be an escape and becomes a real gift - one that a person uses consciously to create a harmony around him that is deeper than the simple absence of conflict. At the same time, diplomacy and patience do not disappear, but are revealed more fully, because they are no longer spent on hiding fear of their own determination.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- How many times have I agreed to a compromise that really didn't suit anyone, just to avoid an awkward conversation?
- Have I ever been silent for too long, and then exploded with emotions out of proportion to the situation?
- How honestly do I distinguish true harmony from fear of conflict or choice?
- Do I give myself the right to a firm "no" when it's really necessary, or do I always look for a middle option?
- For me, what distinguishes wise patience from delaying a decision for fear of making a mistake?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down all the decisions that are currently "postponed for later", and honestly determine for each one: whether it really needs more time, or whether it is an escape from the choice.
2. During the week, before each agreement to a compromise, mentally check: "Is this really the best option for me, or just a way to avoid inconvenience?".
3. For one week, consciously express an uncomfortable thought immediately, in small, diplomatic portions, instead of accumulating silence.
4. Find a person with whom you can consult before important decisions, so that you are not left alone with the endless weighing of options.
5. Once a month, consciously practice a firm "no" in a situation where previously a compromise would have been automatically sought.
6. Write a list of your own desires separately from the expectations of other people, in order to more clearly feel the line between true harmony and self-denial.
