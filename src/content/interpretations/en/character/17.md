# 17. The Star — Birthday, Visible Character, and First Talent

## Brief Essence

The seventeenth arcana at the point of birth describes a person born with something of an inner light—the ability to see perspective and hope where others see only darkness or dead ends. This is not naive optimism, but a real gift: a natural ability to inspire, dream and keep faith in a better future even in difficult times, when everything around says the opposite. Since childhood, this person often notices in himself a talent that attracts the attention of others - in creativity, in communication, in the very way of seeing the world - and it is this talent, combined with inspiration, that becomes his first and most noticeable gift. The main challenge of the seventeenth arcana is twofold: on the one hand, it is the danger of "star disease" — pride that grows out of talent and admiration of others; on the other, the risk of losing faith in oneself so deeply that one's own light goes out, and dreams turn into an empty escape from reality. The real maturity of this energy is to learn to carry your light evenly, not depending on the applause of others and not letting it go out due to doubt.

## Natural Strength

The main feature of this energy is a natural talent to inspire others: a person with the seventeenth arcana on their birthday often, without realizing it, becomes the one to whom they reach out in moments of despair, because with her it is easier to believe that there is a way out even when it is not obvious.

From this talent grows a keen sense of perspective: the ability to see direction, opportunity, light at the end of the tunnel where others are stuck in limbo. This energy rarely seems final—something inside it is always looking for the next step, the next opportunity, the next reason to keep going.

Another natural strength is brightness and expressiveness, which is noticed from childhood: a talent for creativity, a word, an image or an idea, which attracts attention and causes sincere admiration of those around. Often it is this early success or recognition that becomes the first test of character—learning to bear attention without letting it determine one's worth.

The basic motivation of this energy is faith in a better future and in one's own dreams as a source of energy: this person instinctively lives forward, focusing not on what already is, but on what can be, and this forward orientation often becomes the engine of his actions, decisions and creativity.

The natural talent of this point is the ability to transform inspiration into something tangible for others: word, image, example, support. Where someone simply dreams, this energy knows how to pass the dream on - so that another person also feels a glimmer of hope and strength to move.

## Expression in the Positive

When this energy is revealed, talent and inspiration work as a real gift to the environment: a person shares his own light sincerely, without the need to constantly prove his exceptionality, and this is why others naturally gravitate to him, not out of compulsion or envy.

In difficult circumstances, a positive manifestation makes a person a natural guide of hope: she does not promise easy victories and does not embellish reality, but knows how to show that even in the dark there is a direction worth moving in - and does it honestly, without false encouragement.

In creativity and profession, a positive manifestation is a combination of bright talent with real professionalism: a person does not stop at the inspiration itself, but invests in it discipline and concrete steps, thanks to which the dream turns into a visible, sustainable result.

The power to inspire, which is a natural feature of this arcana, is positively combined with generosity of spirit: a person shares his light, knowledge, and support without fearing that this will diminish his own radiance - on the contrary, devoted inspiration returns to him in the form of trust and warm relationships.

Another sign of a positive state is the persistence of hope even after serious failures: a person is able to survive disappointment, the collapse of a project or a painful defeat and again find faith in himself to move on, without losing touch with his own dream.

In addition, a healthy attitude towards recognition also appears: praise and attention are pleasant, but they are not the only source of self-worth. A person knows his worth even without constant confirmation from the outside, so the success of others is not perceived as a threat, and sincere achievements do not turn into a need for constant applause.

Finally, the positive manifestation of this energy is noticeable in the ability to combine a dream with action: a person does not just fantasize about a better future, but every day takes concrete, even small steps in its direction, trusting that it is consistency that turns inspiration into reality.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, this positive energy often manifests itself as a quiet, deep confidence in her own talent, which does not need constant external confirmation: she creates, shares ideas or supports others not for applause, but because it is a natural part of who she is.

Such women usually have a rare ability to ignite hope in others - friends, children, colleagues - simply by their presence or a sincere word, and at the same time remain modest about their own influence, without turning the ability to inspire into a need to be the center of attention.

Another trait is the ability to combine dreaminess with practicality: such a woman is not afraid of big ideas, but at the same time builds a real, stable basis for them, turning inspiration step by step into something tangible and lasting.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, the positive manifestation of this energy often looks like the calm charisma of a person who leads not by pressure or authority, but by example and sincere belief in what he does: he inspires others with deeds, and not just with words about future success.

Such men usually have the ability to see a perspective where others see only a problem, and are able to communicate this vision to a team or loved ones so that it becomes a common goal, rather than a lonely dream of one person.

In a relationship with his own talent, a man with this positive energy knows how to remain open to learning and growth: success does not turn him into a person who considers himself superior to others - on the contrary, he willingly shares the acquired experience with those who are still on the way.

## Expression in the Negative

When this energy does not find a healthy outlet, it manifests in one of two opposite but equally destructive ways. The first is "star disease": talent and recognition inflate the ego to such a level that a person begins to consider himself special, higher than the usual rules or the people around him, and needs constant admiration and confirmation of his own uniqueness to feel good.

With a star disease, a person easily turns into someone who looks down on other people's achievements, devalues ​​other people's work and considers his own talent a sufficient reason for special treatment - in relationships, at work, in everyday life. Criticism in this state is perceived as an insult, not information, and quickly arouses irritation or contempt for the person who dared to express it.

The second, opposite manifestation of the negative is deep self-doubt: a person loses touch with his own talent and dreams, ceases to believe that he is worthy of attention or capable of something significant, and gradually extinguishes the light in himself that once naturally attracted others. This can look like chronic insecurity, self-criticism, or a refusal to even try new things out of fear of disappointment.

The third manifestation of the minus, closely related to the second, is the loss of connection with reality due to infatuation with dreams without concrete steps: a person lives in a world of imaginary plans and future achievements, which remain words, because no dream is supported by real, consistent action. Time passes, and between the desired and actual remains a chasm, which is filled by new fantasies instead of work.

Another typical feature of the minus is the dependence of self-esteem on external recognition: without praise, applause or attention, a person feels empty, as if his value only exists when someone outside confirms it. This makes you either constantly look for new reasons to enjoy yourself, or, on the contrary, shut down when the attention disappears.

The relationship with one's own talent in the minus also weakens: a person either excessively relies on natural gift, neglecting hard work and discipline, believing that "real talent" should not make efforts, or doubts his own abilities so much that he hides them from others and even from himself.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, star disease often looks like a need to be the center of attention and constantly hear confirmation of one's own uniqueness - from a partner, friends, followers - and any decrease in this attention causes anxiety or irritation. The opposite side is despondency, when a woman extinguishes her own talent, comparing herself with others not in her favor, and gradually stops even trying what once brought joy.

The tendency to daydream without action in the negative manifests itself especially acutely in plans for the future: she can imagine another life, another project, another relationship for years, without doing practically anything to bring the desired closer, and over time this gap between the dream and reality begins to cause bitterness.

In a relationship with recognition, the minus can manifest itself as sharp differences: sometimes an excessive need for admiration and compliments, then a sudden devaluation of one's own abilities after the slightest criticism or failure, as if there is no stable middle ground between these two extremes.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, the negative manifestation of star disease is often disguised as confidence or leadership - in fact, it is the need to constantly prove one's exclusivity, look down on others and perceive any disagreement as an attack on one's own authority or talent.

The opposite manifestation is self-doubt, which in men often hides behind irony or cynicism: instead of admitting that he has lost faith in his own gift, he devalues ​​the very idea of ​​dreams and inspiration, calling them childish or impractical, so as not to face the pain of his own unrealization.

In a relationship with his own plans, a man with this minus tends to either build grandiose plans, which remain conversations over coffee, without any real step, or, on the contrary, completely refuse to dream, convincing himself that "it's not for him", and in both cases he loses touch with his own natural talent.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The most common trigger is excessive, unconditional praise or early fame, which trains a person to associate their own worth with the admiration of others, rather than with the real meaning of what they do. When attention becomes the only measure of success, any reduction in it feels like a personal disaster.

The second trigger is sharp criticism or public humiliation of talent, especially at a sensitive moment: one sharp phrase can shake self-confidence so much that a person avoids for years what he felt confident about before, fearing the repetition of pain.

A third trigger is the constant comparison of oneself with others whose achievements seem greater or brighter. This energy reacts especially acutely to the feeling that its light dims next to someone else's, forgetting that the comparison is rarely fair and complete.

The fourth trigger is the long-term lack of results with constant daydreaming: when plans do not turn into actions for too long, there is either despair or an escape into even brighter fantasies, which temporarily silence anxiety, but do not bring you closer to what you want.

Finally, the environment that either constantly feeds the ego with excessive enthusiasm, without giving honest feedback, or, on the contrary, systematically devalues ​​any attempt to dream and create, calling it naivety or vanity, brings into the negative.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to learn to distinguish healthy joy from recognition and dependence on it: after praise or success, ask yourself if something has changed about yourself when the attention is gone, and honestly work with what turns up.

The second step is to practice humility without self-deprecation: to recognize your talent as a real gift, not as a basis for superiority, and at the same time to remember that other people have their own gifts, no less valuable, even if they look different.

The third step is to learn to turn dreams into concrete, small steps: choose one goal and write the first, smallest practical step towards it this week, instead of continuing to just imagine the desired result.

The fourth step is to consciously seek feedback from people you can trust—both those who praise and those who criticize honestly—so that self-evaluation is grounded in reality, not just emotional extremes of admiration or disparagement.

The fifth step is to regularly return to the source of your own inspiration without an audience: to create, dream or act at least part of the time without the intention of showing it to anyone, to see if there is joy in the process itself, and not just in the recognition of the result.

The sixth step is to remind yourself in moments of despair that the decline of hope is a temporary state, and not the final sentence of talent: it is worth remembering at least one moment when self-belief has already returned after a fall, and trust that it is possible again.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy at the point of birth is to learn to carry one's own light evenly and steadily, regardless of how much applause it receives, and not letting it be extinguished by doubt or comparison with others. This involves the development of internal discipline: the ability to appreciate one's talent without pride and to believe in one's own dreams, supporting them with real, consistent actions, not just words about the future.

When this discipline is formed, the need to either constantly prove one's own exceptionality or to hide one's gift due to fear of failure or other people's judgment disappears. Inspiration ceases to be dependent on external conditions and becomes a real gift: a stable inner light that a person is able to carry through any circumstances and which he sincerely and generously shares with those around him.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Does my belief in my own worth depend on how much attention or praise I receive now?
- When I dream of a better future, do I take real steps towards it or just return to the same fantasies?
- Are there people or achievements, comparisons with which extinguish my sense of my own light?

## Practical Plan

1. During the week, record moments when self-esteem changes dramatically depending on someone else's praise or criticism, and notice this pattern.
2. Choose one old dream and write the first concrete, small step towards it, which you can take this week.
3. Once a day, practice creating or acting without the intention of showing it to anyone - to test the connection with your own inspiration outside of the attention of others.
4. Write down three of your own talents and three of someone else's, which you sincerely admire, in order to practice respect for someone else's light without diminishing your own.
5. Recall one situation of serious failure or disappointment and honestly write down how you managed or failed to regain self-confidence after it.
6. Once a month, review the list of dreams that still remain only words, and honestly determine the first practical step for each one or consciously let go of the one that has lost its meaning.
