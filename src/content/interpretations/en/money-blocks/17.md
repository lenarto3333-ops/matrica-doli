# 17. The Star — Financial Blocks

## Brief Essence

A typical financial block of the seventeenth energy is the expectation that money will come simply for the fact of one's own uniqueness and talent, without the need to prove value with specific work or results. The second typical block is the underestimation of one's own gift, when a really strong talent is sold for pennies, because the dreams never received a concrete embodiment in the form of a price, a product or an offer.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block is a valuable gift—the ability to inspire, attract attention, and see opportunities where others see only obstacles. The problem is not the talent itself or the belief in a better future, but the fact that this belief remains only a dream instead of turning into a concrete, paid proposition.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a person monetizes talent in a disciplined manner: he turns inspiration into a product, service or project, brings things to an end and is not afraid to call a decent price for his work. Belief in a better future here becomes fuel for daily concrete steps, not a substitute for them.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, a person either waits for recognition and payment in advance, only for the promise of talent, or, on the contrary, is ashamed to ask for a decent price and constantly dumps, fearing to seem too self-confident. It is also possible to chronically postpone the monetization of the gift — the idea or dream remains unrealized because "it is not yet time."

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is the experience when the talent was praised without measure, but they were never taught how to bring things to a practical result, and now a person confuses recognition with real earnings. The second trigger is one sharp devaluation in the past, after which it is scary to offer anything at all for money, so as not to hear rejection again.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It's useful to learn to separate the dream from the concrete product — and to give every real idea at least one real, monetized step toward realization. The second step is to regularly check whether the price of one's own work corresponds to the real value of talent, and not to fear or, on the contrary, inflated expectations. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

Learn to combine natural inspiration with the discipline to see things through, so that talent turns into a steady income rather than remaining a beautiful but unfulfilled dream.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I have a talent or an idea that I still haven't dared to value in money and offer to someone?
- Where am I waiting for recognition or payment in advance, instead of bringing the matter to a concrete result?

## Practical Plan

1. Choose one idea or talent and determine a specific price or form of offer for it during the coming week.
2. Take one small step that turns a dream into a real product, service or portfolio.
3. Check once a month whether you are underestimating the price of your own work out of fear or, on the contrary, whether you are waiting for payment without a real result.
