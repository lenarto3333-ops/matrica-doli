# 5. The Hierophant — Financial Blocks

## In Brief

A typical financial block for this energy is an outdated earning model that is difficult to abandon even though both the market and the person's competence have long outgrown it. Another common block is undervaluing one's knowledge: a person accumulates experience for years but prices it below its true value out of false modesty or fear of appearing overly confident.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block lies a valuable quality—a deep respect for knowledge and proven methods. The problem is not caution about change itself, but the way it sometimes becomes a complete refusal to update one's approach. Used judiciously, flexibility is one of this energy's most valuable financial tools.

## Positive Expression

In the positive, this energy transforms accumulated experience into a structured, fairly valued product: a course, consultation, or method. A person in the positive can name a price that reflects years of study and practice without embarrassment or artificially inflating it.

## Negative Expression

In the negative, the block appears as chronically postponing an update to one's method or pricing “until I am one hundred percent ready”—a point that never arrives—or categorically rejecting new earning formats that seem “not serious enough.” Another expression is moralizing about other people's financial decisions instead of focusing on one's own strategy.

## What Can Shift the Energy into the Negative

A common trigger is an experience in which the person's expertise or method was questioned or dismissed, strengthening the desire to cling even more tightly to what is proven. Another is comparison with people who earn money through a shallower but more contemporary approach.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is helpful to review one's pricing and working methods regularly against their actual market value rather than habit alone. A second step is to practice naming a fair price for one's knowledge without diminishing it, relying on the facts of lived experience rather than feelings of uncertainty.

## Main Task

The main task is to transform depth of knowledge into a flexible, fairly valued asset rather than a cause of stagnation.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Which update to my method or pricing have I postponed for too long because I fear I am not ready?
- How fairly do I value my accumulated experience in financial terms?
- Do I criticize other people's financial decisions instead of focusing on my own strategy?

## Practical Plan

1. Review the price of your service or consultation and compare it with the true extent of your experience.
2. Choose one outdated element of your working method and update it this month.
3. Identify one situation where moralizing about someone else's finances distracted you from your own focus, and consciously avoid it next time.
