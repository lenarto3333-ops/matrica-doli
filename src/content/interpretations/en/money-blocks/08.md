# 8. Justice — Financial Blocks

## In Brief

The typical financial block of this energy grows out of an old story of injustice: at some point, the person was deceived, underpaid, or denied due recognition, and ever since, money has been associated not only with resources but also with a score that life still has to “settle.” Another common block is hidden guilt over having once received more than others, leading either to excessive stinginess or to compensatory generosity at one’s own expense.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block lies a valuable quality—a keen financial and ethical compass. The person genuinely senses when terms are fair and when they are not, and is unwilling to build an income on deception. The problem is not this sensitivity itself, but the way it can sometimes harden into resentment instead of becoming the foundation for transparent, sustainable agreements.

## Positive Expression

In the positive, this energy transforms its sense of justice into reliability: the person keeps honest accounts, pays on time, and expects the same treatment in return, without feeling embarrassed to speak directly about money. Income grows through a reputation for being someone with whom business can be conducted honestly and transparently.

## Negative Expression

In the negative, the block appears as a chronic feeling that “I’ve been underpaid again” or “I’ve been passed over again,” even when the terms were known in advance—and this makes calm negotiation difficult. Another expression is guilt about one’s own financial success, which compels the person to give more than agreed or undervalue what they offer so as not to be “unfair” to others.

## What Can Shift the Energy into the Negative

A common trigger is seeing someone else receive more for less effort, or remembering an old financial injustice that was never acknowledged. Another trigger is the need to state one’s price or make a demand when it feels as though doing so will sound greedy.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is helpful to consider an old grievance separately from a present decision: the former belongs to the past and needs acknowledgment, not revenge through today’s contract. The next step is learning to state a fair price calmly, as a fact rather than an accusation or an apology.

## Main Task

The main task is to transform a keen sense of justice into a tool for honest agreements, rather than an inner trial of oneself or others over old financial scores.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What old financial injustice am I still trying to “prove” through the decisions I make today?
- Do I feel guilty about my own material success in a way that makes me give more than agreed?
- Can I state my price calmly, without feeling that I have to justify myself?

## Practical Plan

1. Recall one old financial injustice and consciously recognize it as complete and separate from current decisions.
2. Review one current agreement and check whether its terms are truly fair to both parties.
3. Once this week, state your price or requirement directly, without unnecessary explanations or apologies.
