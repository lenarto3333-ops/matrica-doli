# 13. Death — Financial Blocks

## Short summary

A typical financial block of the thirteenth energy is the inability to complete in time what is no longer profitable: a person clings to an outdated business model, an unprofitable job or a source of income that has long since exhausted itself, because completion scares more than slow exhaustion. The second typical block is sudden, unplanned financial breaks: a person suddenly abandons a stable source of income without preparing the transition, because of which he finds himself in even greater instability.

## Natural force

Behind this block is the valuable ability to sense when a financial strategy or income source has exhausted its potential and needs to be renewed. The problem is not in this sensitivity to endings, but in the fact that the fear of the unknown forces either to postpone an obvious decision, or to end everything too abruptly.

## Manifestation in the plus

On the plus side, a person recognizes in time when it is necessary to close an outdated source of income and switch to a new one, and does it thoughtfully - with preparation, a gradual transition, without panic. This approach frees up resources and time for truly profitable areas instead of years of supporting something that no longer works.

## Manifestation in the minus

In the negative, a person clings to a source of income that has long ceased to justify the invested efforts, only because change scares more than gradual exhaustion. Another manifestation is a sudden, emotional decision to drop everything without a plan, which leads to financial failure instead of the expected relief.

## What can be negative

The trigger is often the fear of unknown income after the end of the usual one — anxiety "what if a new source does not appear" makes you hold on to the old one even to your own detriment. The second trigger is accumulated irritation from long-suffering, which at some point explodes in a sudden, rash break.

## How to return energy to the plus side

It is useful to regularly and honestly evaluate whether the source of income is still serving a purpose, or is already held by habit and fear of change. The second step is to prepare the transition in advance when planning the termination, and not wait for an emotional breakdown that will force you to act suddenly and without preparation.

## The main task

To learn to complete financial directions that have exhausted themselves, in a timely and thoughtful manner — without holding on to them out of fear, and without cutting them off abruptly out of irritation.

## Questions for self-reflection

- What source of income do I support only out of habit, although it has not brought results for a long time?
- Do I prepare the transition to a new one in advance, or do I wait for the moment when I have to act suddenly?
- What exactly scares me about the completion of this financial stage?

## Practical plan

1. List all current sources of income and honestly assess which of them has already exhausted its potential.
2. For a direction that needs to be completed, make a concrete plan for a gradual transition instead of an abrupt break.
3. Determine one step to a new source of income that can be done this month, in parallel with the old one.
