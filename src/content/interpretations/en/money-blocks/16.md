# 16. The Tower — Financial Blocks

## Brief Essence

A typical financial block of the sixteenth energy is the fear of building something stable, because somewhere in the past, experience has shown that even reliable things can suddenly fall: the loss of a job, business or savings, which left a deep mark. The second typical block is self-destructive financial behavior, when a person unknowingly provokes a collapse where everything is going too smoothly and calmly.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block is a valuable gift - the ability not to be afraid of destruction and quickly rebuild, even better than before. The problem is not the fragility of financial structures, but the fact that the experienced shock turns natural resilience into a constant expectation of a new disaster.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, a person builds financial stability that withstands crises, because it is laid on realistic, not illusory foundations, and is not afraid to rearrange plans when circumstances change. The once-experienced financial shock turns into a source of sobriety and the ability to quickly recover from losses.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the fear of a new collapse forces one to avoid any financial risks or, on the contrary, to unknowingly destroy what has already been built, as if anticipating the catastrophe with one's own hands. It is also possible to make sudden, impulsive financial decisions in a moment of stress, which only deepens the instability.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a past sudden financial collapse or loss, after which stability has come to be associated with danger rather than safety. The second trigger is long-term ignoring of obvious signs that some financial scheme or habit is no longer working, until the situation explodes on its own.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

It is useful to learn to distinguish real financial risk from the disturbing echo of a past shock that is long gone. The second step is to consciously and gradually rebuild unstable financial habits, without waiting until they themselves fall into a critical moment. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

Learn to build financial stability that is not afraid of shocks and knows how to recover from them, instead of living in constant expectation of collapse or provoking it yourself.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What financial experience of the past still makes me afraid to build something stable?
- Is there a financial habit or scheme that I should have rebuilt a long time ago, but am putting off until a critical moment?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one outdated financial habit or pattern and begin consciously rebuilding it without waiting for a crisis.
2. Think of one past financial failure and write down what it actually taught you.
3. Check financial decisions once a month for signs of panicked risk avoidance or, on the contrary, unconscious self-destruction.
