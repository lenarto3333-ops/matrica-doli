# 12. The Hanged Man — Financial Blocks

## Brief Essence

A typical financial block of the twelfth energy is becoming trapped in financial waiting. A person postpones important money decisions while waiting for the “right moment,” imagined as perfectly safe and clear, and loses time and genuine opportunities instead. Another common block is a subconscious belief that sacrifice or surrendering one’s own interests will inevitably pay off someday. This leads the person to accept unfavorable terms and “endure for the sake of a future reward.”

## Natural Strength

Behind this block lies a valuable ability to see a financial situation from an unusual angle and notice solutions that others miss in their haste. The problem is not the ability to stop and think, but that the pause sometimes becomes endless waiting rather than a tool for reaching a more precise decision.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, a person uses a pause before a financial decision to their advantage. They do not rush, weigh their options, and therefore find a more profitable, unconventional route where others would act by habit. This approach generates income through original solutions rather than speed of response.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the same capacity to wait becomes a pattern of continually postponing financial decisions: an investment is not made because “the time is not right,” a price is not raised because “it is better to wait,” and an opportunity disappears while the person analyzes it yet again. Another expression is the willingness to tolerate unfavorable conditions for a long time in exchange for a vague promise of future reward.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

Uncertainty is often the trigger. With no guarantee of the outcome, the person waits for complete clarity instead of acting on the information available, although that clarity may never arrive. Another trigger is a past loss caused by a hasty financial decision, after which every decision is delayed “just in case.”

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

Set a clear limit for the pause before a financial decision—a few days or a week, rather than an indefinite “when things become clearer.” The second step is learning to distinguish a pause that genuinely provides new information from one that merely postpones the fear of acting.

## Main Task

Turn the natural ability to stop and look from another angle into a tool for more accurate financial decisions, rather than a way to avoid responsibility for choosing.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Which financial decision have I postponed “until a better moment” for longer than is reasonable?
- Is there a situation in which I tolerate unfavorable terms in exchange for a vague promise of future reward?
- What exactly am I waiting to learn before I finally act?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one financial decision being delayed without a clear reason and set a specific deadline for it.
2. Write down exactly what new information further waiting will provide. If there is no answer, act on the data already available.
3. Review one arrangement in which you tolerate unfavorable terms “for the future,” and honestly assess whether it is worth the price you are paying now.
