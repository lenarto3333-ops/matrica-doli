# 21. The World — Financial Blocks

## Brief Essence

A typical financial block of the twenty-first energy is endless tinkering with a financial plan, project, or product that never seems "ready enough" to start making money from. The second typical block is a narrow, defensive view of money, where new opportunities, markets or partnerships are dismissed simply because they are unfamiliar.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block is a valuable gift — the ability to see the financial picture as a whole, to gather disparate sources of income or resources into a single strategy and bring money matters to a real result. The problem is not in the pursuit of perfection itself, but in the fact that it turns into endless procrastination, and not a concrete financial bottom line.

## Expression in the Positive

In a plus, a person sees his financial situation as a single whole, knows how to combine disparate incomes, projects or assets into a coherent strategy and brings things to a real result, and not to an eternal draft. She is open to larger opportunities — new markets, partnerships, even work or clients from other cities and countries.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, a person either endlessly refines a financial plan, product or proposal, never daring to launch it, or narrows the circle of financial possibilities to a narrow, familiar circle, rejecting everything unfamiliar as risky. It is also possible to be suspicious of new monetary instruments, partners or markets just because they do not fit into the usual picture of the world.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is the fear that the result will not be perfect enough, which causes any "launch now" to be postponed for another refinement. The second trigger is an experience in which the new and unfamiliar has been associated with danger, due to which a person unconsciously closes himself off from wider financial opportunities.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is useful to define for yourself a clear criterion of "ready enough" for a specific financial step and consciously stop the refinement when this criterion is reached. The second step is to consciously expand the circle of financial opportunities: to try new formats of income, markets or partnerships, and not stick to the usual ones. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

To learn to bring financial affairs to an honest conclusion, without waiting for perfection, and to keep a broad view of money, not narrowed by fear of the new.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a financial project or plan that I endlessly refine, instead of already completing and launching it?
- Do I reject new financial opportunities just because they are unfamiliar, and not because I have really considered them?

## Practical Plan

1. Choose one financial project or decision, define the criterion "sufficiently ready" and complete it within the stipulated period.
2. Make a list of your sources of income or assets and bring them into one coherent picture - where there are gaps and where there is an excess of effort.
3. Once a month, consciously consider one new, unfamiliar financial opportunity before rejecting it.
