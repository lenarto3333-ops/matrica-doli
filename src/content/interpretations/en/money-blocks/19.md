# 19. The Sun — Financial Blocks

## Brief Essence

A typical financial block of the nineteenth energy is excessive generosity, which exhausts one's own budget in order to remain a "bright and light" person for everyone, who never gives up. The second typical block is pride in money matters, when large-scale ambitions are not supported by real calculations, and the recognition of one's own mistake is perceived as a blow to the image of a successful person.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block is a valuable gift—the ability to think big, to believe in the big, and to attract resources with natural generosity and optimism, not fear of scarcity. The problem is not the generosity or scale itself, but the fact that they have no limits and are not checked with real numbers.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a person is consciously generous - he shares and invests on a large scale, but within limits that do not harm his own stability, and he allows himself to honestly admit financial miscalculations without shame. Here, natural optimism becomes the fuel for bold money decisions backed by a real plan.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, a person either spends and gives beyond his means to support the image of being generous and successful, or, on the contrary, takes on too large-scale financial projects with pride, ignoring warnings and risks. It is also possible to hide real financial difficulties behind the facade of well-being, when admitting the problem seems unacceptable.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is an environment where financial success is perceived as the only measure of a person's worth, which makes any failure seem like a personal humiliation. The second trigger is a childhood habit of "earning" love and attention by being generous or showing success, instead of simply receiving recognition.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is useful to learn how to compare large-scale financial plans with specific numbers before acting on a scale, not just on enthusiasm. The second step is to allow yourself to openly admit financial difficulties to loved ones, without fear that this will erase the image of a bright and successful person. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

Learn to combine natural generosity and large-scale thinking with sober calculation so that joy from money does not depend on constant confirmation of one's own success in front of others.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Have I ever spent or given beyond my means, so as not to destroy the image of a generous person?
- How honestly do I admit financial difficulties to myself and loved ones, and not hide them behind the facade of well-being?

## Practical Plan

1. The next time you get the impulse to make a big financial decision, compare it to your actual budget before you act.
2. Write down one money problem that you have been hiding for a long time and honestly talk about it with a loved one or a professional.
3. Once a month, check whether your financial generosity is based on real opportunities or on the desire to maintain the image of a successful person.
