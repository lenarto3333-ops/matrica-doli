# 18. The Moon — Financial Blocks

## Brief Essence

A typical financial block of the eighteenth energy is disturbing fantasies about monetary disasters that have not yet occurred, but are already paralyzing the ability to act rationally with finances. The second typical block is distrust of one's own intuition in money matters, when a person ignores the quiet inner feeling that "something is wrong" and gets into situations that could have been avoided.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block is a valuable gift - a fine feeling for people, circumstances and hidden risks, the ability to feel what has not yet manifested itself in facts. The problem is not the sensitivity itself, but the fact that it is replaced by a disturbing fantasy instead of serving as an accurate internal reference point.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a person trusts intuition in financial decisions, but checks it with facts before acting - the feeling suggests the direction, and the mind forms a concrete step. The fear of loss here turns into healthy caution, which helps to notice a real risk in time, rather than an imaginary one.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, a person either lives in constant anxiety about money, which cannot be calmed by any real numbers, or, on the contrary, completely ignores financial reality, fleeing in the fantasy that "somehow it will resolve itself." It is also possible to chronically avoid money conversations and decisions due to fear of facing the distressing thoughts they may cause.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a past experience of financial loss or deception, after which any uncertainty about money is automatically imagined to the worst-case scenario. The second trigger is a long period in which real financial problems were silenced or suppressed emotionally, instead of being acknowledged and worked on.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is useful to learn to distinguish a real financial signal from a disturbing fantasy by comparing your gut feeling with concrete numbers and facts. The second step is to allow yourself to feel anxious about money when it arises, instead of suppressing it or, conversely, drowning in it without action. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

To learn to use subtle intuition as a real financial tool — to trust it in combination with facts, and not to replace sober analysis with it or, on the contrary, drowning in disturbing fantasies.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a financial anxiety that I carry constantly, although there is no real reason for it now?
- Where am I ignoring my quiet gut feeling about money, relying only on logic or someone else's opinion?

## Practical Plan

1. The next time you have financial anxiety, check it against concrete numbers—bills, income, expenses—before making a decision.
2. Write down one long-ignored gut feeling about money and test it with facts during the week.
3. Check once a month whether your financial decisions are based on reality or imaginary scenarios that have not yet happened.
