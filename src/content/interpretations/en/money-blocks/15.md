# 15. The Devil — Financial Blocks

## Brief Essence

A typical financial block of the fifteenth energy is dependence on money or things as a source of a sense of security: a person spends impulsively to drown out anxiety, or, conversely, accumulates with an obsessive fear of losing control. The second typical block is money earned or spent as a tool of power over others, which eventually turns into an addiction to the very feeling of control.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block is a valuable gift - a keen sense of the true value of things and people, the ability to see what actually brings wealth and what only creates its illusion. The problem is not in the desire for wealth itself, but in the fact that the fear of lack turns the natural desire for the resource into an obsessive dependence on it.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, a person builds stable material well-being, based on a clear understanding of what he really needs, and on a healthy attraction that attracts profitable opportunities. Money becomes a tool of freedom and influence, not a substitute for inner support.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, money or things turn into a kind of addiction: spending becomes a way to fill an inner void, and hoarding becomes a way to control anxiety due to the feeling of power over the resource. It is also possible to use money or services as a tool to influence others — bribery, keeping people in a dependent position.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a deep fear of loss of control or security, which a person tries to suppress through material accumulation or, conversely, compulsive spending. The second trigger is the experience when money in the family was associated with power and control over others, due to which financial behavior adopts the same logic.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

It is useful to learn to notice the moment when the desire to spend or save arises from anxiety, and not from a real need, and to pause before taking action. The second step is to consciously separate your sense of self-worth from the size of your wealth or possessions.

## Main Task

Learn to use your natural magnetism and sense of value to build real, sustainable wealth, not to let money or things control your emotional state and relationships with people.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- At what moments do I spend or save money not out of real need, but to silence anxiety?
- Do I use money or resources to maintain control over someone?

## Practical Plan

1. During the week, track impulsive spending or hoarding and write down the anxiety behind it.
2. Identify one habit where money replaces internal support and find another, non-financial way to soothe it.
3. Check once a month whether you are using financial decisions as a tool to influence loved ones.
