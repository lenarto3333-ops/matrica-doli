# 14. Temperance — Financial Blocks

## Brief Essence

A typical financial block of the fourteenth energy is chronic indecision about money decisions: a person weighs options for a long time, is afraid of making the wrong choice and in the end makes none, losing time and opportunities. The second typical block is swinging between extremes: periods of austerity are replaced by impulsive spending, because restraint, pushed to the edge, is eventually broken by the opposite.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block is the valuable ability to weigh decisions comprehensively and find a balanced approach to money that takes into account both present needs and future stability. The problem is not in caution itself, but in the fact that the fear of error turns a measured approach into endless procrastination or into a disguised avoidance of responsibility.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, a person knows how to keep a stable financial balance: spends and saves wisely, without going to extremes, and makes monetary decisions gradually, weighing all options in a reasonable time. This approach provides stability without rigid asceticism and without risky disruptions.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, a person can postpone an important financial decision for years - an investment, change of job, revision of the budget - because he cannot make a final decision. Another manifestation is cycles of austerity, suddenly replaced by impulse purchases, when accumulated restraint explodes at the opposite extreme.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The trigger is often the fear of making the "wrong" financial decision, due to which any choice is postponed indefinitely under the pretext of additional consideration. The second trigger is overly strict self-imposed restrictions that sooner or later provoke a backlash in the form of uncontrollable spending.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

It is useful to set realistic deadlines for financial decisions, so that a balanced approach does not turn into endless procrastination. The second step is to choose soft, stable financial habits instead of rigid prohibitions that are difficult to sustain for long and that provoke disruptions.

## Main Task

Learn to make financial decisions gradually, but on time — trusting your own ability to find a balance, and not hiding behind an endless weighing of pros and cons.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What financial decision have I been putting off for a long time under the pretext of "not yet ready"?
- Are there cycles in my financial life of austerity that are then replaced by impulsive spending?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one financial decision you've been putting off and set a specific deadline for making it.
2. Replace one hard money ban with a softer, more stable habit that you can really follow for a long time.
3. Check the balance of expenses and savings once a week to notice extremes in both directions in time.
