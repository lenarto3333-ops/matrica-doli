# 22. The Fool — Financial Blocks

## Brief Essence

A typical financial block of the twenty-second energy is an adventurous attitude to money, when risky decisions are made without thinking about the consequences, and serious financial obligations are taken too lightly. The second typical block is the opposite: the fear of any changes in the monetary sphere, due to which a person clings to a limited but usual income for years, even when it does not suit him.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block is a valuable gift - the ease with which you can start a financial business from scratch, and a sincere trust that new opportunities and resources will be found. The problem is not in openness to the new, but in the fact that it either loses connection with responsibility, or, on the contrary, is suppressed by fear to such an extent that a new opportunity is not even considered.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a person easily tries new sources of income, work formats or financial instruments, without being afraid to start from scratch, and at the same time soberly assesses the risks and is responsible for his decisions. She trusts life, but does not confuse trust with carelessness - freedom here goes hand in hand with sober calculation.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, a person either invests in risky business impulsively, without a plan and preparation, or takes on financial obligations that he then does not fulfill, or, on the contrary, sits for years in an uninteresting, low-paying job or an outdated financial model, fearing the slightest change. Avoiding responsibility for past money decisions is also possible — it's easier to pretend they didn't happen.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a feeling of boredom or routine, which pushes you to take reckless risks for the sake of novelty. The second trigger is an experience in which a past attempt to change something in the monetary sphere ended in failure, due to which the fear of any new financial step appears.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

Before any new financial decision, it is useful to consciously prescribe the possible consequences and take responsibility for them in advance, not after the fact. The second step is to regularly allow yourself one small, measured experiment with a new source of income or format of earning, so that the fear of change does not turn into a complete rejection of it. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

Learn to take risks consciously, not impulsively, and at the same time not to let the fear of the new turn your financial life into a frozen, limited routine.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a financial decision that I made impulsively without thinking about the consequences and responsibility?
- Am I clinging to the usual, but unsatisfactory income only out of fear of change?

## Practical Plan

1. Review your last impulsive financial decision and write down what preparation or thought it lacked.
2. Choose one new, moderately risky way of making money and try it on a small, controlled scale.
3. Once a month, check whether your attitude towards money is closer to sober freedom, or to one of the extremes - adventures or stagnation.
