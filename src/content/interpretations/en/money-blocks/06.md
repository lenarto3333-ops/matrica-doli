# 6. The Lovers — Financial Blocks

## In Brief

The typical financial block of this energy is an inability to choose a clear financial direction out of fear that another option might prove better. Another common block is confusing money with feelings: financial decisions are made from pity, guilt, or a desire to prove love rather than from sober calculation.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block lies a valuable quality—a deep sensitivity to what truly resonates with one's inner values in financial matters. The problem is not sensitivity itself, but the way it can sometimes become paralyzing indecision. When applied thoughtfully, this ability to sense the “right” direction is a valuable financial tool of this energy.

## Positive Expression

In the positive, this energy transforms sensitivity into the ability to choose a financial direction that reflects genuine values rather than mere profit or other people's expectations. A person in the positive can combine sincerity with practicality, making decisions that bring both income and inner peace.

## Negative Expression

In the negative, the block appears as chronic indecision among several financial options, causing opportunities to be lost while the choice drags on. Another expression is making financial concessions in relationships out of fear of conflict or a desire to preserve harmony at any cost, even when doing so harms one's own stability.

## What Can Shift the Energy into the Negative

A common trigger is a situation in which a financial decision is tightly intertwined with an important relationship, making it harder to assess the options soberly. Another trigger is a past experience in which a financial choice led to disappointment or the loss of an important relationship.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is helpful to learn to separate a financial decision from the emotional weight attached to it by asking yourself honestly: “Is this decision about money, or about feelings I am afraid to acknowledge?” The second step is to practice making small, clear financial choices and see firsthand that decisiveness does not destroy relationships and often strengthens trust.

## Main Task

The main task is to turn financial sensitivity into a tool for conscious choice rather than a cause of paralysis or hidden concessions.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What financial decision am I postponing because I am afraid of choosing “wrong”?
- Do I make financial decisions out of guilt or a desire to prove my love?
- What does the “right” financial choice mean to me—profit or inner peace?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one financial decision that has been postponed because of indecision and set a specific deadline for it.
2. Before the next relationship-related financial decision, honestly separate the practical considerations from the emotional ones.
3. Discuss with someone close to you a financial issue that has remained unspoken because of fear of conflict.
