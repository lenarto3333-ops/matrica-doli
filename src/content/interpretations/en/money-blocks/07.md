# 7. The Chariot — Financial Blocks

## In Brief

A typical financial block for this energy is the inability to stop and consolidate what has already been achieved: a person constantly rushes into a new project or risk before stabilizing the previous one. Another common block is an unwillingness to share control over financial decisions or the business itself, which keeps income limited by personal capacity instead of allowing it to grow through a delegated system.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block lies a valuable quality: the courage to act and take financial risks where others hesitate. The problem is not decisiveness itself, but that it can sometimes turn into thrill-seeking for its own sake, without a strategic pause. Speed applied thoughtfully is one of this energy's most valuable financial tools.

## Positive Expression

In its positive expression, this energy turns decisiveness into a controlled financial strategy: a person sets a specific goal, calculates risks, and builds several manageable income streams rather than staking everything on one impulsive leap. Income grows steadily because every new step rests on the securely established result of the previous one.

## Negative Expression

In its negative expression, this block appears as chronically scattering effort across too many financial projects at once, none of which has time to produce a stable result, or as conspicuous spending intended to prove financial success to others rather than serve genuine goals. Another expression is the unwillingness to delegate financial or operational tasks, which limits income growth to one's personal capacity.

## What Can Pull This Energy into the Negative

A frequent trigger is comparing one's financial results with those of more successful people, intensifying the urge to immediately “catch up” through a risky or rushed decision. Another trigger is losing control over a project or financial situation and experiencing it as a personal defeat.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is helpful to regularly check whether a new financial project is being started before the previous one has stabilized, and to consciously restrain that impulse. The second step is to practice delegating some financial or operational tasks and observe that income can grow without constant personal control over every stage.

## Main Task

The main task is to transform decisiveness and speed into a controlled system in which every financial step is consolidated before the next one begins.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- How many financial projects am I managing at once, and how many have truly reached a stable result?
- Do I have expenses that are more about displaying success to others than serving real goals?
- What keeps me from delegating some financial control to someone else?

## Practical Plan

1. List all current financial projects and choose one or two to bring to a stable result before starting anything new.
2. Review the past month's expenses and identify any conspicuous purchases that do not serve a genuine purpose.
3. Delegate one financial or operational task and do not intervene until the agreed checkpoint.
