# 11. Strength — Financial Blocks

## Brief Essence

The financial block of eleventh energy is rarely a lack of earning ability; this energy knows how to work hard and enjoys doing so. The problem lies in how strength is applied: either a person pressures themselves so intensely that earning costs them their health, or they are afraid to show confidence when negotiating their value and diminish themselves to avoid appearing too forceful.

## Natural Strength

This energy has a rare capacity to sustain long, difficult work without losing motivation. A person with this resource can complete a complex, exhausting project after others give up, and this endurance often determines who ultimately achieves the result.

## Positive Expression

In the positive, a person combines persistence with a sensible limit. They work diligently but know when to stop, and can insist confidently on fair compensation without aggression. They are not afraid to state their value directly.

## Negative Expression

In the negative, either workaholism depletes the person faster than it produces income, or the opposite extreme appears: fear of standing firm in negotiations and understating one's value to avoid seeming too assertive or aggressive. Both extremes leave the person without fair compensation for their real effort.

## What Can Shift the Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a past experience in which others interpreted persistence as aggression, reinforcing a fear of defending one's value. A second is an environment that demands constant proof of endurance without recognition or fair compensation. A third is the inner belief that requesting worthy compensation means being “too hard.”

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to state your value calmly and directly, without an apologetic tone or aggression: it is a fact, not a plea. The second is to define a personal workload limit beyond which work ceases to be worthwhile because of its cost to your health, and honor that limit.

It is also useful to practice brief, clear wording in financial negotiations—without lengthy justifications, but also without pressure. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

Learn to apply natural endurance wisely: insist on fair compensation without aggression or self-denial, maintaining a balance between strength and personal limits.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I state my value directly, or diminish it for fear of seeming too assertive?
- How much do I work beyond a reasonable limit without receiving fair compensation?

## Practical Plan

1. Set a specific amount or condition below which you will not agree to work, and honor it in your next negotiation.
2. Calculate how many hours a week you work beyond what is necessary, and reduce that number.
3. Practice saying one sentence about your value aloud, calmly and without apology.
