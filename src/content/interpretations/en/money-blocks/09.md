# 9. The Hermit — Financial Blocks

## In Brief

The financial block of the ninth energy is rarely connected with a lack of ability—the depth of thought and expertise are usually there. The problem is that money requires visibility and exchange, while the Hermit tends to avoid both. Talking about price feels too “superficial” to someone accustomed to valuing substance over form—and that is precisely why the conversation often never happens at all.

## Natural Strength

This energy has a keen sense of the true value of knowledge and deep work, both its own and that of others. A person with this resource can develop expertise to a level people are willing to pay for: research, analysis, one-to-one consulting, and work that requires concentration and time alone with a problem.

## Positive Expression

In its positive expression, a person knows how to monetize depth itself: they offer not a superficial service but a thoughtful, meticulously developed result, and name a fair price for it based on the work’s genuine quality. They choose focused rather than mass-market ways of earning—fewer clients, but deeper and more valuable interaction with each one.

## Negative Expression

In its negative expression, chronic avoidance of self-promotion appears: the person feels uncomfortable “selling themselves,” and talking about money seems tactless, so they either remain silent about their value or accept less than their work is truly worth. Isolation from colleagues and potential partners takes away opportunities for collaboration, while the fear of seeming superficial prevents them from even simply talking about their work.

## What Can Push the Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger is an experience in which deep, serious work went unappreciated while something superficial and loud received praise instead. A second trigger is an environment that demands constant visibility, networking, and self-presentation, with no room to pause and think. The third is the inner belief that talking about money means betraying the true essence of the work.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to accept that naming a price for deep work is every bit as honest as doing it: silence about its value does not make the work nobler, only less visible. The second step is to find a style of self-presentation that suits your nature—not loud networking, but written content, a portfolio, or recommendations: ways to talk about yourself while keeping your own pace.

It is also useful to deliberately choose a few trusted contacts for regular rather than occasional professional exchange. This reduces isolation without requiring you to become a public figure. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

Learn to step out of the shadows just enough for the value of your work to become visible and properly paid—without having to become loud and without allowing yourself to hide behind the phrase “my work speaks for itself.”

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I name a fair price for my deep work, or do I remain silent and hope others will recognize its value on their own?
- What exactly feels “too superficial” to me about self-presentation—and can I find a format that feels right for me?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down three tangible results of your expert work and translate them into specific value for a client or employer.
2. Choose one quiet form of self-presentation—a written piece, a portfolio, or a work sample—and publish or send it this week.
3. Arrange regular, small-scale contact with one or two professional peers instead of remaining completely isolated.
