# 20. Judgement — Financial Blocks

## Brief Essence

A typical twentieth energy financial block is overly harsh self-judgment about past money mistakes that prevents you from moving forward, or inherited family money scenarios and arguments that still influence present decisions. The second typical block is unfinished property or inheritance conflicts in the family, which deplete resources and prevent honest summing up of financial results.

## Natural Strength

Behind this block is a valuable gift — the ability to honestly and soberly evaluate one's own financial path, to feel the support of the family and to see which money habits should be left in the past and which ones should be taken with us. The problem is not in the ability to judge, but in the fact that the trial turns into an endless punishment, and not a single honest conclusion.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a person honestly analyzes past financial decisions, draws a specific conclusion from them and moves on, without carrying guilt for years. Family experience here becomes a resource — an understanding of which money scenarios should be continued and which ones should be consciously changed.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, a person either endlessly punishes himself for the same financial mistake, afraid to act again, or gets stuck in property or inheritance disputes with the family, which do not have an honest conclusion for years. It is possible and unconscious repetition of the money scenario of parents, even if it has not worked for a long time.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is an unfinished family history around money: debts, inheritance, property claims that no one has honestly spoken about and closed. The second trigger is an upbringing where a financial mistake was perceived as a personal judgment rather than an experience from which to draw a conclusion and move on.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is useful to honestly analyze a past financial mistake once, draw a concrete conclusion from it, and consciously allow yourself to close the topic. The second step is to figure out how much of your current money habits are actually inherited from your family and consciously decide what to keep and what to change. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

Learning to pass one fair judgment on yourself and your family history, not an endless one, turning the experience of past mistakes and family scenarios into a resource for renewal rather than a lifelong burden.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a financial mistake or a family dispute that I am reliving in my mind over and over instead of coming to a conclusion and letting go?
- What money habits did I unconsciously inherit from my family, and do they really serve me now?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one financial mistake of the past, formulate one honest conclusion from it and consciously close this topic to yourself.
2. Identify one money habit inherited from your parents and decide whether you want to consciously continue it or change it.
3. If there is an unfinished property or inheritance dispute in the family, take one concrete step towards its honest resolution or contact a specialist.
