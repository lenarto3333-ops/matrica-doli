# 18. The Moon — Relationships with Parents and Children

## Brief Essence

In family relationships, this energy may manifest through deep emotional sensitivity and a rich imagination. A parent with this energy can usually sense a child's mood even without words and create an atmosphere in which experiencing different emotions feels safe. A child with this resource may have a vivid imagination, a rich inner world, memorable dreams, and elaborate fantasies that need understanding rather than dismissal. The risk appears when this sensitivity turns into anxious overprotection and the projection of parental fears onto the child, or into a family atmosphere of ambiguity and concealed emotion in which feelings are present but no one is accustomed to discussing them directly.

## Natural Strength

A child with this energy often has an especially developed imagination and emotional sensitivity, including vivid dreams, a rich fantasy life, and a subtle awareness of the moods of nearby adults. When this inner world is supported rather than mocked, it can become a lifelong source of creativity and self-understanding.

A parent with this energy can naturally sense a child's state even before the child is able to name their emotions. They notice anxiety, sadness, or fear in small changes and respond with understanding rather than irritation or denial.

Another resource of this energy is the ability to create emotional safety. Parents and children with this energy may understand one another intuitively, without many words. This emotional attunement can help a family move through difficult periods with deep mutual understanding when it complements direct and honest conversation.

## Expression in the Positive

In its positive expression, a parent supports the child's imagination and emotional depth without calling their fears or fantasies “nonsense” or an “overreaction.” They help the child express feelings in words and distinguish a real danger from an anxious fantasy while continuing to respect the richness of the child's inner world.

Healthy family communication includes sincere conversations about feelings, fears, and dreams. Parents do not deny a child's emotions with phrases such as “there is nothing to be afraid of.” Instead, they help the child understand the source of the anxiety and look for a way to manage it together, preserving trust between adult and child.

For an adult child in relation to their parents, the positive side of this energy supports gratitude for the emotional sensitivity that was nurtured in childhood. If this support was absent, the person can learn to accept their own emotions without becoming trapped in blame, recognizing that their parents may also have grown up in an environment where feelings were left unspoken.

Another sign of a positive state is the family's ability to discuss difficult subjects openly instead of creating an atmosphere of ambiguity. When something serious happens, it is not concealed or ignored. It is discussed in a way that children can understand and safely process.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

As a mother, a woman expressing this energy positively can sense her child's emotional state without becoming completely absorbed by it. She empathizes and offers support while maintaining her own emotional stability, giving the child a dependable source of safety even during anxious moments.

She supports her children's imagination and sensitivity without projecting her own fears onto them. She helps a child distinguish real danger from imagined danger instead of unintentionally passing her anxiety on to them.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

As a father, a man expressing this energy positively shows his children that sensitivity and emotion are not weaknesses. He acknowledges his own feelings openly and shares doubts or concerns in a form the child can understand, giving them permission to do the same.

He sincerely supports the children's imagination and creative fantasies without dismissing them as “just made up.” He gives them room to explore their inner world while remaining present as a calm and reliable source of support.

## Expression in the Negative

In its negative expression, parental sensitivity can become anxious overprotection. Adults project their fears onto the child, prohibit many ordinary activities “just in case,” and constantly worry about the child's safety without sufficient reason. Over time, the child may learn to fear the world as intensely as their parents do.

Another negative pattern is a family atmosphere of unspoken tension. Difficult subjects, emotions, and conflicts are not addressed directly but remain “in the air” as unease that children can feel without being able to explain it. This uncertainty may create more anxiety than an honest, age-appropriate conversation, even when the subject itself is difficult.

For an adult child, the negative side may appear as chronic anxiety or suspicion. A person raised among unspoken fears and concealed family issues may find it difficult to trust loved ones. They may expect the worst without evidence or move toward the opposite extreme and disconnect from their own emotions because they experience them as dangerous.

At a deeper level, the family may develop a pattern of escaping reality. When authentic feelings have been too painful to experience, some family members—parents or children—may seek refuge in fantasy, compulsive behavior, or isolation. This pattern can be passed on quietly and make genuine emotional contact increasingly difficult.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger for parents is their own unresolved anxiety or fear, which may be unconsciously projected onto the child through excessive protection or unexplained restrictions. A second trigger is the family habit of avoiding direct conversations about difficult subjects and emotions, leaving children to feel the tension without understanding its source. For an adult child, situations resembling the ambiguity or overprotection of childhood may activate disproportionate anxiety, suspicion, or a desire to withdraw emotionally from loved ones.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step for parents is to consider whether they are projecting unresolved fears onto the child and to separate their personal anxiety from the child's actual need for safety. The second step is to speak openly about feelings and difficult family subjects, naming what is happening instead of leaving it “in the air” as silent tension.

For an adult child, the third step is to learn gradually to distinguish real danger from inherited anxiety. It may help to check whether a present fear is supported by specific facts or connected to childhood experiences of ambiguity. The fourth step is to practice sincere family conversations about emotions and create a space in which fears and doubts can be expressed without shame or judgment.

It is also useful to notice whether the family atmosphere has become one of silent anxiety instead of honest dialogue. Deliberately creating moments in which difficult feelings can be named in a safe and supportive setting helps restore trust.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in family relationships is to accept and name one another's emotions honestly, without ambiguity or anxious overprotection. It is important to give children not a fear of the world, but trust in their own feelings and the ability to compare those feelings with reality. Genuine family closeness is built on honesty about emotional experience, not on silently carrying shared anxiety.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I project my unresolved fears or anxiety onto my child or parents?
- How openly does my family discuss difficult feelings, and which subjects always remain unspoken?
- Which childhood anxiety or atmosphere of secrecy still affects my ability to trust myself and others?
- Can I distinguish genuine risks to the child from scenarios created by my own fear?
- What happens in our family when someone expresses sadness, fear, or doubt?
- Do I support the child's imagination while also helping them compare fantasies with reality?
- Which honest conversation could reduce tension between family members right now?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one of your fears or anxieties and honestly consider whether you transfer it to the child through excessive restrictions or warnings.
2. Identify a subject your family has avoided discussing directly for years and take one careful step toward an honest conversation.
3. Talk with your child or parents about feelings and fears without dismissal or hurried reassurance.
4. If you are an adult child, identify one situation that triggers disproportionate anxiety or suspicion and consider its roots in childhood.
5. If you are a parent, practice naming your emotions aloud in the child's presence for one week instead of concealing them.
6. Create a safe family ritual for sharing feelings, such as a brief evening conversation about what each person experienced during the day.
