# 15. The Devil — Relationships with Parents and Children

## Brief Essence

In family relationships, this energy brings a strong charisma and insightful understanding of what each family member really wants or needs. A father or mother with this energy usually has a natural authority and is able to see the ulterior motives of a child's behavior—what is really behind a whim, rebellion, or silence. A child with such a resource often grows up magnetic, attractive to peers, with an early sense of human weaknesses and desires. The risk is when this power turns into either control and manipulation within the family, or unhealthy dependence—of the child on the parent, the parent on the child, or either of them on external recognition and authority.

## Natural Strength

A child with this energy usually has a natural appeal that makes him a leader in a group of children long before he is aware of his own influence: other children gravitate to him, listen to him, imitate him, and this early charisma becomes a powerful resource if learned to use it honestly.

A father or mother with this energy naturally sees what the child really needs in outward behavior: recognizes when a whim is really a plea for attention and a rebellion is an attempt to defend one's own identity, and this insight allows for a wiser response than superficial punishment or indulgence.

Another resource of this energy in the family is the ability to build the material and social well-being of the family: a father or mother with this energy is often able to provide for the family, create a sense of stability and status, relying on their own business acumen and ability to attract resources.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, the father or mother uses natural authority and insight to honestly guide the child rather than control it: they see the true motives behind the child's behavior and respond with understanding rather than suspicion or punishment, and this is why the child feels seen rather than persecuted.

Positive communication in the family includes the open recognition of the desires, ambitions and pleasures of each family member: parents do not shame the child for striving for success, beauty or recognition, but help to direct these natural desires in a healthy direction, showing by their own example that it is possible to strive and get what you want honestly.

In the role of an adult child in relation to his own parents, this energy is able to recognize hidden manipulation or attempts at control on the part of parents, without losing respect and love for them, and builds relationships with his family in which the influence is based on sincerity, and not on feelings of guilt or fear.

Another sign of a positive state is the family's ability to openly talk about money, power and desire without turning these topics into taboos: children learn to understand the value of a resource, a healthy attitude towards success and attractiveness, and are not ashamed of their own ambitions.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

As a mother, a woman with this positive energy naturally draws her children's trust through a warm but convincing presence: she knows how to gently guide a child, seeing the true motives behind their behavior, and at the same time does not use this insight to manipulate or over-control.

She knows how to talk openly with children about desire, success and attractiveness, showing by her own example that a woman can be strong, magnetic and at the same time honest and free, without hiding her own ambitions for the sake of feigned modesty.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In the role of a father, a man with this positive energy knows how to lead a family confidently and convincingly, relying on real authority, not on fear or pressure: he sees the hidden needs of children and his partner, and his influence in the family is based on trust, and not on the dependence of loved ones on his approval or resources.

He teaches children a healthy attitude to power, money and desires by his own example - shows that it is possible to strive for success and recognition without sacrificing honesty and respect for people, and at the same time maintains enough emotional openness so that children are not afraid to approach him with their own weaknesses.

## Expression in the Negative

In the parent's negative role, natural authority turns into control and manipulation: the parent uses an insightful understanding of the child's weaknesses and fears not to support, but to guide the child's behavior—through guilt, comparison with others, or conditional love—and the child grows up feeling loved only for obedience or achievement.

Another manifestation of the negative is parental dependence on the child as a source of self-importance: the father or mother becomes so emotionally or even financially attached to the success, attention or presence of the child that they do not allow him to separate, react jealously to his independent relationships or decisions, turning love into a hidden form of ownership.

In the role of an adult child, the negative manifests itself as a repetition of the parental model of control in one's own life: a person either begins to control loved ones through manipulation and guilt, copying what he saw in childhood, or, on the contrary, remains dependent on parental approval for much longer than is natural for adulthood.

At a deeper disadvantage, a family model is also possible, where power and resources become the main measure of relationships: attention, love or support are distributed depending on the success, obedience or usefulness of each family member, and this atmosphere of constant evaluation creates in children either an unhealthy pursuit of recognition or a deep sense of their own insignificance.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger for parents is their own sense of loss of control or significance, which is offset by increased pressure on the child or emotional attachment to their achievements. The second trigger is a family atmosphere in which love and attention are tangibly contingent on performance or obedience, which reinforces the habit of deserving acceptance. The third trigger for an adult child is the moment of separation from parents, when the attempt to become independent activates in the parents a hidden need for control or jealousy of the child's new independence.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step for parents is to honestly ask themselves whether their influence over their child is based on a genuine respect for their independence or a fear of losing control over their life. The second step is to consciously separate one's need for recognition or significance from parenting decisions that are about the child, not the parent's ego.

The third step for an adult child is to gradually learn to distinguish genuine parental care from attempts to control or manipulate feelings of guilt, and to build boundaries calmly, without severing the relationship. The fourth step is to practice open conversations about power, money, and desire in the family, removing taboo and shame from these topics so that children grow up with honest, not distorted, attitudes about success and influence.

It is also helpful to regularly check that family closeness is not dependent on one family member's dependence on the approval or resources of another, and to consciously create a space where love does not need to be earned by achievement or obedience.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in family relationships is to learn to use natural authority, magnetism and insight honestly: to lead children to their own strength, not to dependence on parental approval, and to be that adult child who respects parents, freeing himself from all forms of hidden control. True family closeness is not built on the power of one over the other, but on mutual respect for everyone's freedom.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is my influence on the child (or parents) based on respect for their freedom, or on a hidden desire to control?
- How much love and attention in my family depend on everyone's success, obedience or usefulness?
- Is there an emotional, financial, or other dependency in my relationship with my parents or children that I want to break free from?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one situation in which your influence over a child or parent is based on control rather than respect, and think about how to change that.
2. Think of the last time love or attention in the family was tangibly dependent on someone's "merit", and describe how to make the acceptance more unconditional.
3. Have a conversation with your child or parents about money, success or desire, removing the usual taboo or shame from this topic.
4. If you are an adult child, name one specific way in which you still depend on parental approval and take a small step toward greater autonomy.
5. If you are a parent, identify one time this week when you tried to control your child out of guilt, and try to replace it with an open conversation.
6. Have a family conversation where everyone can name one of their desires or ambitions without fear of judgment or shame.
