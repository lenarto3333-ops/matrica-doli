# 14. Temperance — Relationships with Parents and Children

## Brief Essence

In family relationships, this energy values calm, gradual dialogue and the ability to combine different characters, views and needs of family members into a harmonious whole. A father or mother with this energy usually naturally acts as a peacemaker in the family - extinguishes children's quarrels, finds a compromise between different opinions about upbringing, equalizes the tension between generations. A child with such a resource often grows up as a soft mediator between parents or between siblings. The risk is when this capacity for balance turns into either excessive indulgence instead of the necessary limits, or endless avoidance of important decisions for the sake of maintaining external family peace.

## Natural Strength

A child with this energy usually has a natural gift for calming family tensions: when parents fight or siblings clash, she intuitively looks for a way to reconcile the parties, sometimes taking on the role of a little diplomat long before her age.

A father or mother with this energy naturally teaches the child the art of compromise: they show by their own example how to listen to the other person, look for a solution that takes into account the interests of everyone, and not resort to shouting or ultimatums to resolve disputes.

Another resource of this energy in the family is the ability to combine different educational approaches or traditions into a single, working model: if there are different views on upbringing in the family - from different generations, cultures or parents' characters - this person naturally knows how to find a common denominator that works for a particular child.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, the father or mother knows how to solve conflicts between children or in the family in general through calm dialogue, and not through punishment or ignoring the problem: they listen to each side, help children find a just solution themselves, and thanks to this, children learn to solve conflicts peacefully, and not by force or silent resentment.

Communication in the family in a positive way includes the willingness to combine different opinions about upbringing or life without one side completely dominating the other: parents find a way to take into account the traditions of the older generation, and modern approaches, and their own intuition, creating a balanced, not one-sided model of upbringing.

In the role of an adult child in relation to his own parents, this energy is able to find a balance between respect for the parental position and his own adult autonomy: not to rebel sharply against everything that the older generation says, and not to obey blindly, but to choose from their wisdom what is really valuable, leaving the right to his own way.

Another sign of a positive state is the family's ability to combine very different people - in character, views, temperament - into a warm, functional community: instead of trying to make everyone the same, the family learns to appreciate the differences of each member as part of the common wealth.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In the role of a mother, a woman with this positive energy naturally acts as the emotional center of the family, which calms conflicts and finds compromise solutions that take into account the feelings of each child. She knows how to gently but consistently convey the rules, combining warmth with sufficient structure, rather than choosing between strictness and permissiveness.

She knows how to mediate between father and children, between different generations of the family, finding words that reduce tension and help each side hear the other, instead of taking sides and deepening the divide.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In the role of a father, a man with this positive energy knows how to extinguish family conflicts with a calm, measured presence, and not with the power of authority: he listens to the arguments of children and his partner, looks for a solution that recognizes the rightness of everyone at least partially, and that is why he uses trust, and not just obedience out of fear.

He teaches children the art of compromise by his own example - shows how to agree, not pressure, how to combine different positions in the family into a joint solution, and at the same time remains firm enough so that important boundaries are not blurred for the sake of feigned peace.

## Expression in the Negative

In the role of parents in the negative, the ability to balance turns into overindulgence: parents are so afraid of conflict or dissatisfaction of the child that they avoid setting the necessary boundaries, agree to any compromises just to avoid an argument, and in the end raise a child who does not know where the limits of what is allowed end.

Another negative manifestation is parental indecisiveness in important educational issues: when a firm decision has to be made regarding school, regime, punishment or other important issue, parents endlessly weigh all options, postpone the decision, and children grow up in instability, not knowing what is really expected of them.

In the role of an adult child, the negative manifests itself as a chronic avoidance of an open conversation with parents about real feelings or disagreements: for years, a person silently agrees with the parental position in order to maintain external peace, accumulating unspoken dissatisfaction, which eventually either breaks out into a sharp conflict or turns into emotional distance.

At a deeper negative level, a family model of total conflict avoidance is also possible: the family pretends that everyone agrees on everything, no one voices real disagreements, and this pretended harmony accumulates so much hidden tension that any little thing can cause a disproportionately sharp explosion.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger for parents is a situation that requires a firm, unpopular decision regarding a child, when the fear of damaging the relationship or causing conflict prevents them from acting decisively. The second trigger is a long period in which one side of the family constantly gives in for the sake of peace, accumulating unspoken resentment. A third trigger for an adult child is the moment to openly disagree with a parent, which activates a deep-seated fear of ruining the relationship or appearing ungrateful.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step for parents is to learn to distinguish between a healthy compromise and avoiding a necessary boundary: it's worth honestly asking yourself whether this decision is really in the best interests of the child, or if it's just a way to avoid an unpleasant conversation. The second step is to set clear deadlines for family decisions that are constantly postponed, so that the search for balance does not turn into chronic instability for the children.

The third step for the adult child is to gradually learn to voice a genuine opinion or disagreement to the parent in a safe, respectful way, instead of silently accumulating resentment. The fourth step is to practice an open family conversation about differences, recognizing that different views in the family are normal and not every conflict threatens the relationship.

It is also useful to regularly check whether family harmony is not dependent on the silence of one family member, and to consciously create a space where everyone can express their own position without fear of disturbing the peace.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in family relationships is to learn to combine warmth and flexibility with sufficient firmness: to be the kind of parent who seeks balance, but is not afraid to set the right limit, and to be the kind of grown-up child who respects his parents without losing his own voice. True family harmony is not built on the silent avoidance of conflicts, but on the ability to go through them with respect for each other.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Am I avoiding a necessary but uncomfortable decision in the family by hiding behind the word "compromise"?
- How often do I concede in family matters, really agreeing, and not just avoiding conflict?
- Do I give myself (or the child) the right to openly disagree without fear of spoiling the relationship?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one family decision that you are putting off under the pretext of finding a compromise, and set a specific deadline for it.
2. Recall the last situation when you agreed for the sake of peace in the family, and honestly describe whether this decision really suited you.
3. Have a conversation with the child or relative in which you consciously state your own position before offering a compromise solution.
4. Notice one time this week when the family avoids discussing an obvious disagreement, and gently bring up the subject.
5. If you're an adult child, write down one concrete step you can take to have a more honest conversation with your parents about a topic you usually keep quiet about.
6. Have a family conversation where everyone can name one thing they really don't agree with in a safe, respectful way.
