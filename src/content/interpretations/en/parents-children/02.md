# 2. The High Priestess — Relationships with Parents and Children

## In Brief

In family relationships, this energy values a quiet, safe atmosphere above all—not constant noise and demands for immediate answers, but a space where everyone can be themselves without fear of judgment. Whether expressed as a parent or a child, this energy thrives where there is trust that not every thought or feeling requires an immediate explanation and where silence is not mistaken for detachment.

## Natural Strength

The natural resource of this energy in the role of a mother or father is the ability to sense what is truly happening with a child, even when the child cannot yet put it into words. It appears in small details: noticing a shift in mood before the child mentions it and waiting patiently until they are ready to share.

A second strength is the ability to create an atmosphere of safe quiet at home, where no one has to constantly prove or explain themselves. Children raised in this atmosphere often carry forward not anxiety about constant evaluation, but trust in their own inner feelings.

In the role of a child, the natural strength of this energy is a subtle intuition about the parents' emotional state, an ability to sense when it is better to remain quiet and when to offer a supportive word, and natural tact even during difficult periods in family life.

## Expression in the Positive

In its positive expression, this energy approaches parenting through quiet presence and respect for the child's inner world. Children are allowed age-appropriate secrets, their own pace for becoming ready to share, and room for dreams and reflections that do not have to be explained to adults immediately. Parents in the positive do not pressure a child to “tell me everything,” but wait until the child is ready.

Communication with children is deep, though not always loud. Difficult subjects are discussed delicately, with respect for the child's emotional pace, rather than forced for the sake of a quick result. The child feels able to come forward with any question and be heard without judgment or haste.

As an adult child in relation to their own parents, the positive expression of this energy can sense their emotional state and support them tactfully without demanding constant explanations or justifications.

Another sign of the positive state is the ability to protect family secrets and whatever a child has shared in confidence, treating this not as leverage for manipulation but as a genuine, reliable foundation of trust within the family.

### Expression in Women in the Positive

As a mother, a woman expressing this energy positively creates an atmosphere of quiet safety and trust at home without turning parenting into constant control or interrogation. She sensitively perceives her children's emotional state and gives them space to be themselves, even when she does not immediately understand everything.

She knows how to support her children through difficult moments without demanding instant explanations, allowing them time to become ready for an open conversation on their own.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

As a father, a man expressing this energy positively pays attention to what remains unspoken—to what a child feels but cannot yet articulate. He creates an atmosphere in which the child is not afraid to sit with him in silence, knowing it will not be interpreted as withdrawal.

Through his own example, he teaches children to trust their intuition, showing that not every decision requires a loud or elaborate justification.

## Expression in the Negative

In its negative expression, quiet parental presence turns into emotional unavailability. The child cannot understand what the parents truly feel because they conceal joy and anxiety with equal care. In such an atmosphere, a child may grow up believing that emotions should be hidden rather than experienced openly.

Another negative expression is the use of silence as punishment. Instead of honestly explaining the reason for displeasure, a mother or father retreats into cold silence, and the child gradually learns to fear unspoken anger more than direct conversation.

For an adult child, the negative appears as difficulty understanding the parents' true feelings because of the emotional reserve they experienced in childhood. The person may keep trying to “solve” their parents or, conversely, give up on emotional closeness with them altogether.

At a deeper negative level, the pattern of emotional distance may be repeated with one's own children: care is present but never expressed openly, so the children feel only its absence rather than the care itself.

## What Can Pull This Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger for parents is a prolonged lack of room for their own quiet and solitude, causing them to compensate for inner exhaustion by becoming even more emotionally closed toward their children.

A second trigger is pressure to be an “open” parent by someone else's standards. This can provoke excessive self-pressure or, conversely, complete withdrawal out of fear of failing to meet expectations.

A third trigger for an adult child is contact with parents at moments when old feelings of mystery or emotional distance from childhood are activated, even if the relationship has objectively changed.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step for parents is to consciously put their feelings into words for their children, at least in a simple form, instead of remaining completely silent about their inner state, even if doing so feels unfamiliar.

The second step is to regularly give the child room to decide when they are ready to share, neither forcing conversation nor using silence as punishment.

The third step for an adult child is to consciously recognize that parental reserve may have been a form of self-protection rather than indifference, without chronically expecting the parents to suddenly become completely open.

The fourth step is to practice direct yet gentle conversations about feelings within the family. If something is troubling you, name it in words instead of leaving others to guess.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in family relationships is to learn to combine a quiet, safe presence with enough openness to give a child or adult relative a sense of genuine emotional connection rather than merely silent care. This applies to both roles at once: to be a parent whose quiet feels like safety rather than detachment, and to be an adult child who learns to understand parental reserve while honoring their own need for sincerity.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I put my feelings into words for children or close relatives, or do I more often remain silent about them?
- To what extent do I let a child decide when to share, without forcing them or punishing them with silence?
- How do I feel when I think about the emotional distance that may have existed in my childhood family?
- Do I still trust the possibility of understanding my parents, or have I given up trying?
- Do I give children—my own, or those I imagine having in the future—room to have secrets without demanding that they tell me everything?
- Do I use silence as punishment rather than as a healthy pause?

## Practical Plan

1. Make a list of moments over the past month when you left your feelings unspoken with children or a close family member.
2. Before raising the next difficult subject with a child, consciously give them time to prepare instead of forcing the conversation.
3. Write a short letter to your parents—you do not have to send it—calmly and honestly describing your attempt to understand their reserve and your own need for greater openness.
4. Once, express a feeling directly to a child or relative instead of remaining silent as usual.
5. Notice one moment when silence is being used as punishment and name the reason for your displeasure in words instead.
6. Create one ritual of quiet shared presence with a child or close family member—with no expectation of conversation, simply being together.
