# 10. Wheel of Fortune — Relationships with Parents and Children

## Brief Essence

In relationships between parents and children, the tenth energy appears through lightness, adaptability, and openness to change, qualities easily confused with inconsistency. A child with this resource often learns early to adjust to new circumstances—moves, new schools, and new rules—and rarely holds on to resentment or remains stuck in disappointment for long. A mother or father with this energy tends to raise a child through an example of cheerfulness and trust in life rather than through a rigid, unchanging routine.

The risk of this energy in a family arises when lightness and love of change become a source of instability that frightens the child. A mother or father who constantly changes plans, rules, or even the family's place of residence without explanation risks raising a child who does not feel safe. An adult raised amid such unpredictability may grow either excessively anxious about any change or just as chaotic in their own parenting.

## Natural Strength

A child with the tenth energy usually adapts easily to new situations: they quickly find common ground in a new group, are unafraid to try something unfamiliar, and recover from setbacks faster than their peers. This child needs less consistency in minor details than others and more of a sense that change can be interesting rather than threatening.

A mother or father with this energy naturally parents through an example of optimism and flexibility: they do not panic at every change in circumstances, show the child that plans can be adjusted without catastrophe, and pass on trust that life itself is not dangerous. This parenting style gives the child a rare gift—the ability not to fear uncertainty.

Another resource of this energy in the family is the ability to restore peace quickly after conflict. Both a parent and a child with this energy rarely hold on to resentment for long, choosing to move forward instead of sustaining tension, which makes the family atmosphere lighter and warmer.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, a mother or father with the tenth energy creates a space where change is perceived as a natural, even interesting part of life rather than a threat, while providing enough stability in basic matters—routine, rules, and a sense of safety—that the child does not confuse flexibility with chaos.

A child with this energy in the positive receives permission from their parents to try new things, make mistakes, and move on without excessive drama, while feeling a clear, dependable foundation at home that gives them the courage to explore the world. They learn to trust life without losing the ability to plan and bring things to completion.

In the positive, a mother or father knows how to combine lightness with consistency: they keep promises to the child even when circumstances change, understanding that this very reliability gives the child the courage to accept change without anxiety.

Adult children with this energy in the positive become the ones who know how to support their parents through difficult periods, reminding them that every crisis passes while offering practical rather than merely emotional help. They know how to be present for aging parents with a lightness that does not minimize the seriousness of care.

Another sign of the positive is the ability to teach a child to accept setbacks without excessive drama: showing them that a mistake or unfavorable turn is part of the path, not a reason to stop.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In the positive, a woman as a mother gives her child a sense that the world is safe and full of possibility without infecting them with her own anxiety about the unknown. As a daughter, she can support her parents through difficult periods with practical help and a lightness that prevents them from becoming trapped in despair.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In the positive, a man as a father passes on to his children the value of flexibility and trust in life while providing a stable, dependable presence they can rely on. As a son, he can support his parents through a difficult turn of fate with practical solutions and optimism that helps the whole family move forward.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, a child with this energy who grew up in an excessively unpredictable family, where plans, rules, or even the place of residence constantly changed without explanation, may develop profound anxiety about any uncertainty or, conversely, absorb chaos as normal and reproduce it in adult life.

A mother or father in the negative may be inconsistent about keeping promises: they easily promise the child something exciting and just as easily forget or cancel it when a new attractive opportunity appears, leaving the child feeling that an adult's word cannot be trusted.

In other cases, the negative appears as a parental rejection of any stable structure. Relying on the belief that “everything will somehow work out,” adults fail to provide the child's basic sense of safety—routine, predictability, and clear boundaries—forcing the child to assume responsibility for their own stability too early.

In relationships between adult children and parents, the negative appears as a repeating cycle of unfulfilled promises and disappointments: one side continually expects changes for the better that never come because consistent action is lacking, while the other gradually stops trusting words and waits only for concrete deeds.

## What Can Shift the Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a chronically unpredictable family environment in which a child never knows what to expect tomorrow. A second trigger is enforced, prolonged immobility or monotony with no opportunity for any change or new experience. A third is an unkept promise in childhood that establishes the pattern “adults' words cannot be trusted,” and this distrust carries into the person's own adult relationships with parents or children.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step for parents is to learn to combine their cherished lightness with conscious consistency: keep promises to the child even when tempted to abandon them for a new idea. It is worth providing the child with a basic structure—a stable routine and clear rules—even if the rest of life remains flexible and dynamic.

The second step for adult children with this energy is to practice speaking honestly with parents about childhood disappointments caused by unfulfilled promises instead of silently carrying that distrust forward. The third step, for both sides, is to agree on concrete, dependable forms of support rather than only general intentions to “help somehow when needed.”

It is also helpful consciously to create shared, stable rituals alongside cherished spontaneity—regular time together that is not canceled for new plans—so a child or parent feels this particular bond can be relied upon even when the rest of life remains dynamic and unpredictable.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in family relationships is to learn to be light and dependable at the same time: allow yourself and your loved ones to enjoy movement and novelty without sacrificing basic consistency and promises kept. This is a path from chaotic spontaneity to mature flexibility that knows how to keep its word.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- As a parent, do I keep my promises to my child even when I am tempted to abandon them casually?
- As a child, do I have experience of unfulfilled parental promises that still affects my trust in people?
- How consistent am I in basic matters despite my natural love of change?
- When did I last provide stability to a loved one precisely when they needed it most?
- Is there a cycle of promises in my family relationships that never become concrete action?
- What would reliability that does not deprive life of lightness and movement mean to me?
- Do I pass on to my children the ability to accept change without anxiety, or do I infect them with my own unpredictability?

## Practical Plan

1. Keep one promise to a loved one this week, even if you are tempted to abandon it casually.
2. If you are an adult with childhood disappointments caused by unfulfilled promises, discuss them with one of your parents or write the memories down for yourself.
3. Create one regular, stable ritual of connection with your child or parents that is not canceled for new plans.
4. Notice one situation in which your spontaneity may have frightened or disappointed a loved one, and consciously add more consistency next time.
5. Offer your child or parents a concrete, dependable form of support instead of a general intention to “help somehow.”
6. Notice when a loved one needs stability more than a new idea, and give them precisely that.
