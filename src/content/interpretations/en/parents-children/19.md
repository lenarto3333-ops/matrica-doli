# 19. The Sun — Relationships with Parents and Children

## Brief Essence

In family relationships, this energy brings natural warmth and joy - a father or mother with this energy usually fills the house with light, easily praises, supports and sees a big, large-scale future in the child. A child with such a resource is usually naturally sincere, cheerful, easily becomes the soul of the company and needs space to shine freely, and not constant reminders to "be more modest." The risk is when this warmth turns into either the narcissism of parents who exhaust themselves, maintaining the facade of an always happy family, hiding real difficulties behind a smile, or an unconscious demand for the child to always be joyful, successful and "convenient" for the parental image, because of which the child learns to hide real, not always sunny feelings.

## Natural Strength

A child with this energy usually has a natural sincerity and cheerfulness - he easily infects others with his joy, attracts the attention of peers and adults, and if this natural brilliance is supported, and not extinguished by requests "not to attract so much attention", it becomes a source of stable inner confidence for a lifetime.

A father or mother with this energy naturally knows how to create a warm and festive atmosphere at home: they sincerely praise the child's achievements, see great potential in him and inspire him to think big — not to be afraid of big dreams, big plans, a big future.

Another resource of this energy in the family is the ability to unite everyone around a shared joy: parents and children with this energy naturally create shared rituals of celebration, gratitude and support, and this warm atmosphere allows the family to live through difficult periods with greater resilience than families accustomed to restraint and formality.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, the father or mother knows how to support the child's natural brightness and joy, without extinguishing it with requests to "be quieter" or "not to show off": they help the child direct this energy in a constructive direction, while at the same time letting him know that it is possible to share and not always sunny feelings.

Communication in the family in a positive way includes a sincere, large-scale vision of the child's future: parents do not limit their dreams to small, "realistic" frameworks, but support big ideas, while helping to plan concrete steps towards them, maintaining a balance between inspiration and practicality.

In the role of an adult child in relation to his own parents, this energy has the advantage of being able to gratefully recognize the warmth that parents gave in childhood, and if warmth was lacking, to find the strength to learn to shine on their own, not blaming their parents, but understanding in what atmosphere of exhaustion or restraint they themselves grew up.

Another sign of a positive state is the ability of the family to rejoice in the success of each of its members without envy and without the need to be the only source of attention: when a joyful event occurs in the family, it is sincerely celebrated together, and not through the prism of who "deserves" recognition more.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In the role of a mother, a woman with this positive energy knows how to sincerely rejoice in the success of her child, inspire her to big dreams, while not exhausting herself by maintaining a constantly joyful image of the family: she allows herself and the child to live and not always sunny days.

She knows how to support the natural brightness of children without comparing them with each other and without demanding that someone be the "sun of the family" at the expense of others: each child gets the space to shine with its own, unique light.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

As a father, a man with this positive energy is able to inspire children with a large-scale, bold vision of the future, while remaining accessible and warm at the same time, and not a distant, idealized figure to whom one must correspond: he shares his own enthusiasm, not imposes it.

He knows how to sincerely admit his own difficulties and not always happy feelings in front of children, showing them that being real is more important than always being cheerful, and thus gives children permission to be honest in their emotions too.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative role of parents, warmth turns into narcissism: parents exhaust themselves by maintaining the facade of an always happy, prosperous family, hide from their children real difficulties or their own fatigue behind a constant smile, and children grow up with the feeling that it is dangerous or unacceptable to show real, not always joyful feelings at home.

Another manifestation of the negative is the unconscious demand for the child to be a constant source of joy and pride for the parents: the child is praised only for successes and "sunny" behavior, and his sadness, fear or failure is met with disappointment or devaluation, and the child learns to hide his true feelings in order to maintain parental affection.

In the role of an adult child, the minus manifests itself as a chronic need to be the center of attention or, on the contrary, a complete suppression of one's own brightness: a person who in childhood had to constantly please his parents or, on the contrary, hide in the shadow of a more "sunny" relative, finds it difficult to find a healthy balance between his own light and attention to others in adult relationships.

In a deeper minus, a family model of despotic leadership is also possible: if one parent was the undisputed "sun of the family", around whose mood all the others had to revolve, children can grow up either too obedient and deprived of their own voice, or reproduce the same model already in their own families.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger for parents is their own fatigue or unlived hardships, which hide behind the facade of a prosperous, always joyful family, until the exhaustion becomes too palpable to ignore. The second trigger is comparing children to each other or to other families, which fuels the need to have the "best", sunniest family to show off. The third trigger for an adult child is situations that remind of a child's demand to always be joyful or, conversely, to remain in the shadows, which activate a disproportionately strong need for attention or, conversely, a desire to hide.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step for parents is to realize whether they are maintaining the facade of an always happy family at the expense of their own exhaustion, and allow themselves and their children to honestly show the full range of emotions at home, not just joy. The second step is to consciously praise children not only for success and "sunny" behavior, but also for honesty in difficult, not always joyful feelings, showing that love does not depend on a constant good mood.

The third step for an adult child is to learn to find a healthy measure of their own brightness, allowing themselves to both shine and be invisible depending on the situation, rather than living in one of the extremes learned in childhood. The fourth step is to practice shared moments of sincere, not ostentatious, joy in the family, where everyone can share both successes and difficulties without fear of spoiling the general mood.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in family relationships is to learn to share warmth and joy sincerely, without exhausting yourself for the sake of a facade and without demanding that one person always be the only sun of the family. True family closeness is built on allowing everyone to shine their own light and honestly experience not only joyful, but also difficult moments together.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Am I not maintaining the image of an always happy family at home at the expense of my own exhaustion or honesty?
- Do I give the child or myself the right not to always be joyful and successful, or do I praise only for "sunny" behavior?
- Am I not reproducing the family model, where one person must be the only center of attention and good mood for everyone?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one way in which you hide your own fatigue or difficulties behind the facade of a happy family and take the first step towards greater honesty.
2. Have a conversation with the child or parents, in which you share not only the good news, but also what is really difficult right now.
3. If you have multiple children, see if you compare them to each other in the categories of "who is sunnier" or more successful, and consciously celebrate the uniqueness of each.
4. If you are an adult child, name one situation where you either excessively sought attention or hid your own light, and think about its childhood roots.
5. Practice for one week praising loved ones for honesty in difficult feelings as sincerely as for achievements and good mood.
6. Create a ritual in the family, where everyone takes turns sharing the good and not so good part of their day, without hasty "encouragement".
