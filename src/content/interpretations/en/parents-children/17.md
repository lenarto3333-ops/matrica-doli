# 17. The Star — Relationships with Parents and Children

## Brief Essence

In family relationships, this energy may manifest through a natural ability to inspire one another and sustain hope. A parent with this energy can usually see possibilities even during difficult times and give their children a sense that the future is worth believing in. A child with this resource may have a bright, noticeable talent or a special inner radiance that needs encouragement and room to develop. The risk appears when this strength turns into a need for parents or children to remain at the center of family attention at any cost, or into disappointment and a fading belief in themselves and their loved ones after receiving too little support or recognition.

## Natural Strength

A child with this energy often has a visible talent that emerges early—in creativity, language, communication, or another form of self-expression. When adults notice and support this gift from childhood, it can become an important foundation for lifelong self-confidence.

A parent with this energy naturally knows how to preserve hope during difficult family periods. When financial, practical, or relationship problems arise, they can find words and actions that help the children understand that the situation is temporary and that there is a future worth working toward.

Another resource of this energy is a sincere interest in one another's talents and dreams. Parents and children with this energy are curious about the inner lives of their loved ones, support even unusual or ambitious aspirations, and create an atmosphere in which it feels safe to speak openly about a dream.

## Expression in the Positive

In its positive expression, a parent can notice and develop a child's talent without placing excessive pressure on them or comparing them with others. They create room for the child to express their abilities freely while also teaching the discipline required to develop a gift beyond a pleasant hobby.

Healthy family communication includes sincere and encouraging conversations about hopes and dreams. Parents do not mock a child's ambitious plans or dismiss them as unrealistic. Instead, they help turn a dream into achievable steps while preserving the sense that believing in something better is both normal and valuable.

For an adult child in relation to their parents, the positive side of this energy supports gratitude for the encouragement their talent received in childhood. If that support was absent, the person can learn to give themselves the recognition they need without becoming trapped in blame, while acknowledging the circumstances in which their parents were raised.

Another sign of a positive state is the family's ability to celebrate one another's success without envy or rivalry. When one family member receives recognition or fulfills a dream, the others experience it as shared joy rather than a threat to their own importance.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

As a mother, a woman expressing this energy positively can be a source of hope for her children during difficult moments. Her belief in a better future is genuine rather than performative, and the children feel that an adult beside them can still see possibilities even when the situation seems discouraging.

She supports her children's creative and personal talents without projecting her unfulfilled ambitions onto them. She helps each child discover a unique path of self-expression instead of trying to achieve through the child what she could not achieve herself.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

As a father, a man expressing this energy positively inspires his children through an example of hope combined with consistent action. He shows them that dreaming and working toward a dream are compatible and form a healthy part of adult life.

He can genuinely celebrate his children's talents and achievements without requiring their success to validate his significance as a parent. He allows them to shine in their own way, even when their definition of success differs from his.

## Expression in the Negative

In its negative expression, a parent's ability to inspire may turn into a need to remain at the center of family attention. Adults may expect admiration and recognition from their children, react jealously to the children's success, or project their own unfulfilled dreams onto them and demand that the child become “special” on their behalf.

Another negative pattern is dismissing or ignoring a child's talent. Parents may overlook natural ability, treat the child's dreams as unserious or unworthy of attention, or repeatedly compare the child unfavorably with others. Over time, the child may learn to hide their inner light because they believe it has little value.

For an adult child, the negative side may appear as a chronic need for recognition. A person whose talent received too little encouragement in childhood may constantly seek confirmation of their importance and find criticism or a lack of attention difficult to tolerate. The opposite extreme is abandoning their dreams entirely because they believe no one will value them.

At a deeper level, a family may develop a pattern of competing for attention and recognition. Children and even adults compete for the role of the “brightest” or “most talented” family member. This tension prevents genuine joy in one another's success and turns natural support into hidden comparison.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger for parents is an unfulfilled dream or a talent that went unrecognized in their own childhood. It may be unconsciously projected onto the child as excessive expectations or a demand for constant achievement. A second trigger is the family habit of comparing siblings with one another or comparing the family with others, teaching a child to link personal worth with winning that comparison. For an adult child, situations reminiscent of insufficient childhood recognition—or of excessive pressure to be “special”—may trigger a disproportionate need for approval or a desire to hide from attention altogether.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step for parents is to consider whether they are projecting their unfulfilled dreams or need for recognition onto the child and to separate their own path of fulfillment from the child's path. The second step is to support each child's talent without comparison, emphasizing that every person's abilities and radiance have value in their own way.

For an adult child, the third step is to distinguish a genuine need for support from a hunger for recognition carried over from childhood. Where possible, it is important to learn to acknowledge yourself rather than relying exclusively on external approval. The fourth step is to speak openly as a family about one another's talents and dreams, creating a space in which people can share them without fear of ridicule or comparison.

It is also useful to notice whether the family atmosphere has turned into a hidden competition for attention and recognition. Deliberately creating moments when everyone sincerely celebrates one family member's success helps replace rivalry with mutual support.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in family relationships is to support one another's inner light sincerely, without comparison or rivalry. Children do not need pressure to be “special”; they need calm confidence that their own talents and dreams deserve attention and support. Genuine family closeness grows from sincere joy in the success of loved ones, not from a struggle to become the brightest person in the family.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I project my unfulfilled dreams or need for recognition onto my child or parents?
- How sincerely does my family celebrate one another's success, and is there hidden competition beneath it?
- Which childhood need for recognition or lack of support still affects my confidence today?
- Can I support a child's talent even when it does not match my expectations?
- How often are children or adults compared with one another in our family?
- Can I celebrate a loved one's success without experiencing it as a threat to my own importance?
- What concrete support does one family member's dream need right now?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one of your unfulfilled dreams and honestly consider whether you expect your child to fulfill it for you.
2. Think of a child or family member's talent or dream that deserves more active support, and take one concrete step to encourage it this week.
3. Talk with your child or parents about one another's talents and dreams without comparisons or judgments.
4. If you are an adult child, identify one situation that triggers a disproportionate need for recognition and consider its roots in childhood.
5. If you are a parent, intentionally acknowledge each child's unique talent without comparing siblings with one another.
6. Celebrate a family member's recent success together, emphasizing that joy for another person does not diminish anyone else's worth.
