# 21. The World — Relationships with Parents and Children

## Brief Essence

In family relationships, this energy brings the ability to see the family as a living whole and at the same time remain open to the wider world beyond - a father or mother with this energy is able to combine different areas of family life into one clear, coherent picture and sincerely rejoice when the child achieves a real, completed result, and not just a formal mark of "done". A child with such a resource usually early seeks to finish what he started, has a natural curiosity about the wide world beyond the family circle - other people, places, ways of life - and needs parents not to limit this curiosity, but to support it. The risk is when this energy turns into either perfectionistic expectations, according to which the child's achievements never seem "perfect enough," or into the defensiveness of parents, who anxiously limit the child to a familiar, safe circle, preventing him from learning about the wider world.

## Natural Strength

A child with this energy usually has a natural need to bring what has been started to a real conclusion - to finish, finish, finish the matter - and if this need is supported, and not interrupted by constant switching to something new, it grows into an adult who knows how to put real, and not fake, points in life.

A father or mother with this energy naturally knows how to combine different parts of family life into one coherent whole - the interests and needs of each child, everyday life, joint plans - and it is this ability to see the family as a living, whole organism, and not a set of separate tasks, that makes family life more stable even in difficult periods.

Another resource of this energy in the family is a sincere openness to the wider world: parents and children with this energy are not afraid of new experiences, travels, getting to know other people and ways of life, and it is this joint openness that often becomes the source of the warmest, richest family memories.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, the father or mother knows how to sincerely rejoice at the real, completed achievements of the child, without devaluing them with the constant "it could be better": they see and appreciate the real result as it is, and thereby give the child the feeling that completing the task makes sense, because it is noticed and recognized.

Communication in the family in the plus includes the ability to combine different voices and needs - of each child, of each parent - into one common, agreed decision, instead of deciding everything according to the principle of strict hierarchy or, on the contrary, leaving everyone in the family alone with their affairs.

In the role of an adult child in relation to his own parents, this energy is able to see the family as a whole, connected story - to accept both the strengths and weaknesses of the parents as parts of one whole - and on this understanding builds a mature, deeper relationship than the attempt to either idealize or completely reject the past would allow.

Another sign of a positive state is joint family openness to the wider world: travel, new acquaintances, other experiences are perceived by the whole family not as a threat to the usual way of life, but as a way to enrich life together, and children grow up with the feeling that the world outside the home is interesting, not dangerous.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In the role of a mother, a woman with this positive energy knows how to sincerely recognize the child's real achievements without demanding unattainable perfection from her, and at the same time supports her natural curiosity for new experiences, new people and places, without trying to keep the child only within the usual, safe circle.

She knows how to unite the family around joint, large-scale plans and trips, finding a way to take into account the interests of everyone, and it is thanks to this ability to see the whole family that family memories in such a family often remain especially warm and connected.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In the role of a father, a man with this energy in a plus is able to complete important tasks and projects shared with children, showing by his own example what a real, and not a fake, completion looks like, and how nice it is to feel proud of what has really been done.

He knows how to support the child's desire to go beyond the usual family circle - new interests, new acquaintances, a different path than the one he himself chose - perceiving this not as a threat to his own authority, but as a natural expansion of the family world.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative role of parents, the sincere recognition of the child's achievements turns into a perfectionist demand: no result seems good enough, any achievement is immediately devalued with the thought "could be better", and the child grows up with the feeling that no matter how much he does, it will never be enough.

Another manifestation of the negative is the defensive closedness of parents to the wider world: out of anxiety for the child or distrust of everything unfamiliar, parents limit him to a familiar, narrow circle - forbid new acquaintances, travel, other experiences - and the child either grows up as closed and wary of everything new, or, on the contrary, rebels against this restriction as soon as such an opportunity arises.

In the role of an adult child, the minus manifests itself as a chronic feeling that the family is a fragmented, disconnected collection of people, and not a whole: the person does not feel a deep connection with parents or siblings, and this disconnection prevents him from finding support in family history even when such support could be.

In a deeper minus, an open conflict of generations is possible due to different views on the world: parents who stick to a narrow, familiar way of life, and children who strive for a wider, different life, find themselves on different sides of the barricade, and instead of finding a way to unite these different visions, the family splits into "own" and "strangers" within itself.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger for parents is their own experience, when their achievements in childhood were also never recognized as good enough, which is unconsciously transferred to children in the form of inflated, unattainable expectations. The second trigger is the moment when the child shows interest in something really new and unfamiliar to the family - a different way of life, other people, a different path - which activates parents' anxiety about losing control or connection. The third trigger for an adult child is the feeling that the family was never truly whole, but only formally connected, which is exacerbated in moments that require real family support.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step for parents is to consciously acknowledge and appreciate a child's real, accomplished achievements for what they are, without automatically adding "could be better," and learning to distinguish helpful developmental support from exhausting perfectionism. The second step is to consciously support the child's natural curiosity about the wider world, even when it causes anxiety, gradually expanding the limits of what is allowed with the child's age and readiness, rather than keeping them invariably narrow out of fear.

The third step for an adult child is to consciously look for the common ground that unites the family despite all the differences and disagreements, and to build a deeper connection on this common ground, and not dwell on what divides. The fourth step is if a conflict has arisen in the family due to different views on the world, to look for a solution that recognizes the value of both positions, and does not force one side to completely abandon its own path for the sake of the other.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in family relationships is to learn to see the family as a living, integral whole, while maintaining openness to the wider world beyond: not to demand unattainable perfection from children or parents, but also not to close the family in a narrow, safe circle out of fear of the new. The true family closeness of this arcana is built on the ability to bring important joint matters to a real conclusion and at the same time remain open to each other and to the ever-expanding world.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I demand unattainable perfection from myself, children or parents instead of sincerely recognizing real achievements?
- Do I - knowingly or not - limit the desire of the child or my own desire to know the wider world due to anxiety or mistrust of the unknown?
- Do I feel my family as a whole, connected whole, or as a disparate group of people united only formally?

## Practical Plan

1. Recall the last real achievement of the child or actually and consciously recognize it as complete and good enough, without adding "it could be better."
2. Identify one area where you limit yourself or your child due to stranger anxiety, and take one small step toward that new experience.
3. Hold a family conversation or joint event, the purpose of which will be to find common ground between the different views and interests of family members, and not to insist on one correct option.
4. If there is a conflict in the family due to different ideas about the "correct" way of life, try to formulate what is valuable in the position of each side.
5. Try one new, unfamiliar experience together with your family - a trip, a new place, a new acquaintance - even a small one.
6. Once a month, together with the family, make a short, warm summary of the tasks and events completed together, consciously noting that it was possible to bring it to the end.
