# 22. The Fool — Relationships with Parents and Children

## Brief Essence

In family relationships, this energy brings lightness, spontaneity and sincerity to the home - a father or mother with this energy does not keep the child in a rigid framework, easily forgives children's mistakes and sets an example of how you can start a new day or a new stage without the burden of yesterday's failures. A child with such a resource usually early shows interest in new experiences, easily makes new acquaintances, loves adventures and surprises, and needs from parents not strict control, but trust and space for their own trials and errors. The risk is when this energy turns into either parental irresponsibility, in which the child is left without the necessary support and consistency, or defensive hyperopia, when parents out of anxiety limit the child's natural curiosity, forbidding him from any risk and new adventure.

## Natural Strength

A child with this energy usually has a natural courage to try new things—a new game, a new acquaintance, a new activity—and if this courage is supported rather than extinguished by excessive anxiety, he grows into an adult who maintains a genuine openness to life instead of dreading each new step.

A father or mother with this energy naturally knows how to maintain lightness in family relationships - not to blow up small conflicts to the scale of a disaster, to be able to laugh heartily with the child and forgive quickly - and it is this atmosphere of lightness that often makes the home a place to which you want to return.

Another resource of this energy in the family is sincerity in communication: parents and children with this energy do not play roles in front of each other, they say what they really feel, and it is this openness that often becomes the basis of trusting, warm relationships, even in difficult periods.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, the father or mother knows how to give the child space for his own trials and errors, without panicking over every small failure: they trust that the child is able to learn from his own experience, and it is this trust that helps the child grow up brave, not timid before new things.

Communication in the family includes sincerity and ease: serious conversations do not turn into difficult, depressing scenes, and conflicts are resolved quickly and without lingering resentment, thanks to which children learn that mistakes do not mean the end of the world, but are a natural part of life.

In the role of an adult child in relation to his own parents, this energy is able to easily let go of old resentments and misunderstandings, building relationships on the present moment, and not on the burden of the past - and it is this ability to start each meeting as if anew that often saves relationships where others would have long been stuck in mutual reproaches.

Another sign of a positive state is joint family openness to new experiences: trips, new acquaintances, unexpected adventures are perceived by the whole family as a source of joy, not anxiety, and children grow up with the feeling that the world is an interesting, not a dangerous place.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In the role of a mother, a woman with this positive energy knows how to give the child enough freedom for his own discoveries, without keeping him under constant control, and at the same time remains that reliable, warm rear to which the child can always return if a new adventure does not work out.

She knows how to maintain ease in relations with her family even in difficult periods, not allowing everyday difficulties or conflicts to turn the home into a place of constant tension, and with her sincerity she shows children that it is safe to be yourself.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In the role of a father, a man with this positive energy knows how to support a child's desire for adventure and new experiences, without extinguishing this natural courage with excessive caution, and at the same time teaches him to be responsible for the consequences of his own decisions, combining freedom with clear, understandable boundaries.

He knows how to easily restore relations with a child after a conflict, without holding a grudge and without demanding long apologies, but quickly returning to warm, trusting communication - and it is this ease that often brings him closer to children than strict consistency.

## Expression in the Negative

In the role of parents, the minus of the adventurous side manifests itself as real irresponsibility: inconsistency in upbringing, promises that are not fulfilled, frequent abrupt changes of plans without explaining to the child, and sometimes a literal absence - physical or emotional - when parents are more engrossed in their own new hobbies than the needs of the child.

The opposite manifestation of the negative is defensive hyperopia: out of concern for the child, parents limit any risk and new adventure, forbid new acquaintances, independent decisions, even small, safe experiments, and the child either grows up anxious and indecisive, or, on the contrary, rebels against these restrictions at the first opportunity.

In the role of an adult child, the negative manifests itself either as one's own irresponsibility in relations with aging parents - avoiding caring for them under the pretext of "new life" - or as a chronic resentment for excessive control in childhood, which prevents the building of a warm, trusting relationship even when the parents are already ready to change.

In a deeper negative, an open generational conflict is also possible: parents, who hold tight control out of fear, and children, who seek freedom and new experiences, find themselves on different sides, and instead of a sincere conversation, the family gets stuck in silent tension or loud quarrels that do not solve anything.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger for parental irresponsibility is one's own fatigue or enthusiasm for a new stage of life, which pushes the needs of the child to the background. The second trigger for hyperacusis is a real or imagined incident in which a child has been injured due to their own carelessness, which permanently leaves parents with heightened anxiety about any risk. A third trigger for an adult child is the moment of having to take responsibility for an aging parent, which activates either an old resentment of control or a fear of losing one's own freedom.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step for parents is to consciously distinguish their own fatigue or desire for new experiences from the child's real needs and find a way to give themselves freedom without sacrificing consistency and presence in the child's life. The second step is to consciously allow the child safe risk and self-discovery, gradually expanding the boundaries of what is allowed as they age, rather than keeping them invariably narrow out of fear.

The third step for the adult child is to consciously let go of old resentments for excessive control or, on the contrary, for the lack of parental presence, and build relationships on the present moment, not on the burden of the past. The fourth step is to find a solution that recognizes both the need for security and the need for new experiences if there is conflict in the family due to different attitudes toward freedom and risk.

It is also useful to regularly remind yourself that real family closeness is not built on strict control and not on the complete absence of boundaries, but on sincerity and trust, which allow everyone in the family to remain themselves.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in family relationships is to learn to combine freedom and trust with consistency and responsibility: not to limit children or parents with excessive control out of fear, but also not to leave the family without the necessary support for the sake of one's own ease. The true family closeness of this arcana is built on sincerity, the ability to easily start anew after misunderstandings, and at the same time on reliability, which gives everyone a sense of security.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I - knowingly or not - limit the child's natural curiosity or my own desire for new experiences due to anxiety?
- Am I sufficiently consistent and present in my relationships with my family, or does lightness turn into irresponsibility?
- Am I able to sincerely and quickly restore relationships after a conflict, or do I hold a grudge longer than necessary?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one area where you are restricting your child or yourself due to risk anxiety and take one small step toward greater freedom.
2. Think of the last promise you didn't keep in your family and find a way to fix it or honestly explain why.
3. Have a conversation with the child or parents in which you sincerely talk about your feelings, without hiding them behind the usual role.
4. If there is an old resentment about control or lack of presence in the family, try to name it out loud in a calm conversation.
5. Try one new, spontaneous experience with your family — without a detailed plan, relying on the moment.
6. Once a month, check whether the balance of freedom and responsibility in the family is preserved, or whether it is leaning towards one of the extremes.
