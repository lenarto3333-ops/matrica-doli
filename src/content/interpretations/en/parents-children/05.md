# 5. The Hierophant — Relationships with Parents and Children

## In Brief

In family relationships, this energy values clear order and transmitted knowledge above all—not chaotic coexistence without rules, but a space with clear guidance, explained reasoning behind decisions, and respect for elders' experience. Whether expressed as a parent or child, it thrives where structure does not become rigid control and knowledge is passed on with love rather than from a position of unquestionable authority.

## Natural Strength

The natural resource of this energy as a parent is the ability to give a child clear points of reference and explain the reasons for rules instead of simply demanding obedience. It appears in small details: patiently explaining “why this way and not another” and consistently maintaining boundaries without changing them according to mood.

A second strength is the ability to pass accumulated experience to children as a gift rather than a burden: discussing personal mistakes and lessons, sharing practical skills, and building family traditions that give a child stability. Children raised in this atmosphere carry forward respect for knowledge and order rather than fear of rules.

In the role of a child, the natural strength of this energy is the ability to learn from parents without blind obedience, take the best from their experience, and ask honest questions where something is unclear. This rare combination of respect and critical thinking supports mature relationships even through difficult periods.

## Expression in the Positive

In its positive expression, this energy teaches through explanation rather than coercion. Children hear the reasons for rules, participate in age-appropriate discussion of family decisions, and receive a clear daily structure without excessive rigidity. Parents welcome questions and the child's search for personal understanding instead of expecting mechanical compliance.

Communication is consistent and meaningful: difficult subjects are explained step by step with respect for the child's level of understanding rather than concealed or simplified into meaninglessness. The child receives not only a rule, but an explanation of why it exists.

As an adult child, this energy can respect parental experience without losing a critical perspective, taking what remains valuable and politely disagreeing with what no longer resonates.

Another positive sign is the ability to pass on family traditions and knowledge so they feel alive rather than frozen. Holiday rituals, family stories, and practical skills are shared warmly instead of from an obligation that “this is how it must be.”

### Expression in Women in the Positive

As a mother, a woman expressing this energy positively creates an atmosphere of clear order and shared knowledge without turning parenting into constant control or instruction. She patiently explains her decisions and encourages curiosity even when it would be easier simply to command.

She is a source of wisdom and support while remaining open to learning something new from her children, offering the example of an adult who never stops learning.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

As a father, a man expressing this energy positively actively passes practical knowledge and life experience to his children through explanation rather than commands. He answers questions thoroughly, showing that understanding reasons matters more than blindly following rules.

He teaches respect for knowledge and order through example, not only instruction, and can admit when he does not know something, demonstrating that learning continues throughout life.

## Expression in the Negative

In its negative expression, structure becomes rigid control: rules are imposed without explanation, and any departure from the established order is treated as a personal challenge to authority. A child learns either to obey blindly or hide personal thoughts and choices from the parents.

Another negative expression is chronic lecturing instead of living dialogue. Rather than listening, a parent immediately moves into instruction and moralizing even when the child only wanted to share an experience, not receive another lesson.

For an adult child, the negative appears as difficulty separating personal beliefs from those imposed through prolonged childhood submission to authority. The person may continue accepting parental views uncritically or rebel against every piece of advice, even when it is sound.

At a deeper level, the pattern of rigid control may be repeated with one's own children or aging parents, with care turning into intrusive instruction that gradually undermines the whole family's trust.

## What Can Pull This Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger for parents is feeling that a child does not respect their accumulated experience and is choosing a path that seems less considered, activating a deep fear of losing authority and control.

A second trigger is pressure to be the “right” role model in every respect, which can provoke excessive certainty out of anxiety about seeming insufficiently competent as a parent.

A third trigger for an adult child is contact with parents at moments when old feelings of pressure and lecturing from childhood are activated, even if the relationship has changed and the parents have become more flexible.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step for parents is to consciously explain reasons for rules and decisions instead of demanding blind obedience, even when this takes longer than saying “because I said so.”

The second step is to practice actively listening to a child without immediately offering advice or instruction, allowing them simply to share what they are experiencing.

The third step for an adult child is to reconsider their image of their parents, recognizing both the valuable experience passed on and their own right to different beliefs, without chronic guilt over disagreement.

The fourth step is to practice flexibility in family traditions: preserve what still has meaning and consciously release what has become an empty formality.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in family relationships is to pass on knowledge and order with love and respect for another person's path, giving a child or adult relative a sense of support rather than pressure. It means being a parent whose experience is valuable because it is shared warmly rather than from authority, and an adult child who takes the best of what was given while preserving a personal voice.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I explain reasons for rules and decisions to children or relatives, or more often demand simple obedience?
- How open am I to a child or younger relative teaching me something new?
- How do I feel when I think about the rules and instructions my parents gave me in childhood?
- Do I still trust my parents' advice, or do I feel a need to reconsider it critically?
- Do I listen to children without immediately shifting into instruction?
- Do I preserve family traditions consciously because they have meaning, or from an obligation that “this is how it is done”?

## Practical Plan

1. Once, fully explain the reason for a decision or rule to a child or close relative instead of saying “because it has to be this way.”
2. Before giving your next piece of advice, consciously ask whether it was requested.
3. Write a short letter to your parents—you do not have to send it—expressing both gratitude for the knowledge passed on and a wish to hold your own view where it differs.
4. Practice actively listening to a child or younger relative once without shifting into instruction; simply let them speak.
5. Review one family tradition and consciously choose either to preserve it with living meaning or release it as a formality.
6. Notice a moment when an old feeling of pressure arises with your parents and consciously pause before reacting.
