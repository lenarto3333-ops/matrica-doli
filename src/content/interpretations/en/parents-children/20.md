# 20. Judgement — Relationships with Parents and Children

## Brief Essence

In family relationships, this energy brings the ability to an honest, fair conclusion and to true reconciliation - a father or mother with this energy knows how to admit their own mistakes to the child, without punishing themselves for them endlessly, and sees the child not as a continuation of their own ambitions or resentment, but as a separate person with his own vocation. A child with such a resource usually early feels injustice - his own or someone else's - more acutely than others, has a keen intuition about what is really going on in the family, and needs honesty from parents more than defensive formulations. The risk is when this energy turns into either an endless cross-generational recrimination that no one dares to end, or a deep-seated resentment against the parents that goes unspoken for years and gradually turns the relationship into a cold, formal distance.

## Natural Strength

A child with this energy usually has a heightened sensitivity to justice and honesty in the family - he notices early when something is being kept quiet, when the words of the parents do not match their actions, and if this sensitivity is supported, and not extinguished with the phrases "it's too early for you to understand it", it becomes the basis of a healthy trust in one's own intuition for a lifetime.

A father or mother with this energy naturally knows how to admit their own mistakes to the child: instead of hiding them for the sake of the image of the "right" father or mother, they are able to honestly say "I did wrong here", and it is this honesty that often brings them closer to the child than any perfect behavior.

Another resource of this energy in the family is the ability to truly reconcile after a conflict: parents and children with this energy are not afraid of difficult conversations and, after a quarrel or insult, are able to honestly sum up what happened and start the next stage of the relationship with a clean slate, and not with accumulated resentment.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, the father or mother knows how to honestly admit their own mistakes to the child, without turning this recognition into either self-flagellation or excuses: they show the child that being wrong is not a shame, but a part of growing up, and thus they give him permission to make mistakes and talk honestly about it.

Communication in the family is a plus, including the ability to resolve conflicts fairly, without biasing someone's fault: parents are not looking for "who is to blame", but together with the child, they are looking for what can be done differently the next time, and this teaches the child to perceive the conflict not as a threat, but as an opportunity to find out something and grow.

In the role of an adult child in relation to his own parents, this energy is able to honestly sum up his childhood - to recognize both the good that the parents gave and what was lacking - without the need to either idealize them or blame them for everything, and thanks to this, he builds a more mature, honest relationship with them than either extreme would allow.

Another sign of a positive state is the family's ability to end conflicts, rather than freeze them: when a quarrel or an insult occurs, it is talked about until the end, and not left to smolder for years, and it is this habit of honestly closing difficult topics that makes family bonds stronger with each difficult moment spent together.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In the role of a mother, a woman with this positive energy knows how to honestly admit her own wrongdoing to her child, without turning the apology into a drama or an excuse for long self-flagellation: she simply admits the mistake, corrects what can be corrected, and moves on with the child.

She knows how to be the first to dare to have a difficult but necessary conversation with an adult child or her own parents, when an unspoken resentment has accumulated in the relationship, and she is not looking for victory or justification in this conversation, but a fair, honest understanding.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

As a father, a man with this positive energy knows how to admit his own mistakes to his children directly, without trying to justify them with authority or the tradition of "father is always right", and shows children that true strength lies in honesty, not in steadfastness.

He knows how to fairly, without excessive severity and without condescension, analyze conflicts between children or his own disputes with an adult child, looking for a solution that recognizes the truth of each side, and not just the formal hierarchy in the family.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative role of parents, the ability to make an honest conclusion turns into a relentless, punitive judgment of the child: the mistakes and failures of the child are not perceived as part of growing up, but as an occasion for harsh judgment, and the child grows up with the feeling that the love of the parents depends on how impeccably he behaves.

Another manifestation of the negative is the endless mutual accusation without ending: the conflict between the parents and the child is not discussed until the end, but freezes in the form of silent resentment or repeated reproaches, and neither of the parties dares to be the first to admit his share and end the dispute.

In the role of an adult child, the minus manifests itself as a deeply hidden resentment against parents, which for years does not receive an honest expression: a person outwardly maintains relations with parents, but internally continues to pronounce a harsh verdict on them for what he experienced in childhood, and this unspoken resentment gradually turns a living connection into a cold, formal distance.

In the deeper negative, an open, protracted family conflict is also possible, sometimes related to property or inheritance: a dispute between parents and adult children or between brothers and sisters turns into a formal dispute, a legal battle, and family ties are sacrificed to the principle of "who is right" or "who belongs."

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger for parents is their own unprocessed resentment against their parents, which is unconsciously transferred to children in the form of excessive strictness or unspoken demands. A second trigger is a family situation involving property, inheritance, or financial support that activates long-standing competition or feelings of injustice between children or generations. The third trigger for an adult child is the moment when you finally have to talk honestly with your parents about the hurt you experienced, and it seems easier to keep silent and maintain a formal, safe distance.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step for parents is to realize whether they are transferring unresolved resentment against their own parents to their child, and to learn to separate their own childhood history and the real situation of the current child. The second step is to practice honest, timely apologies to children instead of accumulating guilt or excuses, showing by your own example that admitting a mistake is strength, not weakness.

The third step for an adult child is to find a quiet moment and honestly, without accusations, but also without diminishing one's own pain, talk to the parents about what has not been said yet, looking not for victory, but for a fair understanding. The fourth step is if a direct conversation is impossible or dangerous, consciously summarize this for yourself - admit the truth of what you experienced, mourn what was missing, and consciously decide not to pass this same burden on to your own children.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in family relationships is to learn to make a fair, merciful verdict for yourself, parents and children: to admit mistakes and wrongs honestly, without embellishment, but also without endless punishment, and to use this honest summary as a door to true reconciliation, and not as an excuse to freeze the relationship forever. True family closeness of this arcana is built on the ability to complete difficult chapters of shared history and begin a new stage with trust, and not from accumulated resentment.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Am I not transferring the unresolved resentment or strictness of my parents to my own child?
- Is there an old, unspoken grudge in my relationship with my parents or children that I avoid speaking outright?
- Am I able to admit my own mistakes in front of my family, without punishing myself for them endlessly?

## Practical Plan

1. Write one situation in which you may be transferring an unresolved grudge or demand from your own childhood onto a child, and think about how to separate the two stories.
2. Have a conversation with the child or parents in which you honestly admit your mistake without excuses and without excessive self-flagellation.
3. If there is an old, unspoken grudge against parents or children, set a specific time when you will try to finally talk it out.
4. If there is a family dispute regarding property or inheritance, contact a mediator or professional before it destroys the relationship for good.
5. Practice for one week to complete every small conflict in the family to the end, and not leave it "hanging" in silent resentment.
6. Write a short letter (not necessarily sent) to the parents or adult child with an honest, compassionate summary of what happened between you and the intention to start a new phase of the relationship.
