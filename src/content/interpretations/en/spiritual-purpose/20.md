# 20. Judgement — Spiritual Purpose

## Brief Essence

The spiritual purpose of the twentieth arcana is to learn to honestly and without excessive severity look back on one's own path, to hear the inner call for renewal and to live in harmony with one's true values. This soul is called to help other people honestly face their own past, as well as carry and heal something important for their kind.

## Natural Strength

The natural spiritual power of this energy is the ability to see the truth clearly, even when others are hiding behind self-deception or denial: one senses where someone, in particular oneself, avoids looking at things honestly.

The second feature is a deep connection with the ancestral roots: this soul feels the power of the ancestors, leans on it in difficult moments and often has developed intuition, clairvoyance, which suggests the right direction where logic is silent.

The third feature is a true ability to regenerate: after difficult periods, losses or crises, this person knows how not only to survive them, but to be truly born again, with a new understanding of himself.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, the spiritual destination is realized as the courage to honestly and fairly evaluate one's own path: a person admits mistakes without being overly harsh on himself, draws real conclusions from the past and moves on, instead of remaining in a sense of guilt.

Another manifestation of the plus is the ability to help others see the truth about their own lives and come to an honest conclusion to old stories, as well as the tangible support of ancestral roots, which gives stability and inner peace even in unstable times.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

A woman's spiritual destiny is often manifested as a subtle intuition and the ability to feel the truth behind words and actions — she helps loved ones to look at themselves honestly, remaining soft and not turning it into condemnation.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

A man's spiritual purpose in a plus often manifests itself as a sober, fair assessment of situations and people around him - he is able to call things by their names, help others finish the old and start new, relying on the perceived strength of their own kind.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, this energy turns into excessive severity towards the person himself: he endlessly turns over his own decisions and actions from the past, judges himself instead of living the conclusion and moving on, and gets stuck in guilt where it is long past time to let go.

The second negative manifestation is directed outward, towards the family: unresolved resentments against parents do not let go of a person, small misunderstandings develop into serious conflicts, and old family patterns of behavior are repeated over and over again, exhausting everyone involved.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The trigger is often the atmosphere in the family, where they were evaluated strictly and without the right to make a mistake - then a person learns that being honest with yourself means harsh judgment, and not a calm look at the truth in the face. The second trigger is an unresolved conflict or an old grudge against parents or relatives, which constantly reminds of itself with new quarrels or the feeling that one has to carry someone else's burden.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is useful to learn to distinguish an honest view of yourself from self-condemnation: to admit a mistake, draw a conclusion and allow yourself to move on without turning over the same story again. The second step is to consciously seek closure in your relationship with your parents or family, even if it's just an internal decision to let go of an old grudge rather than trying to prove a point or win an argument.

## Main Task

The main spiritual task is to learn to honestly and fairly look at one's own path and the people around, to draw real conclusions from the past instead of endless self-condemnation, and to use the perceived power of the family for one's own revival, and not for repeating old battles.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I draw conclusions from the past, or do I mentally return to the same decisions over and over again, judging myself?
- What unresolved resentment do I still hold against my parents or family, and what prevents me from letting it go?
- Am I able to see the truth about the situation without excessive severity — both to myself and to others?

## Practical Plan

1. This week, write down one situation from the past, which I still remember strictly to myself, and consciously formulate a conclusion that allows me to let it go.
2. To notice one old ancestral pattern of behavior that repeats itself in my life and take one step that interrupts it.
3. If there is an unresolved grudge against the parents, write, not necessarily send, an honest letter about what you want to let go of.
