# 21. The World — how to strengthen your energy field

The World's energy field is fed by finishing what they start and a sense of wholeness — and drained when the drive for perfection turns into an inability to call something done.

## Positive

+ **Things carried through to the end.** Finishes what they start, getting real satisfaction from the result.
+ **A sense of the whole.** Sees how separate parts of life fit into one picture, which brings a sense of calm.
+ **Owning the result.** Lets themselves celebrate a completed stage before moving on to the next one.
+ **Integrating experience.** Weaves different life lessons into a coherent understanding of themselves.
+ **Balance across every area.** Gives attention to work, relationships, the body, and the spiritual, without tipping too far one way.
+ **A flexible "good enough."** Calls a result finished even when it isn't a hundred percent perfect.
+ **Celebrating cycles.** Marks the end of important stages with a symbolic gesture or ritual.
+ **A wide-angle view of life.** Sees the long-term meaning in daily actions, not just the immediate task.
+ **Trust in their own path.** Senses that the experience they've been through adds up to something whole, even when it was hard.
+ **Ease of transition.** Once one stage ends, moves openly and calmly into the next.

## Negative

- **Endless refinement.** Can't call something done until it feels "perfectly ready."
- **Fear of incompleteness.** Avoids starting something new until the last thing is polished to absolute perfection.
- **Perfectionism.** Devalues a real result by measuring it against an unreachable ideal.
- **Scattered across areas.** Tries to perfect everything at once — and burns out without a result.
- **Postponed celebration.** Never lets themselves mark an ending, moving straight to the next task instead.
- **Losing the big picture.** Gets so absorbed in details that they lose sight of the overall meaning.
- **Fear of the final full stop.** Drags out the ending of a relationship or a project because the end feels like a loss.
- **Exhaustion from perfecting.** Constantly "fine-tunes" something without ever letting themselves feel it's finished.
- **Comparing to others' results.** Devalues their own achievement upon seeing that someone else "did it better."
- **Stuck between stages.** Can't fully let go of the old but hasn't fully stepped into the new either.

## Recommendations

- Set yourself a clear "good enough" bar before you start working on a task.
- Deliberately mark the end of every meaningful stage before moving on to the next.
- Once a month, check the balance across areas of your life, not just progress in one of them.
