# 16. The Tower — how to strengthen your energy field

The Tower's energy field grows stronger through accepting crisis as a point of renewal and trusting their own resilience — and drains in a constant expectation of catastrophe that won't let them relax even in calm periods.

## Positive

+ **Resilience in crisis.** Adapts quickly when the usual order collapses, instead of getting stuck in panic.
+ **Accepting the unexpected.** Treats sudden change as a chance for renewal, not only as a threat.
+ **Flexible rebuilding.** Puts together a new plan right after the old one falls apart, losing little time.
+ **Trust in their own resources.** Knows from experience they've already survived crises and come out stronger.
+ **Honesty in the face of facts.** Admits the collapse of an illusion or a plan without denial, which speeds up recovery.
+ **Support during a breakdown.** Reaches out for help right away instead of trying to handle everything alone.
+ **Grounding through the body.** Uses breath, movement, and physical contact with the ground to stabilize during crisis.
+ **Relaxing in calm periods.** Lets themselves rest and trust when things are genuinely stable.
+ **Releasing the outdated.** Sees destruction as a chance to shed whatever was only held in place by habit.
+ **Clarity after the shock.** Uses the period after a crisis to reassess values and priorities.

## Negative

- **Chronic expectation of disaster.** Stays braced for a new collapse even during a stable period.
- **Inability to relax.** Holds inner tension "just in case," wearing down the nervous system.
- **A panic reaction.** Loses the ability to act in the moment of crisis due to overwhelming fear.
- **Avoiding risk.** Fears a repeat collapse so much that they refuse any change at all.
- **Isolation in hard times.** Tries to get through a crisis alone instead of reaching out for support.
- **Stuck in trauma.** Can't let go of a past shock, replaying it in their mind again and again.
- **Distrust of stability.** Treats a calm period as "the calm before the storm" instead of a chance to recover.
- **Sharp, destructive reactions.** Forces their own "breakdown" where a gentler, gradual change would have worked.
- **Chronic bodily anxiety.** Constant readiness for a threat shows up as physical tension and insomnia.
- **Avoiding deep connections.** Fears getting attached in case of another loss, staying stuck in emotional isolation.

## Recommendations

- Practice grounding exercises — breath, contact with the body — every day, not just in a moment of crisis.
- Deliberately let yourself relax during calm periods — that's not a reason for new anxiety.
- In a hard moment, reach out for support right away instead of trying to handle it entirely alone.
