# 10. Wheel of Fortune — how to strengthen your energy field

The Wheel of Fortune's energy field is fed by trust in life's natural cycles and flexible adaptation to change — and drained by trying to hold on to control over things that are, by nature, always shifting.

## Positive

+ **Trust in the flow.** Accepts change as a natural part of life rather than a threat.
+ **Flexible adaptation.** Adjusts quickly to new circumstances without wasting energy on resistance.
+ **Sensing the moment.** Catches the right time to act and doesn't hesitate once the opportunity opens.
+ **Accepting downturns.** Treats hard periods as part of a cycle that will inevitably turn.
+ **A variety of experience.** Tries new things instead of clinging to one fixed way of living.
+ **Ease in planning.** Makes plans that leave room for surprises and adjustments.
+ **Gratitude for what's given.** Notices and appreciates lucky coincidences instead of taking them for granted.
+ **A natural rhythm.** Syncs with the cycles of the day, week, and season rather than a rigid schedule.
+ **Calm amid uncertainty.** Doesn't panic when a situation hasn't fully resolved yet.
+ **Openness to new possibilities.** Notices alternative paths when the main one closes.

## Negative

- **Resisting change.** Tries to keep a situation frozen where movement is inevitable, and burns out for nothing.
- **Relying on luck.** Waits for a favorable turn of events instead of acting on their own.
- **Anxiety in the face of uncertainty.** Loses their calm every time a situation slips out of control.
- **Sharp emotional swings.** Mood swings from euphoria to despair depending on outside circumstances.
- **Ignoring the body's cycles.** Lives at a constant, tense pace, ignoring the need for rest between pushes.
- **Stuck in a past cycle.** Clings to what's already over instead of letting go and moving on.
- **Chaotic decisions.** Changes direction too often, never giving anything a real chance to develop.
- **Comparing cycles.** Keeps measuring the current period against "better times," which only deepens dissatisfaction.
- **Loss of grounding.** Without a stable inner anchor, feels completely at the mercy of outside circumstances.
- **Escaping responsibility.** Blames the consequences of their own decisions on "fate" or "bad luck," avoiding any real conclusions.

## Recommendations

- Cultivate one stable inner anchor — a habit, a value, a ritual — that doesn't depend on outside cycles.
- When a situation changes, ask yourself "what does this open up," not just "what does this take away."
- Write down the lucky coincidences you notice, to build trust in the flow for harder periods ahead.
