# 5. The Hierophant — how to strengthen your energy field

The Hierophant's energy field is fed by passing on knowledge and drawing on tried-and-tested experience — and drained by clinging to a "single correct" truth or by constant teaching with no real dialogue.

## Positive

+ **Passing on experience.** Shares knowledge with people who genuinely ask for it — and feels a surge of meaning from doing so.
+ **Ongoing learning.** Regularly studies something new, keeping their mind sharp.
+ **Reliance on the tested.** Draws on structured knowledge and tradition where that gives real stability.
+ **Mentoring without pressure.** Explains complex things in simple words while leaving room for others to draw their own conclusions.
+ **Steady rituals.** Daily practices of reading, learning, and reflection give a sense of ground underfoot.
+ **Valuing others' experience.** Learns even from those who are younger or less experienced, which keeps the mind flexible.
+ **A community of like minds.** Belonging to a group with shared values gives a sense of support.
+ **Structured knowledge.** Organizes what they've learned, which brings order to their inner state.
+ **Patience with the process.** Gives themselves and others time to absorb material instead of rushing the result.
+ **Honesty about mistakes.** Admits when their own knowledge is outdated or was wrong, without losing authority.

## Negative

- **A lecturing tone.** Explains things nobody asked about, provoking resistance whether spoken or silent.
- **Rigid views.** Clings to the "one right way" to do something, ignoring newer approaches.
- **Being judgmental.** Rates people who don't share their knowledge or traditions as somehow lesser.
- **Fatigue from always teaching.** Stays permanently in the role of the one who explains, never allowing themselves to be the student.
- **Fear of admitting not knowing.** Avoids situations where they might look incompetent — which narrows their own growth.
- **Overreliance on authorities.** Doesn't trust their own judgment until they find it confirmed in an outside source.
- **Isolation within their own beliefs.** Only talks to people who think the same way, losing any real exchange of ideas.
- **Information overload.** Takes in too much knowledge with no time to reflect or practice it.
- **Loss of flexibility.** Sticks with tradition even after it stops serving its purpose.
- **Disconnected from lived experience.** Explains theory without living the practice, and loses inner contact with the subject.

## Recommendations

- Once a week, deliberately take the student's seat — learn something from someone younger or less experienced.
- Before explaining something, ask whether your advice is actually wanted right now.
- Allow yourself to admit a mistake or an outdated belief publicly without losing your standing.
