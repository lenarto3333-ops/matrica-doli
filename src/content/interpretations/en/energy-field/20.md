# 20. Judgement — how to strengthen your energy field

Judgement's energy field is fed by an honest look at the road already traveled and the resolve to live according to their own values — and drained by excessive self-judgment over past decisions.

## Positive

+ **Honest reassessment.** Regularly looks back at the road traveled, drawing conclusions without self-flagellation.
+ **Living by their values.** Checks daily decisions against what actually matters rather than others' expectations.
+ **Accepting the past.** Treats old mistakes as part of growth rather than a verdict against themselves.
+ **Responsibility without guilt.** Takes responsibility for consequences without punishing themselves with endless guilt.
+ **Putting insight to use.** Turns conclusions they've reached into concrete change, rather than leaving them as mere reflection.
+ **Sensitivity to an inner call.** Notices when it's time for a new stage and doesn't ignore that signal.
+ **Self-forgiveness.** Lets go of old decisions made with the knowledge and resources they had at the time.
+ **Honesty with others.** Owns their part in past situations without downplaying or exaggerating it.
+ **Clear priorities.** Regularly reviews what actually matters and drops the rest.
+ **Renewal through conclusions.** Turns every experience into a concrete lesson that strengthens them going forward.

## Negative

- **Chronic self-judgment.** Judges themselves for past decisions harsher than they'd judge anyone else.
- **Stuck in re-evaluation.** Keeps sifting through the past without moving toward a new conclusion or action.
- **Fear of repeating a mistake.** Avoids decisive steps out of dread of being "judged" again for the outcome.
- **Comparison to an ideal self.** Constantly measures their current state against who they "should have been."
- **Postponed renewal.** Sees the need for change but puts it off out of fear of a final decision.
- **Overresponsibility for others.** Takes on guilt for situations that were never within their control.
- **Loss of trust in their own decisions.** Keeps doubting whether they did the right thing, even over small things.
- **Public self-criticism.** Airs criticism of themselves in front of others, looking for reassurance or justification.
- **Fatigue from constant reflection.** Gets so absorbed in analyzing the past that they lose touch with the present.
- **Emotional shutdown.** Avoids new experiences, afraid they too will become grounds for harsh self-judgment.

## Recommendations

- After every important realization, set yourself a concrete next step instead of just continuing to analyze.
- Practice the phrase "I did the best I knew how to at the time" instead of harsh self-judgment.
- Once a season, review your own values and priorities, deliberately dropping what no longer resonates.
