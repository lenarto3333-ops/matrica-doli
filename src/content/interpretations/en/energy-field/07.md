# 7. The Chariot — how to strengthen your energy field

The Chariot's energy field is fed by movement, a clear goal, and visible progress — and drains fast in stagnation, long waits, or a fight to control every detail of the road.

## Positive

+ **A clear goal.** Knows exactly which direction they're headed — and that alone brings a surge of energy.
+ **Regular movement.** Physical activity, a change of scenery, travel — all keep their inner drive alive.
+ **Visible progress.** Tracks every stage covered, however small, which keeps motivation fed.
+ **Rising to a crisis.** Knows how to pull together and act decisively exactly when speed is needed.
+ **Healthy competition.** Uses the thrill of competing as fuel rather than a reason for conflict.
+ **Controlled assertiveness.** Pushes hard where it matters and lets go where it doesn't.
+ **A planned route.** Breaks a big goal into concrete steps and moves without chaotic bursts.
+ **Trust in fellow travelers.** Lets others help along the way instead of trying to carry everything alone.
+ **Refueling stops.** Deliberately pauses to recover before pushing on.
+ **A flexible route.** Changes the plan when circumstances call for it, without losing sight of the destination.

## Negative

- **Stagnation with no way out.** A long stretch with no movement or change breeds irritation and inner pressure.
- **Excessive pushiness.** Pressures others for the sake of speed, provoking resistance and conflict.
- **Solo control.** Tries to manage every aspect of the journey alone, draining themselves over small details.
- **Motion for its own sake.** Keeps doing things even after losing direction, pouring energy into nothing.
- **Intolerance of delays.** Reacts sharply to obstacles or other people's slower pace.
- **Ignoring fatigue.** Keeps pushing the pace even when the body is clearly asking to stop.
- **Conflicts over control.** Fights to keep the wheel where sharing the driving would work fine.
- **No reflection.** Moves so fast there's no time to make sense of the road already covered.
- **Frustration at a stop.** Treats a forced pause as a defeat instead of a chance to recover.
- **Chaotic bursts.** Darts between several directions at once, scattering the energy of movement.

## Recommendations

- Break a big goal into concrete weekly steps so progress stays visible at all times.
- Schedule regular recovery pauses before exhaustion hits, not after.
- When you're not traveling alone, deliberately hand off part of the steering to others.
