# 13. Death — Money and Financial Fulfillment

## Short summary

The financial history of this energy is built on the ability to timely close what has already exhausted itself and make room for a new source of income. Where others cling to a job, a business, or a financial habit just because it once worked, this person is able to recognize that that phase is over, and clinging to it only takes away a resource that could be spent on something more vibrant. True financial strength here is born not in accumulation, but in resolutely letting go of the old for the sake of renewal.

The weak point is the same instinct for completion, taken to an extreme: the fear of losing what is already there can make a person hold on to a depleted source of income for years, or, on the contrary, abruptly and recklessly break financial ties, destroying what could still be transformed, instead of a calm, gradual renewal.

## Natural force

The main resource is the ability to soberly see when a financial direction, job or asset has exhausted its potential, and not keep it artificially alive out of nostalgia or fear of uncertainty. It is a rare quality to recognize the death of an old revenue model before it becomes obvious to everyone, and thus be the first to free up a resource for a new one.

The second strength is the courage to start the financial stage of life anew: to change professions, to close a business that no longer brings meaning or profit, to leave a partnership that has outlived itself, and to do it not out of despair, but with a clear understanding that the old one has already fulfilled its role.

The third strength is the ability not to be afraid of the financial void between the end of one stage and the beginning of another: a person endures a period when the old source of income is already closed and a new one has not yet formed, trusting the process of renewal, and not panicking due to temporary uncertainty.

## Manifestation in the plus

On the plus side, this ability to complete turns into a concrete financial advantage: a person closes loss-making areas in time, part with assets that have lost value, and exits doomed projects before they begin to drag him down. It is determination that saves years of wasted investment.

Renewal in the plus becomes a controlled process, and not a chaotic disruption: before closing one financial stage, a person is already preparing the groundwork for the next one - a new qualification, a new contact, a new direction - and the transition takes place as a conscious transformation, not as a leap into the void.

The plus side is also the ability to accept a temporary decrease in income as a natural part of renewal—a period of adjustment in which the old has been let go and the new is still being formed—without a sense of failure or panic, but with the confidence that a new phase always comes after the end.

Another plus sign is the ability to help others go through financial endings: to suggest to a partner or colleague that the direction has exhausted itself, to support the family at the time of closing the family business or changing the source of income, doing it gently, not abruptly.

### Manifestation in women is positive

A woman in the plus knows how to recognize in time that a financial matter in which she has invested for a long time is no longer leading to development, and to close it without feeling guilty for "unrealized investments" in the past. She gives herself the right to start earning in a new way even in adulthood, not considering it a belated step.

She naturally feels the moment when it is necessary to update the financial approach of the family or her own business, and it is this readiness to close the old one in time that often saves the family budget from slow exhaustion due to holding on to something that no longer works.

### Manifestation in the husband is positive

A man in the plus does not hold on to a status or position that has already lost its meaning, just for the sake of the external impression of stability, but is able to decisively close this stage for the sake of a longer financial perspective. He perceives the end of a business or career cycle as a natural part of the journey, not as a personal defeat.

He also knows how to start a new financial direction from a clean slate without dragging with him the burden of old obligations or habits, and it is this readiness for decisive renewal that often opens up opportunities that are not available to those who continue to cling to the past.

## Manifestation in the minus

In the negative, the ability to complete turns into either a paralyzing fear of loss, or a sharp, destructive disruption of everything at once. In the first case, a person clings to a job, business or financial habit for years, which for a long time does not bring either income or meaning, only because he is afraid of the emptiness that will come after the closure of this stage.

In the second case, the minus manifests itself in the opposite way: a person abruptly, without preparation, cuts off financial ties — resigns without a plan, closes a business without transitioning to a new one, breaks off a partnership at the moment of emotional aggravation — and remains in a financial vacuum, which he himself created with a thoughtless decision.

Another manifestation of the negative is the chronic feeling that financial collapse or loss is waiting around every corner: a person lives in constant anxiety about what he already has, and this anxiety prevents him from taking risks even when the risk is justified and necessary for development.

In the deeper negative, there is bitterness about what has been lost: a person gets stuck in regret for a closed business, lost income, a destroyed partnership, and this bitterness prevents him from seeing the resources that have already opened after the end of the old stage.

## What can be negative

The strongest trigger is a sudden, unprepared financial loss in the past that has cemented a deep fear of any change in source of income. The second trigger is the pressure of the situation, which requires immediate closure of the case without time to prepare for the transition, due to which the decision is made in a panic, rather than consciously. The third is a long stay in an environment that is afraid of change and broadcasts the belief that it is better to keep a "tit in the hand", even when this tit has long been dead.

## How to return energy to the plus side

The first step is to learn to distinguish between a business that still has potential and needs renewal, and a business that has truly exhausted itself and is only taking resources: it is useful to honestly assess whether this line of income has brought development over the last year, or is only keeping a person in place. The second step is to prepare financial closures in advance, not at the moment of crisis: form a backup plan, a new source of income or a new qualification even before the old one finally closes.

The third step is to consciously train your tolerance for a brief period of financial uncertainty between the closing of the old and the beginning of the new, reminding yourself that this pause is temporary and a natural part of renewal, not failure. The fourth step is to practice soft, gradual endings instead of abrupt terminations wherever possible: negotiate a transition period rather than burning bridges with a single decision.

It is also useful to regularly review the list of financial affairs that continue "by inertia" and honestly ask yourself whether it is time to give them a dignified conclusion. This text is not financial advice.

## The main task

The main financial task of this energy is to learn to complete exhausted financial stages in a timely and conscious manner, turning the fear of loss into confidence in renewal. The true power of this arcana in money matters is revealed when a person perceives the closing of an old source of income not as a catastrophe, but as a necessary step towards a source that truly corresponds to his current level of development.

## Questions for self-reflection

- What financial matter in my life has exhausted itself long ago, but I still hold on to it out of fear?
- Do I prepare a backup plan before closing an important financial direction, or will I act only at the moment of crisis?
- How much do I trust myself in periods when the old source of income is already closed, and the new one has not yet appeared?

## Practical plan

1. Write down one financial matter that is sustained only by inertia, and honestly estimate how much resource it takes without return.
2. For one old financial habit or trend, make a plan to end it gradually, not abruptly.
3. Start building a backup source of income or a new skill before the old one finally wears itself out.
4. Think of one past financial completion that ultimately led to a better milestone and write down why it was beneficial.
5. During the month, track the moments when the fear of loss prevents you from making a timely financial decision.
6. Conduct a personal "financial audit day" - review all active sources of income and mark which ones need updating or ending.
