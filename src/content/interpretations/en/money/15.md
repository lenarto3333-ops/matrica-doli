# 15. The Devil — Money and Financial Fulfillment

## Brief Essence

The financial history of this energy is built on a rare magnetism: money, favorable offers and influential people themselves are drawn to this person, because he knows how to see the essence of things - the true value of the deal, the true motivation of the partner, the true price that is worth paying or asking. It is a natural talent to attract resources and build material well-being where others only think about the possibilities.

The weak point is the same force of magnetism and desire turned against the person himself: dependence on money, the status or sense of power it gives, a tendency to hold financial control over others or allow others to hold control over oneself, and a deep, almost physical jealousy of another's wealth or success that poisons one's own attitude toward money.

## Natural Strength

The main resource is financial magnetism: a person attracts money, profitable acquaintances and business opportunities almost without apparent effort, because he intuitively radiates confidence and attractiveness, which causes trust and a desire to cooperate with him. It's not always a conscious skill—it's often just a natural charisma that opens doors.

The second strength is the ability to see the essence of a financial situation: a person reads what the negotiating partner really wants, where the weakness of the deal is hidden, which price corresponds to the real value, and which one is only an apparent gloss. This insight allows you to make profitable deals where others focus only on superficial numbers.

The third power is the ability to build material well-being and status: a person naturally understands the mechanisms of influence, power and prestige, knows how to use them for his own growth, and it is this combination of insight and attractiveness that often brings rapid financial growth, inaccessible to more cautious and less charismatic people.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, magnetism translates into honest, sustainable financial success: a person attracts money and opportunities with his natural attractiveness, but builds relationships with clients, partners and employers on the real value he gives, not on manipulation or illusion. This creates a long-term reputation as a person who is profitable and pleasant to deal with.

Insight in the positive becomes a tool for honest, profitable negotiations: one sees what the other party really wants and offers a solution that satisfies both interests, instead of using this knowledge to pressure or manipulate for one-sided gain.

A healthy, free attitude towards material goods also appears in the positive: a person enjoys wealth, status and beautiful things without becoming a hostage to them - he can afford luxury, but he can just as easily refuse it if circumstances or values demand it.

Another positive trait is the ability to help others see the true value of their own resources, ideas or skills: one naturally sees hidden wealth - financial or otherwise - where one does not see it, and shares this vision with sincerity, not for personal gain.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

A woman in the positive uses her natural attractiveness and sense of people as a real financial tool: she confidently negotiates, builds profitable partnerships, attracts clients and opportunities with her charisma, while remaining honest about what she really offers and what her work is worth.

She knows how to enjoy material wealth and beauty without guilt, while not letting possessions, status, or other people's opinion of her success define her self-worth. For her, money is a tool of freedom, not a measure of self-respect.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

A man in the positive turns natural magnetism into a confident business presence: he attracts profitable contracts and influential acquaintances, because people feel in him the strength and understanding of what they want, and willingly engage in cooperation that turns out to be mutually beneficial, and not one-sided.

He knows how to openly strive for status and wealth, without hiding his ambitions, and at the same time does not allow the pursuit of money or power to subordinate his relationships with close people, keeping a clear line between business acumen and personal life.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, financial magnetism turns into addiction: a person becomes a hostage to his own pursuit of money, status or the feeling of power that they give, and cannot stop even when material success has long exceeded real needs, continuing to chase another confirmation of his own value through the numbers on the account.

The second extreme is the use of insight and attractiveness for manipulation: a person consciously or semi-consciously puts pressure on the weak points of partners, clients or relatives in order to obtain more favorable terms, uses someone else's desire or fear for his own financial gain, and gradually builds success on a foundation that destroys the trust of those around him.

The third manifestation of the negative is destructive financial jealousy: a person reacts painfully to someone else's wealth, compares his own wealth with that of others, feels that someone else's success humiliates his own, and this poisonous envy either paralyzes the ability to rejoice in his own achievements, or pushes him to risky, demonstrative expenses in order to prove to himself and others his own weight.

In the deeper negative, financial bondage appears - dependence on loans, debts, gambling or other forms of quick, illusory enrichment, when a person so desires a sense of power and control through money that he loses real control over his own finances.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a long period of financial insecurity or humiliation, which a person tries to compensate for with demonstrative status and hoarding, turning money into proof of his own worth. The second trigger is an environment that openly encourages manipulative tactics in business or negotiations as the "norm of the game," causing the line between honest insight and exploitation to gradually blur. The third is a constant comparison of one's own financial situation with someone else's, which feeds jealousy and prompts impulsive, demonstrative financial decisions instead of measured ones.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to honestly determine how much wealth you really need to feel free and secure, and to separate that number from the endless pursuit of yet another confirmation of status. The second step is to regularly check your own financial decisions and negotiations to see if this benefit is based on the real value I provide or pressure on someone else's weakness.

The third step is to consciously work with financial jealousy, acknowledging it instead of hiding it: someone else's success does not take away a resource from one's own life, and this thought requires practice, not a one-time solution. The fourth step is if there are signs of addiction to debt, gambling, or compulsive spending, seek specific support—a financial counselor, psychologist, or support group—rather than relying on willpower alone.

It is also useful to review once a quarter who exactly benefits from your financial decisions - just you or the people around you - and whether something (habit, debt, someone else's dependence on your money) is keeping you in a position from which you want to get out. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

The main financial task of this energy is to learn to use natural magnetism and insight honestly and consciously: to build wealth and influence on the real value that a person gives to the world, not on manipulation or fear, and at the same time to free oneself from dependence on money, status or other people's recognition as the only source of self-worth. The true power of this arcana in money matters is revealed when abundance serves freedom, and does not become a new master.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Am I chasing status or money as a way to prove my worth?
- Is my financial success based on a real benefit for the other party, or on exploiting its weakness?
- What exactly — debt, habit, someone else's dependence on my money — is keeping me now in a position from which I want to get out?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down a specific amount or level of abundance that gives you a real sense of freedom and compare it to what you are chasing now.
2. Analyze the last major financial deal or negotiation and honestly assess how mutually beneficial it was for both parties.
3. Name one person whose financial success makes you the most jealous, and write down exactly what you want for yourself based on this comparison.
4. If you notice signs of dependence on spending, loans or gambling, take the first concrete step towards support - consultation, conversation, profile group.
5. Conduct an "influence audit" - see where your charisma and insight are being used for honest gain and where they are being used to pressure others.
6. Once a month, consciously allow yourself to enjoy material success without feeling guilty, while checking that this enjoyment does not replace other sources of self-worth.
