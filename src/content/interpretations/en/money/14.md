# 14. Temperance — Money and Financial Fulfillment

## Brief Essence

The financial history of this energy is not built on sudden jerks, but on a patient, gradual movement: money comes where a person knows how to combine different sources of income, different approaches and even different people into one working whole. This is a natural financial diplomat - someone who knows how to come to an agreement where others reach a dead end, and who invests resources evenly, step by step, and not in bursts of excitement.

The weak point is the same moderation taken to the extreme: either the accumulated restraint is eventually broken by impulsive spending or risky investment "over the edge", or, on the contrary, the fear of breaking the balance forces a person to endlessly postpone an important financial decision, sitting "between two chairs" instead of taking a step.

## Natural Strength

The main resource is the ability to combine the incompatible in the financial sphere: mixing several sources of income, combining a conservative and bolder approach to money, bringing together people or ideas that would not work individually, but together give a stable result. This is a rare gift of financial synthesis.

The second strength is natural diplomacy in money matters: a person knows how to agree on terms, negotiate about payment, coordinate the interests of partners so that neither side feels chosen. It is this ability to find a fair middle ground that often results in long-term, stable deals where more aggressive approaches destroy relationships.

The third strength is patience and gradualism: a person does not chase instant wealth, but builds financial well-being step by step, diversifying risks and giving each investment time to mature. This prudent restraint rarely brings a quick effect, but it gives a stable result that does not collapse from the first shock.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, this ability to balance turns into a specific financial advantage: a person knows how to distribute money between several directions - savings, investments, current expenses - without falling into the extremes of either complete accumulation or mindless spending. This approach rarely gives a sudden jump in income, but almost always gives the stability that others build through years of trial and error.

Diplomacy becomes a financial tool: a person successfully negotiates a promotion, negotiates more favorable terms with partners or clients, finds compromise solutions in joint cases that suit all participants and therefore prove to be durable.

The positive expression is also the ability to combine different sources of income into a coherent system — the main job with a side project, conservative savings with careful investments — and it is this ability to mix different things into a harmonious whole that often becomes the main source of this person's financial stability.

Another sign of the positive is the ability to bring peace to the financial conflicts of others: a person naturally acts as a mediator in money disputes in the family or in business, finding a solution that recognizes the interests of each side, and not the victory of one over the other.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

A woman in the positive knows how to gradually, without sudden jumps, build financial independence, combining several sources of income or roles so that they support and do not exhaust each other. She naturally feels when to wait to make a decision and when to act, and this sensitivity to the right pace protects her from reckless financial losses.

She knows how to act as a gentle mediator in family money matters - between husband and children, between different generations - finding a solution that preserves both financial stability and peace in the relationship, instead of insisting on one and only one correct option.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

A man in the positive approaches money without the excitement of extremes: he does not chase after quick enrichment and does not freeze in excessive caution, but gradually builds up a financial base by combining different areas of earnings or investments. This prudence often gives him an advantage where more impulsive competitors burn out or lose their savings.

He naturally knows how to negotiate in such a way that the concluded agreement satisfies both parties, and it is this reputation of a fair, reliable partner that opens him up to long-term business relationships, which are more valuable than a one-time profit.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, this ability to balance breaks into one of two extremes. The first is accumulated financial restraint, which eventually breaks through: a person saves for months or years, restrains the desire to buy something or invest, and then suddenly, under the influence of emotions, makes an impulsive large purchase or a risky investment that erases the entire previous balance.

The second extreme is paralyzing indecision: a person is so afraid of breaking the balance or making the "wrong" financial decision that he endlessly postpones an important step - changing jobs, investing, revising the budget - remaining in a state of constant expectation of a "better moment" that never comes.

Another manifestation of the negative is excessive compromise in money matters: a person is so eager to please everyone in negotiations or in a common cause that he agrees to conditions that are unfavorable to him, just to avoid conflict, and gradually accumulates financial dissatisfaction that he does not dare to voice.

At a deeper negative level, there is a chaotic tossing between frugality and extravagance: a person either sharply limits expenses to asceticism, or just as sharply "indulges" himself in excessive spending, and this swing prevents him from building a truly stable financial foundation.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a long period when a person restrains financial desires or needs for the sake of others, accumulating internal tension, which sooner or later breaks through an impulsive decision. The second trigger is a situation that requires a quick, unambiguous financial choice without time for the usual gradual approach, because of which a person either panics or locks himself in indecision. The third is an environment that constantly pressures with opposite advice about money, forcing a person to be torn between extremes instead of calmly searching for his own middle ground.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to allow yourself regularly, in small portions, what is usually forbidden, so that the tension does not build up to the breaking point: planned, moderate spending is more pleasant and safer than exhausting saving that ends in an impulsive surge. The second step is to set clear deadlines for financial decisions that are constantly postponed, so that the search for balance does not turn into an endless wait.

The third step is to consciously train the ability to say "no" to unfavorable compromises in money matters, remembering that true harmony takes into account one's own interests, not only those of others. The fourth step is to maintain a simple financial structure—distributing income into several categories, regularly reviewing the budget—that provides tangible support for the natural tendency toward gradualism and balance.

It is also useful once a quarter to review financial decisions that have been "stuck" due to indecision, and consciously either bring them to completion or honestly recognize them as closed. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

The main financial task of this energy is to learn to maintain a real, not an apparent balance: to act gradually and measuredly, but in time, not letting restraint turn into stagnation, and flexibility - into endless hesitation. The true power of this arcana in money matters is revealed when a person knows how to combine different sources, approaches and interests into a stable whole, while not being afraid to take a decisive step when it is really needed.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Am I postponing an important financial decision for years under the pretext of "looking for a balance"?
- How often does my financial compromise actually suit me (myself), and not just others?
- Do I accumulate financial restraint to the point where it breaks through impulsive spending?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one financial decision that you have been putting off for several months and set a specific deadline for it.
2. Make a simple plan to divide your income into several categories—savings, current spending, development—so that balance relies on structure rather than self-control.
3. Recall the last impulsive financial action after a long period of restraint and write down exactly what need it was trying to compensate.
4. During the month, allow yourself small, planned "allowable" expenses to relieve the pressure of the accumulated restriction.
5. In the upcoming negotiations about money, consciously state your interest out loud, and do not silently agree to a compromise that does not suit you.
6. Conduct a personal "financial balance audit" - see where you consciously balance and where you actually avoid making decisions.
