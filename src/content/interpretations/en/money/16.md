# 16. The Tower — Money and Financial Fulfillment

## Brief Essence

The financial history of this energy is rarely smooth: it is built through sharp breaks - a sudden job loss, a business collapse, a market crash, an unexpected betrayal by a partner or another financial shock that destroys the usual, seemingly reliable structure of income. But it is precisely in this destruction that real power is hidden — the ability to build a more stable, honestly thought-out financial system after each such blow instead of the illusory one that fell.

The weak point is the same potential for destruction, turned against oneself or against others: impulsive financial decisions in a state of panic, burning business bridges in conflict, chronically waiting for disaster even when everything is stable, or repeating the same financial crisis over and over again if the lesson of the previous collapse has not been learned.

## Natural Strength

The main resource of this energy is resilience hardened by crisis: a person who has already experienced a serious financial blow no longer fears uncertainty in the way that those who have never lost their footing fear. This gives a rare coolness in moments when others panic, and the ability to navigate quickly through the wreckage, looking for a new, more realistic path.

The second force is a keen instinct for false, unsustainable financial structures: one senses when an income, business model, or deal rests on shaky foundations—unhealthy debt, the illusion of stability, someone else's delusion—long before it becomes apparent to others, and can act in anticipation rather than waiting for the inevitable collapse.

The third force is the ability to quickly, decisively rebuild: when the old financial plan no longer works, this energy does not cling to it out of despair, but finds the courage to destroy the ineffective structure completely and start building a new, more accurate one, based not on illusion, but on reality.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, this energy turns financial shocks into the most powerful resource for growth: after each crisis, a person does not just recover, but comes out of it with a stronger, smarter financial strategy than before - because he honestly analyzes what illusion or miscalculation led to the fall, and does not repeat this mistake again.

Insight into unstable structures becomes a tool for early protection: a person knows how to get out of a project, partnership or investment in time, which stands on a shaky foundation, without waiting for the collapse to become inevitable, and is not afraid to tell himself and others the truth that something will not stand the test of time.

A healthy attitude towards financial risk and restructuring also appears as a positive expression: a person is not afraid to radically change the source of income, profession or business model when he sees that the old one no longer serves - perceiving it not as a catastrophe, but as a natural stage of growth that requires courage, not panic.

Another positive sign is the ability to share the acquired resilience with others: a person who himself went through a financial collapse and rebuilt himself becomes a support for those who are now going through their own crisis - not minimizing their pain, but showing by his own example that after a fall, you can indeed build something stronger.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

A woman in the positive turns the experienced financial shock into a source of inner freedom: she no longer clings to the illusion of stability for the sake of a sense of security, but consciously builds income and wealth on real, proven foundations, trusting her own ability to cope if she has to start over.

She knows how to recognize in time when a relationship, job or business partnership is on a shaky foundation, and finds the courage to get out of it before the collapse becomes painful, instead of clinging to the usual out of fear of the unknown.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

A man in profit transforms the experience of a financial blow into the confidence of a person who already knows that he knows how to rebuild from scratch: he is not afraid of risky but balanced decisions about changing course, because the experienced crisis has taught him that the loss of a structure is not the end, but an opportunity to build a more accurate one.

He knows how to close unviable projects or partnerships decisively and without emotional drama, recognizing a miscalculation before it turns into a larger disaster, and uses the experience gained to help junior colleagues or relatives recognize the early signs of financial collapse.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the destructive potential of this energy turns against the financial well-being of the person himself: instead of timely and consciously rebuilding an unstable structure, a person ignores the alarm signals until the crisis explodes in the worst, most painful form - sudden bankruptcy, loss of everything accumulated, catastrophic debt.

The second extreme is financial aggression directed at others: in a state of panic or anger over a loss, a person burns business relationships, breaks up profitable partnerships in a sharp, destructive way, blames others for a crisis that was partly caused by himself, and thus deprives himself of support that could help him recover.

The third manifestation of the negative is chronic financial anxiety and hyperarousal: a person who once experienced a serious blow lives in constant anticipation of the next disaster even in stable periods, makes decisions out of fear, not calculation, and this tension itself provokes new, already optional crises.

In the deeper negative, there is a repetitive scenario of self-destruction: a person finds himself in the same financial disasters again and again—new debts, new ruined businesses, new break-ups of profitable deals—because the lesson of the previous crash was never learned, only repressed.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is long-term ignoring of obvious signs that the financial situation is unsustainable as it is, when one postpones painful but necessary decisions until a crash becomes inevitable and far more devastating than it could be. The second trigger is the experience of betrayal or drastic deception by a business partner or loved one, which leaves a deep distrust and a tendency either to be overly suspicious or to abrupt, burnt bridges in future relationships. The third is accumulated, unspoken financial anxiety from the past, which at the first hint of instability explodes into panicked or aggressive decisions instead of measured steps.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to honestly assess the current financial structure for hidden weaknesses: debts, unstable income streams, deals held on someone else's promise - and do this before the situation itself forces you to face the truth. The second step is, if the crisis has already happened, to give yourself time to admit the loss, without immediately going into panicked actions, and then methodically, step by step, analyze which illusion or miscalculation led to the fall.

The third step is to consciously work with the anxiety or anger left over from the past blow so that it doesn't dictate new financial decisions: therapy, bodywork, or a regular practice that lowers the overall level of hyperarousal can help. The fourth step is to act gradually and thoughtfully, where a necessary but frightening financial course change is imminent, rather than waiting for circumstances to make that choice for you in the most painful way.

It is also useful to review your own financial structure once a quarter to see what is in it based on a real basis, and what is based on the hope that the crisis will bypass me. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

The main financial task of this energy is to learn to deliberately destroy unstable financial structures before they collapse themselves, and to use each experienced blow as material for a more accurate, honest reconstruction, and not as an excuse for panic, aggression or repeating old mistakes. The true power of this arcana in money matters is revealed when the crisis becomes not an enemy, but a teacher who teaches to build stronger.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What part of my current financial situation is based on the hope that the crisis will pass me by, and not on a real stable basis?
- Do I burn business relationships in a state of panic or anger instead of acting in a measured way?
- What lesson from the past financial collapse have I still not fully learned?

## Practical Plan

1. Conduct an honest audit of your current sources of income and debts, identifying those that are shaky.
2. Describe in writing the last major financial crisis in your life and clearly state what illusion or miscalculation led to it.
3. Name one financial decision you've been putting off out of fear, and take the first concrete step toward it this week.
4. If you notice chronic financial anxiety even during stable periods, seek support from a therapist, financial advisor, or someone you trust.
5. Review the list of business contacts that were broken in the past in a state of conflict, and think about whether it is worth and whether it is possible to restore at least one of them.
6. Once a month, consciously check your own financial structure for "cracks", without waiting for them to turn into a collapse.
