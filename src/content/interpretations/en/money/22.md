# 22. The Fool — Money and Financial Fulfillment

## Brief Essence

The source of the financial strength of this energy is a sincere openness to new experiences: a person easily starts his financial life "from a clean slate", without dragging behind him the burden of old failures or outdated earning schemes, and it is this ability to quickly adapt to new circumstances, trust the flow of life and not be afraid of the unknown that opens doors for him that remain unnoticed for others. Money for this energy often comes not through methodical planning, but through ease of ascent, willingness to try something new just now, and sincere, almost childlike trust that fate will lead to the right opportunity at the right time.

The weakness manifests itself in two opposite directions. The first is adventurism and irresponsibility: a person is too easy on serious financial matters, invests money in risky ideas without any calculation, makes commitments without thinking about the consequences, and easily disappears from the field of view as soon as he has to be responsible for the result of his own decision. The second, opposite manifestation is limitation: the fear of new experiences forces a person to close himself in a rigid, defensive framework, to refuse any financial risk even when the chance is really worthwhile, and in the end to lose the very freedom and ease that are the natural source of his financial strength.

## Natural Strength

The main resource of this energy is the ability to start from scratch: where others cannot let go of a failed project, debt or disappointment in a certain way of earning for years, this person is able to leave the past behind and easily take on something completely new, without the internal burden of previous defeats.

The second power is a sincere trust in fate: a deep, almost intuitive feeling that life will bring the right opportunity if you remain open and do not cling too tightly to a single plan, and it is this trust that often gives a person the courage to take a step that others do not dare to take due to excessive calculation.

The third force is opportunism in the best sense of the word: the ability to see unexpected financial opportunities where others see only chaos or chance, and quickly, without undue hesitation, seize the opportunity while it is still open - it is this agility and ease of ascent that often bring this energy financial luck that seems almost inexplicable to outsiders.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a person naturally gravitates to work that does not tolerate rigid frameworks and routine: own business, freelance, project employment, seasonal or traveling earnings, creative or entrepreneurial initiatives that can be started quickly and just as easily changed if something did not work out — and it is this flexibility, and not attachment to one stable place, that becomes a source of income.

The second plus sign is that money comes with tangible ease: without excessive tension, without many years of accumulating fear about each step, but due to the willingness to try, trust in the process and the natural courage to act first when a new opportunity appears.

The third sign is a comfortable attitude to financial risk, when it is really thought out: a person is not afraid to invest in a new idea or change the direction of earning if he feels that the chance is worth considering, and it is this willingness to act, rather than endlessly weighing, that often brings results sooner than more cautious people.

The fourth plus sign is sincerity in financial relationships: a person does not cheat, does not hide true intentions for profit, and it is this openness and honesty that is often the reason why partners and clients trust him more than someone with an impeccable but cold calculation.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

A woman in the plus easily starts a new financial chapter of her life when the previous one has exhausted herself - she changes her profession, starts her own business, moves for a new opportunity - and does it without many years of hesitation, trusting that the new experience will lead her to a place where she will really be good.

She naturally attracts resources and opportunities with her very openness and sincerity: people and circumstances seem to come to her by themselves, when she does not try to control everything in advance, but remains easy-going and ready to take advantage of what life offers.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

A man in the plus knows how to spot and quickly use an unexpected financial opportunity where others take a long time to weigh the risks, and it is this speed of reaction, combined with a sincere trust in his own intuition, that often gives him the advantage of being the first to take advantage of the chance.

He naturally easily lets go of unsuccessful financial ideas, does not hold on to them with stubbornness or pride, but quickly switches to new ones, while maintaining responsibility for commitments already made - and it is this combination of ease and reliability that makes him an attractive business partner.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the side of adventurism turns lightness into real irresponsibility: a person invests money in dubious, ill-considered get-rich-quick schemes, makes impulsive purchases or investments under the influence of the moment, and when the plan does not work, easily disappears from the field of view or shifts the blame to circumstances, instead of honestly admitting the consequences of his own decision.

Another sign of this negative side is the accumulation of debts and open obligations: a person takes on new financial affairs without completing and paying the previous ones, lives today without any plan for tomorrow, and eventually finds himself in the chaos that he himself created by his refusal to take responsibility for the consequences.

The opposite side of the minus is the limitation of fear: a person is so afraid of repeating someone else's or his own past failure that he refuses any financial risk, even a completely calculated and profitable one, clings to a familiar, often modest routine simply for the sake of the illusion of security, and gradually loses the very freedom and ease that is his natural source of financial strength.

In a deeper negative, a person can rush between these two extremes: after a painful experience of reckless risk, he sharply closes himself in a rigid, defensive framework, then, tired of the narrowness, breaks off into an adventure again - and this unstable swing between recklessness and rigidity prevents him from building a stable, trusting attitude towards his own money.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger for the adventurous side is the temptation of a quick, easy result: an offer that promises instant, effortless profit activates the desire to act immediately, bypassing any verification or calculation. A second limiting trigger is a painful experience of a failed risk in the past, one's own or someone else's, which leaves a deep fear of any new financial move. A third trigger is prolonged monotony or excessive external control, which creates a rebellious desire to break into something unpredictable, even if it is financially unwise.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step before committing to a new idea or risky opportunity is to consciously pause and ask myself a simple question: am I willing to bear the consequences of this decision if it doesn't work out, not to extinguish natural courage, but to distinguish deliberate risk from blind adventurism.

The second step, if a tendency to irresponsibility is recognized in oneself, is to take on smaller, more clearly defined obligations and bring each of them to completion before taking on the next one, teaching oneself that freedom and responsibility are not mutually exclusive. The third step, if you recognize that you are limited by fear, is to consciously allow yourself one small, calculated financial risk at a time, reminding yourself that trust in life and prudence can very well exist side by side.

It is also useful to regularly check whether old experience or someone else's fear is holding you in place where it is long past time to take an easy, bold step, and at the same time whether ease is pulling you in a direction where you should stop and think about the consequences. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

The main financial task of this energy is to learn to combine natural freedom and trust in fate with a willingness to be responsible for the consequences of one's own decisions: not to lose the ease of climbing due to fear, but also not to turn this ease into an escape from responsibility. The real financial maturity of this arcana comes when a person knows how to boldly face a new experience and at the same time honestly keep his word where he has already made a commitment.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I take financial decisions too recklessly, without thinking about the consequences if the idea does not work?
- Am I not giving up a really worthwhile opportunity just because of fear of the new and unknown?
- Am I able to trust the flow of life in financial matters, while remaining responsible for my own decisions?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one financial matter that you started, but never completed or responded to, and take one concrete step toward closing it.
2. Before the next financial decision, ask yourself whether you are ready to be responsible for its consequences, and only then act.
3. Identify one financial opportunity that you are rejecting only out of fear, and take the time to honestly, without emotion, assess its real risks and benefits.
4. If you have outstanding debts or obligations, make a simple, concrete plan to gradually close them.
5. Allow yourself one small, deliberately calculated financial risk in the near future and monitor your own reaction to it.
6. Once a month, check whether your financial situation is moving in the direction of chaotic adventure or, conversely, in the direction of fear-bound stagnation, and consciously seek a balance between the two.
