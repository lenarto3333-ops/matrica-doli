# 8. Justice — Money and Financial Fulfillment

## In Brief

The financial story of the eighth energy is about balance between what a person gives and what they receive in return. Money is closely tied to a sense of fairness: fair pay for work, honored agreements, and a transparent division of contributions and benefits. When the exchange is honest on both sides, financial stability grows stronger. When injustice—real or merely perceived—creeps in, money stops being simply a resource and becomes a courtroom where everyone continually weighs who owes what to whom.

## Natural Strength

This energy has a keen sense of what work, time, and expertise—both one's own and others'—are truly worth. A person with this resource finds it difficult to accept being underpaid, yet also feels uncomfortable knowingly overpaying or receiving more than they have earned. It is a natural calculator of fair exchange.

This financial mindset thrives wherever objectivity and precision matter: accounting, auditing, law, insurance, brokering agreements, dividing an inheritance or jointly owned property, and compensation consulting. Such a person can view a financial situation from the outside without emotional bias, which is precisely why others trust them to resolve contentious money matters.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, a person builds financial relationships on clear, explicitly stated agreements: what costs how much and when, and who is responsible for what. They keep their word regarding payment and deadlines and expect the same from others—calmly, without drama, simply naming when an agreement has been broken.

They can set a fair price for their work based on the real value they create, rather than fear of appearing greedy or, conversely, a need to prove their generosity. Whether personal, household, or professional, their accounting is transparent: they can always explain where a sum came from and where it went.

In shared finances—with a partner, family, or business team—a person in the positive seeks a solution fair to everyone, not merely advantageous to themselves. They can acknowledge what they owe as readily as they request repayment of what is owed to them, without guilt in either case.

### Expression in Women in the Positive

A woman in the positive can state her financial worth directly, without diminishing it or feeling compelled to justify her wish to be paid fairly. She approaches financial agreements—in a couple, family, or business—as an equal dialogue, not as something too uncomfortable to discuss openly.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

A man in the positive treats financial obligations as a matter of personal honor, not merely practical necessity. He can honestly acknowledge a financial mistake or debt without turning it into a blow to his self-esteem, and can just as calmly demand fair treatment for himself.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, financial relationships become a running ledger of grievances: who still owes whom and how much, who underpaid, and who failed to value the work invested. A person may remember one unfair financial situation for years and revisit it mentally even after the outward conflict was settled long ago.

Another negative expression is deliberately undervaluing oneself to prove that money is not what matters most—or, at the opposite extreme, imposing rigid, almost forensic control over every cent of a shared budget, exhausting the relationship more than any actual lack of money would.

In the negative, it is difficult to accept financial help or an advantageous offer without inwardly asking, “What will I owe in return?” It is equally difficult to feel genuine happiness for someone else's financial success when it seems unfairly easy compared with one's own path.

## What Can Shift the Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a real experience of financial injustice: being underpaid, deceived in a deal, facing a broken agreement, or seeing someone's work or integrity go unrecognized. A second trigger is an environment without clear and consistent financial rules, where no one knows who is responsible for what or what anything costs. A third is watching someone “get everything dishonestly” and go unpunished, provoking outrage far out of proportion to the immediate situation.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to separate genuine financial injustice that calls for a concrete action or conversation from an old grievance that needs closure, not another reckoning. The second is to communicate financial expectations in advance—regularly, calmly, and without accusation—instead of silently waiting for the other party to guess them.

Keep transparent but not obsessive records: a simple system that offers clarity is enough, without weighing every dollar every day. Practice setting a fair price for your work based on its real value rather than fear or guilt, checking against the market or consulting an independent professional when needed. This text is not financial or legal advice.

## Main Task

Learn to be an honest witness to your financial life rather than a judge of yourself and others: recognize real injustice without turning it into a lifelong debt, and allow the exchange of money to remain alive rather than constantly balancing it to the last cent.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there an old financial grievance I still mentally “keep on the books,” even though it was outwardly resolved long ago?
- Do I set a fair price for my work, or do I underprice or overprice it for emotional reasons?
- How transparent are my financial agreements with those close to me—is everything stated, or is some of it merely implied?
- Can I feel genuine happiness for someone else's financial success, even if it came more easily than my own?
- Where has concern for fairness in my financial life turned into constant weighing and tallying?
- Can I acknowledge my own financial debt or mistake without excessive shame?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one financial grievance or unsettled account that still influences your decisions, then decide what concrete action or inner release is needed to bring it to a close.
2. Check whether your current compensation reflects the real value you create, and adjust the figure if necessary.
3. State one financial agreement that has so far rested only on an unspoken understanding, clearly and directly.
4. Create a simple system for tracking shared expenses that provides clarity without daily monitoring.
5. Notice one moment this week when you compare your financial outcome with someone else's, and consciously release the comparison.
6. Practice saying, “That was unfair, and I am closing this chapter,” about one old financial situation that no longer needs to continue.
