# 21. The World — Money and Financial Fulfillment

## Brief Essence

The source of the financial power of this energy is the ability to see financial life as a whole, and not as separate, unrelated episodes: a person naturally knows how to bring together different sources of income, projects, assets or even several family budgets into one coherent system and bring the matter to a real, tangible conclusion, and not to another "almost done". The second feature is the scale of thinking: this energy is not satisfied with a local, small result, but gravitates to large, sometimes international projects, travels for the cause, partnerships that unite people and resources from different environments, and it is on this scale that a person finds true financial satisfaction.

The weakness manifests itself in two opposite directions. The first is perfectionist paralysis: it is difficult for a person to put an end to a financial project until it seems to him perfectly perfect, and because of this, a profitable deal, a ready-made business plan or an almost completed project remains "on the final touch" for years, without bringing any real income. The second, opposite manifestation is narrow-mindedness and defensive hostility to the new: a person closes himself off from unfamiliar financial approaches, foreign partners or markets, clings to the old simply because it is familiar, and loses large-scale opportunities that he himself would have been able to see first.

## Natural Strength

The main resource of this energy is the talent of unification: a person knows how to assemble disparate financial elements - several sources of income, various assets, the interests of several parties to the transaction - into one coherent, working system where others see only a chaotic collection of unrelated parts, and it is this ability to see the whole before the details that makes him a valuable architect of large financial solutions.

The second force is the natural scale of thinking: from the beginning, this energy is tuned not to a small, local result, but to projects that go beyond the usual circle - an international partnership, a business that unites several cities or countries, trips that bring both new contacts and new income - and it is on this scale that a person feels in his place.

The third power is the ability to bring financial matters to a real conclusion: where others stop halfway or disperse to new ideas without closing the previous one, this energy has the rare endurance to stay with the project until the moment when the result becomes real, tangible and recognized, and not just planned.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a person naturally gravitates to work that requires a vision of the big picture and unification of disparate parts: managing large-scale projects, international business, coordinating several sources of income or assets into a single strategy, consulting, which brings together the interests of different parties to a deal — a person earns precisely because he is able to build one working whole from the chaos of individual elements.

The second plus sign is income related to scale and travel: international trade, projects that require relocation or regular travel, partnerships with people from other cities, countries or cultures - a person is not afraid to go beyond the usual geographical or professional circle for a greater result.

The third sign is the ability to really complete financial affairs: the agreement is signed, the project is handed over, the product is launched not at some abstractly ideal moment, but when it is really ready to bring results, and a person knows how to feel and recognize this moment, instead of endlessly polishing the details.

The fourth plus sign is a peace-making, unifying role in financial matters: a person knows how to bring parties with different, sometimes conflicting interests to the same table - investors, partners, family members - and find a solution that is beneficial for everyone, instead of allowing the dispute to split the common cause.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

An advantaged woman is able to bring together different parts of her financial life - several sources of income, family and personal budget, various projects - into one clear, manageable system, and thanks to this integrity, she feels financial security even when individual components seem unstable.

She is easily open to large-scale, sometimes international opportunities — studies, partnerships, work that involves travel or communication with people from other countries and cultures — and it is this openness that often opens financial doors for her that are inaccessible to those who stick only to the usual, local circle.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

A man in the plus naturally takes on the role of one who unites the disparate financial interests of several people or parties into one common, working project, and it is this ability to see how everyone's interests can be combined into a whole that makes him a valuable partner and leader in great affairs.

He knows how to feel the moment when the project is really ready to launch or the deal is ready to be signed, and he has the courage to put an end to it right then, and not delay completion in pursuit of unattainable perfection, thanks to which he brings many more things to a real, tangible result.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the perfectionist side of this energy turns into a real financial paralysis: a project, product or deal remains "almost ready" for years, because a person always seems to lack one more touch, one more check, one more improvement, and while this endless grinding continues, real income never appears.

The opposite manifestation of the negative is narrow-mindedness and defensive closed-mindedness: a person refuses to consider new financial approaches, unfamiliar markets, foreign partners or even simply other than usual ways of earning, treats everything new with distrust or outright hostility, and because of this closed-mindedness loses large-scale opportunities that he himself was able to see first.

Another negative sign is dispersion without completion: a person takes on too many large-scale projects at the same time, is fascinated by the idea of ​​​​a large, global result, but none of them leads to a real end, and in the end, instead of one tangible achievement, several unclosed, energy-consuming beginnings accumulate.

In a deeper minus, chronic devaluation of the already achieved result is also possible: even a really completed and successful project does not bring a sense of satisfaction, because a person immediately looks for what is "not ideal enough" in it, and this constant dissatisfaction gradually undermines both motivation and confidence in one's own financial abilities.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is when a financial project or deal is nearing completion and you need to finally put an end to it, not add another improvement. The second trigger is contact with an unfamiliar financial environment, market, or partner from a different culture, which activates defensive mistrust instead of natural curiosity. The third trigger is the need to simultaneously keep in the field of attention too many disparate financial projects, which creates a sense of chaos and pushes either to paralysis or to abandon the scale altogether.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to clearly define concrete, measurable criteria for its completion in advance, even before the start of the project: what exactly means "ready" and "good enough", so as not to look for infinite perfection where the result has already been achieved.

The second step, if you recognize perfectionist paralysis in yourself, is to consciously set a date for the launch or closing of the deal and stick to it even when you are tempted to postpone for "one more improvement". The third step, if narrowness and defensive closedness is recognized in oneself, is to consciously open oneself to one new financial opportunity at a time: an unfamiliar market, a different approach, a partner with a different experience, and give yourself time to get used to it, and not reject it immediately.

It is also useful to regularly celebrate actually completed financial milestones, even small ones, consciously capturing the moment "it's done" and not immediately jumping with thoughts to the next, not yet perfected goal. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

The main financial task of this energy is to learn to distinguish true completion from endless improvement and to keep the natural drive to scale and unification open, not defensive: not to close yourself off from a new world of possibilities due to fear of the foreign or unfamiliar, but also not to drown in perfectionism, which prevents you from recognizing the already achieved result. The real financial maturity of this arcana comes when a person is able to gather the disparate parts of his financial life into a single whole and confidently say to himself: it is truly complete.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I have a financial project that has been "almost ready" for a long time, but I'm still looking for flaws in it instead of completing and launching it?
- Am I closing myself off from new financial opportunities, partners or markets just because they are unfamiliar?
- Do I really know how to recognize and celebrate a financial result when it is achieved, or do I immediately look for the next, yet unattainable goal?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one financial project or deal that has long been "almost done" and identify three specific criteria when you will complete it.
2. Set a specific completion or launch date for one of your open projects and stick to it, even if the result seems imperfect.
3. Consider one unfamiliar financial opportunity, market, or approach that you've previously dismissed with disbelief, and take the time to learn more about it.
4. List all your current sources of income or financial projects and determine whether they fit together or are scattered without a common strategy.
5. Celebrate one recent financial achievement that has actually been completed—with a concrete action, not just a thought.
6. Once a quarter, review whether perfectionism is keeping you in a place where it is long past time to move on with the result that is already there.
