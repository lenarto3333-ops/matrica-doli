# 20. Judgement — Money and Financial Fulfillment

## Brief Essence

The source of the financial power of this energy is the ability to honestly look back at one's own financial path and draw real conclusions from it: a person knows how to recognize which decisions worked and which were costly, and use this knowledge to not repeat the same mistake again, instead of either justifying oneself or endlessly reprimanding oneself. The second feature is a tangible connection with the resourcefulness of one's own lineage: the intuitive knowledge that behind one's shoulders is the experience of generations who knew how to survive, recover and rebuild after losses, and this sense of ancestral support adds confidence just when one needs to start the financial phase from a clean slate.

The weakness is manifested in two directions. The first is judgment directed inward: a person so severely reevaluates his own past financial decisions that instead of one honest conclusion he receives an endless internal judgment, reliving a long-closed deal, failed investment or debt over and over again, and this constant self-criticism takes away resources that could be spent on real actions. The second is judgment directed outwards toward the family: unresolved resentments against parents through money, inheritance, or financial support that have not received an honest explanation or apology gradually develop into open conflicts and sometimes into actual lawsuits between relatives, and the person wears himself out by carrying the financial script inherited from his family instead of consciously rewriting it.

## Natural Strength

The main resource of this energy is the ability to make an honest financial conclusion: a person knows how to stop, look at the path traveled without embellishments and without self-flagellation, see where exactly the decisions were wrong, and where they were justified despite the result, and it is this sobriety that allows him to make better decisions in the future than those who avoid looking back.

The second power is access to family financial stability: even if a person's personal experience has been difficult, he feels behind him the experience of ancestors who knew how to recover from losses, build anew, survive in difficult times, and this feeling of family support often gives strength to start a new financial stage where others would have given up.

The third power is a sharp intuition in financial matters, almost clairvoyance: a person is able to feel when a deal is not fair, when a partner is hiding something important, when the moment has come to close one financial chapter and open another, and this ability to recognize the end of the cycle in time protects him from clinging to outdated, no longer working financial strategies.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a person is capable of a fair, but not cruel, judgment on his own financial past: he admits mistakes without self-destruction, draws a concrete, applicable conclusion from them and moves on, without carrying the burden of guilt years after the decision has already been made and the consequences have been experienced.

The second plus sign is work in which the ability to draw honest conclusions and see the hidden is valued: financial consulting, auditing, business restructuring, support of inheritance cases, mediation in property disputes, management of family or ancestral capital, a person earns by being able to unbiasedly analyze a complicated financial situation and offer a fair solution.

The third sign is the courage to close old financial chapters and start anew: a person does not cling to a loss-making business or an outdated strategy just because years have already been invested in it, but finds the strength to recognize the end of the cycle and consciously revive financial life in a new, more honest form.

The fourth plus sign is maturity in family financial matters: a person is able to settle long-standing disputes about inheritance, debts or financial support of the family fairly and humanely, without the need to either silence the resentment forever or bring the matter to an open conflict and a lawsuit.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

A woman in her advantage knows how to honestly and without being overly harsh on herself sum up her own financial decisions - admit where she acted out of fear or other people's expectations, and where she really acted from her own values ​​- and uses this conclusion to continue accepting money and offers that really correspond to her inner guidelines.

She is able to raise a difficult but necessary conversation about family money or inheritance fairly and calmly, without hiding an old grudge in silence and without bringing the matter to the point of breaking up, but looking for a solution that is fair to all parties of the family.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

A man with an advantage knows how to soberly assess his own financial path, in particular those decisions that were dictated by pride or a desire to prove something to the family rather than a real strategy, and brings much more honesty with himself into subsequent decisions.

He is able to close a long-standing financial conflict with family or partners for justice, not for victory: he looks for a solution that recognizes the contribution and losses of each side, and thanks to this, he preserves the relationship where others would bring the matter to a break or a lawsuit.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, judgment turned inward turns into exhausting self-punishment: a person returns to the same financial mistake, unsuccessful investment or lost opportunity for years, checking again and again "what if", and this endless internal reassessment does not give any new conclusion, only takes away the energy needed for real action today.

The opposite manifestation of the negative is a court directed at the family: unresolved grievances against parents due to money or inheritance are not spoken out directly, but accumulate for years until they spill into an open conflict, the division of property turns into a protracted legal process, and relations between relatives are destroyed for the sake of the principle of "who is right."

Another negative sign is exhaustion from inherited financial scenarios: a person unconsciously repeats ancestral patterns of dealing with money - constant anxiety, distrust of stability, the habit of living in survival mode - even when his own circumstances have long allowed him to live differently, because no one in the family has consciously "closed" this scenario.

At a deeper disadvantage, it is possible to avoid an honest conclusion altogether: a person postpones the analysis of financial affairs, debts or agreements indefinitely, because he is afraid of what he will see, and this delayed honesty over time only increases the scale of the problem that will eventually have to be solved.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is the need to take stock of a difficult, ambiguous financial period, when it seems easier to either blame yourself for everything or avoid an honest look altogether. A second trigger is a family situation involving inheritance, debt, or financial support that activates long-standing, unspoken resentments toward parents or other relatives. A third trigger is long-term avoidance of an honest conversation about money in the family, which allows tension to build up until the conflict becomes too great to be resolved amicably.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to consciously conduct an honest, but not punitive, summary of past financial decisions: write out what worked and what did not, without emotional epithets like "I screwed it up", but only with facts and one concrete conclusion for the future.

The second step, if you recognize yourself as a constant self-punishment, is to assign a single "trial" for each financial mistake: honestly analyze it, draw a conclusion, and consciously close the topic, returning to it mentally only if new important information appears. The third step, if you recognize an unresolved conflict with your family, is to choose a quiet moment and honestly talk about an old financial grudge directly with the person it affects, looking for a just solution, not victory or punishment.

It is also useful to consciously track what financial habits or fears a person has inherited from the family, and decide separately for each one whether it is worth carrying on or can be left in the past. This text is not financial advice.

## Main Task

The main financial task of this energy is to learn to pass a fair, merciful verdict on yourself and your family regarding past financial decisions: not to justify everything in a row, but also not to judge endlessly, but to turn an honest conclusion into a real point of revival. The true financial maturity of this arcana comes when a person closes the old financial chapters with dignity and begins a new phase, relying on the strength of the family, and not on its unresolved wounds.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Am I not punishing myself endlessly for financial decisions that should have been honestly summed up and let go a long time ago?
- Is there an unresolved financial grudge or dispute in my family that I avoid talking about directly?
- What financial habits or fears have I inherited from my family and do I really want to carry them on?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down the three most important financial decisions of your past—successful and unsuccessful—and formulate one specific, actionable conclusion for each without self-incrimination.
2. Identify one financial topic in the family that still causes tension or resentment, and set a specific time when you will discuss it.
3. Look for one money habit you may have inherited from your parents or family and decide consciously if you want to keep it.
4. If there is an unresolved financial issue with inheritance or property, seek professional advice before emotions turn into a protracted conflict.
5. Once a month, conduct a short "honest summary" of your own financial decisions during this period - no punitive epithets, only facts and one conclusion.
6. Choose one outdated financial project or strategy to which it is long time to say goodbye, and consciously close this section, freeing up resources for a new one.
