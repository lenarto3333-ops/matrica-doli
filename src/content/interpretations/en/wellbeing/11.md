# 11. Strength — the energy of financial wellbeing

At its best, the eleventh energy lifts other money channels through strength, endurance, and a leadership stance; at its worst, this is exactly where the main block hides — an illusion of being busy, and a fear of taking responsibility for a big result.

## Positive

+ **Career climbing.** Steady, consistent advancement up the career ladder.
+ **A career breakthrough.** A noticeable leap in position, income, or recognition.
+ **A productive workload.** A heavy load that genuinely converts into a substantial result.
+ **Full commitment.** One hundred percent engagement in what they do.
+ **A leadership stance.** Strength directed at leading, not at carrying out someone else's orders.
+ **Motivation toward big results.** Energy aimed at large-scale goals, not small ones.
+ **Rational use of strength.** Knows how to allocate their energy rather than spend it aimlessly.
+ **Working for their own good.** Income that genuinely serves their own interests.
+ **Working for the good of others.** Strength invested in something that also benefits the people around them.
+ **Delegating authority.** The ability to hand off part of the workload to others instead of carrying it all alone.

## Negative

- **An illusion of being busy.** A lot of bustle that never turns into a real result.
- **Working at half strength.** Energy deliberately held back, afraid to go all in.
- **Fear of risk.** Avoids bold moves that could bring a breakthrough.
- **Fear of responsibility.** Unwilling to take on a leadership role or major decisions.
- **Laziness, and no strength left to overcome it.** An exhaustion so deep it prevents even starting to move.
- **Money slipping through their fingers.** What's earned gets spent quickly, leaving no reserve.
- **"The more I work, the more money I make."** A mindset that ties income only to hours of exhaustion.
- **Depression after workaholism.** A state where, after a grueling push, there's neither the strength nor the desire left to work.
- **Controlling and pressuring people.** Trying to manage others through force instead of trust.
- **Demandingness and aggression.** A sharpness in communication that drives away partners and clients.
- **Destructive activity.** Channels powerful energy into destruction or pressure — on themselves or others — instead of putting it into creation.

## Recommendations

- Direct part of your strength toward helping those weaker than you — it gives strength its meaning back.
- Give your body regular physical exercise, so tension doesn't build up and hit your finances.
- Plan rest in advance, rather than waiting until your strength runs out completely.
