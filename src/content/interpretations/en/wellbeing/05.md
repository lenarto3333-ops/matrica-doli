# 5. The Hierophant — the energy of financial wellbeing

At its best, this energy turns knowledge and experience into stable income through teaching and mentorship. At its worst, stagnation, fear of competition, and self-doubt about their own competence block the passing on of value to others.

## Positive

+ **Polished professional skills.** Deep expertise, honed to mastery over years.
+ **Work as a personal mission.** Earns from what they genuinely believe in, which amplifies the return.
+ **A gift for persuasion.** Conveys the value of their knowledge and services so well that people are willing to pay.
+ **A clear structure and plan.** Builds income through a consistent system rather than scattered attempts.
+ **Sharing knowledge through personal experience.** Teaches by filtering information through their own perspective, which makes it valuable.
+ **Confidence in themselves and their knowledge.** Never doubts their own competence when naming a price.
+ **Ease in passing on knowledge.** Shares experience without fear that a student or client will "outgrow" them.
+ **Respect for everyone they talk to.** Acknowledges they can still learn something new too, which opens up new sources of income.
+ **A mentor's position.** Earns by passing on experience to others, not just doing the technical work.
+ **A reputation as an expert.** Income grows alongside recognition within their professional circle.

## Negative

- **Ignoring feedback.** Doesn't accept feedback, which slows down professional and financial growth.
- **Refusing to upskill.** Falls behind in their field, losing their competitive edge.
- **Superficial work.** Takes on everything "just to get it done fast," and quality — along with income — suffers.
- **Office politics.** Spends energy on backstage games instead of actually growing the business.
- **Excessive people-pleasing.** Takes on extra responsibility just to be liked, and burns out without fair pay for it.
- **Fear of competition.** Hides knowledge and experience, afraid others will use it, and loses the chance to earn from teaching.
- **Lack of confidence in their own knowledge.** Doubts their own competence even where their expertise is obvious.
- **A feeling of not being competent enough.** Avoids the role of mentor or expert, believing they still know too little.
- **Perfectionism about teaching.** Puts off sharing knowledge until they feel "ready enough," and loses time.
- **Closed off to other people's experience.** Doesn't recognize the value of other viewpoints, which narrows their range of opportunities.
- **The eternal theorist.** Eagerly absorbs new information but rarely puts it into practice, believing life will change from the learning process itself.

## Recommendations

- Keep clear records and plan your finances instead of relying on memory or intuition.
- Go public and share your expertise openly instead of keeping knowledge to yourself.
- Connect with like-minded people and stay open to other viewpoints — this opens up new sources of income.
