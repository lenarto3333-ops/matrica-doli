# 8. Justice — the energy of financial wellbeing

At its best, this energy builds income on honesty, discipline, and fair exchange — giving back as much as was received. At its worst, it clings to under-the-table schemes, resentment over being undervalued, and judgment of other people's wealth, all of which block its own flow.

## Positive

+ **Honesty in business.** Doesn't cut corners, which is exactly what builds long-term trust.
+ **Working within the law.** Sticks to official, transparent arrangements, which avoids unnecessary risk.
+ **Prudence.** Thinks steps through in advance before committing resources.
+ **Sticking to the plan.** Doesn't stray from the intended course for a quick gain.
+ **Follow-through discipline.** Carries things through to the end, precisely and on time.
+ **Responsibility.** Keeps their word on financial agreements, even when it's inconvenient.
+ **A strong reputation.** A reputation for honesty becomes, over time, their best asset.
+ **Balance between giving and receiving.** Aware that they give as much as they receive, and doesn't break that balance.
+ **Generosity offered first.** Gives before waiting for guarantees, trusting it will come back around.
+ **Genuine joy at others' wealth.** Feels real happiness for other people's success instead of envy, which frees up their own flow.
+ **Faith in a fair world.** A conviction that effort and honesty are ultimately rewarded.

## Negative

- **Losing themselves in work.** Forgets about rest and boundaries, burns out chasing results.
- **Over-responsibility.** Takes on more than they can actually handle, afraid of letting someone down.
- **Hiding part of their income.** Chooses under-the-table arrangements over transparent ones, which creates risk down the line.
- **Getting involved in shady deals.** Chases quick profit through arrangements that damage their reputation.
- **Clinging to dead-end stability.** Won't change jobs, afraid of losing a stable but played-out position.
- **No clear plan.** Acts without a strategy, making the financial outcome unpredictable.
- **Off-the-books pay.** Settles for "grey" arrangements that don't protect their own interests.
- **A feeling of being undervalued.** The belief that their skills go unappreciated and underpaid feeds resentment instead of action.
- **Judging other people's wealth.** Criticizes and resents wealthy people's lives, subconsciously pushing away their own abundance.
- **Wanting only to receive.** Expects reward without being willing to give in return, which breaks the natural balance of exchange.

## Recommendations

- Stick to the law and to transparent arrangements instead of under-the-table deals — it's the most reliable path to stable income.
- Consciously study the law of cause and effect: what you give eventually comes back.
- Keep family and close ones a priority, and favor discipline and regularity in your actions over one-off bursts of effort.
