# 2. The High Priestess — the energy of financial wellbeing

At its best, this energy brings financial wisdom and trust that build long, stable partnerships without unnecessary rush. At its worst, it turns into secrecy and distrust that get in the way of sharing resources and accepting help.

## Positive

+ **Diplomacy.** Negotiates so both sides win, without forcing the issue.
+ **A gift for smoothing conflicts.** Puts out financial disputes while they're still small, preserving relationships and shared ventures.
+ **A knack for bringing people together.** Gathers people around a shared project who bring out the best in one another.
+ **Intuitive feel for deals.** Senses whether a partnership is trustworthy before any rational analysis kicks in.
+ **Observant and foresighted.** Notices financial trends and risks before others do.
+ **The ability to restore trust.** Knows how to bring harmony back where money or relationships have been shaken.
+ **Financial wisdom.** Makes decisions with care, relying on experience rather than the emotion of the moment.
+ **Inner balance.** Doesn't chase quick results, but builds income gradually and steadily.
+ **A talent for mediation.** Easily finds common ground between parties with different financial interests.
+ **Genuine care for the common good.** Treats money and resources as something that unites rather than divides the people around them.

## Negative

- **Distrust and doubt.** Constantly suspects a trick where there isn't one, which gets in the way of building partnerships.
- **Fickleness and changeability.** One decision about money today, a completely different one tomorrow — others can't keep up.
- **Secrecy and hidden agendas.** Hides their real financial situation even from close people.
- **Insincerity.** Says one thing about money while actually thinking or planning another.
- **Withdrawal and isolation.** Avoids joint ventures and support, preferring to manage alone even at the cost of the result.
- **Unreliability.** Fails to follow through on financial agreements, forgets what was promised.
- **Indifference.** Loses interest in a shared venture or agreement as soon as the initial spark fades.
- **An inner contradiction.** Wants stability while fearing any kind of commitment, which makes decisions hard to reach.
- **Indecision and passivity.** Puts off an important financial step for a long time, waiting for the "perfect" moment.
- **Inertia and laziness.** Doesn't put in the effort where income needs regular, consistent action.

## Recommendations

- Give yourself real emotional rest regularly, not just physical rest.
- Spend time with people you trust instead of withdrawing into solitude with financial worries.
- Openly own your mistakes in money matters instead of hiding them — this is what rebuilds others' trust.
