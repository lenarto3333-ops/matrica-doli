# 16. The Tower — Ancestral Tasks, Patterns, and Resources

## Brief Essence

The ancestral script of this energy is symbolically related to the experience of a sudden, devastating break in the family history: the family may have had wars, uprooting, forced emigration, fire, the destruction of the family home or business, the sudden death of a breadwinner, or some other catastrophe that in one moment took away everything that seemed to be an unbreakable support. These events were rarely reinterpreted openly - mostly they were either kept silent or talked about only as a fact of loss, without acknowledging the strength it took to rebuild a life afterwards. That is why this experience is passed on not as a conscious lesson of resilience, but as a hidden anxiety before a catastrophe mixed with unassimilated rage or traumatic tension.

The ancestral task of this energy is to learn to inherit the hidden gift of resilience and the ability to rebuild after the worst, without inheriting with it either the chronic fear of a new collapse, or the ancestral habit of outbursts of aggression or silent traumatic numbness.

## Natural Strength

The ancestral resource of this energy is a symbolic legacy of people who experienced a catastrophic loss and were still able to start anew: there could be those in the family who lost their home, fortune, loved ones in war or other tragedy, and still found the strength to rebuild their lives literally from scratch. This ability, even if it was talked about only as a painful fact, left descendants with a profound gift of endurance in the face of the most difficult circumstances.

A second resource is the ancestral ability to recognize the approach of danger: if the ancestors had to survive in times of upheaval, a heightened sensitivity to the signs of an impending crisis may have been established in the family, an ability that, freed from paralyzing fear, becomes a valuable instinct for the descendants.

The third force is the ancestral experience of spiritual awakening through loss: if someone in the family has found a new meaning, a new faith, or a new, deeper way of living after a disaster, this experience of transformation, even if silenced, remains a powerful resource for descendants who themselves go through their own crises.

## Expression in the Positive

When the ancestral karma is worked out, the descendant is able to consciously take the ancestral gift of resilience and turn it into the confidence that even the worst fall can be survived and rebuilt after it: he does not panic in the face of difficulties in the way that those who have never seen an example of overcoming a disaster in their family panic.

In the positive, a person becomes the one who breaks the ancestral cycle of traumatic anxiety or hidden aggression: instead of passing on to children a chronic expectation of disaster or outbursts of anger, he consciously learns to live through crises calmly and teaches this to younger relatives as well.

Ancestral resources of endurance and perceptiveness to danger are transformed into a conscious gift of support: a person helps relatives to pass through their own crises, relying on the ancestral memory that ancestors, despite the most severe losses, still found the strength to start anew.

Another positive expression is the ability to talk about ancestral disasters without silencing or numbing them: acknowledging the scale of loss experienced by ancestors, honoring their strength, and at the same time consciously not passing on unspoken fear or pain, but turning it into a story of resilience.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, the family resource in the positive is manifested as the ability to keep calm and clarity in moments of crisis, based on the example of women of the family who experienced the loss of a home, loved ones or fortune and still kept the family together, finding the strength to rebuild life anew.

She is able to talk openly with daughters and younger relatives about birth disasters, without turning these stories into a source of anxiety or fear, but showing that you can really emerge stronger from the deepest fall.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, the ancestral resource in a positive way is manifested as the ability to act decisively and calmly in moments of crisis, relying on the example of ancestors who, after losing everything - property, home, loved ones - found the strength to rebuild their lives without breaking down on those who were close to them.

He assumes the role of a person in the family who helps younger relatives not to panic in the face of difficulties, but to rely on the inherited, albeit often silent, ancestral wisdom that even the worst can be survived.

## Expression in the Negative

When the ancestral karma is not worked out, the descendant may unconsciously carry within him the ancestral anxiety of disaster: a constant, vague feeling that everything is about to collapse, even when the external circumstances are quite stable, an anxiety that is not really his own, but inherited from the ancestral memory of the experienced loss.

Another manifestation of the negative is a hereditary tendency to outbursts of aggression: if someone in the family was unable to process the accumulated pain after a disaster other than through anger or violence against loved ones, the offspring may unconsciously repeat this pattern, reacting disproportionately sharply to relatively minor stresses.

In the negative, it is also possible that the ancestral ban on the manifestation of weakness or grief: if the ancestors had to survive without the right to grieve - "there was no time to cry, it was necessary to survive", - a subconscious instruction to suppress any emotional reaction to the loss could be established in the family, and the descendant may feel an inexplicable emotional freeze in his own moments of crisis.

Another sign of the negative is a repeated family trauma: a cycle of sudden losses - financial, domestic, relational - that seem to deliberately haunt several generations in a row, as if the family has not yet learned the lesson that the first disaster should have taught.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is situations of sudden loss or instability, which unconsciously activate the ancestral memory of an ancient catastrophe in the history of the family. The second trigger is contact with ancestral topics that were traditionally kept silent: war losses, forced relocations, the sudden death of a breadwinner, or other disasters that were not accepted to be discussed in the family. The third trigger is the moment when one's own life begins to seem too stable or prosperous, which unconsciously provokes the anxiety "this can't last long" and pushes to self-destructive decisions that seem to bring the expected disaster closer.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to find out as much as possible about the history of disasters, losses and reconstruction in the family: what exactly happened, how the ancestors survived it, and at what cost was the next reconstruction given to them. The second step is to consciously separate one's own anxiety about the future from ancestral scenarios: to recognize that part of the fear of crisis does not actually belong to a person personally, but is inherited from ancestral memory.

The third step is to allow oneself to live and mourn ancestral losses, even those that one did not personally witness, creating space for emotions that the ancestors did not allow themselves in the times when they lived. The fourth step is, if possible and willing, to carefully talk with relatives about the silenced topics of birth disasters, creating space for greater understanding and healing between generations.

The fifth step is to consciously create new family examples of living with difficulties without panic or aggression: moments when a difficult situation in the family is resolved calmly, by supporting each other, and not by accusations or numbness.

## Main Task

The main ancestral task of this energy is to interrupt the cycle of inherited traumatic anxiety and hidden aggression and to pass on to the next generations not the doom of experiencing new disasters, but a healthy, calm confidence that even after the heaviest fall, it is possible to rebuild. This means respectfully accepting the scale of loss that the ancestors experienced, and at the same time consciously choosing a different, more sustainable trajectory for living through crises — without panic, without outbursts of anger, and without silent numbness.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What do I know about the history of sudden losses, disasters or forced reconstruction in my family?
- Do I feel vague anxiety before a catastrophe even when my life is stable?
- To what extent is my reaction to crisis situations — panic, anger, or numbness — personal to me, and not inherited from my family?

## Practical Plan

1. Ask an older relative about a history of disaster, loss, or forced reconstruction in the family that has not been openly discussed before.
2. Write down one subconscious cue about a crisis or loss that may have been inherited, and question whether it is still relevant to you personally.
3. Identify one situation in your life where you react with panic or anger out of proportion to a real threat, and trace its possible ancestral root.
4. To create or support one family ritual commemorating the losses experienced by the ancestors - a joint memory, a candle, a story to the next generation.
5. If there is an opportunity and readiness, carefully talk with a relative about the topic of an old family disaster that was previously kept silent.
6. Write a short letter (it is not necessary to send) to one of the ancestors with thanks for the transmitted resistance and recognition of the pain that you want to heal in this generation.
