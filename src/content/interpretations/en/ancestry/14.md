# 14. Temperance — Ancestral Tasks, Patterns, and Resources

## Brief Essence

The ancestral scenario of this energy is symbolically connected with the experience of extremes in family history: the family may have had periods of sharp conflicts, excessive severity or, on the contrary, excessive indulgence, separation of families due to irreconcilable positions that never found reconciliation. These extremes were rarely spoken openly, and that is why they are passed on not as a learned lesson, but as a hidden thirst for harmony, which the offspring feels without even understanding where it comes from.

The ancestral task of this energy is to learn to take from the family the hidden gift of reconciliation and balance, without inheriting with it either the ancestral habit of conflicts and extremes, or the opposite habit of endless, exhausting compromises for the sake of pretended peace.

## Natural Strength

The ancestral resource of this energy is the symbolic heritage of peacemakers: there could be people in the family who knew how to extinguish conflicts between relatives, to unite separated branches of the family, to find a common language where others saw only opposition. This capacity for reconciliation, even if it was rarely acknowledged out loud, left descendants with a profound gift of diplomacy.

The second resource is the family tradition of combining different things: mixed marriages, the combination of different cultures, traditions or professions within the same family could teach the family the art of bringing together the incompatible and building something new and whole from it. This ability to synthesize is a valuable legacy.

The third strength is ancestral resilience in the midst of chaos: examples of ancestors who maintained inner peace and clarity even in times of family splits or external upheavals, and thanks to this endurance kept the family from final disintegration, become a support for descendants in their own search for balance.

## Expression in the Positive

When ancestral karma is worked out, the descendant is able to consciously bring to the family the reconciliation that he lacked for generations: he can become the one who brings together the quarreling branches of the family, smoothes out old grudges between relatives, creates a space where different generations or different views can coexist without enmity.

In the positive, a person becomes someone who restores a healthy attitude to differences and conflicts in the family: he can show children and younger relatives that different views, characters or traditions within the same family are not a threat, but a source of wealth, if you learn to combine them with respect.

Ancestral resources of peacemaking and synthesis turn into a realized gift: the ability to support the family at the time of disagreements, an example of how ancestors, despite the extremes and conflicts of their time, still found a way to remain a family, and not completely disintegrate.

Another sign of the positive is the ability to talk about family conflicts and extremes without silencing or dramatizing: to recognize that there were irreconcilable confrontations in the history of the family, to respect the complexity of this experience, and at the same time consciously not to transfer this model into one's own life, but to choose the path of reconciliation.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

A woman's ancestral resource is manifested in the positive as the ability to be the one who brings the family together despite long-standing differences: she knows how to organize a joint holiday, conversation or tradition that unites relatives with different views, based on the example of women of the family, who once knew how to extinguish conflicts with gentle wisdom, not force.

She is able to openly talk with her daughters and younger relatives about family stories of confrontations and divisions, turning these stories not into an excuse for resentment, but into a source of understanding of how valuable the ability to find a common language is.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, the ancestral resource in the positive is manifested as the ability to act as a fair mediator in family disputes, based on the example of ancestors who knew how to keep the family together even in times of serious disagreements. He takes over their endurance without taking over their tendency to extremes.

He assumes the role of a person in a family who knows how to combine different generations or branches of the family in a common cause or tradition, relying on the inherited, albeit often silent, family wisdom that the family is stronger when it knows how to combine differences, and not destroy them.

## Expression in the Negative

When the ancestral karma is not worked out, the descendant may unconsciously repeat the ancestral scenario of extremes: abruptly moving from softness to hardness in relations with relatives, from excessive compliance to a sudden, sharp break in communication, repeating the same cycle of conflict and division as previous generations.

Another manifestation of the negative is the ancestral tendency to endless, exhausting compromises for the sake of feigned peace: if the family was afraid of open conflict, the descendant may unconsciously repeat this model, agreeing to everything for the sake of maintaining external harmony, even when it harms him.

In the negative, a family ban on the manifestation of true feelings is also possible: if the ancestors had to restrain disagreement for the sake of the family's survival in difficult times, the subconscious instruction "it is better to remain silent than to quarrel" could be fixed in the family, and the descendant may feel vague anxiety when he needs to openly state his position.

Another negative sign is family silence about long-standing conflicts: stories about splits, arguments, or broken ties at critical moments can be hushed up to such an extent that subsequent generations feel indescribable tension between relatives without understanding where it comes from.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is situations of family conflict, which unconsciously activate the family memory of old, unresolved disputes in the history of the family. The second trigger is contact with ancestral topics that have traditionally been kept silent: the split of the family, broken ties between branches, conflicts that have never been reconciled. The third trigger is the moment when one's own attempt to find a fair balance contrasts with the extremes of previous generations of the family, causing unconscious doubt as to whether true reconciliation is possible at all.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to find out as much as possible about the conflicts and disagreements in the history of the family: who quarreled, when and over what, and whether there was ever a reconciliation. The second step is to consciously separate one's own attitude to conflict and compromise from ancestral scripts: to recognize which fears about quarrel or division are not really personal to the person, but inherited from the family.

The third step is to allow yourself to openly express disagreement where the family once knew only silence or division: learning to talk about different views calmly, seeking a real, not a pretend middle ground. The fourth step is, if possible and willing, to carefully talk with relatives about the hushed up topics of family conflicts, creating space for greater understanding between generations.

The fifth step is to consciously create new family examples of healthy reconciliation: moments when the family allows themselves to disagree and at the same time stay together, not in silent resentment or a complete break.

## Main Task

The main ancestral task of this energy is to interrupt the cycle of ancestral extremes and silent compromises and to pass on to the next generations not a doom for conflict or forced submission, but a healthy ability to find a true middle ground in which every voice counts. It means respectfully accepting the complexity of the experience of ancestors, whose conflicts and extremes were often dictated by the circumstances of their time, and at the same time consciously choosing for themselves a different, freer trajectory of reconciliation and balance.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What do I know about ancient conflicts and disagreements in the history of my kind?
- Are there topics, conflicts or divisions in my family, about which it is customary to remain silent?
- To what extent is my fear of open disagreement personal to me, and not inherited from my family?

## Practical Plan

1. Ask an older relative about a period of conflict or division in the family history that has not been openly discussed before.
2. Write down one subconscious guideline for conflict or compromise that may have been inherited, and question whether it still applies to you personally.
3. Identify one family situation in which you choose silent compromise over honest conversation, and make a plan for gradual open dialogue.
4. To create or support one family tradition that unites different generations or views — a joint holiday, ritual, conversation — that will become a resource of reconciliation for future generations.
5. If there is an opportunity and readiness, carefully talk with a relative about the topic of an old conflict, which was previously kept silent in the family.
6. Write a short letter (it is not necessary to send) to one of the ancestors with thanks for the transferred ability to seek peace and recognition of the extremes that you want to heal in this generation.
