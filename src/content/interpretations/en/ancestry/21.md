# 21. The World — Ancestral Tasks, Patterns, and Resources

## Brief Essence

The ancestral script of this energy is symbolically associated with a figure in the family, whose story contains either an unfinished business, interrupted halfway, or, on the contrary, a real feat of unification: an ancestor who went far from home - to another city, another country, another world - and managed to bring together parts of the family torn apart by migration or war, to build something large from scratch, or to connect two different worlds through marriage, trade or common cause Often in this same story there is another side - an ancestor who never completed an important task in his life (unfinished house, unfinished household, broken relations with a distant family) and left behind a feeling of incompleteness, which is transmitted to descendants as a vague anxiety "something remains unfinished". A third possible trace is an ancestor whose stubbornness, mistrust of strangers, or fear of the unfamiliar world cost the family a lost opportunity: a marriage broken up because of disapproval, refusal to move or a deal that could have changed the family's fortunes for the better, hostility toward neighbors or strangers that cost the family support in a time of need.

The ancestral task of this energy is to learn to complete what the family left unfinished, and at the same time to take from the family not the fear of the wider world or the habit of sticking only to one's own, familiar circle, but the real gift of unification: the ability to bring together the scattered, build on a large scale and remain open to a new, wider life that awaits beyond the usual.

## Natural Strength

The ancestral resource of this energy is a symbolic legacy of large-scale thinking: if among the ancestors there were those who dared to go far, to build something significant from scratch, or to connect people and worlds that would otherwise remain separated, this ability to think large-scale and unifying remains alive in the family and can awaken in the descendants just when they themselves need to go beyond the ordinary.

The second resource is the ancestral memory of the real completion of important matters: if the family valued completing what was started, despite the difficulties, this skill is passed on to the descendants as an internal support that helps them not to abandon important matters halfway even when it would be easier to retreat.

The third force is the ancestral experience of openness to the foreign and unfamiliar: if the ancestors were able to accept another way of life, another culture or another person without hostility, this openness, cleansed of possible fear or mistrust that may have accompanied it, leaves descendants with the ability to meet the wider world with curiosity, not with defensive anxiety.

## Expression in the Positive

When ancestral karma is worked out, the descendant is able to consciously complete what was left unfinished a generation ago: to finish the family business, to reconcile the branches of the family separated by a move or conflict, to put an unfinished family project in order - and with this internal act of completion, free both himself and the family from the long-lasting feeling of incompleteness.

In the plus, a person becomes the one who breaks the ancestral cycle of fear of the wider world and brings the very gift of unification into modern times: instead of passing on to children the habit of sticking only to a familiar, safe circle, he learns to build on a large scale, to travel, to bring together people and opportunities from different environments, while maintaining a deep connection with the roots of the family.

The ancestral resources of large-scale thinking and openness in the plus turn into a conscious gift to unite the family around a common cause or goal: a person takes on the role of someone who is able to gather a scattered — geographically or emotionally — family together, reminding everyone of common roots, without requiring anyone to abandon their own, new path.

Another plus sign is the ability to talk about ancestral histories of broken affairs, long-distance moves, or lost connection to ancestors without judgment: to recognize the circumstances in which they lived and made decisions with understanding, and at the same time consciously choose for themselves a more complete and open way of living their own life.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, the reproductive resource in a plus is manifested as the ability to unite a scattered family - physically or emotionally - finding a way to maintain a connection even at a great distance and not allowing relocations, conflicts or years of silence to finally tear the family fabric.

She is able to convey to her daughters and younger relatives not anxiety before the wider world or the habit of not completing what has been started, but an example of how it is possible to build a large-scale life outside the usual circle, and at the same time bring important matters to a real, tangible conclusion.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, the ancestral resource in a plus is manifested as the ability to bring to an end a large-scale family or business business that was started even by previous generations, combining in it both respect for what was done by ancestors, and actually, a broader vision of what this business can become.

He takes on the role of a person in the family who knows how to be the first to take a step towards the wide world - a new city, a new country, new people - breaking the possible ancestral script of fear of the unknown and showing younger relatives that scale and openness do not threaten family roots, but strengthen them.

## Expression in the Negative

When ancestral karma is not worked out, the descendant may unconsciously carry within him an ancestral fear of the wider world: a deep, often vague feeling that it is safe only within the familiar, familiar circle, and this inherited wariness manifests itself as a rejection of large-scale opportunities, moves, partnerships or projects that go beyond what the family has already experienced.

Another manifestation of the negative is the inherited pattern of incompleteness: if there are several important unfinished business left in the family, the descendant may unconsciously reproduce this same pattern in his own life, repeatedly leaving important projects, relationships or milestones halfway, never bringing any of them to a true end.

In the negative, the ancestral model of defensive hostility towards strangers is also possible: if the ancestors suffered through contact with foreigners, other authorities or a different way of life and responded to this with withdrawal and mistrust, the descendant can inherit this same wariness, perceiving everything unfamiliar - new people, new ideas, other cultures - as a threat, and not as an opportunity.

Another negative sign is an ancestral rupture that has never been healed: if a move, emigration, or conflict has split a family into parts that have never been reunited, the descendant may feel a vague, unexplained longing for wholeness or relatives he never even knew, and this longing, if not acknowledged, drains energy without providing any resolution in return.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is contact with a family topic that was traditionally kept silent: the story of a long move, a broken connection with a part of the family, or an unfinished family business that was not used to be discussed directly in the family. The second trigger is a situation that requires you to personally go beyond the usual, familiar circle - a move, a large-scale project, contact with another culture - which unconsciously activates inherited ancestral anxiety before the wider world. The third trigger is the need to complete an important matter when it seems easier to postpone it indefinitely, as someone in the family has already done.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to find out as much as possible about the history of migrations, long-distance connections or unfinished business in the family: which of the ancestors moved far away, who united the family or, on the contrary, left behind a gap, which business remained unfinished. The second step is to consciously separate one's own attitude to the scale and the new world from the ancestral scripts: to recognize how much of the caution or reticence is actually not personal to the person, but inherited from the family.

The third step is to mentally or in writing complete what the family could not complete: to mentally "settlement" the unfinished business of the ancestor, to symbolically reunite a family divided by relocation or conflict, at least in one's own imagination or through real contact with distant relatives. The fourth step is, if possible and willing, to consciously take one step towards the wider world, which the family has traditionally avoided: a trip, a new acquaintance, a project outside the usual circle.

The fifth step is to consciously create new family examples of completion and reunification: bringing things started to the end, maintaining contact with distant relatives, meeting the new and unfamiliar with curiosity, not with inherited anxiety.

## Main Task

The main ancestral task of this energy is to honestly finish what was left unfinished in the family, and to pass on to the next generations not the fear of the wider world and not the habit of sticking only to a familiar circle, but the real ancestral gift of unification, large-scale thinking and the ability to bring important things to the end. This means respectfully accepting the circumstances in which the ancestors lived, and at the same time consciously choosing for oneself a more open, more holistic way of living one's own life and one's own connection with the world.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What do I know about the history of migrations, distant connections or unfinished business among my ancestors?
- Do I not keep an inherited caution or hostility to everything new and unfamiliar, which actually belongs not to me, but to the family?
- What important matter am I perhaps leaving unfinished due to ancestral habit - and what will happen if I finally bring it to an end?

## Practical Plan

1. To ask an older relative about the history of long-distance moves, broken ties or unfinished business in the family, which was not discussed openly before.
2. Write down one important thing in your life that you leave unfinished, and take one concrete step towards completing it.
3. Identify one situation where you avoid a large-scale opportunity or contact with something new because of inherited, rather than personally experienced, anxiety, and question that decision.
4. If there is a branch of the family separated by a move or a conflict, try to restore contact with it, even a small one.
5. If there is an opportunity and readiness, talk with a relative about the ancient family history of a long-distance move or unification.
6. Write a short letter (it is not necessary to send) to one of the ancestors with the recognition of his unfinished business or feat of unification and the intention to continue or complete this story for himself and his descendants.
