# 3. The Empress — Ancestral Tasks, Patterns, and Resources

## In Brief

In an ancestral context, this energy indicates that abundance, care, and the ability to receive the fruits of one's labor hold special meaning for the family line. Family history may include periods of scarcity and hard physical work for survival or, conversely, years of warmth and prosperity. The task of this lineage is to pass down not anxiety about resources or limitless self-sacrifice, but a healthy capacity to create, receive, and share generously without becoming depleted.

## Natural Strength

The ancestral resource of this energy is a deeply rooted ability to care for others and create comfort even in difficult conditions. In many lineages carrying this energy, there are women and men who could create a sense of home from very little, feed and warm others, and raise crops or children through the hardest times. This resource of generosity and care is often passed on even when no one speaks about it directly.

The second resource is ancestral memory of the value of the earth, home, body, and labor: the experience of ancestors who increased their resources through patient, consistent work rather than haste or gambling. When understood consciously, this experience becomes a valuable guide for descendants building prosperity of their own.

The third strength is the natural fertility of the lineage in the broadest sense: the capacity to give birth not only to children but to ideas, ventures, and traditions that take root and nourish future generations. This is a lineage that knows how to cultivate, both literally and symbolically.

## Positive Expression

When ancestral karma has been worked through, a descendant can consciously create abundance and comfort while respecting personal limits, rather than from an obligation either to continue or completely reject the family's pattern of self-sacrifice. This appears as an inner freedom to care for others without forgetting self-care.

In the positive, the person becomes the one who restores a healthy relationship with resources and care within the family: a home where generosity nourishes rather than exhausts, relationships in which caring for relatives does not require disappearing into their needs, and bonds between generations grounded in abundance rather than constant deprivation.

The ancestral resources of care and working the land become conscious gifts shared with one's own family and wider circle: creating a welcoming home, cultivating a business or garden, and supporting relatives materially and emotionally without losing one's own fullness.

Another sign of the positive is an ability to speak about the family's history of scarcity and self-sacrifice without shame or guilt over one's own prosperity, turning painful family stories into a source of gratitude rather than a burden silently pressing on future generations.

### Positive Expression in Women

For a woman, the ancestral resource in its positive expression appears as a conscious continuation of the tradition of care and generosity without repeating the complete self-denial that may have marked earlier generations. She takes the best of the inherited ability to create comfort while leaving exhausting self-sacrifice behind.

She can speak honestly with daughters, sisters, and nieces about care and abundance, showing that it is possible to care for others without forgetting to replenish oneself rather than passing on only a silent duty to give everything away.

### Positive Expression in Men

For a man, the ancestral resource in its positive expression appears as an ability to break a possible family pattern of hard, exhausting labor for survival. He consciously chooses to build prosperity with pleasure and concern for his family's quality of life, rather than merely from a duty to provide the minimum.

He takes on the role of creating stable, warm abundance within the family, supporting family traditions not from pressure to continue his ancestors' hardship, but from a sincere wish for later generations to live more easily.

## Negative Expression

When ancestral karma remains unresolved, a descendant may unconsciously repeat family patterns: chronic anxiety about resources even amid objective abundance, a habit of giving themselves to others until exhausted, or fear that allowing oneself peace and pleasure would somehow “betray” the ancestors' hard work.

Another negative expression is an ancestral prohibition against ease and prosperity. If the family's history includes severe scarcity or the loss of land, property, or home, an unconscious rule may have taken hold that prosperity is dangerous, temporary, or must always come through hard labor and self-sacrifice.

In the negative, there may also be an ancestral cycle of “earn and lose” that repeats from generation to generation. Family members may repeatedly find that what they have accumulated is suddenly destroyed until someone in the lineage consciously lives through and completes the pattern.

Another sign is silence around ancestral wounds connected with hunger, poverty, or loss of home. These stories are suppressed so completely that later generations feel an unspoken anxiety about resources and abundance without understanding its source, and unconsciously sabotage their own well-being.

## What Can Pull the Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a moment of genuine abundance or peace that unconsciously provokes anxiety or guilt by contrasting with the family's difficult experience. Fears of losing everything may surface even though they do not belong to the present moment.

The second trigger is contact with ancestral subjects traditionally left unspoken: hunger, poverty, or the loss of land or home. Trying to discuss these matters may provoke unexpectedly strong resistance from older relatives.

The third trigger is effortless prosperity or self-care that sharply contrasts with the exhausting, self-sacrificing experience of earlier generations. This can unconsciously create guilt “because life is easier for me than it was for them.”

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step, wherever possible, is to learn more about the history of resources and care in the family: who knew how to create prosperity, who lived in constant scarcity, and which stories of hunger, loss, or hard labor were silenced.

The second step is to consciously separate one's own relationship with abundance and self-care from ancestral patterns, recognizing which fears or expectations concerning resources are inherited rather than personally one's own.

The third step is to allow oneself gradually to receive abundance and peace without excessive guilt, consciously working with any belief that ease is “not for people like us” or must be paid for through exhaustion.

The fourth step, when possible and desired, is to speak gently with relatives about suppressed stories of scarcity or loss, creating room for gratitude for the path already traveled rather than only silent pain.

The fifth step is to consciously create new family traditions in which self-care exists alongside caring for others—traditions that become a resource for future generations even if the family lacked this balance in the past.

## Main Task

The principal ancestral task of this energy is to break the cycle of unspoken anxiety around resources and self-sacrifice, and to pass on to the next generations not fear of abundance but a healthy capacity to create, receive, and share generously without becoming depleted. This means respecting ancestors who likely struggled to survive under far harder circumstances while consciously choosing for oneself and one's descendants a fuller, calmer relationship with resources, care, and the body.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What do I know about abundance and scarcity in my family history—did people live prosperously or in constant anxiety about survival?
- Are there stories of hunger, poverty, or loss of home in my family that people are expected not to discuss?
- Do I feel guilty when I allow myself ease, peace, or prosperity that earlier generations did not have?
- How much of my fear of losing resources belongs personally to me, and how much was inherited from my lineage?
- Do I allow myself to care for myself as generously as I care for others?
- What tradition of healthy abundance would I like to pass on to my children or younger relatives?

## Practical Plan

1. Ask an older relative about a story of abundance or scarcity in the family that was not previously discussed openly.
2. Write down one unconscious belief about resources or self-care that may have been inherited from the family, and question whether it is still personally relevant to you.
3. Allow yourself one act of self-care this week without guilt over its supposed “excess.”
4. Create or support one family tradition of care and comfort—a shared meal or a ritual of gratitude for a harvest or income—that can become a resource for future generations.
5. If there is an opportunity and you feel ready, speak gently with a relative about a story of scarcity or loss that was previously silenced.
6. Write a short letter, which you do not need to send, to one of your ancestors, thanking them for the ability to care that they passed down and acknowledging the hard work you wish to ease in this generation.
