# 13. Death — Ancestral Tasks, Patterns, and Resources

## Short summary

The ancestral script of this energy is often associated with the experience of abrupt, often traumatic endings in family history: dispossession, forced emigration, loss of home or property, severing of ties between generations due to war or repression. These endings were rarely voluntary or gradual, and that is why the memory of them is passed down not as wisdom about renewal, but as a deep, often silent fear of any change, separation or closing of an important stage.

The ancestral task of this energy is to learn from the family the experience of survival through the most difficult transformations, without inheriting with it paralysis before any completion or, on the contrary, the habit of abruptly and painfully severing ties instead of a conscious, gradual farewell to the old.

## Natural force

The ancestral resource of this energy is the generation-proven ability to start life anew after losing everything: ancestors who survived dispossession, exile, loss of home or status, and despite this were able to build a new life from scratch, left their descendants with a profound ability to regenerate after the hardest blows of fate.

The second resource is the family tradition of decisive changes at critical moments: there could be people in the family who made a difficult decision in time to leave a dangerous place, break a harmful connection, change their lifestyle for the sake of family survival, and this courage to act decisively in the moment of need is a valuable legacy.

The third strength is the ancestral ability to preserve dignity and meaning even in the midst of destruction: examples of ancestors who, having lost almost everything, managed to preserve the main thing - family, faith, name - and pass it on, become a support for descendants in their own transformations.

## Manifestation in the plus

When the ancestral karma is worked out, the descendant is able to consciously complete the exhausted stages of his own life, without experiencing it as a repetition of the ancestral disaster. He can close an old business, relationship or habit calmly and consciously, relying on the inherited stability of the family, and not on his traumatic fear of any change.

On the plus side, a person becomes someone who restores a healthy attitude to endings in the family: he can tell children and younger relatives about difficult periods of loss and new beginnings in the history of the family, not as a doom for a permanent catastrophe, but as a confirmation that the family knows how to revive after the most difficult.

Ancestral resources of resilience and determination in the plus turn into a realized gift: the ability to support the family in a moment of crisis, as an example of how ancestors overcame the loss of a home, status or ordinary life and built a new one, relying on the very power of regeneration that the family had produced for generations.

Another positive sign is the ability to talk about ancestral losses without silencing or dramatizing them: to recognize that the ancestors went through difficult breaks and transformations, to honor their experience, and at the same time consciously not to continue living in the shadow of this fear, but to build one's own life with trust in change.

### Manifestation in women is positive

In a woman, the ancestral resource is manifested in the plus as the ability to bear the ancestral resistance without frozen fear: she knows how to complete what has exhausted herself - relationships, role, familiar environment - relying on the example of women of the genus, who built a new life after the heaviest losses. She consciously takes on their power of regeneration, leaving their fear behind.

She is able to talk openly with her daughters and younger relatives about family stories of loss and new beginnings, turning these stories into a source of confidence that after destruction, recovery is always possible.

### Manifestation in the husband is positive

In a man, the ancestral resource in a plus is manifested as the ability to resolutely close the stage of life that has exhausted itself, relying on the example of ancestors who knew how to maintain internal support even when they had to start life literally from scratch. He takes on their determination without taking on their trauma.

He assumes the role of a person in the family who knows how to lead the family through the necessary changes, relying on the inherited, albeit often silent, family wisdom that a new beginning is possible even after the most difficult ending.

## Manifestation in the minus

When the ancestral karma is not worked out, the descendant can unconsciously repeat the scenario of paralyzing fear of any change: clinging to exhausted relationships, work, place of residence, fearing that any completion will inevitably turn into a disaster, as it once happened to the ancestors.

Another manifestation of the negative is a family tendency towards sharp, destructive breaks instead of gradual transformations: if there were sudden, violent losses in the history of the family, the descendant may unconsciously repeat this pattern, abruptly and painfully breaking off relationships or affairs instead of a conscious, gradual completion.

In the negative, a family ban on saying goodbye or mourning the lost is also possible: if the ancestors had to move on immediately after the tragedy, without time to grieve, the subconscious instruction "you can't stop and grieve" could be fixed in the family, and the descendant may feel vague anxiety when he needs to give himself time to experience an important ending.

Another sign of the negative is family silence about the lost: stories about the property, home, status or people that the family lost at critical moments can be silenced to such an extent that subsequent generations feel an indescribable fear of the topic of loss and change, not understanding where it came from.

## What can be negative

The strongest trigger is situations that require a person to complete an important stage of life, which unconsciously activates the ancestral memory of catastrophic losses in the history of the family. The second trigger is contact with ancestral themes that have traditionally been silenced: dispossession, exile, loss of home or loved ones in critical times. The third trigger is the moment when one's own decision to break up with an old man consciously and calmly contrasts sharply with the dramatic, forced breaks of previous generations of the family, which sometimes causes an unconscious feeling of guilt for the ease of one's own choice.

## How to return energy to the plus side

The first step is to learn as much as possible about the periods of loss and new beginnings in the history of the family: who, when and why was forced to start life anew, and how exactly the family managed to be reborn. The second step is to consciously separate one's own attitude to changes and endings from ancestral scripts: to recognize which fears about loss or closing a stage are actually not personal to the person, but inherited from the family.

The third step is to allow yourself a gradual, conscious ending where the family once knew only a sharp, traumatic break: learning to say goodbye to the old person calmly, giving yourself the time to grieve that your ancestors did not have. The fourth step is, if possible and willing, to carefully talk with relatives about the silenced topics of family loss, creating space for greater clarity between generations.

The fifth step is to consciously create new family examples of healthy completion and renewal: moments when the family allows itself to close the old calmly and with dignity, not in panic or silent denial.

## The main task

The main ancestral task of this energy is to break the cycle of traumatic fear of endings and to pass on to the next generations not the doom of catastrophe at every change, but a healthy ability to calmly close exhausted stages and trust in renewal. It means respectfully accepting the experience of ancestors, whose losses were often sudden and painful, and at the same time consciously choosing a different, freer relationship trajectory with endings, goodbyes and new beginnings.

## Questions for self-reflection

- What do I know about periods of loss and new beginnings in the history of my kind?
- Is there experience in my family of sharp, traumatic breaks, about which it is customary to keep silent?
- To what extent is my fear of completion or change personal to me and not inherited?

## Practical plan

1. Ask an older relative about a period of loss or forced new beginning in family history that has not been openly discussed before.
2. Write down one subconscious guideline for change or loss that may have been inherited and question whether it is still relevant to you personally.
3. Identify one exhausted stage of your own life that you are putting off completing, and make a plan to gradually close it.
4. Create or support one family tradition of conscious farewell to the old — a year-end ritual, a symbolic farewell to an important milestone — that will become a resource for future generations.
5. If there is an opportunity and readiness, carefully talk with a relative about the topic of losses or breaks, which was previously kept silent in the family.
6. Write a short letter (it is not necessary to send) to one of the ancestors with thanks for the transferred ability to be reborn and recognition of the pain due to losses that you want to heal in this generation.
