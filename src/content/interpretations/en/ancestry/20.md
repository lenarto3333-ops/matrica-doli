# 20. Judgement — Ancestral Tasks, Patterns, and Resources

## Brief Essence

The ancestral script of this energy is symbolically associated with a figure in the family whose history contains an unlived, unfinished judgment: an ancestor who was unjustly accused or punished, whose rights to property or inheritance were unfairly taken away, or, conversely, an ancestor who himself passed a too harsh, unforgiving verdict on someone in the family and never forgave it - a quarrel over land, property or inheritance that split the family into decades, a rift between siblings that no one has ever dared to mend. Often in this story there is another side - an ancestor who, despite difficult circumstances, managed to honestly sum up what he had experienced, let go of resentment and start life anew: survive loss, ostracism, exile or catastrophe and still find the strength to be reborn, and not stuck in bitterness forever. Both scenarios—the unfinished, frozen conflict and the capacity for true rebirth after loss—remain side by side in the lineage, waiting for a descendant to consciously choose which of the two legacies to continue.

The ancestral task of this energy is to learn honestly, without excessive severity and without silencing, to sum up the ancestral histories of resentment, judgment and division, and to take from the lineage not an unfinished conflict or a thirst for justice at any cost, but a real gift of rebirth, ancestral intuition and the ability to start anew after the heaviest losses.

## Natural Strength

The ancestral resource of this energy is a symbolic legacy of true resilience and rebirth: if the ancestors survived loss, exile or destruction and were still able to start life anew, this ability to rise after the worst remains alive in the family and can awaken in the descendants with new clarity just when they themselves need to start a new phase.

The second resource is the ancestral memory of clairvoyance and intuition: if there were people in the family who knew how to sense the approach of danger, recognize dishonesty or make the right decisions at a critical moment, this sensitivity is passed on to descendants as a heightened intuition, capable of guiding them even when rational grounds for a decision are lacking.

The third force is the family experience of honesty in the face of difficult circumstances: if the family valued the ability to admit the truth, even when it is inconvenient, and to take responsibility for one's own decisions, this ability, cleansed of the rigidity and relentlessness that could accompany it, leaves the descendants with the ability to look at life soberly, without running away from difficult conclusions.

## Expression in the Positive

When ancestral karma is worked out, the descendant is able to consciously complete what was left unfinished a generation ago: to look honestly at the ancestral history of resentment, injustice or division, to recognize the pain of all parties without the need to justify or blame anyone, and with this inner act of justice to free both himself and the family from a burden that no one has dared to lift.

In the plus, a person becomes the one who breaks the ancestral cycle of unfinished judgment and brings the very gift of rebirth into modern times: instead of passing on unresolved resentment or the habit of splitting relationships due to the principle of "who is right", he learns to close conflicts honestly and with dignity, preserving relationships where the family previously lost them due to unforgiveness.

Ancestral resources of intuition and honesty turn into a recognized gift of supporting the family at critical moments: a person takes on the role of someone who is able to calmly and fairly analyze a difficult family situation - inheritance, property, an old grudge - relying on the ancestral memory of the courage to face the truth without turning the discussion into a new war.

Another plus sign is the ability to talk about difficult ancestral histories of trial, exile or division without judgment towards the ancestors: to recognize the circumstances in which they lived and made decisions with understanding, and at the same time consciously choose for themselves another, more just and compassionate way of experiencing conflicts.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, the ancestral resource in the plus is manifested as the ability to honestly end long-standing family disputes, without being afraid to be the first to recognize the share of her own family in the conflict: she finds words that acknowledge the pain without rekindling it.

She is able to convey to her daughters and younger relatives not the fear of injustice or the habit of holding a grudge for years, but an example of how you can honestly summarize a difficult experience and start a new stage with dignity, relying on ancestral intuition, and not on bitterness.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, the ancestral resource is manifested as the ability to make fair, not punitive decisions in family matters - inheritance, property, long-standing disputes - weighing the interests of all parties, and not just his rightness or formal law.

He takes on the role of the person in the family who knows how to be the first to reach out for reconciliation after a long-standing conflict, breaking the possible family script of unfinished court and showing the younger relatives that real strength is the ability to forgive and start anew, not to be right at any cost.

## Expression in the Negative

When ancestral karma is not worked out, the descendant may unconsciously carry within himself the ancestral thirst for justice at all costs: a deep, often vague feeling that someone in the family's past has not been rewarded, and this unfinished history manifests itself as excessive principledness, a tendency to protracted conflicts or even lawsuits over property or inheritance, which are actually a continuation of an ancient, even ancestral split.

Another manifestation of the negative is the inherited pattern of endless mutual blame: if someone in the family could not forgive or forget the offense for years, the descendant may unconsciously reproduce this same pattern in his own relationship, holding the offense on loved ones for decades instead of honestly talking about it and letting it go.

On the negative side, there is also a possible ancestral pattern of escape from an honest conclusion: if the ancestors were too painful to look back on the experienced loss or injustice, and they chose silence and silence instead of honestly dwelling on the grief, the descendant can inherit this same inability to take stock, stuck in uncertainty where a clear, honest decision is needed.

Another negative sign is ancestral exhaustion from unresolved property disputes: if the family has been fighting for inheritance, land or property for a long time, the descendant may unconsciously continue this struggle - literally or symbolically - spending energy on an ancient war, the meaning of which has long been lost for him personally.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is contact with a family topic that has traditionally been silenced: a story of an unfair trial, banishment, ostracism, or a split over property, about which it was not customary to speak directly in the family. The second trigger is an actual family dispute about inheritance, property or an old grudge, which unconsciously activates a much older, even ancestral conflict. The third trigger is a situation that requires you to personally take stock of a difficult period, when it seems easier to either blame someone else or avoid the discussion altogether.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to find out as much as possible about the history of the trial, injustice or family split in the family: which of the ancestors suffered from an unfair decision, who himself gave someone too harsh a verdict, how this story ended, and whether it ended at all. The second step is to consciously separate one's own attitude to justice and conflicts from family scripts: to recognize what part of excessive principledness or, on the contrary, fear of standing up for the truth does not actually belong to a person personally, but is inherited from the family.

The third step is to mentally or in writing complete what the family failed to complete: to acknowledge the injustice if there was one, to mourn the loss if it remained unlived, and to consciously decide not to pass on this unresolved burden. The fourth step is, if possible and willing, to carefully broach the topic of an old property or family dispute with relatives, looking not for victory, but for an honest, just conclusion.

The fifth step is to consciously create new family examples of honest, compassionate resolution: moments when conflict is resolved through conversation and acknowledgment, not through silence or disconnection.

## Main Task

The main ancestral task of this energy is to honestly finish what remained unclosed in the family: injustice, resentment or division, and to pass on to the next generations not a thirst for justice at any cost and not a habit of holding grudges for years, but a real ancestral gift of rebirth, intuition and the ability to start anew after the heaviest losses. This means respectfully accepting the circumstances in which the ancestors lived, and at the same time consciously choosing for oneself a more just and compassionate way of living family conflicts.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What do I know about the history of injustice, judgment or property division among my ancestors?
- Am I holding an old grudge against someone in the family instead of honestly talking about it and letting it go?
- Am I not continuing, consciously or not, the ancient ancestral struggle for property, justice or recognition?

## Practical Plan

1. Ask an older relative about a history of litigation, injustice, or division over property in the family that has not previously been discussed openly.
2. Write down one ancestral grudge or conflict that still affects family relationships, and question whether it is worth carrying it further for you personally.
3. Identify one situation in your life where you reproduce the ancestral pattern of unfinished trial or endless accusation, and trace its possible ancestral root.
4. If there is an actual property or inheritance dispute in the family, contact a mediator or specialist to resolve it fairly, and not through a protracted conflict.
5. If there is an opportunity and readiness, carefully talk with a relative about an ancient family history of injustice or division.
6. Write a short letter (it is not necessary to send) to one of the ancestors with the recognition of the injustice experienced by him and the intention to complete this story for himself and his descendants.
