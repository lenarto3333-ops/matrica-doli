# 14. Temperance — Relationships and Partnership

## Brief Essence

In a partnership, this energy is not looking for a storm of passions or a cold distance, but a living, warm balance in which both partners feel heard and balanced. Relationships here are rarely built on drama; what is more important is not the intensity of emotions, but how naturally two people complement each other, merging into something whole, without losing their own individuality. The main challenge of partnership is to learn to transform this natural capacity for harmony into a living, honest connection, and not into endless avoidance of conflicts and suppression of one's own needs for the sake of external peace, which does not really make anyone happy.

## Natural Strength

The natural resource of this energy in a relationship is the ability to feel exactly what a partner needs at a specific moment and adapt to it without losing yourself. This is a rare ability to create an atmosphere in which another person feels accepted, understood and safe.

The second strength is the natural gift of diplomacy in difficult conversations: a person with this energy knows how to tell the difficult truth gently, to find a wording that does not hurt, but does not hide the essence. She rarely brings the matter to an open conflict, because she knows how to defuse tension long before it turns into a quarrel.

The third force is attention to the balance of giving and receiving in relationships: the partnership of this energy rarely turns into a one-sided service to one of the two, because a person intuitively feels when the balance is shifted and tries to restore it.

The fourth power is the ability to bring aesthetics and creativity to relationships: joint rituals, beautiful little things, an atmosphere of coziness and harmony. This energy is able to make the couple's everyday life warmer and more beautiful than it would be without this talent for combination.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, the partnership is built on lively, honest harmony: a person not only adjusts to the partner, but also confidently voices his own needs, finding solutions that suit both. It's not addiction—it's a mature form of caring that the relationship remains fair for two, not just convenient for one.

Communication in the positive is direct and soft at the same time: uncomfortable topics are voiced in a timely manner, diplomatically, without silence and without sharp accusations. Partners in such a union feel that they can talk about difficulties without fear that honesty will destroy the atmosphere of harmony.

In the positive, this energy brings a rare quality to the relationship - the ability to reconcile conflicts on the ground, and not just on the surface: when a disagreement arises, a person looks for a solution that truly takes into account the needs of both, and not just quickly extinguishes tension with the appearance of agreement.

A creative approach to the life of a couple becomes an instrument of closeness: joint projects, rituals, aesthetics of everyday life, a person knows how to turn all this into a source of warmth and combination, making the everyday special.

Another sign of a positive partnership is the ability to support the partner's individuality without dissolving it in common harmony: a person understands that the true balance of two is not the erasure of differences, but their respectful coexistence.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, a positive partnership manifests itself as the courage to speak honestly about one's own needs, without waiting for the accumulated fatigue to develop into a silent resentment. She naturally feels when to initiate an important conversation and when to give the couple time for natural alignment.

She knows how to combine softness with firmness in matters important to her, choosing an honest dialogue instead of quiet self-denial. Her ability to create harmony is aimed at a living, fair connection, and not at constant adjustment in order to preserve peace at any cost.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, a positive partnership manifests itself as the ability to initiate honest conversations about the needs of both, instead of silently accumulating dissatisfaction until the moment of explosion. He does not adapt to his partner just because it is easier, but dares to voice what is important to him as well.

He knows how to combine diplomacy with firm respect for his own boundaries: if his partner needs support or a compromise, he sincerely meets it, but does not lose his own position where it is really important.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the partnership turns into chronic self-denial: a person is so afraid of disturbing the peace or offending the partner that he constantly gives in to his own desires, accumulating unspoken dissatisfaction, which over time turns into a heavy, silent burden in the relationship.

Communication in the negative becomes unique: instead of a direct, diplomatic conversation about the need for changes, a person keeps silent about the problem, hoping that it will disappear by itself, or, conversely, after a long restraint, explodes with emotions disproportionate to the specific occasion.

Another manifestation of the negative is getting stuck in a relationship that has long lost its balance, due to the fear of conflict or divorce. A person stays in a couple not out of love, but out of unwillingness to go through the uncomfortable but necessary process of having an honest conversation about what isn't working.

In the deeper negative, the partnership turns into a cycle of suppression and explosion with no real lesson: the person keeps silent again and again, accumulates irritation, and then explodes, then tries to "make it right" with silent adjustment again, and the cycle repeats itself.

The fifth manifestation of the negative is excessive yielding, which deprives the relationship of honesty: a person is so eager to avoid any tension that he loses his own position so much that the partner ceases to understand what he really wants.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, the negative manifestation often looks like years of silent adaptation to the needs of a partner, after which comes exhaustion and a sense of lost self. She holds back her displeasure for so long that when it finally breaks through, the reaction seems disproportionate and sudden to her partner.

The tendency to excessive indulgence manifests itself in the negative as an inability to distinguish a healthy compromise from self-denial: she can give up her own desires for years just because it is "easier" to maintain external peace in the couple.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, the negative display is often disguised as calmness - in fact, it is a way to avoid an uncomfortable, confrontational conversation about real feelings. He can avoid the topic that bothers his partner for years, convincing himself that "everything is normal" instead of honestly admitting the accumulated tension.

The negative tendency to avoid manifests itself as passivity at critical moments of the relationship: when the partner most needs a clear answer or solution, he postpones the conversation, hoping that the tension will dissipate by itself, which usually does not happen.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a long-term accumulation of unexpressed needs in the relationship without an attempt to honestly talk about the imbalance. When tension builds up for a long time, it eventually erupts in a sharp, uncontrollable flash instead of gradually leveling off.

The second trigger is an experience where trying to stand up for yourself in a past relationship led to a painful conflict or breakup. After such an experience, there is either excessive indulgence in a new relationship, or, on the contrary, a willingness to avoid any deep intimacy, as soon as there is a hint of such a scenario.

The third trigger is the pressure of a partner or environment to maintain the image of an "easy", conflict-free partner, even when internally everything has long been ready for an honest conversation about changes. The constant expectation of "don't cause trouble" depletes this energy faster than anything else.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to learn to check whether a compromise is really necessary before automatically giving in to your own needs. It is useful to pause before the next agreement: is it really the best decision for both, or is it a habitual escape from an uncomfortable conversation.

The second step is to practice direct, diplomatic communication about your own needs instead of silently building resentment until the moment of explosion: it is better to say something difficult in time than to let the tension break out in a sudden flash.

The third step is to honestly distinguish true harmony from self-denial due to fear of conflict: before remaining silent once again, it is worth asking yourself what exactly pushes you to remain silent - the real insignificance of the issue or the usual fear of embarrassment.

The fourth step is to bring into the relationship the practice of regularly, honestly discussing the balance of giving and receiving, so that the imbalance does not build up to the point where an emotional outburst or breakup seems like the only way out.

The fifth step is to work on the fear of conflict or, conversely, the fear of loneliness separately, through self-reflection or the support of a specialist, so as not to transfer this fear to a partner in the form of chronic silence or, conversely, sudden outbursts.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in a partnership is to learn to combine a natural capacity for harmony with honesty about one's own needs, so that the balance in the relationship is fair for both, and not just convenient for maintaining external peace. When this task is accomplished, the partnership becomes a space that is not afraid of uncomfortable conversations: it knows how to honestly voice what is important, and at the same time it knows how to find solutions that keep the connection alive and warm much longer than a relationship built on the silent self-denial of one of the two.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a need in my relationship that I am keeping quiet instead of having an honest conversation about balance?
- How do I react to a disagreement with my partner — do I look for a fair solution for both or do I immediately give in to my own desires?
- Did I happen to restrain discontent for so long that it exploded out of proportion to the occasion?
- How much of my willingness to compromise is based on real harmony, and how much — on the fear of conflict at the moment?
- Do I give myself the right to my own position in the relationship, or do I always adjust for the sake of peace?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one need in the relationship that has been kept silent for a long time and express it to your partner honestly and diplomatically this week.
2. Before agreeing to another compromise, pause and ask yourself: "Is this really the best solution for both of us, or is it just an escape from discomfort?"
3. Have a conversation with your partner about whether the balance of giving and receiving feels fair to both of you.
4. Honestly write out one habit in a couple where silent dissatisfaction has accumulated, and determine a plan for an honest, gradual conversation about it.
5. Noticing the desire to remain silent once again, do the opposite - say one small truth out loud this week.
6. Once a month, have a conversation with your partner about whether the harmony in the relationship feels alive and not forced.
