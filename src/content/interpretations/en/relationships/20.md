# 20. Judgement — Relationships and Partnership

## Brief Essence

In a partnership, this energy brings a rare ability to be honest - with such a person, the partner often feels that the relationship is built on real, not recorded ground, because she knows how to honestly say what is, even when it is uncomfortable. This energy knows how to deeply feel another person, almost intuitively understanding what is really going on in a relationship, and it is this acuteness of feeling, combined with the ability to hear one's own inner call, that often tells when a partnership needs real changes. The main challenge of the partnership of this arcana is two-fold: on the one hand, there is a risk that this very ability to make an honest assessment turns against the relationship itself — an endless internal judgment, when a person again and again re-evaluates already experienced conflicts or decisions of the couple, not allowing them to remain peacefully in the past; on the other hand, there is the risk of transferring open accounts with one's own family to the partnership, when insults to parents or family scenarios are unconsciously projected onto the partner, and ordinary misunderstandings escalate to the scale of a real "trial" between two people. The true maturity of this arcana partnership is learning to make an honest but compassionate verdict on the couple's shared past, using that clarity as a way to renew the relationship, not as an excuse for endless litigation.

## Natural Strength

The natural resource of this energy in relationships is the ability to see a partnership as it really is: a person with this energy rarely indulges in illusions about relationships, and that is why he is able to notice both real problems and the real value of what is between him and his partner.

The second strength is a sharp intuitive sensitivity to a partner: this person often feels the state and needs of the other half faster than they manage to voice them, and this sensitivity, if used carefully, allows you to react to difficulties in a couple much earlier, before they turn into a serious crisis.

The third strength is the ability to sense the moment when the relationship needs renewal: this person is not afraid to admit that the usual format of partnership has exhausted itself, and has the courage to initiate an honest conversation about changes where others would prefer to continue living by inertia.

The fourth force is a deep respect for honest, fair agreements in a couple: a person naturally gravitates to relationships built on real equality and truth, not on silence, and it is this demand for honesty, despite its complexity, that ultimately makes the partnership stronger.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a partnership is built on sincere, honest trust: a person knows how to openly talk about difficulties in a couple, draw real conclusions from them and move on, without holding old grudges as a hidden trump card for future quarrels.

Positive communication is filled with mature directness: a person knows how to honestly admit his own share of responsibility in a conflict, without shifting all the blame to the partner, and at the same time does not take on more than is really his.

On the plus side, this energy brings a rare quality to the relationship — the ability to let go of already passed crises: instead of returning again and again to an old quarrel or partner's mistake, the couple honestly takes stock together and consciously decides to leave it behind.

Sharp intuition becomes a tool of care, not control: a person senses when a partner is having a hard time and delicately creates a space for conversation without pressing or demanding immediate explanations.

Another sign of a positive partnership is a healthy attitude towards relationship renewal: a person is not afraid to honestly say when the couple has outgrown the old format of interaction, and together with the partner consciously builds new arrangements instead of holding on to the usual for fear of change.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, a positive partnership manifests itself as a calm, honest presence that is not afraid to call a spade a spade: she openly talks about what worries her in a relationship, and at the same time knows how to listen to her partner without the desire to immediately make a final judgment on his words or actions.

She knows how not to transfer unsettled accounts from her own family to her partner: instead of reacting to him through the prism of old resentments against parents or family scenarios, she learns to see this particular person and this situation as they are.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, a positive partnership is manifested as the ability to honestly admit his own mistakes in a relationship, without perceiving it as a threat to his own strength: he knows how to say "here I was wrong" and move on, without needing to make excuses or punish himself for it for years.

He is naturally able to sense the moment when his partner does not need advice or a solution, but simply an honest recognition of her feelings, and this ability to pause before his own "verdict" makes the relationship deeper and safer for both.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the partnership can develop in one of two opposite scenarios. The first is the endless internal judgment of the relationship: the person mentally returns to old conflicts, insults or disappointments again and again, not allowing himself or the couple to simply live the lesson and close this page, due to which the atmosphere in the relationship gradually permeates with old, unspoken tension.

With this manifestation of the negative, a seemingly long-resolved dispute can unexpectedly "surface" in a new quarrel, because in fact it was never really closed - only temporarily postponed until enough "evidence" is collected for a new trial.

The second, opposite manifestation of the negative is the transfer of family scores to the partner: a person unconsciously projects unresolved resentments about parents or family scenarios onto the other half, reacting to ordinary partner misunderstandings with the intensity of an ancient, much deeper pain, which in fact has no direct relation to this person.

The third manifestation of the minus, closely related to the second, is the escalation of conflicts to a formal confrontation: instead of an honest, timely conversation, the accumulated claims can flow into sharp, categorical ultimatums or even formal disputes - property, legal - that turn the partnership into a kind of legal process instead of a space of trust.

Another manifestation of the minus is exhaustion from the role of someone who always has to "prove justice": a person feels a constant need to receive from a partner recognition of his own rightness or an apology for the past, and until this happens, he internally cannot allow himself to move on, even when the partner has long been ready for reconciliation.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, the endless internal judgment of the relationship often looks like a habit of returning to old images in moments of new misunderstandings - she can remember specific words or actions of her partner for years, using them as an argument in each new argument, instead of honestly closing each of them separately. The opposite side is the transference of family pain, when a woman unconsciously expects from her partner the recognition or apology that she did not receive from her parents at one time, and reacts painfully when he cannot fill this long-standing void.

The tendency to escalate conflicts in the negative manifests itself especially acutely in moments when she feels injustice: instead of a gradual, honest clarification of the situation, there may be a sharp transition immediately to categorical demands or threats to break off the relationship.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, the negative manifestation is often disguised as a cold principle in the relationship - in fact, it is a way to protect himself from admitting his own vulnerability or guilt, which he considers inappropriate for the image of a strong, self-sufficient partner. Instead of having an honest conversation about his own doubts or mistakes, he can keep a silent, unspoken distance for years, which gradually erodes the closeness of the couple.

The opposite manifestation is the transference of an unresolved conflict with his own family to his partner, when the man unconsciously expects from her the recognition or support that he did not receive from his parents, and perceives common partner misunderstandings as confirmation of the old feeling that he is never appreciated enough.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is the moment when the partner (knowingly or not) reminds with behavior or words of an open conflict with his own parents - that's when the reaction often turns out to be disproportionately sharp, because it does not come from the current situation, but from an old, not yet spoken pain.

The second trigger is the long-term lack of honest summation after a conflict in a couple: when the quarrel seems to die down by itself, but neither party has actually admitted their share of the fault and received recognition of their rightness, the accumulated undecided sooner or later breaks through a new, much sharper confrontation.

The third trigger is the feeling that the partner does not recognize the obvious, in the person's opinion, injustice or is not ready to honestly admit his own mistake. It is where this energy most needs an honest verdict, and instead receives evasion or denial, that the risk of escalation grows fastest.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to learn to close each conflict separately, immediately after its resolution, consciously agreeing with the partner not to raise this topic again as an argument in future quarrels.

The second step is to practice pausing before an intense reaction to a partner and ask yourself if this intensity is really about the current situation or if it is bringing up an old, unresolved family theme.

The third step is to consciously create space as a couple for an honest, calm debriefing after difficult periods — not with the goal of winning an argument, but with the goal of honestly hearing each other out and moving on.

The fourth step is to learn to ask for recognition directly, instead of waiting for the partner to figure it out on his own to compensate for an old, unfulfilled need for justice or recognition.

The fifth step is to practice honest acknowledgment of your own share of responsibility in the conflict, rather than waiting for your partner to do so, which often unlocks a stuck argument faster than any demand.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in a partnership is to learn to make an honest but compassionate verdict on the couple's shared past and use this clarity as a way to renew the relationship, not as an excuse for endless litigation. When this task is carried out, the partnership becomes a space of real, not recorded trust: it knows how to honestly close conflicts without dragging them into the future, distinguish between own pain and pain inherited from the family, and together consciously build new, renewed arrangements instead of holding on to old grudges or the usual, exhausted relationship format.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Are there old conflicts in my relationship that I mentally return to again and again, although they should have been closed long ago?
- Do I transfer onto my partner resentments or expectations that actually concern my parents or family?
- Am I ready to admit my own share of responsibility in the conflict without waiting for my partner to admit his first?

## Practical Plan

1. Once a week, deliberately "close" one old topic in the relationship, agreeing with the partner not to raise it as an argument in new disputes.
2. Having noticed a sharp reaction to a partner, stop and ask yourself: "Is it really about him now, or about my family and the past?"
3. Choose one moment this week to have an honest conversation with your partner about the results of the last difficult period in the relationship.
4. Once a week, directly ask your partner for recognition or support in a specific matter, instead of waiting for him to guess on his own.
5. After the next conflict, be the first to admit your own share of responsibility before expecting recognition from your partner.
6. Once a month, honestly evaluate together with your partner whether the relationship is built on real trust or on old, open resentments that need attention.
