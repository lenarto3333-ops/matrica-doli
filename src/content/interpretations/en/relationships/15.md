# 15. The Devil — Relationships and Partnership

## Brief Essence

In a partnership, this energy seeks intensity—a deep, passionate, almost hypnotic intimacy in which both partners feel drawn to each other on a level deeper than words. Relationships here are rarely fresh; this energy knows how to see through a partner - their true desires, fears, weaknesses - and it is this depth of understanding that often creates a bond that feels like destiny. The main challenge of partnership is to learn to transform this insight and magnetism into sincere intimacy, and not into a tool of control, jealousy or dependence on each other, when passion replaces trust, and the desire to possess - true love.

## Natural Strength

The natural resource of this energy in a relationship is the ability to feel a partner so deeply that an almost wordless understanding arises between two people: a person with this energy guesses the desires, moods and hidden needs of the other before he himself realizes them.

The second force is a powerful personal magnetism that makes the relationship with this person exciting: next to her, the partner feels alive, wanted, in the center of attention. This passion rarely leaves a relationship fresh or indifferent.

The third force is attentiveness to what actually holds a couple together: a person with this energy intuitively feels the real dynamics of power and desire in a relationship and knows how, if he wants, to deepen the connection by bringing to the surface what the partner is afraid to talk about directly.

The fourth power is the ability to bring a sense of value and abundance into relationships: generosity, luxury of experiences, willingness to invest in a joint life materially and emotionally. This energy can make relationships rich, not just functional.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, a partnership is built on a living, honest passion: a person not only attracts and fascinates a partner, but also gives him real freedom, finding a balance between deep closeness and respect for the individuality of the other. It's not possessiveness, it's a mature form of desire that wants a partner to flourish, not depend.

Positive communication is open and honest about desires: a person does not hide his own needs and does not play games for power over a partner, but directly speaks about what he wants, trusting that honesty strengthens the bond, not weakens it.

In the positive, this energy brings a rare quality to a relationship — the ability to see a partner for who they really are and love that authenticity, not an idealized image or a convenient role that the partner plays to maintain the relationship.

Material generosity in the positive becomes a tool of closeness: a person knows how to share resources, create comfort and pleasure for two, without using it as a lever of control or a way to buy affection.

Another sign of a positive partnership is the ability to keep passion alive for years without allowing the relationship to settle into a routine: a person understands that true intensity is born from constant honesty and attention, not from drama or manipulation.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, a positive partnership manifests itself as a confident, open sensuality: she is not afraid to show her own desire and attractiveness, while not using them as a tool to control her partner. She naturally feels when to get closer, and when to give her partner space for his own development.

She knows how to combine passion with trust, choosing honest intimacy over jealous control. Her ability to see through her partner is aimed at deeper understanding and support, not at the search for leverage or proof of his fidelity.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, a positive partnership manifests itself as the ability to be sincere and vulnerable, despite natural strength and magnetism: he does not hide his feelings behind a mask of confidence, but allows his partner to see the real him, trusting that intimacy does not weaken his position.

He knows how to combine passionate interest with respect for his partner's freedom: if she needs space or independence, he sincerely gives her this space, without perceiving it as a threat to his own possession or control.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, partnership turns into a struggle for power: a person is so afraid of losing control over the relationship or over the partner that it turns intimacy into a constant competition — who is more dependent, who is less vulnerable, who holds the leverage.

Jealousy in the negative becomes the central theme of the relationship: a person monitors, suspects, checks messages and contacts, perceiving any independence of the partner as a threat to his own position, instead of trusting the connection that he himself attracted.

Another manifestation of the negative is dependence on the intensity of the relationship: if the passion is blunted, there is a panic of emptiness, which a person tries to fill with drama, conflicts or even new connections on the side, instead of working on the depth of the existing relationship.

At a deeper disadvantage, the partnership turns into a cycle of manipulation and attachment without true intimacy: the person uses knowledge of the partner's weaknesses again and again to keep him close, and the partner, in turn, stays more out of dependence than out of genuine choice.

The fifth manifestation of the negative is the materialization of the relationship: love and attention begin to be measured by gifts, status or financial contribution, and not by the sincerity of feelings, turning the partnership into an unspoken agreement instead of a living connection.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, the negative manifestation often looks like chronic jealousy and the need to control every step of the partner - she can unconsciously check, suspect and demand proof of fidelity, even when there is no real reason for this, turning the relationship into a constant field of tension.

The negative tendency to addiction manifests itself as an inability to imagine oneself outside of a relationship, even a destructive one: she can stay with a partner who harms her for years, convincing herself that without this relationship her life will lose meaning.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, the negative manifestation is often disguised as care or guardianship — in fact, it is a way to maintain control over his partner under the pretext of protection or care. He can limit her freedom, circle of communication or independence, convincing himself and others that he is doing it "for her sake".

The tendency to addiction in the negative manifests itself as a compulsive need to constantly confirm one's own attractiveness and power through new conquests or superficial connections, instead of investing in the depth of existing relationships.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is the experience of betrayal or the loss of an important relationship in the past, which leaves a deep fear that trust always ends in pain. This fear pushes for preventive control instead of open intimacy.

The second trigger is the feeling that the partner begins to live his own life, independent of the couple - with new interests, friends, successes. Instead of joy for the partner, the anxiety of losing power over the relationship appears, which manifests itself as jealousy or attempts to limit his freedom.

The third trigger is a prolonged lack of passion and intensity in the relationship, which is perceived by this energy as a threat to the very essence of the relationship. Routine and predictability drain this energy faster than anything else, because it disrupts the very thing for which it builds relationships.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is learning to distinguish between genuine care for the relationship and control dictated by fear of loss. It is useful to pause before the next inspection or demand for a report: whether it is about a real threat or about an old fear that has no relation to a specific partner.

The second step is to practice direct conversation about your own fears and jealousies instead of silent control or covert manipulation: it is better to honestly say "I'm afraid of losing you" than to monitor or restrict.

The third step is to consciously invest in the depth of the relationship instead of constantly searching for new intensity: joint projects, honest conversations about desires, intimacy rituals that support passion without the need for drama.

The fourth step is to learn to give your partner true freedom, trusting that a relationship built on sincerity does not need a cage to stay strong.

The fifth step is to work with the old fear of betrayal or abandonment separately, through self-reflection or professional support, so as not to transfer this fear to the partner in the form of control or constant tests of his fidelity.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in partnership is to learn to combine natural passion and a deep understanding of the partner with trust, so that intimacy is a space of freedom for both, and not a territory of control of one over the other. When this task is accomplished, a partnership becomes a space that is not afraid of true vulnerability: it knows how to maintain intensity through honesty, not through power, and that is why it is able to stay alive and passionate much longer than relationships built on jealousy and hidden struggles for control.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a moment in my relationship when I try to control my partner out of fear of losing him, instead of trusting the connection?
- How much of my passion for a partner is connected with a sincere desire for intimacy, and how much — with the need to possess and control?
- Do I give my partner real freedom, or do I perceive his independence as a threat to our relationship?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one situation where jealousy or a need to control arose without a real basis, and honestly explore the fear behind it.
2. Before another check or demand for an explanation, give yourself a pause and ask: "Is this about a real threat or about my old fear?"
3. Have an honest conversation with your partner about your own fears of loss, instead of silent control or hidden suspicions.
4. Consciously create one moment of deep intimacy this week — through an honest conversation, shared ritual, or openness about desires, without the need for drama.
5. Having noticed the desire to limit the partner's freedom, do the opposite - consciously support his independent interest or plan.
6. Once a month, have a conversation with your partner about whether the relationship feels like a space of freedom or a field of hidden control.
