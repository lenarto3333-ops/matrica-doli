# 16. The Tower — Relationships and Partnership

## Brief Essence

In a partnership, this energy rarely lives in a relationship evenly and predictably - they rather resemble a cyclical story of upheavals and restorations, in which the couple either goes through serious trials together and becomes stronger from it, or repeatedly destroys what they have built, without having time to really preserve it. This energy can sense when a relationship is based on falsehood or fear, rather than sincerity, and it is this sense that often pushes to sharp, uncompromising decisions where another, softer way could be found. The main challenge of the partnership is to learn to transform this destructive insight into honest, constructive openness, and not into angry outbursts, jealous storms, or the habit of breaking off the relationship abruptly, instead of giving it a chance for real reconstruction.

## Natural Strength

The natural resource of this energy in relationships is the ability to feel when the partnership has ceased to be honest: a person with this energy rarely lives for long in the illusion that "everything is fine" when in fact something in the relationship has rotted or stopped developing.

The second strength is the ability not to be afraid of serious conversations and challenges: where others try to avoid conflict or difficult topics, this energy is ready to directly name the problem, even if it threatens a comfortable but false calm.

The third strength is a rare resilience in the face of a crisis in a relationship: if a couple is experiencing a real upheaval - betrayal, loss, serious illness, financial collapse - this person is able to become a pillar without losing the ability to act and feel.

The fourth power is the ability to build relationships anew after a difficult period, and not live in constant fear of repeating the past: this energy, if it has learned to trust the process, knows how to start with a clean slate, keeping valuable experience, but not the burden of resentment.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, a partnership is built on an honest, even harsh, truth delivered with care: a person does not hide problems for the sake of illusory peace, but speaks directly about what is not working, giving the couple a chance to change before the tension becomes unbearable.

Positive communication is open about difficulties: a person does not remain silent when he feels a crack in the relationship, but voices it in a timely and constructive manner, trusting that an honest conversation about the crisis strengthens the connection more than years of feigned well-being.

In the positive, this energy brings a rare quality to the relationship — the ability not to be afraid of serious challenges: the crisis experienced together, instead of destroying the couple, becomes a point from which the partnership enters a new, deeper level of trust and closeness.

Stability in the positive becomes a support for two: a person knows how to be there in the most difficult moments of a partner, without panicking and without running away from difficult emotions, giving both the space to survive difficulties, without losing connection with each other.

Another sign of a positive partnership is the ability to let go of what has truly drained you with dignity: if the relationship no longer serves the growth of both, the person admits it honestly and ends the relationship without destructive drama, taking care of his own dignity and the dignity of the partner.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, a positive partnership manifests itself as sincere, fearless openness: she is not afraid to tell her partner about what bothers her or does not suit her, and she does it in time, and not when her patience has already run out and the accumulated anger erupts uncontrollably.

She knows how to be a support during real crises, maintaining clarity and care even when the circumstances are difficult: instead of panicking looking for the culprits, she focuses on how to go through difficulties together and come out of them stronger.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, a positive partnership manifests itself as the ability to recognize difficulties in a relationship directly, instead of silently accumulating irritation: he voices a problem as soon as he notices it, trusting that an honest conversation will better preserve the connection than years of hidden resentment.

He knows how to be a reliable support during serious upheavals, while remaining emotionally available: the crisis does not make him cold or aloof, but on the contrary, reveals his ability to truly support his partner by sharing his own vulnerability.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the partnership turns into a cycle of accumulating tension and sudden explosions: a person remains silent about dissatisfaction for a long time, and then bursts into a sharp quarrel or accusations out of proportion to the specific occasion, leaving the partner confused and hurt.

Destructiveness in the negative becomes the central theme of relationships: a person breaks the connection abruptly and permanently at the first serious crisis, not giving the couple a chance to recover, as if it is easier to destroy everything at once than to go through the painful but gradual process of restoring trust.

Another manifestation of the negative is the dependence on the intensity of the conflict: if the relationship becomes calm and predictable, there is a subconscious desire to provoke a crisis - with quarrels, accusations, jealousy - in order to feel again the sharpness of the connection, which a person confuses with real closeness.

In the deeper negative, the partnership turns into a constant expectation of betrayal or collapse: a person lives in hypervigilance, looking for signs that the partner is about to leave or cheat, and this anxiety itself provokes the very crisis that was so feared.

The fifth manifestation of the negative is the use of past pain as a weapon: instead of honestly processing the old trauma, a person takes out the accumulated resentment on the current partner, forcing him to pay for the pain that he did not even cause.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, the negative manifestation often looks like sharp, unpredictable outbursts of accusations or anger that frighten the partner — the accumulated, long-repressed resentment breaks out in words that she later regrets, but cannot always restrain the next time.

The tendency to destroy in the negative manifests itself as a sudden, final break in the relationship after the first serious crisis: instead of trying to restore trust, she disappears or breaks off the connection abruptly, convincing herself that this is the only way to protect herself from further pain.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, a negative manifestation is often disguised as a cold impassivity - in fact, it is a way to hide an accumulated resentment, which one day breaks out in a sharp, destructive conflict, disproportionate to the specific cause of the quarrel.

The tendency to provoke crises in the negative manifests itself as a subconscious creation of tension in a relationship when everything is going too calmly - through harsh words, jealousy or accusations, as if the calm feels more threatening than the quarrel itself.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is the experience of a sudden betrayal or a painful breakup in the past, which leaves a deep fear that any stability in the relationship is illusory and will sooner or later collapse. This fear pushes for pre-emptive destruction instead of trusting the recovery process.

The second trigger is the long-term accumulation of unspoken resentment, where a person keeps silent about a problem in the relationship, hoping it will go away on its own, until the internal pressure reaches a limit and erupts in a sharp conflict.

The third trigger is long-term routine and predictability in the relationship, which is perceived by this energy as a threat to the very essence of the relationship. A lack of real depth and honesty saps that energy faster than any crisis, because it destroys the very thing it builds the partnership for.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to learn to voice your discontent in a timely manner, in small portions, instead of silently building up to an explosion. It's helpful to consciously check in with yourself every week to see if there's something in the relationship that's bothering you and if it's worth talking about now, before it turns into a storm.

The second step is to practice pausing before making a devastating decision in a conflict: Before you cut off the connection abruptly or say words that are difficult to take back, give yourself time to calm down and make sure that the decision comes from clarity, not from accumulated pain.

The third step is to consciously work with past traumas separate from the current relationship so as not to force the current partner to pay for the pain someone else caused in the past.

The fourth step is to learn to trust the quiet periods of the relationship without provoking a crisis for the sake of feeling intense: to consciously seek depth through honest conversations and shared rituals, not through drama or conflict.

The fifth step is to practice rebuilding trust gradually after each serious argument, instead of either pretending nothing happened or breaking up immediately: having an honest conversation about what happened and how to avoid it in the future.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in a partnership is to learn to transform a keen sense of truth and the ability to withstand a crisis into an honest, caring candor that strengthens the bond, not destroys it prematurely. When this task is accomplished, the partnership becomes a space that can survive real trials without falling apart at the first serious dispute: it knows how to recognize difficulties in time, recover from crises and build a stronger foundation each time, instead of remaining in a cycle of accumulating resentment and sudden destructive ruptures.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there dissatisfaction in my relationship that I silently accumulate instead of voicing it in time?
- When I break the relationship abruptly, does this decision come from clarity or from the accumulated pain and long-standing fear of betrayal?
- Do I trust calm, stable periods of relationships, or do I subconsciously look for a reason for a new crisis?

## Practical Plan

1. Once a week, consciously check yourself for unspoken dissatisfaction in the relationship and express it to your partner in small, specific portions.
2. Before making a sudden decision to break up or say harsh words, take a break for at least a day and check whether the decision changes after it.
3. Identify one past trauma or breakup that is still affecting reactions in the current relationship and work through it separately from your partner.
4. Having noticed the desire to provoke a conflict in a calm period, replace it with a conscious gesture of closeness - an honest conversation or a joint ritual.
5. After every serious argument, have a short conversation with your partner about what happened and how to avoid it next time, instead of silently accumulating resentment.
6. Once a month, honestly assess with your partner whether the relationship is built on trust and openness, or on a cycle of tension and sudden explosions.
