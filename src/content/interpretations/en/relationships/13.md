# 13. Death — Relationships and Partnership

## Short summary

In partnership, this energy seeks not the preservation of form for form's sake, but a living, honest relationship that has the right to change, renew, and, if necessary, end. Relationships here are rarely maintained by inertia; what is more important is not how many years the union lasts, but whether it continues to nourish both. The main challenge of a partnership is to learn to transform this natural willingness to end into a wise ability to renew the relationship from within, rather than to abruptly break up every time there is discomfort or an ordinary crisis that could be overcome together.

## Natural force

The natural resource of this energy in a relationship is the ability to sense when a partnership needs a deep change: a new level of honesty, a new stage of intimacy, a new form of interaction. This is a rare ability not to be afraid of the fact that the relationship is transformed, and does not remain forever as it was at the beginning.

The second force is the natural courage to complete what has really run out: a toxic connection, a relationship without reciprocity, a partnership that has long since turned into a habit without warmth. This energy does not keep others, and itself, in a form that no longer has life inside.

The third strength is attentiveness to the moments when the old version of the relationship has to give way to the new one: when the partners outgrow the initial format of the union and need an honest conversation about what the relationship will become next. This energy is able to initiate such conversations, even when they are uncomfortable.

The fourth power is the ability to accept inevitable changes in the partner and in oneself without panic: the relationships of this energy withstand the fact that people in them grow, change, sometimes differ in views - and she knows that this does not always mean the end, but often means the need for a new stage.

## Manifestation in the plus

On the plus side, a partnership is built on a lively, honest dynamic: a person does not hold on to the initial form of the relationship, if it no longer corresponds to who the partners have become, but consciously updates the agreements, roles, habits of joint life. This is not instability - it is a mature form of concern to keep the relationship alive.

Positive communication is direct and honest about change: uncomfortable topics about what is no longer working in the relationship are brought up in a timely manner, without silence and without a harsh ultimatum. Partners in such a union feel that they can talk about difficulties without fear that any mention of a crisis means an immediate breakup.

On the plus side, this energy brings a rare quality to a relationship—the courage to end what is truly exhausted with dignity, not war. If the union comes to a natural end, a person is able to let it go honestly, without trying to maintain appearances where there is no substance, and without destructive revenge or accusations.

Ending the old phase in the positive becomes a tool for intimacy: a couple going through a crisis uses it not to destroy, but to consciously restart the relationship on a new, more honest level - letting go of the old dynamics for a more alive one.

Another sign of a positive partnership is the ability to support a partner in moments of personal transformation, without keeping him in the old role for fear of losing the familiar: a person with this energy understands the value of growth for others as well as for himself.

### Manifestation in women is positive

In a woman, a positive partnership manifests itself as the courage to honestly say that the relationship needs renewal, even before the accumulated fatigue turns into a sharp break. She naturally feels when to initiate a difficult conversation about change, and when to give the couple time for a natural transformation.

She knows how to complete what is truly exhausted, without drama and revenge, choosing dignity even in a painful break. Her ability to let go of the old is to make room for something more alive, not to punish her partner or herself.

### Manifestation in the husband is positive

In a man, a positive partnership manifests itself as the ability to initiate honest conversations about the necessary changes in the relationship, instead of silently accumulating dissatisfaction until the moment of explosion. It doesn't stick to the usual dynamic just because it's easier, but dares to offer updates where it's needed.

He knows how to combine determination to finish with respect for what the relationship was before: if the union has really come to an end, he ends it honestly and with dignity, without the need to destroy everything around him to prove his own strength.

## Manifestation in the minus

In the negative, the partnership turns into a series of sharp, destructive breaks: a person reacts to any crisis or discomfort by wanting to end everything immediately, not giving the relationship a chance to go through a difficult period and come out of it strengthened. A couple can break up and get back together again and again, with no real ending or real renewal.

Communication in the negative becomes sharp and uncompromising: instead of a gradual, honest conversation about the need for change, the person issues an ultimatum or silently cuts off the connection, leaving the partner in shock and confusion where they could have gone through the crisis together.

Another manifestation of the negative is being stuck in a relationship that has long since exhausted itself, due to the fear of uncertainty after a breakup. A person stays in a couple not out of love, but out of unwillingness to go through the painful but necessary process of completion, justifying it by fear of loneliness or habit.

In the deeper negative, the partnership turns into a cycle of destruction and reconstruction without a real lesson: the person destroys the connection again and again at the first discomfort, and then tries to restore everything without changing anything in essence - and the cycle repeats itself.

The fifth manifestation of the minus is imposing sudden changes on a partner for which he is not ready: a person demands an immediate renewal of the relationship at a pace that is much faster than the pace of the other person, and perceives his resistance as betrayal or a refusal to grow together.

### Manifestation in a woman is negative

In a woman, the negative manifestation often looks like impulsive breaks at the moment of greatest stress, followed by regret and attempts to restore what could have been simply passed together, if not for the haste in the break. She destroys the connection in anger, and then suffers the consequences of this drastic step for a long time.

The tendency to abrupt endings in the negative manifests itself as an inability to distinguish between a temporary crisis and the real end of a relationship: it can break off a promising union just because it is going through an ordinary, temporary difficult period.

### Manifestation in the husband is negative

In a man, the negative manifestation is often disguised as principle - in fact, it is a way to avoid the slow, uncomfortable work on the relationship. He can abruptly break the connection at the first serious difficulty, convincing himself that "everything is over", instead of giving the couple a chance to go through the crisis together.

The propensity for destructive breakups in the negative manifests itself as sudden, unpredictable decisions to end the relationship just when the partner needs support the most, as if the inner tension demands an immediate discharge through a breakup, rather than through an honest conversation.

## What can be negative

The strongest trigger is the long-term accumulation of unspoken dissatisfaction in the relationship without an attempt to honestly talk about the need for change. When tension builds up for a long time, it eventually erupts in a sharp, uncontrollable burst instead of a gradual renewal.

The second trigger is the experience of a previous relationship ending in a painful, sudden collapse. After such an experience, there is either an excessive fear of a new attachment, or, on the contrary, a readiness to destroy new relationships in anticipation, as soon as there is a hint of such a scenario.

The third trigger is pressure from a partner or environment to keep the relationship unchanged, even when internally everything has long been ready for renewal or completion. Constantly waiting to "let things stay as they are" depletes this energy faster than anything else.

## How to return energy to the plus side

The first step is to learn to check whether a relationship has truly exhausted itself before ending it abruptly. It is useful to pause before a decisive break: whether it is the real end of the union or a temporary crisis that can be overcome together.

The second step is to practice direct, gradual communication about the need for change instead of silently building up resentment until the moment of explosion: it is better to say what is difficult in time than to allow tension to erupt in a sudden rupture.

The third step is to honestly distinguish a wise ending from a destruction out of fear or anger: before breaking off a relationship, you should ask yourself what exactly is pushing you to break up - the real exhaustion of the connection or a momentary impulse.

The fourth step is to introduce into the relationship the practice of regular, honest renewal of agreements, so that the crisis does not build up to the point where the only way out seems to be a sharp break.

The fifth step is to work on the fear of attachment or, conversely, the fear of loneliness separately, through self-reflection or the support of a specialist, so as not to transfer this fear to a partner in the form of sudden breaks or, conversely, being stuck in a dead relationship.

## The main task

The main task of this energy in a partnership is to learn to combine the courage to complete what has really run out, with the ability to renew and survive crises where the relationship still has a living resource. When this task is accomplished, a partnership becomes a space that is not afraid of change: it knows how to honestly end what it ended up being, and at the same time, it knows how to transform together, staying alive much longer than a relationship stuck in an unchanging, exhausted form.

## Questions for self-reflection

- Is there tension in my relationship that I am keeping silent instead of having an honest conversation about the need for change?
- How do I react to a crisis in a relationship — do I try to get through it together or do I immediately think about breaking up?
- Have I ever abruptly ended a relationship that actually still had a chance to be renewed?
- How much of my willingness to break up is based on the actual exhaustion of the connection, and how much — on fear or anger at the moment?
- Do I give my partner time for personal transformation, or do I keep him in the old, familiar role?

## Practical plan

1. Identify one tension in the relationship that has been kept silent for a long time and voice it honestly with your partner this week.
2. Before any sudden decision to break up, pause and ask yourself: "Is this the real end of the union or a crisis that can be overcome together?"
3. Have a conversation with your partner about which agreements or roles in the relationship have long been in need of updating.
4. Honestly write out one relationship or habit in a couple that has exhausted itself, and determine a plan for a dignified, gradual ending, rather than a sharp break.
5. Noticing the desire to cut off the connection in anger, wait a day before acting, and return to the decision with a clear head.
6. Once a month, have a conversation with your partner about how the relationship has changed during this time and what they need next.
