# 9. The Hermit — Relationships and Partnership

## In Brief

In relationships, this energy brings depth, loyalty, and quality over quantity. Partnership comes more easily when both people value a few genuine words more than constant chatter and do not treat shared silence as a problem that must be filled. It becomes a source of tension when the need for solitude turns into emotional unavailability and depth becomes a distance the partner cannot bridge, no matter how hard they try.

For this energy, partnership does not mean constant closeness without pauses. It is a space where you can be entirely yourself, including your need to be alone at times. The maturity of these relationships is revealed by how capable you are of communicating that need directly instead of disappearing without explanation and leaving your partner to wonder what happened.

## Natural Strength

The natural resource of this energy in relationships is a capacity for deep, enduring loyalty. When a person with this energy truly chooses someone, they rarely tire of them or look elsewhere for novelty. The quality of the bond matters more than the number of experiences or dramatic displays of affection.

The second strength is the ability to listen genuinely. The partner of a person with this energy often feels truly heard, rather than sensing that the other person is merely waiting for their turn to speak. This creates a rare trust in which it becomes possible to share what could not be shared with anyone else.

The third strength is respect for a partner’s personal space. A person with the ninth energy rarely tries to merge with another person until all boundaries disappear. They naturally grant their partner the right to their own silence and separate interests, expecting the same in return.

## Positive Expression

In the positive, a partnership with this energy becomes a space of deep, peaceful intimacy. Both people feel that they can be fully themselves, including their need for quiet, and that this need does not threaten their bond but makes it more resilient.

Care in the positive is expressed as meaningful rather than performative presence. You do not fill your time together with empty conversation, but know how to sit beside your partner in silence with the same warmth you bring to a deep conversation about something important. Your partner senses the value of every word precisely because there are not too many of them.

The positive also brings the ability to state your need for solitude directly: “I need a few hours alone; this is not about you,” said clearly and in time instead of disappearing into silence, which your partner might interpret as hurt or detachment.

Another sign of the positive state is a willingness to share your inner world even when it feels uncomfortable or takes effort. You understand that intimacy is built precisely through vulnerability, not only through time spent together.

### Expression in Women in the Positive

In a woman, care in relationships is expressed positively as deep, unobtrusive devotion. She does not need constant reassurance of her partner’s feelings, trusting the relationship even in silence, while opening up sincerely when her partner is genuinely ready to listen.

### Expression in Men in the Positive

In a man, care in relationships is expressed positively as calm, dependable presence. He neither pressures his partner nor demands constant attention, giving them space, while allowing himself to be emotionally open in the moments when it truly matters.

## Negative Expression

In the negative, the need for solitude becomes emotional unavailability. You disappear into yourself without explanation, leaving your partner uncertain and anxious, while the depth that should create closeness becomes an impenetrable wall instead.

### Expression in Women in the Negative

In a woman, this may appear as silent withdrawal instead of a direct conversation about difficulties. She retreats into herself when something hurts rather than saying it aloud, and her partner gradually grows tired of trying to guess what is really happening.

### Expression in Men in the Negative

In a man, this may appear as chronic emotional reserve that he justifies as “simply the way I am.” He shares facts and thoughts but rarely feelings, and over time his partner feels lonely beside someone who is physically present but emotionally out of reach.

A second negative expression is quiet superiority within the relationship. You decide inwardly that your partner “will not understand anyway” and stop even trying to explain, leaving the relationship at the level of superficial household arrangements.

## What Can Pull the Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger is an intrusion into personal space without warning. A partner who demands constant presence and leaves no time alone makes this energy defend itself through even greater detachment.

The second trigger is ridicule or dismissal of the need for quiet and depth. Comments such as “you’re shutting yourself away again,” made without any attempt to understand the reason, only reinforce the desire to hide further.

The third trigger is forced, prolonged closeness with no right to pause—constant shared activity, a noisy environment, and no separate space. This is especially draining for an energy accustomed to recovering in solitude.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to assess honestly how much your need for solitude in the relationship is truly about restoration and how much it is about avoiding a difficult conversation that feels easier not to have.

The second step is to practice communicating your need for a pause directly instead of disappearing into silence. One brief, honest sentence can spare your partner weeks of anxious speculation.

The third step is to learn to share not only finished conclusions, but also the process—your doubts, unfinished thoughts, and vulnerability—trusting that this will deepen the relationship rather than weaken it.

The fourth step is to return regularly from periods of solitude to your partner with warmth instead of carrying forward the distance created by an old defensive habit of disappearing.

The fifth step is to ask questions instead of assuming that your partner “will not understand anyway.” Give them a real chance to hear you and try to understand before deciding on their behalf.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy in relationships is to learn to combine the need for depth and solitude with a willingness to be truly seen by your partner. This means recognizing that genuine intimacy does not require uninterrupted presence without pauses, but it does require honesty about when and why a pause is needed—and the willingness to return from it with an open heart rather than another brick for the wall.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I tell my partner directly that I need solitude, or do I simply disappear without explanation?
- How much of my distance in relationships is restoration, and how much is avoidance of a difficult conversation?
- Do I share not only my thoughts with my partner, but also my feelings and doubts?
- Where does the belief that my partner “will not understand” my depth live within me?
- Do I return from periods of solitude with warmth, or with the distance left behind by self-protection?
- What kind of intimacy—with space for both of us, but no walls—do I want to build?

## Practical Plan

1. The next time you want to be alone, tell your partner directly why and for how long instead of disappearing in silence.
2. Share with your partner one doubt or feeling that you would usually keep to yourself.
3. When you notice the thought “they will not understand anyway,” test that assumption in a real conversation.
4. Return from your next period of solitude with a warm gesture or word for your partner instead of remaining silent.
5. Discuss how much time alone each of you needs and agree on it directly rather than leaving it unspoken.
