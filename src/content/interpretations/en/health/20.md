# 20. Judgement — Chakra Health Map

> This section describes only the traditional esoteric associations and symbolic counterparts accepted in the popular chakra tradition. It is not a medical diagnosis, does not establish the causes of any diseases and does not replace a doctor's consultation. If there are real symptoms or health concerns, this is a reason to consult a specialist, and not to rely on an esoteric interpretation.

## Brief Essence

In traditional chakra symbolism, the twentieth arcana is usually associated with the throat chakra, or Vishudhya, a center that in popular esotericism is associated with honest self-expression, the ability to voice the truth about oneself and speak out loud what has long remained unspoken. This is not a medical statement about the causes of diseases, but a traditional symbolic view: if a person has been holding back an honest assessment of his own past for years or carries unspoken resentment towards his family, in this tradition it is customary to pay attention to the condition and well-being associated with this symbolic zone, and to pay more attention to honest, calm speech with himself and loved ones.

## Natural Strength

In traditional symbolism, this energy is associated with the natural power of the throat chakra to speak the truth calmly and clearly: the ability to honestly assess the path taken, to call things by their names, and at the same time not turn this honesty into accusations - neither yourself nor others. Symbolically, this is associated with the general feeling of a free, unforced voice that sounds when it is really needed.

The second traditional association is the connection of the voice with the family: the ability to speak honestly with the family, to close old, unspoken topics with a word, and not with silence or a break. In this symbolic system, the very combination of honesty and calmness in speech is considered a sign of a harmonious state of the throat chakra.

## Expression in the Positive

In the traditional symbolic sense, the "plus" of this energy is associated with a clear, free voice: a person is able to honestly and calmly say what he really thinks about past decisions, his own and others', without excessive harshness and without silence. She is symbolically credited with the ability to voice difficult topics in time, without putting them off for years of silence.

In this tradition, it is considered beneficial to regularly allow yourself to speak the truth out loud - about your feelings, about family history, about things that should have been discussed a long time ago - and to do it calmly, without yelling and without self-recrimination. This is seen as a symbolic support for the harmonious state of the throat chakra.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In traditional symbolism for a woman, the favorable state of this energy is associated with the ability to honestly name one's feelings and needs out loud, in particular in family relationships, without turning the conversation into either a silent insult or a sharp rebuke. It is recommended to symbolically support practices that give the voice the freedom to sound calmly - sincere conversations with loved ones, keeping a diary as a way to "say" what is difficult to say out loud right away.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

For a man in this tradition, a favorable state is associated with the ability to openly admit one's own conclusions from the past and talk about them without being overly harsh on oneself or others. Symbolically, it is considered useful to allow oneself to voice a disagreement or long-standing grudge in a calm tone, instead of either remaining silent for years or moving immediately to a sharp confrontation.

## Expression in the Negative

In traditional symbolism, either chronic inhibition of words, when a person does not voice what he really thinks and feels for years, or, on the contrary, a harsh, accusatory manner of speaking is associated with a state of throat chakra imbalance. This is not a medical diagnosis, but rather a symbolic description of an experience that is traditionally associated with a general feeling of tension or stiffness in the throat and jaw, hoarseness for no apparent reason, and a "lump in the throat" feeling when important words are left unsaid.

### Expression in Women (Negative)

In a woman, this can be symbolically manifested as a habit of swallowing insults at family or loved ones, keeping silent about disagreement for the sake of "peace in the family", which is traditionally associated with a general feeling of tightness in the throat, tension in the neck and shoulders, which accumulates imperceptibly.

### Expression in Men (Negative)

In a man, this can be symbolically manifested as a habit of talking about the past and about the family too harshly, in the form of accusations or judgment, or, on the contrary, complete silence about real feelings, which in this tradition is associated with chronic tension in the area of ​​the jaw and throat, which is difficult to admit out loud.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

In traditional symbolism, triggers include years of silent, unspoken resentments in the family, an environment where an honest assessment of the past is perceived as an attack or ingratitude, and a habit of either judging yourself too harshly mentally or lashing out at others instead of having a calm conversation. The long habit of deciding that it is "better to remain silent" when there is actually something to say is considered in this tradition to be one of the strongest debilitating factors for this particular symbolic center.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

General recommendations that are safe for anyone accepted in this symbolic tradition - and at the same time useful regardless of any esoteric views:

1. Be honest with yourself about what you really think and feel about a decision from the past, instead of continuing to suppress it.
2. Practice saying difficult but true things out loud in a calm tone — first alone with yourself, then, if you wish, with loved ones.
3. If there is an old, unspoken resentment against the family, gradually find words for it - orally or in writing - without waiting for the perfect moment.
4. Watch for tension in the throat, jaw and neck area and, if necessary, practice light relaxation exercises for this area.
5. Avoid extremes in conversation — neither prolonged silence about important matters, nor sharp accusations; look for a calm, honest tone.
6. Pay attention to practices that symbolically support the free sound of the voice - singing, reading aloud, breathing exercises.
7. Allow yourself to recognize achievements as loudly as mistakes, without diminishing either the first or the second.
8. If necessary, seek support from a doctor or psychotherapist — this complements and does not replace any spiritual practice.

## Main Task

In traditional symbolism, the main "task" of this energy is considered to be the ability to speak the truth calmly: to honestly sum up what has been passed and to voice difficult topics with the family, without turning language into either a silent sentence for oneself or accusations of others. This is a general recommendation to preserve a free, not compressed voice, and not a medical prescription.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there something from the past that I still haven't dared to tell myself honestly?
- Do I carry an unspoken grudge against someone in the family, instead of finding calm words for them?
- When I talk about my mistakes, is it an honest conclusion or an endless inner sentence?
- Am I not going from silence immediately to sharp accusations, bypassing a calm conversation?
- Do I seek support - from people or professionals - when I feel persistent tension in my throat or jaw for no apparent reason?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one topic from the past or family history that you are still avoiding in silence, and honestly tell yourself why.
2. Today, try to say out loud - alone or to someone close - one thing that you usually keep to yourself.
3. Spend a few minutes doing throat and jaw exercises—singing, reading aloud, or doing simple breathing exercises.
4. If there are signs of prolonged tension in the throat, hoarseness or stiffness of the jaw for no apparent reason, consult a doctor.
5. Allow yourself one calm, honest conclusion instead of another silent sentence for yourself or your family.
