# 15. The Devil — Personal, Social, and Spiritual Purpose

## Brief Essence

The personal purpose of this energy is to learn to consciously own one's own magnetism: to see the essence of people and situations, to attract attention, resources and opportunities almost effortlessly, and to use this gift not for power over others, but for honest influence and real contribution. At a younger age, this often manifests as charisma that attracts friends and the attention of teachers, in maturity - as the ability to lead and persuade, at a later age it grows into the wisdom that real power is not in subjugating others, but in freeing oneself and the people around from what controls them.

Social purpose is manifested through the role of a leader, mentor or influential figure who knows how to see the hidden desires and motives of the group and direct this energy in a constructive direction. Spiritual purpose leads through one's own experience of dependence and liberation—when one encounters the power of desire, temptation, or power over oneself or others, and overcoming that power, rather than running away from it, becomes the source of true wisdom and freedom, which one ultimately shares with others.

## Natural Strength

The personal resource of this energy is rare magnetism and charisma: a person naturally attracts attention, trust and the desire to cooperate, even without making special efforts. Her presence is felt, and the words have a weight that often exceeds their literal meaning.

Social resource is a deep intuitive understanding of human psychology: a person sees what others really want, fear or hide, and knows how to speak in a language that resonates with these hidden motives. This is a rare quality that makes her a natural leader, negotiator or mentor.

A spiritual resource is the ability to transform through one's own shadow: a person is not afraid to look into their own desires, addictions and weaknesses, and it is this honesty with themselves that gives them a depth of understanding of human nature, inaccessible to those who avoid their own dark side.

## Expression in the Positive

At a younger age, personal purpose is revealed through natural charisma and leadership qualities: a child or teenager intuitively attracts the attention of peers, easily persuades, leads them in games or projects, and gradually learns to use this influence honestly, and not for manipulation for their own benefit.

Social purpose is manifested as the role of a person who knows how to mobilize a group around a common goal, seeing and naming that hidden desire or need that unites different people: he naturally understands what drives a team or community, and uses this understanding to lead people to a real result, and not to his own benefit at their expense.

Spiritual purpose in the positive is manifested as the ability to consciously work with one's own desires and addictions, turning the experience of fighting them into true wisdom: a person who himself went through addiction to something - money, recognition, relationships, substances - and found a way to freedom, becomes a living example for others that liberation is possible.

Another positive expression is the ability to see beauty and potential where others see only the surface: a person naturally recognizes hidden talents, resources, or opportunities in people and situations, and shares this vision wholeheartedly, helping others see their own worth.

A common feature of fulfilled purpose is a sense of freedom from what used to rule a person: addiction, fear, the need to control or be controlled, and using the freedom and influence gained for honest, inspiring leadership.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, this purpose often manifests as the ability to be the center of attraction for a group or community thanks to her natural charisma and deep understanding of people: she knows how to inspire, negotiate, influence decisions, while remaining honest about her own intentions and respecting the autonomy of others.

As she ages, she increasingly uses her own insight and magnetism to help other women see their own power and break free from addictions or limiting relationships, becoming an example for them to consciously, freely use their own influence.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, this purpose manifests itself as the ability to lead people behind him through real persuasion and understanding of their motives, and not through pressure or fear: he naturally takes leadership positions, because people feel in him both strength and honesty of intentions.

Over time, this ability often develops into a recognized role as a mentor or authority figure who helps other men recognize and overcome their own addictions—to work, status, control—based on their own experience of breaking free from what once controlled them.

## Expression in the Negative

When purpose is not fulfilled, a person's magnetism and influence turns against himself and against others: he uses charisma and understanding of human weaknesses to manipulate, subjugate or control those around him, building relationships and careers on power over others rather than real contribution.

In a social role, the negative manifests itself as toxic leadership: a person unites people around a common goal, but uses group dynamics for his own benefit, inflames the dependence of followers on him personally, and gradually loses the trust of those who feel this hidden exploitation.

Another manifestation of the negative is self-enslavement: a person is so engrossed in the pursuit of recognition, power, pleasure, or control that they lose the ability to stop, and their life path becomes a series of addictions—to work, relationships, substances, the approval of others—each of which promises freedom but actually takes it away.

At a deeper negative level, destructive jealousy and the need to control appear: a person cannot allow people around to be free, he is afraid of losing influence or power over them, and this need for control gradually distances from him the very people whose presence he most desires.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger is an experience where power, recognition, or the satisfaction of a desire brings a quick, intoxicating result, and the person begins to chase a repetition of this feeling, ignoring its true value. The second trigger is an environment or environment that encourages manipulative influence tactics as the normal path to success. The third is a deep, often hidden fear of losing control or significance, which drives us to hold on ever tighter to power over people or situations.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

The first step is to honestly admit if there is an area in your life where a desire, habit, or need to control is already controlling you, and not the other way around: money, work, relationships, recognition, substances—and call it straight, without excuses. The second step is to regularly check your own influence on others by asking: am I leading this person to their own strength, or am I binding them to me.

The third step is to consciously practice letting go of control where it's not needed—allowing other people their own decisions, their own mistakes, their own freedom—even when it's unsettling. The fourth step is, if it is a literal addiction, to seek professional support rather than relying on willpower or charisma alone, which may themselves be part of the problem.

It is also useful to periodically ask yourself how many people and situations around you are based on real respect for you, and how many are based on fear or dependence on your influence.

## Main Task

The main task of this appointment is to learn to master one's own magnetism and insight consciously: to lead people and influence the world honestly, without manipulation, and at the same time to free oneself from everything that controls the person himself - addictions, the need for control, the insatiable pursuit of recognition. Realized purpose means that influence serves freedom—both one's own and another's—and not a new form of enslavement.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What in my life now controls me more than I control it?
- Does my influence on the people around me lead to their own strength, or to dependence on me?
- When was the last time I felt an overwhelming need to control a person or a situation - and what was really behind this fear?

## Practical Plan

1. Name one area of your life—work, relationship, habit—where you feel you no longer have complete control, and describe it honestly.
2. Think back to the last time you used your influence on another person and evaluate whether it was for their benefit or just yours.
3. Pick one area where you are overly controlling of another person and consciously let go of some of that control this week.
4. If you notice signs of addiction in the literal sense, take the first concrete step to professional support.
5. Find a person or community where you can apply your natural influence for honest mentoring, not for personal gain.
6. Once a month, conduct a personal "power audit" - see where your influence on people is honest, and where it is built on fear or dependence.
