# 10. Wheel of Fortune — Personal, Social, and Spiritual Purpose

## Brief Essence

The personal purpose of the tenth energy is to learn to trust life's movement while remaining an active co-creator of one's own destiny. Its social purpose appears through the role of someone who brings others hope and the sense that change for the better is possible: a guide through transitional periods, an inspirer, a person whose presence reminds others that no stretch of difficulty lasts forever. Its spiritual purpose leads to the understanding that trust in a higher order and personal responsibility do not contradict each other, but work together like two sides of the same wheel.

This is the energy of the wheel itself: it turns whether a person wants it to or not, but the person decides whether to seize the right moment when it arrives. The temptation is either to force the wheel to stop or to wait passively for it to deliver what is desired. True purpose means moving with it consciously and actively.

## Natural Strength

The personal resource of this energy is the ability to see opportunities where others see only chaos or threat. A person with this purpose does not become stuck analyzing why something went wrong, but quickly looks for where to move next—and this flexibility allows them to pass through changes that paralyze others.

The social resource appears as the natural role of a guide through uncertainty: people are drawn to someone with the tenth energy precisely during periods of change, crisis, and transition because they can preserve calm and trust where others panic. They do not promise that everything will be easy, but their presence demonstrates that every situation has a way through.

The spiritual resource is the ability to sense a higher order in seemingly random events. A person with this energy rarely believes that anything happens for no reason, and this trust allows them to remain calm even in the most unpredictable circumstances without losing the capacity to act.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, personal purpose is realized through a life in which a person consciously participates in their own turns of fate instead of merely watching them: they trust the process while making consistent efforts where they have influence, understanding that trust and action reinforce each other.

Social purpose appears as the role of someone who helps others move through difficult transitions: a mentor in periods of crisis, a person who can remind others that change is not the end but part of natural movement. In the positive, the person shares their own experience of overcoming difficulty in a way that inspires rather than sounding like empty reassurance.

Spiritual purpose in the positive is mature trust in a higher order combined with personal responsibility. The person knows that not everything is subject to their will while acting where something does depend on them, without shifting all responsibility onto “fate” or “luck.”

Another sign of the positive is the ability to accept both fortune and misfortune with balance: the person neither loses their head in success nor falls into despair after defeat, understanding that both states are temporary and belong to one cycle.

In the positive, the person also learns to value movement that brings genuine growth rather than mere external novelty: they choose changes that lead somewhere instead of chaotic rushing for the sake of feeling active.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In the positive, a woman uses her natural adaptability and sense of timing to be the voice in her family or community that reminds others there is a way out of every difficult situation. She does not fear change and returns from every turn not depleted but wiser—able to offer genuine support to others in their own transitions.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In the positive, a man fulfills his purpose through the role of someone who can combine courage with good judgment: a person unafraid to take a risk at the right moment but unwilling to gamble recklessly with fate. Through his own example, he can inspire others to trust the process while taking an active part in creating it.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, personal purpose is blocked either by excessive control or complete passivity: the person either tries to hold every aspect of life in place by force, afraid to trust the process, or relinquishes all responsibility and waits for fate to resolve everything.

Social purpose in the negative is distorted into the irresponsible spread of careless optimism: the person promises others that “everything will work itself out” without offering real support or concrete steps, and over time this superficial reassurance ceases to inspire trust.

Spiritual purpose in the negative appears as fatalism used as a convenient excuse for inaction. The person convinces themselves that the outcome is predetermined and effort is therefore pointless, and this thought becomes a shield against all responsibility for their own life.

Another sign of the negative is dependence on constant intensity and risk: the person becomes so accustomed to dramatic turns that they unconsciously provoke instability where calm, stable fulfillment could exist.

## What Can Shift the Energy into the Negative

The strongest trigger is a prolonged series of setbacks that shakes trust in life and raises doubts that the wheel will ever turn toward the favorable side. A second trigger is enforced stability with no room for movement or change, perceived as stagnation that deprives life of meaning. A third is an experience in which active efforts failed to produce the desired result, leaving the impression that action is pointless.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to distinguish mature trust, which works together with responsibility, from passive waiting, which is an escape from action disguised as spirituality. The second is to practice small, consistent actions in the desired direction, trusting that they prepare the ground for a favorable turn even if the result does not come immediately.

The third step is consciously to seek a balance between control and release: act decisively where something depends on personal effort, and consciously release anxiety where the outcome lies beyond your influence. The fourth is to practice moving from fatalism into action even without complete certainty about the result: a small step taken now is often more valuable than a perfect plan waiting for guarantees.

It is also helpful to check regularly whether the person unconsciously provokes dramatic turns for the sake of intensity, and consciously to choose calmer, more stable fulfillment where it is genuinely needed.

## Main Task

The main task of this energy is to become a conscious co-creator of one's own destiny, neither its passive passenger nor its sole conductor: trust the cycles of existence while making personal, consistent efforts where they matter, and share this mature trust with others by helping them move through their own turns.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I trust life, or do I use that trust as an excuse for inaction?
- To what extent do I help others through real action rather than empty reassurance that “everything will be all right”?
- Where does fatalism live within me as a shield against responsibility?
- Do I unconsciously provoke dramatic turns for the sake of intensity?
- How consistently do I act where the result genuinely depends on me?
- What would change if I trusted the process and acted more actively at the same time?

## Practical Plan

1. This week, take one conscious step in a direction where you have long been waiting for a “sign,” without seeking further confirmation.
2. Support someone going through a difficult transition with a concrete action, not only words of reassurance.
3. Write down one situation in which you maintained excessive control, and consciously release part of that control this week.
4. Recall three moments in your life when a difficult situation eventually changed for the better, and use them as support amid current doubts.
5. Choose one consistent habit that prepares the ground for a desired change, and practice it daily for a week.
