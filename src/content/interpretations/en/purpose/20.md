# 20. Judgement — Personal, Social, and Spiritual Purpose

## Brief Essence

The personal purpose of this energy is to learn to hear one's own inner call and honestly, without being overly harsh on oneself, to evaluate the path traveled in order to move forward not out of guilt for the past, but out of a clear understanding of where one's true calling leads. At a younger age, this often manifests itself as an acute feeling of "I'm not going there" or "it's not mine", which a child or teenager catches earlier than adults and which should not be drowned out by arguments about "practicality", but should be learned to listen; in maturity, as the ability to regularly stop and honestly check whether the current path still corresponds to inner values, and have the courage to change direction if not; at a later age, it is like wisdom to sum up one's life without bitterness and without embellishment, passing on to the younger ones not ready-made answers, but the very experience of an honest look at one's own life.

Social purpose is manifested through the role of a person who knows how to remind a community or family of justice and a fair result where others tend to either remain silent or blame: she naturally sees when real change is ripe and has the courage to voice it, even if it would be easier to remain silent. Spiritual purpose leads through the connection with ancestral, intuitive wisdom and the ability for true rebirth: a person who learns to listen to the inner voice that calls for renewal, and not for the endless repetition of the old script, and who finds the courage to leave behind what has already passed away, even when it is scary, eventually becomes an example for others of how to dignify closing some chapters of life and opening new ones.

## Natural Strength

A personal resource of this energy is the ability to hear an inner call earlier and more clearly than others: when a moment comes that requires a change of direction, this person feels it almost physically, as a call that is difficult to ignore, and it is this sensitivity, and not a logical calculation, that most often leads him to his true calling.

Social resource is the ability to take an honest, fair view of a group or family situation: when others are either justifying an outdated state of affairs or blaming someone else, this energy naturally sees the bigger picture and can name what is worth acknowledging and changing, without being overly soft or cruel.

A spiritual resource is a natural connection to ancestral and intuitive wisdom: a sense of family support, clairvoyance, the ability to perceive meaning and direction where others see only chaos, and if one learns to trust that inner voice, rather than drowning it out with doubt, it becomes a source of deep, enduring meaning.

## Expression in the Positive

At a younger age, personal purpose is revealed by supporting the child's natural sensitivity to his own vocation: when adults do not extinguish his intuitive signals "it's not mine" with phrases like "you'll outgrow" or "you have to", but help to listen to them more carefully, the child grows into an adult who trusts his own inner voice even when it contradicts the expectations of the environment.

The social purpose is manifested as the role of a person who knows how to sum up honestly and fairly where a community or a family is stuck in silence or mutual accusations: it finds words that acknowledge the past without embellishment, but also without excessive severity, and it is this honesty that opens the way for the group to real renewal.

Spiritual purpose in the plus manifests itself as trusting the inner call even when it requires completing something familiar: a person learns to recognize the difference between temporary fatigue and a real signal that a stage of life has exhausted itself, and finds the courage to start a new chapter, relying on intuition and ancestral support.

Another positive sign is the courage to honestly and without self-flagellation look back at one's own past decisions: a person does not run away from difficult conclusions about himself, but does not punish himself endlessly, but turns an honest look back into a concrete, applicable step forward.

A common feature of realized purpose is the ability to truly regenerate: a person has learned to regularly close exhausted chapters of life and open new ones not out of despair or flight, but from a clear inner call, and it is this ability to renew again and again that makes his purpose alive, not frozen.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, this purpose often manifests itself as the ability to hear her own inner call even when the environment expects stability and constancy from her - she allows herself to honestly admit when a stage of life has exhausted herself, and finds the courage to start a new one, without fear of other people's condemnation for this change.

As she ages, she increasingly becomes an example for younger women of how to draw honest conclusions from life without bitterness and self-blame, showing that admitting a mistake or completing a stage is not a defeat, but a necessary condition for true renewal.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, this purpose manifests itself as the ability to lead a family or a team through an honest summary of the past stage, without blaming anyone or silencing the problem for the sake of peace: he names what is worth acknowledging and offers a direction for real renewal.

Over time, this ability often translates into the recognized role of someone whose voice is perceived as fair and balanced in difficult, controversial situations, because he knows how to combine honesty with compassion, rather than using the truth as a weapon.

## Expression in the Negative

When the purpose does not find fulfillment, one extreme manifests itself as an endless inner court: a person goes over past decisions again and again, looking for where exactly he went wrong, instead of making one honest conclusion and moving on, and this constant self-criticism gradually paralyzes the ability to hear a new call, because all attention is fixed on what has already been lived.

In a social role, this extreme manifests itself as a tendency to pass harsh verdicts on others, not noticing one's own part in the situation: a person conveniently sees other people's mistakes and demands recognition from others, remaining blind to his own, and this undermines trust in him as a fair voice of the group.

The opposite extreme is deafness to one's own call: a person feels for years that he is not living his life, not there, not in the right way, but drowns out this signal with arguments about stability, duty or fear of disappointing others, and gradually the inner voice becomes quieter until it is almost completely silent.

In a deeper minus, it is possible to get stuck in old, unresolved grudges - against parents, against circumstances, against oneself - when a person cannot make an honest conclusion and move on, because he continues to demand from the past justice that it can no longer give.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger for the inner court is the period when one has to admit a serious mistake or a bad choice, and instead of one honest conclusion, a person plunges into endless self-criticism. A second trigger for deafness to one's calling is long-term stability or obligations that make a change in direction scary or "impractical." The third trigger is a family or social situation that requires an honest, public summary of the past, when it seems easier to remain silent or blame someone else than to honestly deal with it.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to honestly determine in which direction the energy is currently shifted: towards endless self-judgment for the past or towards deafness to the inner call for change. The second step, if it is self-judgment, is to assign one "trial" for each difficult decision: honestly analyze, draw a conclusion, and consciously close the topic without returning to it again and again.

The third step, if it is a deafness to the call, is to regularly set aside time in silence, without external voices and advice, to hear if the current path is still truly in line with inner values. The fourth step is to practice honest but compassionate debriefing: learn to acknowledge both your own mistakes and your own achievements without skewing in either direction.

It is also useful to regularly ask oneself whether a person acts out of inertia and fear of disappointing others, or from a genuine inner call - and gradually shift the weight of decisions to the second source.

## Main Task

The main task of this assignment is to learn to hear one's own inner call and make an honest, fair, but not punitive verdict on one's life path. Realized purpose means the ability to regularly close exhausted chapters of life and be reborn in new ones, relying on intuition and family support, and not remaining hostage to old resentments or fear of change.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Am I punishing myself endlessly for past decisions instead of making one honest conclusion and moving on?
- Do I still hear my own inner call, or have I long since drowned it out with arguments about stability and duty?
- Which chapter of my life should be honestly summed up and closed long ago to make room for a new one?

## Practical Plan

1. Determine in which direction your energy is currently shifted - towards endless self-judgment for the past or deafness to the inner call.
2. Choose one difficult past decision and conduct an honest “case review” for it: draw one specific conclusion and consciously close the topic.
3. Set aside thirty minutes of silence without outside advice to honestly ask yourself if the current path still aligns with your values.
4. Name one chapter of life that has long exhausted itself, and take the first concrete step towards its completion.
5. Practice taking stock of the day for one week without self-blame and without embellishment—just an honest, balanced view.
6. Once a month, check whether your decisions are dictated by an inner call or fear of disappointing others.
