# 22. The Fool — Personal, Social, and Spiritual Purpose

## Brief Essence

The personal purpose of this energy is to live life with an open heart to new experiences, without fear of the unknown and without dragging along the burden of old stories that have long lost their meaning. At a younger age, this is often manifested as a natural ease with which a child takes on a new game, a new acquaintance or a new idea, without having time to be afraid of a possible failure; in maturity - as the ability to start new stages of life - profession, relationships, place of residence - from a clean slate, trusting that fate leads in the right direction; at a later age - as wisdom to maintain lightness of spirit and sincere curiosity about life even when the external circumstances have long been established.

The social purpose is manifested through the role of a person who, with his sincerity and spontaneity, gives others silent permission to relax a little, to leave the rigid framework of decency or duty and remember that life can be easy. Spiritual purpose leads through a deep, almost childlike trust in the fact that everything will turn out as it should, a trust in fate that does not require constant control over every step, and through an openness to new experience that expands one's inner world instead of locking it in tested, familiar forms.

## Natural Strength

The personal resource of this energy is the innate ability to let go of the past and start anew: where others carry resentments, disappointments or outdated beliefs about themselves for years, this person is able to leave it all behind and easily step on a new path, as if opening a clean page.

A social resource is a sincere, unguarded openness that attracts people: this energy does not play roles or hide real feelings for the sake of impression, and it is this sincerity that often gives others the courage to also be themselves, and not the version of themselves that is expected of them.

A spiritual resource is a deep trust in fate: an intuitive sense that life does not need to be controlled down to the smallest detail in order for it to turn out well, and it is this trust that gives a person a rare inner peace even in moments of uncertainty when others feel only anxiety.

## Expression in the Positive

At a younger age, personal purpose is revealed by supporting the child's natural openness to new experiences: when adults do not try to protect the child in advance from every possible disappointment, but allow him to try, make mistakes and try again, he grows into an adult who retains the courage to start a new life.

The social purpose is manifested as the role of a person who, with his lightness and sincerity, defuses a tense atmosphere, reminds others that not everything should be taken too seriously, and shows by his example that it is possible to be honest and at the same time not lose lightness.

Spiritual purpose in the plus is manifested as a sincere trust in the flow of life: a person does not try to write out every step of his destiny in advance, but listens to the moment, trusts intuition and unexpected turns, and it is this openness that often leads him to where no careful plan could take him.

Another plus sign is the ability to combine freedom with responsibility: a person does not run away from the consequences of his own decisions for the sake of a new adventure, but learns to bear both at the same time, remaining true to himself and honest to others.

A common feature of a realized purpose is the ability to live life easily without losing depth: a person does not disperse in an empty escape from responsibility, but also does not freeze in fear of the new, but knows how to combine spontaneity with inner maturity.

### Expression in Women (Positive)

In a woman, this purpose often manifests itself as the ability to inspire others with her own example of lightness and sincerity: she is not afraid to start a new chapter of life - a new profession, a new relationship, a new place - and with her openness she shows others that change does not always mean danger.

With age, she increasingly becomes an example of how to live a life full of surprises, spontaneous and at the same time remain reliable to those who rely on her - combining freedom with quiet, steady responsibility.

### Expression in Men (Positive)

In a man, this purpose manifests itself as the ability to be the first to take a step into the unknown - a new business, a new idea, a new direction - without waiting for guarantees and without demanding complete certainty from life, and it is this courage that often opens the way for those who follow.

Over time, this ability often turns into the recognized role of a person who, with his sincerity and ease, gives others permission to take risks, try new things and trust their own intuition, while remaining a man of his word who can be relied on.

## Expression in the Negative

When the purpose does not find fulfillment, one extreme manifests itself as an irresponsible escape from any obligations: a person constantly seeks new experiences for the sake of experience itself, abandons what has been started as soon as it requires a real return, and easily hides the unwillingness to be responsible for the consequences of one's decisions - to himself or to others.

The opposite extreme is the fear of the new, which freezes into a rigid, defensive frame: a person, out of fear of the unknown, refuses any changes, clings to the usual life even when it has long exhausted itself, and gradually loses the lightness of spirit that is its natural resource.

In a social role, this extreme manifests itself either as an irresponsible influence on others - a person infects the environment with adventures, the consequences of which someone else then has to deal with - or as a loss of one's own sincerity: a person hides his true feelings behind the mask of careful, safe behavior so as not to disturb anyone.

In a deeper minus, a chronic feeling of internal disconnection between the desire for freedom and the fear of its consequences is possible: a person sometimes rushes into another reckless adventure, then, frightened by the result, locks himself in complete inactivity for a long time, and this swing prevents him from feeling the true meaning and ease of his own path.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

The strongest trigger for adventurous extremes is the temptation of instant novelty, which promises a quick sense of freedom with no consequences. The second trigger that leads to fear and limitation is the painful experience of a past risk that did not pay off and the fear of repeating the same mistake. The third trigger is the long-term pressure of other people's expectations or strict rules, which either provokes a rebellious escape into adventure, or, on the contrary, forces a person to lock himself even more tightly in defense for the sake of illusory security.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The first step is to honestly determine in which direction the energy is currently shifted: toward irresponsible escape from obligations or toward fear that limits any new experience. The second step, if this is adventurism, is to consciously make smaller, more clearly defined commitments and see them through to completion before looking for a new adventure.

The third step, if it is a limitation of fear, is to allow yourself small, consciously chosen steps into the unknown: a new acquaintance, a new idea, a small change of the usual route, reminding yourself that trusting fate does not mean abandoning common sense. The fourth step is to practice honesty with yourself and with others, without hiding your true feelings behind either a mask of carelessness or a mask of cautious detachment.

It is also useful to regularly remind yourself that true freedom is not about running away from consequences or refusing to take any risk, but about being able to easily follow a new path while remaining responsible for your own choices.

## Main Task

The main task of this appointment is to learn to combine natural freedom and sincere trust in fate with a willingness to be responsible for the consequences of one's decisions, without losing lightness of spirit due to fear and without turning this lightness into an escape from responsibility. Fulfilled purpose means the ability to meet each new experience openly and boldly, while remaining at the same time a person whose word can be trusted.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Am I running away from obligations or the consequences of my own decisions, hiding behind ease and the search for new experiences?
- Am I not giving up on a new path only because of fear of the unknown, clinging to a long-exhausted habitual life?
- Am I able to trust the flow of life while remaining responsible for my own choices?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one commitment or task you've started that you're avoiding for a new experience, and take a step toward completing it.
2. Take one small step toward something new and unfamiliar that you previously refused out of fear.
3. Write down one situation where your levity hurt another person, and think about how to combine spontaneity with concern for the consequences.
4. Practice being honest in the little things for one week—saying how you really feel instead of the more convenient, safer version of the answer.
5. Find an example of someone whose combination of freedom and responsibility inspires you, and identify one specific trait you want to develop in yourself.
6. Once a month, look back on the period you have lived and honestly assess whether you are moving towards greater freedom with responsibility, or whether you are stuck in one of the extremes.
