# 13. Death — Soul Tasks Before Age 40

## Short summary

A soul with the energy of Death comes into this life with a need to learn to complete—to let go of roles, relationships, and beliefs that have exhausted themselves instead of holding on to them out of fear of the unknown. Until the age of forty, the main task is to accept that true renewal is possible only through the honest completion of the old, and not through its stubborn preservation or abrupt destruction.

## Natural force

The natural strength of this soul is the ability to sense when a phase of life has come to an end and find the courage to start anew. It is a gift of deep transformation, the ability to be reborn after losses and crises stronger than before. This power becomes true wisdom only when completion is lived consciously, not through panic or denial.

## Manifestation in the plus

In a positive way, a person uses the ability to transform as a tool for development: he ends in time what no longer serves him - toxic relationships, outdated beliefs, roles that he has grown out of - and moves on with dignity. Her example inspires others not to be afraid of change and their own endings.

## Manifestation in the minus

In the negative, the same ability turns into a cycle of painful crises: the person clings to the old until life itself forces it to let go—through loss, breakup, or crisis—and each such ending is experienced as a catastrophe rather than a natural transition. There is a fear of any changes, even when they are obviously for the better.

## What can be negative

Most often, an early experience of a painful, sudden loss or separation leads to the negative, after which any ending began to be associated with a catastrophe, and not with a natural stage of life. This is also facilitated by the environment, which taught to hold on to stability at any cost, even when it has long ceased to serve development.

## How to return energy to the plus side

A key step toward turning forty is to learn to recognize when a phase of life is truly over and let it go gradually and consciously, rather than waiting for a forced, abrupt break. The second is to regularly check which roles, relationships and beliefs no longer correspond to who a person has become.

It's also helpful to practice small, safe endings—cleaning up excess, closing unfinished business, saying goodbye to old habits—to gradually reduce the fear of larger transformations.

## The main task

By the age of forty, learn to complete what has exhausted itself, consciously and with dignity, trusting that behind every real completion comes a space for renewal.

## Questions for self-reflection

- What role, relationship or belief do I keep in my life only out of fear, even though they have long since exhausted themselves?
- Have there been cases in my life when the necessary change came suddenly precisely because I refused to recognize it for a long time?

## Practical plan

1. Identify one area of life that needs conscious completion and take the first step towards it.
2. Conduct a small farewell ritual with the old stage to mark the transition and reduce its pain.
3. Recall a situation where ending the old really opened up the new, and use it as a support when the fear of change becomes too strong.
