# 20. Judgement — Soul Tasks Before Age 40

## Brief Essence

A soul with the energy of Judgment comes into this life with a natural ability to honestly evaluate one's own path and a deep connection to lineage—and with the task of learning to judge oneself fairly without being overly harsh. Until the age of forty, the main task is to draw honest conclusions from the past and experiences, and not carry guilt or resentment as a lifelong burden.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this soul is the ability to look at one's own life clearly and honestly, to hear the inner call for renewal and to feel the support of the family even in difficult times. It is a gift that becomes a real strength only when a person learns to make one calm verdict on himself, and not an endless series of accusations.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a person uses this gift to honestly sum up, forgive himself for past mistakes and build a new life based on real conclusions, not forgotten images. She finds resistance and strength in family history, not a reason for guilt or justification.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the same ability to judge turns to two extremes: either excessive severity towards oneself, when the same decision is experienced again and again without a final conclusion, or unresolved claims to parents and family, which result in long-term conflicts or even arguments. Exhaustion is also possible from the fact that a person unconsciously continues family scenarios that have long ceased to serve him.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

Most often, childhood or family experience, where mistakes were punished more severely than they were explained, leads to a disadvantage, because of which a person learns to judge himself harshly instead of drawing conclusions. Also contributing to this is an unfinished story in the family - a grudge, a debt or a silent conflict - that no one has ever spoken to the end.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

A key step toward forty is to learn to judge yourself honestly, but not cruelly: to admit a mistake, draw a conclusion from it, and consciously move on, without returning to it again and again. The second is to gradually understand what family scenarios and images a person unconsciously carries forward, and to make a conscious choice of what to keep and what to let go of.

It is also useful to regularly check whether a person relies on the support of the family, or, on the contrary, continues to carry its unlived conflicts as his own.

## Main Task

By the age of forty, learn to judge yourself and your past honestly and fairly, turning experience and family history into a resource for renewal rather than a source of guilt.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there a decision or act from the past for which I am still punishing myself, although the conclusion from it has long been drawn?
- What family resentments or conflicts do I carry on without even realizing that they are not mine?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one situation for which you still judge yourself harshly, and formulate one honest, final conclusion from it.
2. Over the course of a month, observe what family habits or reactions you repeat unconsciously, and choose whether you want to continue them.
3. Once every few months, check whether you allow yourself and your family the right to a completed, not an endlessly contested past.
