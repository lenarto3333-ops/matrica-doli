# 17. The Star — Soul Tasks Before Age 40

## Brief Essence

A Star-energy soul comes into this life with a natural gift for inspiring others and seeing perspective even in difficult times—and with the task of learning to carry that light steadily, not just in flashes dependent on the recognition of others. Until the age of forty, the main task is to turn talent and faith in a better future into something sustainable, rooted in real actions.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this soul is the ability to be the very inner beacon that helps yourself and others not to lose hope even in the darkest periods. It is a gift of true inspiration that only becomes powerful when one learns to hold onto one's truth regardless of whether anyone is looking at it at that moment or not.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, a person uses this gift to sincerely share talent and dreams, combining them with discipline and concrete steps towards realization. She becomes a source of real, not ostentatious, hope for others - someone whose example helps them believe in their own capabilities.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the same power turns to two extremes: either star sickness with a constant need for admiration and recognition, or deep despair, when a person hides his gift and stops believing in his own dreams after the first serious disappointments. It is possible to live a long life in the world of fantasies without any real step towards their realization.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

Children's or youth's experience, when talent was either excessively praised without real support for practical skills, or, on the contrary, harshly devalued the child's dreams and special features, is often negative. This is also facilitated by an environment where a person's worth was measured solely by the recognition of others, and not by one's own sense of truth.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

The key step before turning forty is to realize how much self-esteem depends on the applause of others, and gradually learn to feed the inner light from the inside, and not just from the outside. The second is to regularly transform at least part of the dreams into concrete, measurable steps, so that faith in a better future does not remain just a beautiful idea.

It is also helpful to learn to accept periods of silence and lack of recognition without feeling that the person's talent or worth is being diminished.

## Main Task

By the age of forty, learn to hold your own light steady — to share inspiration with others and act on your dreams, relying on inner self-belief, not constant external validation.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- To what extent does my belief in my own value depend on whether others see and praise me?
- What is my dream or talent that still exists only in imagination, without any real embodiment in life?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one dream or talent that has not yet received a concrete form, and take the first practical step towards its realization.
2. During the week, practice supporting yourself from within — without expecting praise or recognition for every action.
3. Every few months, check if you are clinging to the image of "special" instead of just doing your job honestly and consistently.
