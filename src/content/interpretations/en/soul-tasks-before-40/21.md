# 21. The World — Soul Tasks Before Age 40

## Brief Essence

A soul with the energy of the World comes into this life with the natural gift of seeing the whole and connecting the disparate parts into a single picture - and with the task of learning to bring things to a true conclusion, and not to keep them in the eternal "almost done". Until the age of forty, the main task is to maintain a broad, open view of the world, preventing it from narrowing into protective closedness.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this soul is the ability to look at the big picture, to unite people, ideas or things around a common goal and to feel deep satisfaction from a truly completed result. This is a gift that becomes a real strength only when a person learns to put an honest point, and not endlessly search for perfection.

## Expression in the Positive

In a positive way, a person uses this gift to bring important matters to a real conclusion, unite people and ideas around him, and live with a sense of scale — his own path, opportunities, the wider world. She easily goes beyond the usual limits when it serves growth, and at the same time knows how to recognize the work completed on time.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the same capacity for perfection turns to two extremes: either endless refinement, when no matter seems ready enough to be completed, or a narrowing of the worldview to a protective, almost hostile alertness to everything new and unfamiliar. Exhaustion from constantly postponing significant steps for the sake of illusory ideality is also possible.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

More often than not, experience leads to the negative, where the result was never recognized as good enough, because of which a person learns to grind endlessly instead of completing. This is also facilitated by the experience when openness to new things turned into disappointment or pain, after which the habit of protecting oneself by narrowing the circle of trust and interests appears.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

A key step toward turning forty is to learn to recognize the moment when something is truly done and allow yourself to call it done, even without feeling perfect. The second is to consciously train openness to the wider world: new people, places, ideas, without allowing past experiences to narrow the current view.

It is also useful to regularly check whether a person sees his life as a complete picture or is stuck in the details of one unfinished business.

## Main Task

By the age of forty, learn to bring important matters to an honest conclusion without endless refinement and to maintain a broad, open view of the world instead of protective closedness.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- What significant work have I kept "almost ready" for years because I am afraid to recognize it as complete?
- How open am I to new experiences, people or places — or, on the contrary, do I stick to the usual?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one important thing that should be completed a long time ago, and determine a specific step and deadline for it.
2. During the month, consciously open yourself to one new opportunity — an acquaintance, place, or idea — instead of automatically rejecting it.
3. Once every few months, check if you see your life as a complete picture or if you are stuck in one part of it.
