# 2. The High Priestess — Soul Tasks Before Age 40

## Brief Essence

Before age forty, the task of this energy is to learn to trust your intuition and open up gradually, neither hiding behind a veil of constant mystery nor forcing an openness for which you are not ready. This is a time to distinguish a healthy need for silence from defensive withdrawal and to lay a foundation of self-trust on which the second half of life can grow.

## Natural Strength

At a younger age, this energy has a natural ability to sense people and situations deeply. Early relationships and first experiences of trust and disappointment become a field for practicing how to listen to the inner voice. This sensitivity often emerges earlier than it does among peers—a young person with this energy frequently senses sincerity or falsehood before others do.

Another strength at this age is the ability to recover quickly from betrayed trust. An early disappointment in friendship or love is treated not as a catastrophe, but as an experience that teaches the person to sense more accurately whom they can trust. This resilience makes it possible to learn gradually to trust again without closing off forever.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, a person gradually learns before age forty to trust intuition while testing it against experience—in relationships, friendships, and important decisions—step by step, understanding that depth pays off over time. They learn to open up gradually without abandoning their boundaries and do not blindly copy other people’s expectations about the “right” degree of openness or reserve.

The positive expression also brings an early ability to become a quiet source of support for others—in friendship, work, or shared projects—without fearing that one has too little experience. A young person uses these opportunities as practical ground for developing trust in self and others.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the period before forty may bring either chronic reserve that makes any deep intimacy difficult—often because of an early betrayal of trust—or, conversely, indiscriminate openness that leads to repeated disappointment. In both cases, the person loses the chance to learn calmly and at their own pace whom they can genuinely trust.

Another negative expression is blindly repeating a family pattern of silence about important matters without reflecting on it, reproducing the same limitations observed in childhood.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

A common trigger is an early experience in which openness or trust was used against the person—a secret revealed or vulnerability mocked. Another is the absence of adults who model healthy, gradual trust.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

Find your own pace of trust rather than one imposed from outside, allowing yourself to open gradually and learn through experience whom you can trust. A second step is to distinguish consciously between a healthy need for silence and defensive withdrawal rooted in fear. A third is to practice small acts of trust before attempting major vulnerability in an important relationship.

## Main Task

The main task before age forty is to trust your intuition and open up gradually, drawing on personal experience rather than the fear of repeating an old disappointment or a tendency to trust everyone indiscriminately. This capacity will become the foundation on which the second half of life unfolds.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I trust my intuition about people at my own pace rather than according to others’ expectations?
- Where do I still close myself off because of an old fear that no longer matches the present situation?
- What family pattern of silence about important matters am I unconsciously repeating?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one person to whom it would be healthy to open up gradually, and take the first step.
2. Review one pattern of reserve learned in childhood and consciously decide whether it still serves you.
3. Practice a small act of trust this week by sharing something vulnerable with someone who is genuinely trustworthy.
