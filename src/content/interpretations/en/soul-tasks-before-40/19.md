# 19. The Sun — Soul Tasks Before Age 40

## Brief Essence

A Sun-energy soul comes into this life with a natural sincerity, warmth, and ability to think big—and with the task of learning to radiate that joy without needing constant validation from the outside. By the age of forty, the main task is to learn to share the light with others, without exhausting either yourself or them.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength of this soul is the ability to fill the surrounding space with warmth, optimism and openness, attracting people with its very sincerity. This is a gift that becomes a real power only when a person learns to act consciously on a large scale, and not out of a need to constantly prove his worth.

## Expression in the Positive

In a positive way, a person uses this gift to inspire others, to do great things and sincerely rejoice in success - his own and others'. She becomes an example for those around her of how to be a source of warmth while remaining vulnerable and honest about her own boundaries.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the same power turns to two extremes: either narcissism, when a person exhaustingly criticizes himself for any "not bright enough" moment, hiding true feelings behind constant cheerfulness, or pride, when the recognition and attention of others become the only meaning of actions, and loved ones are "burned out" by the demand to maintain this pace.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

Most often, childhood or youth experience, when love and attention had to be "earned" by a good mood, success, or entertainment of others, instead of receiving them just like that, leads to the negative. This is also facilitated by an environment where sincere, difficult emotions were devalued or considered inappropriate.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

A key step toward turning forty is learning to separate your self-worth from constant recognition and success, allowing yourself to be not always shining and still worthy of love. The second is to gradually learn to share the scope of one's actions with others, without appropriating all the success and without demanding constant support of one's pace from loved ones.

It is also useful to regularly check whether the joy that a person shows to the world is real, or whether it is a show in order to maintain the image of a bright person.

## Main Task

By the age of forty, learn to be a source of sincere warmth for yourself and others, without exhausting your own resources and without demanding from the environment a constant reflection of your own light.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- How often is my joy and cheerfulness sincere and not a performance for others?
- Do I demand from loved ones to maintain my pace and mood, instead of allowing them to be different?

## Practical Plan

1. Write down one situation where you showed joy that you did not really feel, and think about what was behind this choice.
2. During the week, practice sharing your success or good news with someone instead of keeping the recognition to yourself.
3. Once every few months, check whether you allow yourself and your loved ones to experience moments without the requirement of constant cheerfulness.
