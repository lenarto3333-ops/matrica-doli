# 10. Wheel of Fortune — the balance point

The point where money and relationships connect in the Wheel of Fortune is fed by trust in life's cycles — the ability to catch the current when it's flowing upward. But fear of change easily flips that trust into its opposite: clinging to what has already run its course.

## Positive

+ **Moving with the cycles.** Invests more actively when the flow is favorable and holds back carefully during downturns.
+ **Calm through a downturn.** Rides out financial dips without panic, trusting the natural rhythm.
+ **Accepting the waves of closeness.** Accepts the natural rise and fall of intensity in a relationship without demanding constant peak intensity.
+ **A sense of rhythm.** Knows when to act and when to wait — in both money and relationships.
+ **Trust in recovery.** Believes an upturn always follows a downturn, and acts from that trust.
+ **Recognizing the phase of a cycle.** Tells a temporary dip apart from a signal that the cycle has genuinely ended.
+ **Letting go at the right time.** Lets an outdated income source, or a relationship that's run its course, come to a natural close.
+ **Flexibility in the face of change.** Adapts to a sudden shift instead of treating it as a catastrophe.
+ **Drawing on past experience.** Remembers past downturns that were followed by an upturn, and uses that as an anchor.
+ **Balance of action and waiting.** Doesn't try to freeze a good moment in place forever — moves in step with the current phase.

## Negative

- **Clinging to an outdated income source.** Holds onto work that has already run its course, purely out of fear of the unknown.
- **Holding on to a relationship that's run its course.** Stays in a partnership that passed its natural cycle long ago.
- **Energy spent on holding on.** The strength needed for the next stroke of luck gets spent instead on holding onto what's already letting go on its own.
- **Panic at change.** Lost income or cooling in the relationship is experienced as a catastrophe rather than a natural phase.
- **Fear of the unknown outweighing stagnation.** The unknown feels more frightening than staying in something that no longer works.
- **Confusing a dip with an ending.** Can't tell a temporary phase of the cycle apart from a signal that it's time to let go.
- **Freezing a good moment in place.** Tries to hold onto a peak forever instead of accepting the natural downturn.
- **Resisting adaptation.** Responds to change with resistance instead of flexible adjustment.
- **Losing trust in the rhythm.** Forgets that downturns are just as natural as upswings.
- **Ignoring the signs of an ending.** Keeps investing in something that's clearly already letting go on its own.

## Recommendations

- Write down one area — financial or relational — where you're holding onto the status quo out of fear, and honestly assess whether it still nourishes you.
- Notice the current phase of the cycle in your income and in your relationship, and let yourself act accordingly — more actively on the upswing, more carefully during a dip.
- Recall one past downturn that was followed by an upturn, and use it as an anchor during the current period of uncertainty.
