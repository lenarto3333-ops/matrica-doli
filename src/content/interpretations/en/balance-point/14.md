# 14. Temperance — the balance point

The point where money and relationships connect in Temperance is fed by the very nature of this energy — the ability to blend opposites. The paradox is that a person who holds balance so naturally often drains all her patience into one area, while swinging to extremes in the other.

## Positive

+ **A steady rhythm in both areas.** Spends and saves wisely, gives and receives closeness without sharp swings.
+ **Carrying balance across.** Consciously moves patience built up in one area into the other, where it's needed more.
+ **Blending opposites.** Brings together caution and boldness, spending and saving, closeness and personal space.
+ **Calm harmony at home.** Gives and receives closeness without extremes — neither smothering nor distant.
+ **A sensible budget.** Makes financial decisions thoughtfully, without sharp swings.
+ **Accepting an uneven balance.** Accepts that perfect equilibrium in both areas at once isn't always possible — and that's fine.
+ **Consciously redistributing resource.** Notices which area is currently "eating up" all her patience, and compensates in the other.
+ **Stability with money.** Keeps a balanced approach to spending and saving, without slipping into impulsiveness.
+ **Emotional steadiness in the relationship.** Gives closeness smoothly, without sharp swings of mood or distance.
+ **Consciously transferring a habit.** Takes an approach that works in one area and applies it to the one where balance is slipping.

## Negative

- **Depleted patience.** All her capacity for balance goes into one area, leaving the other with nothing.
- **Extremes in the relationship alongside stable finances.** A carefully weighed budget coexists with sharp swings between closeness and distance.
- **Impulsive financial decisions alongside harmony at home.** A calm relationship sits right next to chaotic, unconsidered money decisions.
- **A used-up reserve of patience.** Doesn't have time to replenish for both areas at once.
- **Extreme reactions instead of balance.** One area gets only unbalanced, sharp reactions, while all the stability flows into the other.
- **Imbalance without awareness.** Doesn't notice that all the resource for balance is shifting into one area at the expense of the other.
- **Sharp swings in closeness.** Alternates between overwhelming closeness and sudden distance with no visible cause.
- **Ignoring the area that's suffering.** Doesn't carry the harmony she's built over to where it's needed most.
- **Exhaustion from constant balancing.** Fatigue from maintaining balance in one area leaves no strength for the other.
- **Automatic decisions with no reflection.** Impulsiveness shows up wherever all the conscious attention has drained away from.

## Recommendations

- Identify the area where balance comes more easily, and write down the specific habit or approach that helps there.
- Consciously carry that habit into the area where extremes are showing up now, even on a smaller scale.
- Once a week, check both areas together — are you shifting all your reserve of patience into one of them at the expense of the other?
