# 12. The Hanged Man — the balance point

The point where money and relationships connect in The Hanged Man is fed by the ability to stop and see a situation from another angle. But that same pause, applied where it doesn't belong, turns into passivity disguised as wise waiting.

## Positive

+ **A conscious pause.** Doesn't act under pressure, but waits for the moment a financial decision genuinely ripens.
+ **Giving a situation time to reveal itself.** In relationships, lets circumstances unfold before drawing conclusions.
+ **A fresh perspective.** Sees what others miss because of their own rush and hurry.
+ **Telling a pause apart from being stuck.** Senses when it's worth waiting for the right moment, and when waiting has already become avoidance.
+ **A pause as a choice, not a surrender.** Chooses to wait consciously, not out of fear of taking on responsibility.
+ **Honest self-check.** Asks whether the pause is genuinely letting a decision ripen, or is already a hidden escape.
+ **A deadline for waiting.** Sets a specific time after which a decision gets made regardless of "perfect clarity."
+ **Tolerating uncertainty.** Sits with a period of not-knowing without panicking or forcing premature action.
+ **Willingness to take a small step.** Takes a step toward a decision even when it doesn't yet feel fully ready.
+ **Transparency about where he stands.** Tells the partner what's actually being weighed, instead of hiding behind a silent "let's wait and see."

## Negative

- **Inaction disguised as wisdom.** Passivity around money looks like measured waiting but is actually avoidance.
- **A missed opportunity from delay.** The pause before a financial decision drags on so long that the chance disappears on its own.
- **"Let it work itself out."** Reluctance to commit in the relationship hides behind a phrase about letting things unfold naturally.
- **Fear of responsibility.** Avoiding a clear position is a way of dodging the consequences of a choice.
- **Blaming inaction on "it had to be this way."** Excuses missed opportunities as a necessary wait.
- **A disguised fear of getting it wrong.** Waiting masks a fear of making the wrong call — financial or relational.
- **No clear deadline.** There's no point at which the pause was ever meant to resolve into a decision.
- **Drawn-out uncertainty in the relationship.** The partner goes a long time without a clear position from him.
- **Hiding behind the idea of wisdom.** Uses the notion of "the right moment" to justify inaction.
- **A backlog of unresolved questions.** Several important decisions get postponed at once under the excuse of "not yet the time."

## Recommendations

- Identify one decision you're putting off, and set a clear deadline by which you'll make it regardless of circumstances.
- Ask yourself honestly about the current pause: is it letting the decision ripen, or hiding a fear of making it?
- Take one small step this week toward a decision you've long put off — even if it doesn't yet feel fully ready.
