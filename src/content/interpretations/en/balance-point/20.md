# 20. Judgement — the balance point

The point where money and relationships connect in Judgement is fed by the ability to honestly reassess the road already traveled — to see financial and family patterns for what they are. But that same ability easily turns into excessive judgment of herself and others.

## Positive

+ **Honest analysis of family patterns.** Sees financial patterns absorbed since childhood and consciously rewrites them.
+ **Freedom from what's outdated.** Lets go of scripts that no longer serve her, instead of holding on out of habit.
+ **A clear view of the partner.** Sees herself and the partner honestly, without excessive harshness toward either.
+ **A conclusion instead of self-flagellation.** Ends the reassessment with a concrete conclusion, not an endless rehashing of mistakes.
+ **Forgiving herself.** Forgives a specific past financial mistake and consciously closes the subject.
+ **Moving forward in the relationship.** Doesn't remind the partner of old missteps, and lets the relationship keep moving.
+ **Telling analysis apart from judgment.** Distinguishes an honest look at a situation from judgment that only keeps her stuck in the past.
+ **Freedom from repetition.** Uses conscious conclusions to avoid repeating the same financial mistakes.
+ **Gentleness with herself.** Treats her own missteps without endless self-flagellation.
+ **A completed reassessment.** Draws a conclusion and moves on, instead of getting stuck reviewing the past.

## Negative

- **Endless self-flagellation.** Every failed financial decision gets picked apart again and again without forgiveness.
- **Harsh judgment of the partner.** The partner's flaws or past mistakes get brought up over and over, blocking any forward movement.
- **A family habit of judging kicks in.** A new setback or conflict triggers the habit of harshly judging herself for repeating "the same" mistakes.
- **An unforgiven mistake from the past.** A financial misstep still not forgiven keeps weighing on her.
- **Stuck in the past.** Constantly returning to old missteps keeps the relationship and the finances from developing.
- **Judgment instead of analysis.** Reassessment turns into judgment that only keeps her stuck in the past instead of freeing her from it.
- **Reminding the partner of past mistakes.** Uses old missteps as ammunition in new arguments.
- **Expecting the partner to have measured up.** Judges the loved one for not meeting her inner standards.
- **No sense of closure.** Never draws a final conclusion from an analyzed situation, and keeps coming back to it again and again.
- **Carrying harshness from finances into the relationship.** The same unforgiving inner judge she applies to money gets turned on the partner.

## Recommendations

- Write down one family financial pattern absorbed in childhood, and decide whether it still serves you.
- Forgive yourself one specific financial mistake in writing — and consciously close the subject.
- Next time you feel the urge to remind your partner of a past mistake, stop and ask yourself whether it will actually help you move forward.
