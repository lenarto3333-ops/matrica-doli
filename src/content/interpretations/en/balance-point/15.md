# 15. The Devil — the balance point

The point where money and relationships connect in The Devil is fed by magnetism — the ability to see the true worth of things and draw resource toward himself, whether money or people. But that same power easily turns into a dependency that's hard to walk away from even once it's already doing harm.

## Positive

+ **Seeing true worth.** Senses where the real resource lies and where it's only an illusion — in both money and people.
+ **Steady income through magnetism.** Draws in earnings consciously, without a compulsive need to prove his own worth.
+ **A relationship built on free choice.** Builds deep, honest connections grounded in choice, not dependency.
+ **Conscious use of his pull.** Doesn't keep money or a partner "on a leash."
+ **Telling magnetism apart from dependency.** Sees honestly where drawing in resource is free, and where it's turned into control.
+ **Letting go of control.** Practices trusting that resource and connection hold on their own, without a leash.
+ **Knowing his own "enough."** Understands the point past which earning or the relationship stops being about need and starts being about habit.
+ **Freedom for the partner.** Gives the loved one real moments of free choice, without his control or influence.
+ **Calm without overworking.** Works exactly as much as needed, not out of anxiety about his own worthlessness.
+ **Trust instead of jealousy.** Builds the relationship on confidence rather than constant checking and control of the partner.

## Negative

- **Compulsive earning.** Works more than needed, because without it an anxiety about his own worthlessness sets in.
- **Attachment in the relationship.** Jealousy and attempts to control the partner replace a free choice to be together.
- **Holding on through guilt.** Keeps the partner close through pressure or guilt rather than mutual desire.
- **Financial dependency as a lever of control.** Uses the partner's financial dependency to hold the relationship together.
- **Anxiety from a sense of lack.** An inner feeling of not having enough money, attention, or confirmation of his worth pushes him toward compensating for it.
- **Earning to prove his worth.** Works not from real need but to prove his value to himself and others.
- **A relationship held together by fear.** The partnership exists not through choice but through fear of losing control.
- **Losing the free pull.** Magnetism turns into a compulsion to accumulate money or people.
- **Controlling the pace of earning.** Doesn't allow himself a natural rhythm of work, chasing constant confirmation of his worth instead.
- **Jealousy as the norm.** Constant checking and suspicion become the ordinary backdrop of the relationship.

## Recommendations

- Notice one moment this week when you're working not from real need but from anxiety about your own worth, and pause.
- Give your partner one situation of free choice this week, with no control or influence from you.
- Write down what "enough" means to you in money and in the relationship — and check it against how you're actually living.
