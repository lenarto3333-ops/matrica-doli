# 7. The Chariot — the balance point

The point where money and relationships connect in The Chariot is fed by the will to push forward and overcome obstacles. This energy is locked onto a goal, and its easiest path is to pour its entire drive into career or income, turning the relationship into just another "stop" to get through quickly.

## Positive

+ **Determined movement toward goals.** Pushes toward financial results with confidence, without stalling in the face of difficulty.
+ **Checking direction with the partner.** Aligns the course together instead of moving as though the decision has already been made unilaterally.
+ **The partner as a fellow traveler.** Brings the loved one along on the journey rather than dragging them by force.
+ **Speed set by a shared goal.** Sets the pace not only by personal ambition but by shared plans too.
+ **Holding the course.** Doesn't stall in the face of difficulty — a valuable asset for building steady income.
+ **Conscious slowing down.** Eases off exactly when being present matters more than winning against an obstacle.
+ **Regularly checking the pace.** Asks the partner whether they can keep up with the speed toward the goal.
+ **A shared course.** Moves toward financial goals together with loved ones, rather than leaving them behind.
+ **A managed pace.** Steers the drive so that someone nearby can actually keep up.
+ **Pausing for presence.** Consciously puts the drive on hold for the sake of time together with the partner.

## Negative

- **Career swallows the relationship.** The drive toward the goal takes up so much will and attention that the partner is left behind.
- **The partner cast as the one catching up.** The loved one has to adapt to someone else's pace with no say in it.
- **A fight for control in the relationship.** All the will goes into holding control over the direction of the relationship rather than into financial matters.
- **All-in on overcoming the obstacle.** A competitor, a deadline, or stalled income triggers throwing every resource into the fight while ignoring the people nearby.
- **No checking of the course.** Moves toward the goal without asking whether the pace is still workable for the partner.
- **Chasing results without pauses.** Beating the obstacle becomes more important than being present.
- **The relationship as a stop, not a journey.** The partnership is treated as something to get through quickly on the way to the goal.
- **Exhaustion from constant motion.** There's never a moment when the speed is consciously eased for someone nearby.
- **A one-sided pace.** The course is set unilaterally, without regard to whether the partner can keep up.
- **Lost trust from the chase.** Constantly outpacing the partner erodes the sense of a shared path.

## Recommendations

- Ask your partner directly whether they can keep up with the pace of your financial goals, and adjust the course if needed.
- Choose one evening a week when the drive toward the goal is consciously put on pause for shared time.
- Before your next financial push, talk with your partner about how it will affect the relationship, and what will help you get through that stretch together.
