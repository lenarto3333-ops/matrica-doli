# 8. Justice — Common Relationship Difficulties

## In Brief

The typical relationship challenge of this energy is turning a partnership into a ledger: “I did this, and you failed to do that.” Living dialogue is replaced by an internal tally of merits and faults that is rarely voiced directly but gradually poisons intimacy. Another common knot is honesty used as a weapon: “I'm only telling the truth” becomes a way to inflict pain under the guise of openness.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength behind these challenges is a sincere desire for an honest, equal partnership in which each person fulfills their part of the agreement. The problem arises not from indifference toward the partner, but from the habit of measuring love and care through visible, countable actions.

The second strength behind it is the ability to voice an uncomfortable truth when it is genuinely needed. This makes the person a dependable partner in moments that call for honesty rather than comforting illusions.

## Positive Expression

In its positive expression, this energy resolves common difficulties through open discussion of expectations. Partners say what matters to each of them instead of silently keeping score. Conflicts become an opportunity to clarify the rules of the relationship rather than settle an old account.

In the positive, there is also an ability to forgive once a situation has been clarified and its lesson learned, without returning to it in every subsequent disagreement.

## Negative Expression

In its negative expression, difficulties intensify. Partners begin reminding each other of old faults during new conflicts, turning every disagreement into a trial of the relationship's entire history. They develop a habit of taking offense in silence rather than saying directly what felt unfair.

Another typical negative expression is the need to be right in an argument becoming so powerful that a partner remains defensive even when they understand deep down that they are wrong.

## What Can Pull the Energy into the Negative

Most often, tension escalates when one partner breaks an agreement or promise and the other feels this is not the first time. A second trigger is a situation in which a partner receives support or recognition while the person feels their own contribution has gone unnoticed.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is useful to abandon the internal accounting of merits and speak about a specific need in the here and now rather than reciting the entire list of past offenses. The second step is to examine one's intention before telling a partner an “honest” truth: is it to clarify the situation, or to hurt them after all?

## Main Task

The main task is to learn to build fairness in relationships through dialogue and mutual trust rather than scorekeeping and judgment, trusting that balance in a couple is restored through shared effort, not one person's victory over the other.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Do I mentally keep a list of how much each of us has done for the relationship?
- When did I last use “honesty” to hurt rather than clarify?
- Can I forgive my partner fully, or do I return to the same story again and again?

## Practical Plan

1. Tell your partner about one specific need you have now, without listing past offenses.
2. Before your next “honest” statement, check your intention and soften the wording if necessary.
3. Consciously bring closure to one old grievance in a conversation with your partner this week.
