# 13. Death — Common Relationship Difficulties

## Short summary

A typical difficulty of this energy in a relationship is the inability to complete in time what has already exhausted itself: a person clings to a relationship for years that has not brought joy for a long time, out of fear of loneliness or the unknown. The second typical knot is abrupt, destructive breaks: instead of a gradual, honest ending, the person suddenly ends the relationship, leaving the partner in shock and pain.

## Natural force

The natural force behind these difficulties is the ability to sense when a phase of a relationship is truly over and needs honest recognition rather than further prolongation. The problem does not arise from cruelty, but from the fact that the fear of the unknown or the pain of separation often makes one either endure too long or act too harshly.

The second power behind this is the ability to help a partner go through difficult transformations, support in crises and changes, when others run away from difficulties.

## Manifestation in the plus

On the plus side, this energy solves typical difficulties through an honest and gradual ending of what is no longer working: the partner recognizes when the relationship or a certain stage of it has exhausted itself, and speaks about it openly, rather than silently suffering or suddenly disappearing. The ability to support each other in crises becomes the basis of trust, not a source of additional pain.

## Manifestation in the minus

In the negative, the difficulties are exacerbated: the partner stays in a relationship for years, which has not brought satisfaction for a long time, fearing a breakup more than chronic dissatisfaction, or, on the contrary, abruptly and without explanation breaks off the connection, leaving the other person in confusion. Conflicts arise around unpredictability - the partner never knows whether the relationship will end gradually or with a sudden blow.

## What can be negative

Most often, the aggravation happens at the moment when the relationship objectively needs to change or end a certain stage, and the fear of the unknown blocks any honest conversation about it. The second trigger is the accumulated irritation of long-suffering that explodes in a sharp, destructive breakup instead of a gradual, conscious goodbye.

## How to return energy to the plus side

It is useful to honestly and timely talk to your partner about the fact that a certain stage of the relationship has exhausted itself, instead of silent patience or a sudden break. The second step is to learn to live the transformation in the relationship gradually, giving yourself and your partner time to adapt, and not forcing sudden changes.

## The main task

The main task is to learn to complete what has really exhausted yourself with respect and honesty, preserving dignity and caring for your partner even at the moment of separation or deep change.

## Questions for self-reflection

- What relationships or their stages do I keep out of fear, even though they haven't brought joy for a long time?
- Do I warn my partner honestly about impending changes, or do I allow tension to build up to a sharp break?
- What exactly scares me in an honest conversation about ending a certain stage of a relationship?

## Practical plan

1. Identify one topic in the relationship that needs honest closure or transformation, and schedule a conversation about it.
2. The next time you feel the urge to abruptly end everything, give yourself a break and think about a gradual, honest transition.
3. Discuss with your partner one way to support each other during future changes or crises in the relationship.
