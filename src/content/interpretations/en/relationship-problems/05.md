# 5. The Hierophant — Common Relationship Difficulties

## In Brief

The typical difficulty of this energy in relationships is care turning into instruction. A partner may sincerely want to help and share experience, yet do so in a way that makes the other person feel like a student constantly being corrected rather than an equal. A second common knot is difficulty accepting that a partner may be right when their view differs from convention, turning simple disagreements into prolonged arguments about the “right” way to live.

## Natural Strength

The strength behind these difficulties is deep respect for knowledge and experience. The person genuinely wants the relationship built on something reliable and tested rather than chaotic decisions. The problem arises not from indifference, but from excessive certainty in being right that leaves too little room for the partner's own path.

A second strength is the ability to provide stable support through difficult moments, making this person a reliable long-term partner even when current arguments feel intense.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, this energy resolves its difficulties through open discussion of the boundaries of advice. Partners agree on when one person's opinion is truly wanted and when it is better simply to listen without comment. Conflict becomes an opportunity to clarify communication rather than trade accusations of condescension.

The positive also brings the ability to acknowledge when a partner's unfamiliar perspective proves more accurate and accept this without feeling that personal authority has been lost.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, difficulties intensify: advice feels like condescension or distrust of the partner's ability to decide independently, and conflicts arise over “who knows better” in parenting, finances, or household decisions. Arguments turn into battles over whose position is more correct instead of searches for shared solutions.

Another common negative expression is a partner's accumulated silent irritation. They gradually tire of constant instruction but do not dare say so until tension reaches a critical point.

## What Can Pull This Energy into the Negative

Escalation most often occurs when a partner chooses something that contradicts the other person's accumulated experience or beliefs, intensifying the desire to intervene with advice. A second trigger is previous disappointment after trusting someone else's method.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It helps to openly name when advice begins to feel like a lecture and jointly find a way to share experience that genuinely helps rather than diminishes. A second step is to regularly check whether the relationship's established “right” ways still suit both partners rather than only one.

## Main Task

The main task is to distinguish a genuine wish to help a partner from the need to prove personal competence or correctness.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- In which situations does my advice most often feel like a lecture to my partner?
- What am I truly trying to prove when I insist that I am right in the relationship?
- Do I give my partner room to be right when they are right?

## Practical Plan

1. Talk with your partner about where the boundary between care and lecturing feels violated.
2. Once this week, consciously refrain from giving advice your partner did not request.
3. Ask your partner directly whether anything remains unspoken because they are tired of constant instruction.
