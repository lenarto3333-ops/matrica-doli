# 3. The Empress — Common Relationship Difficulties

## In Brief

The typical difficulty of this energy in relationships is self-sacrifice: caring for a partner gradually displaces care for oneself, while unspoken exhaustion accumulates into hidden resentment. A second common knot is care that imperceptibly turns into overprotection, causing the partner to feel controlled where love was intended.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength behind these difficulties is a sincere desire to create warmth and comfort for a partner. The problem arises not from insincerity, but because care is given without measure and without regularly checking whether enough resources remain for oneself.

A second strength behind this is the ability to be a stable, warm source of support in difficult moments, making this person a reliable partner over the long term.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, this energy resolves its typical difficulties through open conversation about the boundaries of care. Partners agree on what help is genuinely needed and what becomes excessive caretaking. Conflict becomes an opportunity to clarify how care is given, not a reason for mutual accusations.

The positive also brings the ability to receive a partner's care as generously as one gives it, completing the cycle of one-sided self-sacrifice.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the difficulties intensify: the partner experiences care as pressure or control, and arguments arise around personal space and independence. Meanwhile, the person providing care feels that their efforts are undervalued and accumulates hidden exhaustion.

Another typical negative expression is substituting material care for emotional closeness—gifts, financial support, and domestic caretaking in place of the sincere, simple connection the partner actually needs.

## What Can Pull This Energy into the Negative

Escalation occurs most often when invested care goes unrecognized or when the partner directly asks for less caretaking, which is experienced as rejection of a sincere gift. A second trigger is a prolonged lack of care in return, gradually exhausting the person who always gives.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It helps to discuss openly with a partner what kind of care is truly needed instead of acting on assumptions. A second step is to check your own resources regularly and allow yourself to receive care from your partner as readily as you give it.

## Main Task

The main task is to learn to give care without control and receive it without guilt, building a mutual rather than one-sided cycle of care in the relationship.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Does my partner experience my care as support, or sometimes as control?
- How much energy remains for me after I care for my partner?
- Can I receive my partner's care in return, or do I always remain the one who gives?

## Practical Plan

1. Ask your partner directly what kind of care they truly need right now instead of acting on assumptions.
2. Once this week, allow your partner to care for you without trying to immediately “repay” them.
3. Write down one situation in which exhaustion accumulated because your care went unrecognized, and discuss it gently with your partner.
