# 20. Judgement — Common Relationship Difficulties

## Brief Essence

A typical difficulty with this energy in a relationship is overly harsh judgment directed at oneself or one's partner, where one mistake or misdeed is discussed over and over instead of being lived and let go. The second typical knot is family scenarios from childhood, which are imperceptibly lost in the couple, bringing there foreign, unlived conflicts.

## Natural Strength

The natural force behind these difficulties is the ability to see the relationship honestly and clearly, to trust intuition and draw on the support of family experience instead of acting blindly. The problem is not honesty itself, but the fact that it becomes a permanent judgment rather than a point of reconciliation.

## Expression in the Positive

On the plus side, this energy makes the relationship a space for honest but careful conversations: partners can openly admit mistakes, forgive each other, and draw a joint conclusion from the experience, rather than a collection of mutual accusations. Difficult periods pass more easily, because there is trust that the past can be honestly closed and we can move on together.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, the difficulties are exacerbated: one of the partners can endlessly "judge" the other for an old mistake, not letting it become the past, or, on the contrary, judge himself excessively harshly, closing himself off from the partner due to shame. It is also possible to repeat family scenarios - the style of quarrels, silent insults or claims, learned from the parental family, which the couple reproduces without realizing it.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

Most often, the aggravation happens when the couple is not in the habit of ending the conflict honestly, and each new argument imperceptibly brings with it the whole list of old resentments. The second trigger is an unfinished relationship with the parents or family of one of the partners, which is projected onto the couple instead of being worked on separately.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is useful to learn to end conflicts with an honest conclusion, and not to leave them to smolder and return to them in every new quarrel. The second step is to notice when the response in the relationship is actually from an old family script, rather than from the current partner, and consciously separate one from the other.

## Main Task

The main task is to learn to judge yourself and your partner honestly, but not cruelly, turning your experiences into a shared resource of trust, and not a list of mutual accusations.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Is there an old mistake or grudge in our relationship that I bring up in my mind or out loud over and over again?
- How much does my reaction to a partner really belong to the current situation, and not to a long-standing family experience?

## Practical Plan

1. Choose one old grudge in the relationship and consciously agree with your partner to consider it closed after an honest conversation.
2. During the week, notice the moments when your reaction resembles a family scenario from childhood, and say it out loud to yourself or a partner.
3. Once a month, check together whether there are unspoken resentments in the couple that should be honestly completed, and not carried silently.
