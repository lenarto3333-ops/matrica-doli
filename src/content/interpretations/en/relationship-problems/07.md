# 7. The Chariot — Common Relationship Difficulties

## In Brief

A common relationship difficulty for this energy is allowing care or initiative to turn into control. One partner may sincerely want to help or accelerate the couple's progress, yet do so in a way that leaves the other feeling like a passenger rather than an equal participant in decisions. Another common knot is treating the relationship as a hidden competition, in which one partner's achievements or opinions are involuntarily compared with the other's.

## Natural Strength

The natural strength behind these difficulties is a sincere desire to move the relationship forward and protect one's partner in difficult moments. The problem arises not from indifference, but from the habit of taking responsibility for the direction without always leaving the partner room to choose for themselves.

Another underlying strength is the ability to mobilize for a shared goal, making this person a dependable partner in a crisis even when everyday disagreements may feel sharp.

## Positive Expression

In its positive expression, this energy resolves its typical difficulties through open conversation about how initiative is shared. Partners agree on who leads in which area and trust each other's pace where the other is stronger. Conflict becomes an opportunity to clarify the boundaries of support rather than a struggle over who is in charge.

The positive expression also brings the ability to celebrate a partner's successes as shared victories rather than perceive them as a hidden comparison or threat to one's own position.

## Negative Expression

In its negative expression, the difficulties intensify: one partner's initiative is perceived by the other as pressure or imposition, and disputes arise over the right to make decisions concerning domestic life, finances, or raising children. Arguments may turn into a struggle for control instead of a search for a shared solution.

Another common negative expression is the accumulation of silent exhaustion in the partner who gradually feels led rather than equal, yet does not dare to say so directly until the tension reaches a critical point.

## What Can Pull the Energy into the Negative

Tensions most often intensify when a partner makes an independent decision that conflicts with the other person's expectations, strengthening the impulse to intervene and take control. Another trigger is comparing one's own or a partner's achievements with those of other couples.

## How to Return the Energy to the Positive

It is helpful to name openly the moment when care begins to feel like control and agree together which partner takes the initiative in which areas. The second step is to acknowledge a partner's successes sincerely and regularly, without comparing them inwardly with one's own achievements.

## Main Task

The main task is to learn to guide the relationship together with one's partner rather than in their place, trusting that shared movement is possible without constant personal control over its direction.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- In which situations is my initiative most often perceived by my partner as pressure?
- Do I experience my partner's successes as a shared joy or as a hidden comparison?
- Do I give my partner genuine room to make decisions in the areas where they are stronger?

## Practical Plan

1. Have a conversation with your partner about the areas in which each of you takes initiative and makes decisions.
2. Once this week, consciously release control over a decision your partner is capable of making independently.
3. Sincerely acknowledge one of your partner's successes this week without comparing it with your own achievements.
