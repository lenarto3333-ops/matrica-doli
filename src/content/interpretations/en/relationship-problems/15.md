# 15. The Devil — Common Relationship Difficulties

## Brief Essence

A typical difficulty of this energy in relationships is dependent, possessive relationships: partners cling to each other not out of free choice, but out of fear of loss or habit, and the relationship gradually turns into a kind of cage for both. The second typical node is jealousy and covert manipulation, where love and control are intertwined so closely that it is difficult to tell them apart.

## Natural Strength

The natural force behind these difficulties is deep passion and the ability to see through a partner, to feel his true desires and fears even when he is silent. The problem is not because of this closeness itself, but because the fear of loss turns a sincere vision into a tool of containment and control.

## Expression in the Positive

In the positive, this energy creates a relationship of deep, honest passion: partners are attracted to each other freely, openly admit their desires, and trust each other without the need to control. Intimacy is built on sincere interest in the true essence of another person, and not on the fear of losing it.

## Expression in the Negative

In the negative, difficulties intensify: jealousy appears without a real reason, attempts to control the partner's place, time or environment, hidden manipulations to maintain the relationship. Dependent attachment is also possible, when a person remains in a relationship that has not satisfied him for a long time, because he is afraid of loneliness more than pain.

## What Can Push You Into the Negative

Aggravation often occurs when one partner experiences a deep fear of abandonment and tries to compensate by controlling or undercover pressure on the other. The second trigger is a relationship experience in the past where trust was betrayed, so that the new intimacy is automatically perceived as a threat that needs to be kept under control.

## How to Return Energy to the Positive

It's helpful to learn to distinguish true intimacy from fear of loss and honestly call your partner jealous or anxious instead of acting out of control or innuendo. The second step is to consciously give each other space and freedom, testing the relationship not by the frequency of control, but by the quality of sincerity between partners.

## Main Task

The main task is to learn to build a passionate, deep closeness based on trust and freedom, and not on the fear of loss or a hidden desire to possess another person.

## Questions for Self-Reflection

- Where in the relationship do I try to control my partner out of fear of loss, rather than out of real need?
- Do I stay in a relationship because of free choice or because of fear of loneliness?

## Practical Plan

1. Identify one recent situation of jealousy or control and honestly tell your partner the anxiety behind it.
2. Consciously give your partner a little more space this week and watch your own reaction to it.
3. Once a month, honestly assess whether the relationship is based on free choice or on the fear of being alone.
